\begin{abstract}
EXHALE carries H$_2$ as a single species. The state vector holds one number
density for it, the network reacts it as one reagent, and no rovibrational
level is transported. Every quantity that depends on how the molecules are
distributed over $(v,J)$ therefore has that distribution supplied by
assumption, and the assumption is not the same in every place. The infrared
line cooling is explicitly LTE and says so in its own function name; the
thermal dissociation and its three-body reverse rest on vibrational
equilibrium and are now built from one another through the equilibrium
constant, so that axis is consistent by construction; the Lyman--Werner
photodissociation rate no longer imports a fit at all: it reads a table of a
line-by-line calculation, with the lines absorbing each other's beam, run at
the temperature and density of this layer, and states where that calculation
itself stops being a value; and the H$_3^+$
cooling, alone among the four, carries an explicit departure factor. This memo
records each assumption, the published evidence behind it, the range over which
it holds, and where the layer EXHALE actually computes leaves that range.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is and is not carried}

H$_2$ enters the state vector as species \texttt{isp\_H2} of
\texttt{src/modules/init/species\_table.f90}, one of the four molecular
columns (\texttt{H2}, \texttt{H2p}, \texttt{H3p}, \texttt{HeHp}) that the
molecular option adds. It is written to \texttt{output/Ion\_species.txt}
and \texttt{output/Ion\_species\_adv.txt} as the number density of the
molecule; the four molecular columns are the last four of the file, but their
absolute position depends on which metal elements the run carries, so they
must be located from the \texttt{\# columns} schema header rather than
counted.

What the state vector does \emph{not} carry is any resolution of the
rovibrational ladder. There is one H$_2$ continuity equation, not one per
level, and no state-to-state collisional network. Nothing in the code
transports a vibrational temperature, an ortho/para ratio, or a level
population of any kind. The rovibrational ladder appears twice as
\emph{tabulated data}: the partition function of the equilibrium constant
(\texttt{mol\_rates.f90}) and the line list of the infrared cooling
(\texttt{molecular\_infrared\_data.f90}), but in both places it is
evaluated at the local kinetic temperature and never solved for.

The consequence is the subject of this memo. A rate coefficient measured on
one distribution of levels does not transfer to another, and EXHALE uses
coefficients measured under at least three different distributions in the same
cell. Each of them is defensible where it is used, and the point of collecting
them is to say exactly where that is.

\subsection{The layer the assumptions are evaluated in, and what the numbers
below are read from}
\label{sec:layer}

Every claim about whether a fit is used inside or outside its range needs a
statement of the conditions EXHALE actually reaches. The reference case here
is \texttt{mol\_lyman\_werner} of the regression matrix (the hot-Uranus
molecular base with \texttt{Stellar LW flux [erg/cm2/s]: 343.0}) because
it is the only default case that carries the Lyman--Werner channel.
Measured on its \texttt{output/Lyman\_Werner.txt} and
\texttt{output/Ion\_species.txt}:

with $x_{\rm H_2} = 2n_{\rm H_2}/n_{\rm H}$ and $n_{\rm H}$ the total hydrogen
nucleus density, over the $500$ physical cells only (the innermost ghost at
$r = 0.9998$ is excluded); the H$_2$ density itself peaks at

\paragraph{These are relaxation snapshots, not converged solutions.}
Thirteen of the sixteen default regression cases are pinned by a
\texttt{maxsteps} file and stop there rather than at a convergence criterion:
the seven molecular ones and \texttt{lower\_profile} at $12{,}000$ steps,
\texttt{oxygen\_chemistry} at $1000$, \texttt{hydrostatic\_column} at $300$
and the three \texttt{hp\_*} seeds at $100$. \texttt{mol\_lyman\_werner} ends
at \texttt{du} $= 0.785$, i.e.\ a $78.5\%$ radial spread in the mass flux
$\rho v r^2$, well short of the $10^{-3}$ threshold a converged run meets;
only the three \texttt{wasp} cases run to a stop of their own, and only
\texttt{wasp\_full\_newton} certifies. Every profile number in this memo
therefore describes a relaxation snapshot. That is adequate for the purpose
(the question is which decade of density and temperature a fit is being asked
about, not the fourth digit), but it is not adequate for quoting a mass-loss
rate, and none is quoted here.

\paragraph{The snapshots are the goldens.} The numbers above come from the
case output directories, and those outputs now agree with
\texttt{backup/regression/golden/}: \texttt{mol\_lyman\_werner}'s
\texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} and \texttt{Ion\_species.txt} were compared line for
line against the snapshot and are identical. This paragraph replaces one
saying that every default case differed from its golden, which was true while
the goldens predated a series of uncommitted source changes; the goldens were
re-snapshotted on 2026-09-10, and \texttt{lower\_profile} again the same day
after the element-projection mass fix.

\section{The level-distribution assumptions}

\subsection{Infrared line cooling: LTE, stated explicitly}
\label{sec:ir}

\texttt{molecular\_infrared\_cooling.f90} evaluates the H$_2$ line emission
as a Boltzmann sum,
%
\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm H_2}(T) = \sum_l f_u(T)\, A_l\, \Delta E_l ,
  \qquad
  f_u(T) = \frac{g_u\,e^{-T_u/T}}{Z(T)},
  \qquad
  Z(T) = \sum_{\rm lev} g\, e^{-T_{\rm lev}/T},
\end{equation}
%
and names the routine that returns it \texttt{h2\_line\_emission\_lte}. There
is no ambiguity about the assumption here; the question is only where it
holds.

The line list is Roueff et al.\ (2019), A\&A 630, A58, table 2 (electric
quadrupole plus magnetic dipole), and the level ladder comes from the same
table. As carried in \texttt{molecular\_infrared\_data.f90} it is
\texttt{n\_h2\_lev} $= 302$ levels and \texttt{n\_h2\_line} $= 1833$ lines.

Rotational levels are not the constraint: they thermalize at densities far
below anything in this layer. The vibrational levels are. The code records the
comparison at its own site. The critical density of $v=1$ is
$n_{\rm crit} = A_{10}/\gamma_{10}$ (Hollenbach \& McKee 1979, ApJS 41, 555,
p.~576), and with their $A_{10} = 8.3\times10^{-7}$~s$^{-1}$ (p.~583,
$J$-averaged, from Turner, Kirby-Docken \& Dalgarno 1977) and the rate
coefficients of their eq.~(6.29) it is $2\times10^{6}$--$6\times10^{6}$~cm$^{-3}$
over $900$--$1350$~K with H$_2$ as the collider and
$5\times10^{4}$--$8\times10^{4}$~cm$^{-3}$ with atomic H. The measured base
density of the molecular gate case, $n({\rm H_2}) = 5.15\times10^{12}$~cm$^{-3}$,
is six to eight decades above either.

Two caveats on that comparison. Neither Hollenbach \& McKee nor Burton,
Hollenbach \& Tielens (1990) gives a \emph{helium} collision rate for
H$_2$ $v=1$ (their colliders are H, H$_2$ and, in the latter, electrons),
so a helium-dominated gas is outside both sources; omitting helium from the
collider sum understates the de-excitation rate and therefore errs against
claiming LTE, not for it. And the figure quoted here previously,
$10^{9}$--$10^{11}$~cm$^{-3}$, carried no citation and is three to five
decades too high; correcting it widens the margin rather than narrowing it.

Above the base the density falls, but so does the temperature, and the
vibrational bands then carry a small share of the emission. That is the
quantitative statement that makes the LTE overestimate harmless where LTE is
least justified, and it can be evaluated directly from the line list: sum the
emission of the lines whose upper level has $v \geq 1$ and divide by the
total. Table~\ref{tab:vibshare} does so.

The reading is that the LTE assumption is safe at the base, where the density
supports it, and that where the density no longer supports it the vibrational
bands (the levels the density fails to thermalize) have already stopped
carrying the emission. Below about $500$~K the error LTE can make is bounded
by a few percent of the total, because that is all the vibrational levels
contribute.

\subsection{Thermal dissociation and three-body recombination: vibrational
equilibrium}
\label{sec:diss}

The pair R12 (H$_2$ + M $\rightarrow$ H + H + M) and R15 (H + H + M
$\rightarrow$ H$_2$ + M) is one channel run in two directions. Since the
change recorded below, \texttt{mol\_rates.f90} builds R12 from R15 through the
equilibrium constant,
%
\begin{equation}
  k_{\rm diss}(T) = \frac{k_{\rm rec}(T)}{K_{\rm eq}(T)},
  \qquad
  K_{\rm eq}(T) = \frac{n_{\rm H_2}}{n_{\rm H}^2}\ [\mathrm{cm^3}],
\end{equation}
%
rather than transcribing an independent dissociation fit.

That construction is what makes this axis consistent. $K_{\rm eq}$ is built in
\texttt{h2\_thermochemistry\_init} from the translational partition functions
and the \emph{rovibrational} internal partition function \texttt{q\_rovib\_H2},
summed over the Morse ladder of H$_2$ $X\,^1\Sigma_g^+$ with the ortho/para
nuclear-spin weights. It therefore contains vibrational excitation explicitly:
both sides of the equilibrium refer to a vibrationally equilibrated gas, and
the reverse rate built from it inherits that and nothing else.

The measured coefficient it is anchored on refers to the same state, and the
source says so. Breshears \& Bird (1973), 14th Symposium (International) on
Combustion, 211, read their initial rate off the minimum of the postshock
density-gradient dip, which they identify as ``the rate corresponding to
conditions of vibrational equilibrium in the thermal sense but with negligible
extent of dissociation''. The same paper bounds where its rate law applies:
the ``observation of induction periods for diatomic dissociation clearly
indicates that the assumptions implicit in Eqs.\ (2)--(4) are not valid during
the very early course of the reaction; nevertheless, they may be applicable
during the `steady state' which follows the induction period.'' A
hydrodynamic model that resolves nothing faster than a vibrational relaxation
time is in that steady state throughout. Their Eq.\ (3), $k_d/k_r = K_{\rm
eq}$, states the detailed-balance relation outright, so deriving one direction
from the other is the relation the measured coefficient already carries rather
than an assumption added here.

The critical density for vibrational thermalization, $10^{4}$--$10^{8}$~
cm$^{-3}$ depending on the collider, sits many orders below the
$10^{13}$--$10^{15}$~cm$^{-3}$ of the base, which is the independent check
that the premise holds where the rate is evaluated.

\paragraph{Status of this change.} R12 as detailed balance is present in the
working tree and \emph{not} in the last commit: \texttt{git show
HEAD:src/modules/lower\_atmosphere/mol\_rates.f90} still carries the
independent Baulch et al.\ (1992) fit $1.5\times10^{-9}e^{-48350/T}$, and
the changelog carried no entry for the
change. It is therefore behind the code on this point.

\subsection{Lyman--Werner self-shielding: no longer an imported fit}
\label{sec:lw}

This was the weakest of the four assumptions, and it is the one that has been
replaced by a calculation rather than better-argued. \texttt{lyman\_werner.f90}
took the self-shielding factor from Draine \& Bertoldi (1996), ApJ 468, 269
(hereafter DB96), their eq.~(37); for a time it took it from the temperature-%
dependent fit of Richings, Schaye \& Oppenheimer (2014) instead. Both are
closed forms of the same two-term shape, both were built for interstellar gas,
and neither had ever been tested at the temperature of a planetary molecular
base.

\paragraph{What was done instead.} Both fits were measured against a
level-resolved \textsc{Cloudy} c25.00 calculation run on our own grid:
$T = 700$--$3200$~K at $n_{\rm H} = 10^{12}$--$10^{14}$~cm$^{-3}$, columns to
$5\times10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$, pure H/He, no dust, thermal Doppler width, and our
own band flux, and neither survived it. Over $10^{18} \le N_{\rm H_2} \le
5\times10^{20}$~cm$^{-2}$, the columns where our H$_2$ sits, DB96 runs from
$1.85\times$ high at $900$~K to $2.3\times$ low at $2700$~K, and Richings is
low by $3.3$--$4.3\times$ at every temperature of the layer. The failure is
one of \emph{shape} and not of level: the calculated
$f_{\rm shield}(N_{\rm H_2})$ has a trough near $10^{16}$--$10^{17}$ and a
shoulder near $10^{19}$~cm$^{-2}$ that a two-term algebraic form of this kind
has no freedom to place, so no rescaling of either fit could have worked.

\paragraph{And then the \textsc{Cloudy} calculation was replaced as well
(2026-09-03).} It carried two errors of opposite sign that crossed near
$N_{\rm H_2} = 2\times10^{20}$~cm$^{-2}$ and cancelled below it. First, the
quantity tabulated was \textsc{Cloudy}'s \texttt{Shield(H2)} column, which is
the dissociation rate of H2g alone (the sub-reservoir $v = 0$, $J \le 8$
that \textsc{Cloudy} splits off at \texttt{ENERGY\_H2\_STAR} $= 4100$
cm$^{-1}$) divided by the H2g density, while EXHALE carries one H$_2$
species and applied it to all of it. The vibrationally excited part starts
from levels $10^{2}$ to $10^{3}$ times less populated, saturates far later,
and keeps dissociating after the H2g lines have gone black; weighting the two
reservoirs by their own densities raises the rate by up to a factor $11$ at
$N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{19}$~cm$^{-2}$. Second, \textsc{Cloudy} applies the
Federman, Glassgold \& Kwan (1979) shielding function to each transition using
that transition's own optical depth, so no line absorbs any other line's beam.
Sec.~135 of \texttt{Update\_EXHALE} and
\texttt{md/p38\_line\_overlap\_shielding.md} are the measurement.

What is tabulated now is a line-by-line calculation of the same three
quantities on the same $39 \times 9 \times 3$ grid: a plane-parallel slab, a
normally incident beam, flat $F_\lambda$ normalized to
$F = 343$~erg~cm$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ over $912$--$1110$~\AA, and
$\tau(\nu, N) = N \sum_i x_i \sigma_i(\nu)$ summed over every Lyman and Werner
transition between $911.75$ and $1200$~\AA\ on a $4\times10^{5}$-point
frequency grid, so every line is shielded by every other line. The profiles
are Voigt with $b = (2kT/m_{\rm H_2})^{1/2}$, the populations LTE at the
imposed $T$, and the line data are Abgrall, Roueff \& Drira (2000), the same
measurements \textsc{Cloudy}'s \texttt{dissprob\_*} and \texttt{transprob\_*}
files and the Meudon PDR code carry, so no line-list difference enters the
comparison. \texttt{h2\_self\_shielding\_table.f90} holds
$\log_{10}\sigma_{\rm diss}$, $\log_{10} p_{\rm single}$ and
$\log_{10} p_{\rm eff}$, trilinear in
$(\log_{10} T, \log_{10} n_{\rm H}, \log_{10} N_{\rm H_2})$, with every axis
clamped rather than extrapolated. The generator is
\texttt{src/utils/h2\_shielding\_table\_line\_by\_line.py};
\texttt{md/h2\_self\_shielding\_cloudy.md} and
\texttt{src/utils/h2\_shielding\_table\_from\_cloudy.py} record the
\textsc{Cloudy} runs, which still supply the fluorescent trapping.

\paragraph{The one piece that is not line-by-line.} The trapping ratio
$p_{\rm eff}/p_{\rm single}$ (the suppression of the branching by
re-absorption of the fluorescent decay photons) is read node by node from
the same $27$ \textsc{Cloudy} runs and multiplied in, rather than
reimplemented as a second and unvalidated version of the same thing. Its
geometry is plane-parallel, illuminated on one face and closed on the other.
Our layer has the same thick-inward side, which is the side that matters, but
it is spherical and open outward. This is the only ingredient of the table
that carries a geometry our layer does not have, and it is an open item.

\paragraph{What the level distribution now is.} It is an LTE population at the
imposed $T$, not one imported from a $10^{2}$~K interstellar front, and the
assumption is checked rather than asserted: at $n_{\rm H} \geq
10^{12}$~cm$^{-3}$ the collisional rates exceed the pumping rate by orders of
magnitude, and the \textsc{Cloudy} runs on the same grid, which solve the
populations, return departure coefficients of $1.000$ at every level. That is
what our base is, and it is precisely the population no published fit was
built on. The open item this subsection used to carry, ``a calculation of
$f_{\rm shield}$ on a $10^{3}$~K collisionally equilibrated population would
settle whether the fit transfers'', has been done, and the answer was that it
does not.

\paragraph{Three axes, now measured rather than assumed.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Density.} The line-by-line pumping has no density in it at all:
  the populations are LTE, so the tabulated $\sigma_{\rm pump} =
  \sigma_{\rm diss}/p_{\rm eff}$ is identical to every stored digit across
  $n_{\rm H} = 10^{12}$--$10^{14}$~cm$^{-3}$. The axis survives only because
  the trapping ratio came from \textsc{Cloudy}, and through it
  $f_{\rm shield} = \sigma_{\rm diss}(N)/\sigma_{\rm diss}(N_{\min})$ moves by
  at most $2.5\%$ below $N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{19}$~cm$^{-2}$ and by up to $23\%$
  at $10^{21}$: measured on the installed table. The absence of a density
  argument in either published fit was never what was wrong with them.
\item \textbf{Radiation field.} One incident flux only,
  $F = 343$~erg~cm$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ over the band. With LTE populations the
  line-by-line shielding cannot depend on it; in the \textsc{Cloudy} runs,
  where the populations were solved, dropping the band flux by four decades
  ($G_0$ from $1.25\times10^{6}$ to $125$) moved $f_{\rm shield}$ by at most
  $9\%$ anywhere and by under $5\%$ below $N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{20}$. There is no
  radiation-field axis to carry.
\item \textbf{Doppler parameter.} Thermal only, $b = (2kT/m_{\rm H_2})^{1/2}$,
  $2.7$--$4.7$~km~s$^{-1}$ over the grid: the same expression the code's
  own \texttt{h2\_doppler\_parameter} evaluates, so the table and the code
  agree on it by construction. The table needs no $b$ argument: $b$ is a
  function of $T$, which is already an axis.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Where the table stops being a value, and why that is the honest
statement.} There is no longer a column above which the tabulated factor is an
upper bound. Line overlap (what DB96 \S5.2 say produces ``the rapid falloff
\ldots\ for $N_2 > 10^{20}$~cm$^{-2}$'') is inside the calculation, and at
the columns a planetary molecular base reaches it is not a correction but the
dominant term. Two calculations differing only in whether the lines absorb
each other's beam give, at $1300$~K, ratios of $0.62$ at
$N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{19}$, $0.33$ at $10^{20}$, $0.042$ at $10^{21}$ and
$0.0016$ at $4.4\times10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$.

\texttt{h2\_shielding\_overlap\_column}, which returned the column at which
the summed equivalent width of the pumping lines reached the whole band
($1.4$--$1.8\times10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$ at $700$~K falling to
$2.5\times10^{20}$ at $3200$~K), is gone from the module together with the
plane it interpolated. The boundary it drew was about a decade optimistic: at
the column it returned, the one-line-at-a-time table already stood $7$ to $10$
times above the overlapping-line answer.

What replaces it is a grid boundary and nothing more.
\texttt{h2\_shield\_max\_column()} returns the top of the tabulated column
axis, $5.0\times10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$, above which the edge value is used, and
the two run-time warnings that quoted the overlap column now report that
extrapolation instead. Our base sits at $N_{\rm H_2} = 4.4\times10^{21}$,
inside the axis; only the transient excursion to $5.3\times10^{21}$ crosses
it.

\paragraph{What the calculation was checked against.} At $100$~K it reproduces
the DB96 self-shielding function to $2$--$9$ percent over
$10^{16} \le N_{\rm H_2} \le 10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$. That fit rests on a
calculation which includes overlap (their \S5.2), and it is the one published
overlap-including curve there is to check against; the same calculation run
one line at a time departs from DB96 exactly where DB96 say overlap sets in,
by $3.3\times$ at $10^{21}$ and $12\times$ at $4.4\times10^{21}$. Against a
second code that carries overlap: the Meudon PDR code (Le Petit et al.\
2006) with its exact UV line transfer, $30$ H$_2$ levels and $20$ global
iterations on pure H/He at $n_{\rm H} = 10^{13}$~cm$^{-3}$: the two agree
to $5$--$22$ percent over $10^{19} \le N_{\rm H_2} \le 10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$ at
$900$, $1300$ and $1800$~K. At the illuminated face, the lowest tabulated
column $N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{12}$~cm$^{-2}$, the absolute quantities are
$\sigma_{\rm pump} = 2.59\times10^{-17}$ ($700$~K) to
$2.88\times10^{-17}$~cm$^2$ ($3200$~K) against DB96's own
$3.4520\times10^{-18}/0.135 = 2.557\times10^{-17}$~cm$^2$, and
$p_{\rm single} = 0.143$ to $0.159$, which at $1300$~K is $0.1475$ against
$0.1465$ from the \textsc{Cloudy} runs. The Fortran table reproduces the
Python calculation at all $1053$ grid points to $3.1\times10^{-9}$ relative on
$\sigma_{\rm diss}$.

\paragraph{The rate is the tabulated cross section itself (2026-09-02).} The
module no longer multiplies a constant cross section by this factor. What is
tabulated is $\sigma_{\rm diss}(N_{\rm H_2}, T, n_{\rm H})$, the dissociation
cross section per \emph{incident} band photon, with the self-shielding and the
trapping of the fluorescent decay photons both inside it; $f_{\rm shield}$
survives as the ratio $\sigma_{\rm diss}(N)/\sigma_{\rm diss}(N_{\min})$, a
diagnostic. The earlier reading of the same runs (that $\sigma_{\rm LW}$
was $23$--$60\%$ low and $\langle p_{\rm diss}\rangle$ $31\%$ high) was a
comparison of two different quantities: the line data agree to $0.8$ percent,
and the gap is line trapping. One absorption has \emph{two} branching ratios
and the code now carries both from the same table: $p_{\rm single}$, the
untrapped $D/(D+\sum A_{ul})$, which fixes how many fluorescent decays
accompany each dissociation, and $p_{\rm eff}$, which fixes how many band
photons the beam loses per dissociation. Sec.~132 of \texttt{Update\_EXHALE}
and \texttt{md/p39\_lw\_cross\_section\_sources.md} carry the derivation;
Sec.~135 and \texttt{md/p38\_line\_overlap\_shielding.md} carry the rebuild
of the table these three are read from.

\paragraph{What DB96 still owns.} The band share $A$ that H$_2$ removes from
the shared $912$--$1110$~\AA\ beam is DB96 eq.~(39), the closed-form integral
of their own eq.~(37), and it stays there. Its normalization is the one
quantity in this module that no line transfer touches (the \emph{pump}
cross section), and there the two sources agree: $2.640\times10^{-17}$
cm$^2$ at $1300$~K at the illuminated face of the line-by-line calculation
against $2.557\times10^{-17}$ from DB96's own Table~1--2, $3$ percent.

\paragraph{The energy the fluorescence leaves behind is computed, not
adopted.} Burton, Hollenbach \& Tielens (1990) adopt $2.0$~eV per pump in one
sentence of their Appendix~A, without a derivation. The same quantity follows
from the Abgrall, Roueff \& Drira (2000) transition probabilities: summing
every decay of every in-band pump, weighted by the $X(v'',J'')$ energy it
lands on, gives a mean landing energy of $2.060$~eV at $700$~K rising to
$2.129$~eV at $3200$~K per \emph{bound} fluorescence, $3$ to $6$ percent
above the adopted value. \texttt{h2\_energy\_per\_bound\_fluorescence\_eV(T)}
replaces the constant, and the photoelectron channel of
\texttt{electron\_energy\_degradation.f90} shares it with its own
state-resolved branching. The average is over unshielded line weights; with
shielded weights it is $2.17$--$2.25$~eV, so inside the layer the term is a
lower bound by about as much as the correction it applies.

\subsection{H$_3^+$ cooling: a departure factor, not LTE}
\label{sec:h3p}

The one place where EXHALE does not assume LTE is the H$_3^+$ infrared
cooling, and it is worth stating because it runs the other way from
\S\ref{sec:ir}. \texttt{h3p\_cooling.f90} multiplies the Miller et al.\
(2013) LTE emission fit by the departure factor $s(T, n_{\rm H_2})$ of their
Table~6, bilinearly interpolated in $(T, \log_{10} n_{\rm H_2})$, clamped at
the table edges and set to $1$ for $n_{\rm H_2} \geq 10^{14}$~cm$^{-3}$. Here
H$_2$ enters as the collider density that sets how far the H$_3^+$ levels
depart from a Boltzmann distribution.

The net exchange is written in Kirchhoff form,
%
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{\rm net} = n_{\rm H_3^+}\, 4\pi\, s(T, n_{\rm H_2})
     \left[ E(T) - W\,E(T_{\rm rad}) \right],
\end{equation}
%
with $W$ the dilution of the field the unresolved lower atmosphere presents,
switched on by the input key \texttt{Base IR field} (default \texttt{False}).
The departure factor multiplies both terms, which it must: $s<1$ applied to
the emission alone would heat a gas hotter than the field, and the form above
is exactly zero at $T = T_{\rm rad}$, $W = 1$.

The factor is doing real work rather than sitting at its LTE value: evaluated
over the $x_{\rm H_2} > 0.01$ layer of \texttt{mol\_lyman\_werner}, $s$ runs
from $0.996$ in the base cell down to $0.438$ at the top of that layer.
Assuming LTE there would overstate the H$_3^+$ cooling by a factor $2.3$.
\emph{Those two values of $s$ are STALE}: they were taken on an earlier state
of the case, whose base carried $n_{\rm H_2} = 4.63\times10^{13}$~cm$^{-3}$
and whose $1\%$ edge sat at $4.42\times10^{9}$~cm$^{-3}$, $r = 1.172\,R_{\rm
p}$; the state the goldens now hold spans $5.15\times10^{12}$ down to
$4.48\times10^{9}$~cm$^{-3}$ over $1.0002$--$1.075\,R_{\rm p}$
(\S\ref{sec:layer}). Re-measuring them means evaluating
\texttt{h3p\_departure} of \texttt{h3p\_cooling.f90} on the two ends of that
layer; the density range still sits inside the Miller et al.\ table, so the
verdict of Table~\ref{tab:validity} does not turn on it.

The table is a vacuum departure factor (collisions against spontaneous
decay, no incident field), so a field strong enough to matter pumps the
levels back toward LTE and the true departure is smaller than $s$. The
treatment therefore underestimates the magnitude of the exchange, not its
sign or its fixed point.

For comparison, Frelikh \& Murray-Clay (2026), ApJ 996, 96 treat the same
coolant in pure LTE. On this one channel EXHALE carries the more complete
physics.

\subsection{The chemical-equilibrium mixing ratio}
\label{sec:qh2}

\texttt{q\_h2\_equilibrium} (\texttt{lower\_column.f90}) is the
Koskinen et al.\ (2022) Eq.~(11) fit to the Visscher et al.\ (2006)
chemical-equilibrium H$_2$/H partition,
%
\begin{equation}
  q_{\rm H_2} = \frac{1.9845 + 10^{u} - \sqrt{10^{u}(3.9690 + 10^{u})}}{2.3670},
  \qquad
  u = -\frac{23672}{T} - \log_{10} p[\mathrm{bar}] + 6.2645 ,
\end{equation}
%
with limits $1.9845/2.3670 = 0.83840$ as $u \rightarrow -\infty$ (fully
molecular, the fit's approximation of the solar-composition volume mixing
ratio $0.5/(0.5+{\rm He/H}) = 0.863$) and $0$ as $u \rightarrow +\infty$.

It is used in four places: the mean molecular weight of the analytic lower
column, the base H$_2$ mixing ratio when no \texttt{base.inp} supplies a
photochemical one, the initial-condition seed of the H$_2$ column, and the
$\lambda \rightarrow 0$ end of the continuation path in
\texttt{constrained\_chemical\_equilibrium.f90}.

The assumption here is not about level populations but about the chemistry:
this is \emph{thermochemical} equilibrium, and it knows nothing about
photodissociation. It is therefore not a statement about an irradiated wind,
and the code says so at its own site: in chemical equilibrium
$q_{\rm H_2}(1\,\mu\mathrm{bar})$ stays large up to $T \sim 2000$~K and the
gas is fully atomic only above $\sim 2400$~K, while photochemistry plus the
hotter actual base temperature dissociate H$_2$ well below that. Using it as a
\emph{seed} for a solve that then applies the full radiation field is exactly
the use it supports; using it as a discriminant for whether a cell should be
molecular is not. The changelog records an
attempt in that direction being withdrawn, together with the repair of an
asymptotic guard in the same function that had been inverted since
transcription and handed every cell below about $529$~K a fully atomic state
labeled ``the dense molecular limit''.

\subsection{Three sites that inherit a ground-state assumption silently}

Three further quantities are measured on ground-state H$_2$ and used without
a level-distribution correction. None is flagged in the code as such, and
none is likely to matter at the level the three above do, but the list should
be complete.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Photoionization.} \texttt{sigma\_H2} in
  \texttt{functions/cross\_sec.f90} is the Yan, Sadeghpour \& Dalgarno (1998),
  ApJ 496, 1044 fit (their Eqs.\ 17--19), threshold $15.4$~eV, with the
  near-threshold resonance structure smoothed as in the source fit. It is a
  cross section for H$_2$ in its ground vibrational state; a vibrationally hot
  molecule has a lower threshold and different near-edge structure. The
  branching between its two product channels
  (\texttt{frac\_H2\_dissociative\_ionization}, Sec.~\ref{sec:h2-photo-branch})
  carries the same assumption: it is measured on a room-temperature target.
\item \textbf{Diffusion.} \texttt{binary\_element\_diffusion.f90} gives H$_2$
  the static dipole polarizability $\alpha = 5.315\,a_0^3$
  (Schwerdtfeger \& Nagle 2019) for the ion-neutral coefficient. This is the
  $v=0$ value; polarizability rises with vibrational excitation.
\item \textbf{Metastable-helium Penning ionization.}
  \texttt{ioniz\_HeI23S\_H2} in \texttt{radiation/Cool\_coeff.f90} is the
  Maxwell--Boltzmann average of the Cohen \& Lane (1977) cross sections, taken
  from the Garcia Munoz (2025) network file, valid over $500$--$10^4$~K
  following its tabulation. It is a thermal average over relative translational
  velocity with the internal state of the H$_2$ fixed.
\end{itemize}

\section{The two product channels of the H$_2$ photoionization}
\label{sec:h2-photo-branch}

A photon absorbed by H$_2$ above threshold leaves one of two things behind,
and the network treats them as different reactions because they are:

\begin{align}
  \text{(a)}\quad \mathrm{H_2} + h\nu &\;\to\; \mathrm{H_2^+} + e^-,
    & E_{\rm th} &= 15.4~\text{eV},\\
  \text{(b)}\quad \mathrm{H_2} + h\nu &\;\to\; \mathrm{H} + \mathrm{H^+} + e^-,
    & E_{\rm th} &= 18.076~\text{eV}.
\end{align}

Channel (b), \emph{dissociative} photoionization, destroys the molecule
without making H$_2^+$, so it does not feed R8 (H$_2^+ +$ H$_2 \to$ H$_3^+ +$
H) and does not reach the H$_3^+$ cooling at all; what it produces is a
proton and an H atom. In the shielded molecular base, where the H\,\textsc{i}
photoionization that normally supplies protons is starved, it is the
dominant photon-driven proton source: measured on the converged
He/H\,$=0.0793$ hot Uranus, $P_{\rm H_2,di}\,n_{\rm H_2}$ is $21$ times
$P_{\rm HI}\,n_{\rm HI}$ at $1.004\,R_p$ and falls through unity near
$1.04\,R_p$.

\texttt{sigma\_H2} is the \emph{total} photoionization cross section, so (b)
is a branching of it and not an addition: the H$_2$ destruction rate is
unchanged, and only the products move. The branching is
\[
  f_{\rm di}(E) \;=\;
  \frac{\sigma(\mathrm{H^+})}{\sigma(\mathrm{H^+}) + \sigma(\mathrm{H_2^+})},
\]
tabulated at the $69$ energies of Chung, Lee, Masuoka \& Samson (1993),
J.\ Chem.\ Phys.\ 99, 885, Table~II, and interpolated linearly in $E$. It
rises within $0.03$~eV of threshold to a plateau near $0.02$, carries the
$Q_1$ Rydberg autoionization resonance ($0.108$ at $35$~eV, back to $0.091$
at $37.5$~eV), peaks at $0.222$ at $74$~eV, and is held at its $124$~eV value
$0.2048$ above the top of the measured range. The rows of the coupled system
this drives are (4) H$_2$, which sees the whole rate; (5) H$_2^+$, which sees
$P_{\rm H_2} - P_{\rm H_2,di}$; and (1) H$^+$, which gains
$P_{\rm H_2,di}\,n_{\rm H_2}$. The H atom of the pair is supplied by the
H-nucleus closure, exactly as the two Lyman--Werner fragments are.

The secondary electrons do the same thing: Dalgarno, Yan \& Liu (1999) give
the dissociative channel a mean energy per ion of $22\,W_m({\rm H_2^+})$, so
the fast electrons make one such proton for every $22$ H$_2^+$ ions, an
additional yield rather than a share of the H$_2^+$ one.

\textbf{Yan et al.\ (1998) misdescribe this branching, and the code does not
follow them.} Their Sec.~4 gives the dissociative ``ratio to the total
photoionization cross section'' as $0.284$ at $76$~eV and $0.258$ at
$124$~eV. Those are Chung et al.'s H$^+$/H$_2^+$ \emph{column}, a ratio to the
H$_2^+$ partial cross section; the ratio to the total is $r/(1+r)$, i.e.\
$0.221$ and $0.205$. Using the quoted numbers as a branching of
\texttt{sigma\_H2} overstates the channel by a factor $1.26$--$1.28$ above
$70$~eV and understates it by up to $20\times$ below $20$~eV.

\section{The third body}

R12, R13 and R15 are three-body reactions written as a two-body coefficient
times a density. Koskinen et al.\ (2022) print that density as ``$n$'' and do
not say which particles it counts; their models are H$_2$/H-dominated Jovian
and Neptunian envelopes, where M is H$_2$ and H. EXHALE passes the total
gas-particle density with electrons excluded
(\texttt{ion\_cell\_state\%ntot}), so every heavy particle is treated as an
equally efficient third body.

The coefficient this assumes uniform is the M~$=$~H$_2$ one: R15 carries
Cohen \& Westberg's (1983) recommended $k_1({\rm H_2}) = 2.8\times10^{-31}
T^{-0.6}$~cm$^6$~s$^{-1}$ over $50$--$5000$~K, labeled M~$=$~H$_2$ on their
data sheet and separate from their $k_1({\rm H})$.

Breshears \& Bird measured all three of their colliders in one experiment with
one analysis, so their dissociation coefficients are the clean statement of
how much the third body matters:
%
\begin{align}
  k_d(\mathrm{M}={\rm Ar}) &= 1.55\times10^{-10}\,e^{-44736/T}, \nonumber\\
  k_d(\mathrm{M}={\rm H_2}) &= 5.48\times10^{-9}\,e^{-52989/T},
  \qquad [\mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}] \\
  k_d(\mathrm{M}={\rm H})  &= 3.52\times10^{-9}\,e^{-43881/T}. \nonumber
\end{align}
%
Evaluated here, $k_d({\rm H})/k_d({\rm H_2})$ is $8.67$ at $3500$~K, $6.26$ at
$4000$~K, $3.97$ at $5000$~K and $2.01$ at $8000$~K: a factor of a few
across their measured $3500$--$8000$~K range. Cohen \& Westberg's independent
\emph{recombination} pair, $k_1({\rm H}) = 8.8\times10^{-33}$ against
$k_1({\rm H_2}) = 2.8\times10^{-31}T^{-0.6}$, gives $4.21$ and $5.21$ at
$3500$ and $5000$~K, which is the agreement detailed balance requires of two
independent sources inside their ranges.

Below the measured range the two Arrhenius forms diverge, because their
activation temperatures differ by $9108$~K: the ratio reaches $13.4$ at
$3000$~K, $61.0$ at $2000$~K and $5.80\times10^{3}$ at $1000$~K, against
$1.98$ from Cohen \& Westberg at $1000$~K. Take a factor of $2$ to $5$ as the
collider spread where H$_2$ exists, and read the $5.8\times10^{3}$ as the
cost of extrapolating an Arrhenius fit rather than as physics.

\paragraph{Helium is the collider nobody measured.} The equal-efficiency
assumption is weakest in two places, and only one of them is quantified above.
Above the H$_2$ front atomic H dominates the collider population, and the
factor of $2$--$5$ is the size of the error there: carried, at least, by a
vanishing H$_2$ density. In a helium-rich envelope M is helium, and there is
no measurement to compare against at all. Breshears \& Bird shocked H$_2$ in
argon and in xenon ($5.0$, $10.0$, $20.0\%$ H$_2$ in Ar; $5.0$, $10.0$,
$20.2\%$ in Xe) and extracted M $=$ Ar, Xe, H$_2$ and H; helium appears in
their paper only as a shock-driver gas and in ``helium--neon laser''. None of
the sources behind R12, R13 or R15 supplies a helium efficiency, and none is
applied. On general grounds a monatomic third body has no internal modes to
take up the released energy and should be \emph{less} efficient than H$_2$, so
in a helium-dominated base these three rates are upper bounds; the size of
a plausible factor is of order $0.5$, a guess rather than a
measurement. Nothing in this repository supports a helium efficiency near
unity, and no such number should be quoted from it.

\section{The caloric equation of state of the molecular layer}
\label{sec:caloric_eos}

The thermal equation of state of the code is composition exact: the pressure
is $p = (n_{\rm tot} + n_e) k T$ with the species count of the cell. The
caloric half, the relation between the internal energy density and the
pressure, was not. It used the monatomic
$e = p/(\gamma-1)$ with $\gamma = 5/3$ everywhere, which asserts that every
particle stores $\tfrac{3}{2} k T$.

Molecular hydrogen does not. Its rotational constant gives
$\theta_{\rm rot} = 85.35$~K, so at the 400--1300~K the molecular layer runs
the rotational ladder is fully excited and an H$_2$ molecule stores about
$\tfrac{5}{2} k T$; the vibrational ladder adds to that above $\sim 800$~K.
In a gas that is 80 percent H$_2$ the specific heat is therefore wrong by a
factor $1.67$.

\subsection{The mixture}

For a mixture in which every atom, ion and free electron carries
$\tfrac{3}{2} k$ and H$_2$ carries its own rovibrational heat capacity, the
internal energy density is
%
\begin{equation}
  e \;=\; \tfrac{3}{2}\,(n_{\rm tot} + n_e)\,k T \;+\; n({\rm H_2})\,k\,
          u_{\rm rv}(T) ,
  \label{eq:caloric_e}
\end{equation}
%
with $u_{\rm rv}$ the mean rovibrational energy of one H$_2$ molecule in units
of $k$ (so $u_{\rm rv}$ has the dimension of a temperature). Because
$p = (n_{\rm tot}+n_e) k T$ is unchanged, the exponent that makes
$e = p/(\gamma_{\rm eff}-1)$ exact is
%
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{\rm eff} \;=\; 1 + \frac{n_{\rm tot} + n_e}
      {\tfrac{3}{2}(n_{\rm tot}+n_e) + n({\rm H_2})\,c_{\rm rv}(T)} ,
  \qquad c_{\rm rv} = \frac{{\rm d} u_{\rm rv}}{{\rm d} T} .
  \label{eq:gamma_eff}
\end{equation}
%
$\gamma_{\rm eff}$ is a function of the composition \emph{and} of the
temperature, and it is not a free parameter: both quantities in
(\ref{eq:caloric_e}) are already carried by the composition module.

\subsection{Where the level data comes from}

$u_{\rm rv}$ and $c_{\rm rv}$ are evaluated from the complete bound
rovibrational ladder of H$_2$ $X\,^1\Sigma_g^+$ (302 levels reaching
51\,966~K, the 4.478~eV dissociation limit), which the code already carries
for its infrared line cooling (Roueff et al.\ 2019). The level term energies and
the weights $g_I(2J+1)$ come from that same table, so there is no second copy
of the H$_2$ spectroscopic constants in the code to drift out of step with the
first.

Using the observed ladder is not a refinement for its own sake. A rigid rotor
plus a harmonic oscillator, with $\theta_{\rm rot} = 85.35$~K and
$\theta_{\rm vib} = 5987$~K, gives $c_v({\rm H_2})/k$ smaller by 1.4 percent
at 200--900~K, 2.3 percent at 1300~K and 7.3 percent at 3000~K.

Because the sum runs over a single partition function with the nuclear-spin
weights inside it, (\ref{eq:caloric_e}) is the ortho/para \emph{equilibrium}
mixture. A frozen $3\!:\!1$ mixture gives the same $c_{\rm rv}$ to four
decimals at and above 300~K and differs only below $\sim 250$~K, which is
colder than the layer reaches, so the distinction does not arise here.

H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$ and HeH$^+$ are given $\tfrac{3}{2} k$ each, as if monatomic.
Measured over every cell in which H$_2$ exceeds $10^{-3}$ of the particle
count, the largest ratios to H$_2$ are $1.7\times10^{-7}$ (H$_2^+$),
$1.2\times10^{-7}$ (HeH$^+$) and $3.2\times10^{-8}$ (H$_3^+$), so giving all
three the full H$_2$ heat capacity would move $C_V$ by less than $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Inverting it}

With the vibrational ladder in $c_v$, the map from internal energy to
temperature stops being a multiplication. It is inverted exactly, by a
bisection-safeguarded Newton iteration on the bracket $0 < T \le u/1.5$, which
holds because $u_{\rm rv}$ is non-negative and increasing in $T$. It is
\emph{not} lagged at a previous temperature: a residual that depended on the
previous state would blur both the fixed point of the steady solver and the
finite-difference Jacobian it is found with.

To keep the cost of that iteration bounded, $u_{\rm rv}$ is tabulated once on a
4096-point logarithmic grid from 1~K to $5\times10^{4}$~K and interpolated by a
cubic Hermite in $\ln T$ built on exact nodal values of both $u_{\rm rv}$ and
${\rm d}u_{\rm rv}/{\rm d}\ln T$. The interpolant is therefore $C^1$ and its own
derivative (the quantity the Newton iteration differentiates) is exact at
the nodes. Against the direct 302-level sum over 200--5000~K the largest
relative error is $10^{-12}$ in $u_{\rm rv}$ and $9\times10^{-10}$ in
$c_{\rm rv}$.

\subsection{What carries the local index, and what does not}

The physical content of $\gamma$ sits entirely in the $E \leftrightarrow p$
conversion, and that is where the local value is used: the primitive/conservative
conversions, the geometric source, the total energies and the physical flux of
the Riemann solver, the low-Mach dissipation pressure, the semi-implicit energy
update, the heat capacity of the conduction operator, the state scales of the
steady solver, and the advected energy equation of the post-processor. The sound
speeds likewise use each cell's own $\gamma_{\rm eff}$.

The reconstruction needs no index at all: it works in primitive variables. Each
face state's energy and sound speed are built with the heat capacity of the cell
it was reconstructed from, which is well defined because the two stored
composition quantities are ratios of number densities to $\rho$ (functions of
the mass fractions alone), and so are unchanged by the density difference
between a cell average and its face value.

One place still needs a single index for both sides: the Roe average
$a_{\rm avg} = \sqrt{(\gamma-1)(H_{\rm avg} - v_{\rm avg}^2/2)}$ and the
adaptive $p_\star$ estimate of Toro's hybrid scheme, both derived for one
ideal gas.
They are given the arithmetic mean of the two face values. This is a wave-speed
estimate, not a definition of the steady state: the converged solution is the
root of a residual assembled from the conservative fluxes, and those carry each
side's own equation of state. The HLLC branch needs no such average, its
Davis--Einfeldt speeds taking one sound speed per side already.

\subsection{Reduction to the atomic case}

An atomic run must reproduce the constant-$\gamma$ arithmetic to the bit, and
the formula cannot be relied on to do it: with $n({\rm H_2}) = 0$,
(\ref{eq:gamma_eff}) evaluates $1 + 1/1.5$, which is not the same double as
$5/3$. The reduction is therefore structural. A run with molecular chemistry
off leaves every map on the legacy expression verbatim, and inside a molecular
run a cell with exactly zero H$_2$ takes the same expression. Every
non-molecular case of the regression matrix is byte-identical across the
change.
\section{The element budget the carriers spend, and what the
stationary solve does with it}
\label{sec:budget}

Everything above treats one cell's chemistry. This section is about the
bookkeeping that holds across cells and across the two solvers, because that
is where the stage-2 work of 2026-09 landed and because it changes what a
molecular number in this memo means.

\subsection{One map, three contexts}

Every stoichiometric coefficient H$_2$ carries is now read in exactly one
place, \texttt{nuclei\_per\_particle} of
\texttt{src/modules/lower\_atmosphere/element\_inventory.f90}: two hydrogen
nuclei for H$_2$ and H$_2^+$, three for H$_3^+$, one hydrogen and one helium
for HeH$^+$. \texttt{element\_inventory\_of\_cell} builds from it the full
inventory of a cell grouped into what the carriers hold, what is locked, what
the reservoir specifies and what the closure supplies, together with the
budget each side spends; \texttt{element\_inventory\_of\_candidate} does the
same for a trial state of the Newton against frozen element densities;
\texttt{EXHALE\_INVENTORY\_REPORT=1} prints either at the operator's entry.

The map exists because the coordinate box the carrier solve used to be
limited by was the wrong feasible set. Five separate box sides, one per
carrier, let two carriers of the same element each spend the whole budget: a
cell sitting at both of its hydrogen-bearing ceilings breached the shared
hydrogen budget by exactly $1.0$ of the available hydrogen (MEASURED by the
map on a coupled state). The marching path never showed it, because there
\texttt{limit\_to\_element\_budget} enforces the shared sums; what had no
shared statement was the coupled stationary solve, whose residual compared a
count of cells.

\subsection{The constraint replaces the box}

Since 2026-09-09 the trial step of the stationary solve is limited by the
shared rows themselves,
\[
   2n({\rm H_2}) + n({\rm H^+}) + n({\rm OH}) + 2n({\rm H_2O})
   \;\le\; n_{\rm H}^{\rm avail},
\]
and its oxygen and carbon counterparts, in place of the five corners (each
corner is implied by the row, so nothing is lost and the direction along the
constraint surface is gained). A nonlinear feasibility screen runs at every
candidate, and an infeasible start is repaired by a restoration phase before
the step is taken (on the coupled element-and-carrier fixture, $52$ trials
restored over $1165$ cell-steps, worst violation $1.630 \rightarrow
2\times10^{-16}$; READ from the changelog).
Two switches expose the change for measurement:
\texttt{EXHALE\_ELEMENT\_CONSTRAINT\_ROWS=0} restores the five corners and
\texttt{EXHALE\_ELEMENT\_WRITE\_BACK} governs the element-conserving
write-back below; no input key, output column or unknown layout changed.

The write-back is the part that mattered numerically. Each Newton iterate
writes its carrier densities back into the species vector, and the old
write-back did not return the element totals it had been handed: inside one
coupled carrier solve the He/H nucleus ratio drifted from
$1.39\times10^{-11}$ to $2.016\times10^{-1}$ of the value the run was given
over $79$ iterates, while every carrier box reported a breach of exactly
zero, so no gate the code had could see it. The element-conserving write-back
holds the same ratio at $1.39\times10^{-11}$ at all $68$ iterates of the same
fixture (MEASURED, N4b). This is bookkeeping, not chemistry, but a
composition whose helium content has moved by $20\%$ is not the composition
any rate in this memo was evaluated for.

\paragraph{A clamp that creates nuclei, still in the tree.} When the
proportional share of a hydrogen write-back leaves a cell whose local stages
hold nothing (\texttt{held} $\le 0$), the remainder is deposited as
\[
   f({\rm H\,\textsc{i}}) = \max(\text{rest}, 0)/n_{\rm d},
\]
and with $\text{rest} < 0$ the carriers keep what they hold while the closure
cannot go negative, so the written element total exceeds the handed one by
$|\text{rest}|$: nuclei are created. The same shape appears in the oxygen and
carbon deposits of the same routine
(\texttt{diffusive\_photochemistry.f90}). Only
\texttt{element\_census\_verify} under \texttt{EXHALE\_ELEMENT\_ASSERT} $=1$
or $2$ sees it, and that is off by default. The finding is recorded and the
clamp is unchanged; it is not a defect of a rate but of the reconciliation
around it.

\subsection{H$_2$ is transported; the molecular ions are eliminated}

The carrier option transports H$_2$ alone and closes H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$ and
HeH$^+$ locally, and that split is now measured rather than asserted. On the
resolved gradient length of the hot-Uranus state the product of the advection
time and the chemical decay rate, $t_{\rm adv}\lambda$, is $3.68$ at the
base, $4.0\times10^{-3}$ at the front and $0.82$ in the wind for H$_2$, while
H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$ and HeH$^+$ decay three to twelve decades faster than
transport everywhere (READ from the changelog). H$_2$ is therefore the only molecular species whose local balance the
flow can break on this grid, and none of the three ions is proposed for
promotion. The parameterization removes the conservation modes by itself: the
neutral stage closes each element.

\paragraph{The two-root H$_2$ front does not reproduce once H$_2$ is
transported.} With the eliminated closure, seeding the H$_2$ partition from
ten different starting points found two distinct roots at the front.
With H$_2$ among the transported carriers, $x({\rm H_2})$ at the front is
bitwise identical over the same ten seeds, because the transported value
replaces the seed in the H$_2$ row. The H\,\textsc{i} partition still shows
one root on the carrier fixture and two on the element-and-carrier fixture
($9$ seeds and $1$, $1.05\times10^{-1}$ apart); whether the second is a
distinct branch or a slow iteration is not settled. Both readings are from the
branch-discovery option \texttt{EXHALE\_RESID\_BRANCH\_REPORT=1} of the residual
contract (N5).

\subsection{The upstream caloric energy in the \texttt{\_adv} residual}
\label{sec:advcaloric}

The advection-corrected post-process does not carry the molecular columns,
but its energy equation is no longer molecule-free. The upwind term of the
\texttt{\_adv} energy residual receives the upstream cell's specific internal
energy $e_{\rm up} = E(x_{{\rm H_2},{\rm up}}, T_{\rm up})/\mu_{\rm up}$
formed by the caller, and the caloric branch is taken when \emph{either} side
of the interface carries H$_2$, so an atomic cell sitting above a molecular
one no longer loses the incoming rovibrational energy; both sides atomic
gives the monatomic expression verbatim
(\texttt{post\_process\_adv.f90}, \texttt{T\_equation.f90},
\texttt{ion\_cell\_state.f90} fields \texttt{x\_h2\_up} and \texttt{e\_up}).
A larger defect was fixed in the same path: \texttt{x\_h2\_cell} had been
assigned only inside the stationarity loop and read by the temperature loop
for every cell, so the whole molecular layer was solved at the H$_2$ fraction
of the outermost ghost ($1.5\times10^{-7}$ on \texttt{mol\_base\_handoff},
$2.3\times10^{-5}$ on \texttt{mol\_carrier}, against $0.84$ at the base).

The energy convention is unchanged and nothing is counted twice: the caloric
equation of state carries the bound rovibrational ladder only, and the
$4.478$~eV bond lives in the formation reservoir of
\texttt{molecular\_reaction\_heat.f90}.

The consequence for anyone reading an \texttt{\_adv} file of a molecular run
is large. Between the two defects, \texttt{T\_adv} moved in $469$ and $467$ of
$504$ rows on \texttt{mol\_base\_handoff} and \texttt{mol\_carrier}, by up to
$94.2\%$ ($218 \rightarrow 424$~K at $r = 1.094$) and $103.2\%$
($169 \rightarrow 343$~K), in both cases a warming of the molecular layer,
which is what carrying $\tfrac52 kT$ per H$_2$ instead of $\tfrac32 kT$ does
(numbers READ from the changelog). The
molecular \texttt{\_adv} goldens were refreshed at the 2026-09-10 gate. The
product is still not a molecular stationary solution, and the writer says so
in its own header.

\section{Validity ranges}
\label{sec:validity}

Table~\ref{tab:validity} collects every range in one place: what each
treatment stands on, and what EXHALE asks of it.

\textbf{One of those rows is about the closure and not about a coefficient,
and it is the one that fails.} Everywhere in this document H$_2$ is a root of a
local algebraic balance, recomputed from scratch in each cell by
\texttt{ioniz\_eq}. That is a statement about time scales, not a property of
the network: it holds where the chemistry is fast against the flow and nowhere
else. Evaluated on our own converged solution it does not hold across the
H$_2$ front: the term the closure drops is two decades larger than the whole
net chemical rate there, so the front position and the H$_3^+$ extent that follow from
it are properties of the closure as much as of the chemistry. The transported
alternative exists; it is off by default while the question of whether the
transported problem has a steady state on that planet is still open.

\textbf{The transported operator has a validity range of its own, and it is
numerical rather than chemical.} Its advection was first-order donor-cell until
2026-09-03, carrying a numerical diffusivity $|v|\Delta r/2$ that on the
500-cell grid of the hot Uranus is 8 to 22 times the molecular diffusivity of
H$_2$ over $1.15$--$1.50\,R_p$, so the front it produced was set by the scheme
as much as by the flow. It is now second order (van Leer limited slope added
as a deferred correction), which on the coarse grid reproduces what doubling
the grid gave. The residual front motion after that fix is grid-converged, so
it is the state relaxing and not the discretization; but the state is not
steady, the steady solver stalls on the energy row inside the front, and no
number from a transported run is a converged one yet.

\textbf{And the way the transported problem was being closed against the wind
was itself wrong, in sign.} Until 2026-09-03 the steady route alternated a wind
solve at a frozen H$_2$ partition with a carrier relaxation at a frozen wind.
That alternation does not converge and cannot: its acceptance gate stood below
the smallest move the transport operator can make, the fixed-wind steady state
it relaxed to sat on the hydrogen element ceiling over a quarter of the grid,
and its convergence measure (the carrier row divided by
$n_{\rm H}(|v|+c_s)/\Delta r$) understated the row's own imbalance by a
factor $10^{2.7}$ to $10^{6.4}$, so a state carrying $0.60$ of its own terms at
the front was reported at $1.6\times10^{-4}$. Worse, the splitting moved the
H$_2$ front OUTWARD at 83\% of the local gas speed, whereas solving the
carriers and the wind together moves it INWARD (from $x_{\rm H_2}=0.5$ at
$1.142$ to $1.062\,R_p$) because H$_2$ costs particles, fewer particles at
the wind's own pressure means a hotter cell, and a hotter cell destroys H$_2$.
A fixed wind cannot see that feedback. The carrier row now carries the same
convergence statement as every other row (its own largest terms), and
\texttt{Coupled carrier solve} puts $n({\rm H_2})$ among the Newton unknowns
so that the two halves are solved at once.
The coupled solve improves the
residual by a factor three and the front's own carrier imbalance by four, and
then stagnates with the molecular layer 0.6 to 4\% out of carrier balance, so
the closure question above stands: the transported problem still has no
converged steady state on that planet, and the option stays off by default.

\section{Open items}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{No $(v,J)$ resolution, and why it has not been added.} Carrying
  the ladder as transported species would mean $302$ further continuity
  equations, the $1833$-line radiative network as a level-coupling matrix
  instead of a summed emissivity, and a state-to-state collisional set that
  the code does not have and that none of its current sources supply. Against
  that cost, the place it would most obviously buy something (the
  self-shielding) is now supplied from outside by a line-by-line
  calculation that carries the ladder for us (\S\ref{sec:lw}), so the
  transported ladder would buy consistency rather than a missing number. The
  trade is recorded here rather than settled.

\item \textbf{Self-shielding at our temperature: DONE, and it did not
  transfer.} This item asked for $f_{\rm shield}$ on a $10^{3}$~K
  collisionally equilibrated population at the columns of
  Table~\ref{tab:validity}. It was computed
  (\S\ref{sec:lw}), both published fits failed against it, and the rate now
  reads the calculation.

\item \textbf{Line overlap: DONE; the geometry of the trapping is what is
  left.} The successor item (that above $N_{\rm overlap}(T)$ the tabulated
  calculation was itself an upper bound, because it shielded one line at a
  time) is closed. The table is now built with every Lyman and Werner line
  absorbing every other line's beam, \texttt{h2\_shielding\_overlap\_column}
  is gone, and the only column boundary left is the top of the grid
  (\S\ref{sec:lw}). What replaces it is narrower: the trapping ratio
  $p_{\rm eff}/p_{\rm single}$ is still \textsc{Cloudy}'s, computed in a
  plane-parallel slab illuminated on one face and closed on the other, while
  our layer is spherical and open outward. The thick-inward side is the side
  that dominates, but the size of the mismatch has not been quantified.

\item \textbf{An internal inconsistency in the published DB96 fit.} DB96 cap
  the overlap-corrected equivalent width of the pumping lines at $W_{\max}
  \approx \ln(1110/912) \approx 0.2$ (their \S4.3, eqs.\ 29--30), i.e.\ at a
  band fraction of exactly $1$. Their closed-form integral, eq.~(39), has the
  asymptote $1.05(1 + 0.0117\,b_5)$ as $N_{\rm H_2} \rightarrow \infty$, which
  at our Doppler parameters is $1.0843$ ($b = 2.79$~km~s$^{-1}$) to $1.1050$
  ($4.48$~km~s$^{-1}$): $8.4$--$10.5\%$ above their own ceiling. Their remark
  below eq.~(39) is only that it ``corresponds to an equivalent width
  $W \approx \Delta\ln\nu \approx 0.2$ in the limit $N_2 \rightarrow
  \infty$''. The overshoot is a property of
  the published fit, not of our use of it, and is the same whether the band
  fraction is taken from eq.~(39) or from integrating $f_{\rm shield}$ on the
  grid. \texttt{fuv\_lw\_photon\_field} clamps it at $1$ so the transmission
  handed to the continuum absorbers of the same interval cannot go negative.

\item \textbf{The \texttt{mol\_ir\_bands} regression case does not test what
  its name says.} It sets \texttt{Base IR field: True} and
  \texttt{Molecular IR bands: True} but leaves \texttt{Oxygen chemistry} off,
  as its \texttt{EXHALE\_resolved.out} confirms, so there are no H$_2$O or CO
  carriers. Measured on
  \texttt{output/\allowbreak Cooling\_breakdown.txt}, columns 13 and 14 are exactly zero
  in all $504$ rows, while column 12, the H$_2$ line channel, is nonzero
  in all $504$ and peaks at $2.148\times10^{-8}$~erg~cm$^{-3}$~s$^{-1}$ at
  $r = 1.0059\,R_{\rm p}$, $T = 1248$~K. The zeros are structural rather than
  numerical: with $n({\rm H_2O}) = n({\rm CO}) = 0$ the two net-rate functions
  return $0$ on their own guard. The case gates the H$_2$ line channel and
  nothing else, and a case that exercises the H$_2$O and CO paths would have
  to add \texttt{Oxygen chemistry: True}.
  \emph{Note also} that the golden directory for this case carries
  \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt}, \texttt{Ion\_species.txt} and their two
  \texttt{\_adv} twins, plus \texttt{run.log};
  \texttt{Cooling\_breakdown.txt} is not snapshotted, so the infrared columns
  are not under regression control at all.

\end{itemize}
