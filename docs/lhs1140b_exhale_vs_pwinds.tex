\section{Goal, and the answer up front}

The objective is to reproduce the measured He\,\textsc{i} $10830$\,\AA{}
transit line of LHS\,1140\,b with EXHALE, which solves the wind rather than
imposing it, and to establish what --- if anything --- has to be added to
the physics for the model to match the observation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Result.} The \emph{amount} of absorption can be matched,
but the composition it takes is not one number: it splits in two according
to what is placed at the lower boundary. Above a prescribed metal-free
scalar base the measured equivalent width is reached at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$; above a lower atmosphere solved as a photochemical
column and handed over as a profile, C, N and O included, it is reached at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 10.7$. Both runs have binary H/He element diffusion active
and both sit at the same $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^{2}$\,s$^{-1}$, so what
separates the two values is the base and not the mixing. The \emph{width}
is not matched over either base, and the velocity it asks for is the same
number every time. Solving the lower atmosphere instead of prescribing it
does not remove the match --- it moves it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(i) The amount is always matchable, so what the line
returns is a conditional pair rather than a measurement.}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The prescribed-base branch.} With the base a scalar state
carrying no metals, every combination of assumed spectrum and assumed mixing
reaches the observed red-pair equivalent width at some helium abundance, and
the conditions stack inside this branch. In the well-mixed limit the
crossing is $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy spectrum and
$0.060$, below solar, on the GJ\,699 one --- the second stand-in for
LHS\,1140's unobserved XUV, $5.1$ times brighter, which the paper also uses.
With binary H/He element diffusion active --- helium then settles out of the
base unless an eddy term lifts it --- it rises again, to $4.68$ wherever
$K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ does nothing against the molecular
coefficient, $2.09$ at the adopted $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and $1.11$ at
$10^{11}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}). The dependence on the eddy
coefficient is weak and monotonic, as $K_{zz}^{-0.14}$ wherever the eddy
term acts at all; the dependence on the assumed spectrum is a factor $9$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The solved-column branch.} Replacing that base with a
photochemical column --- Photochem with its climate step solved, handed to
EXHALE as a profile at a $1\,\mu$bar match, at the same
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and with the same diffusion active --- brings C, N and O
into the base, and the same reservoir then absorbs $2.6\times$ less:
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$ gives $EW = 0.421$\,\%\,\AA{} against the observed
$1.108 \pm 0.030$, where over the metal-free base it gave $1.129$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}). A ladder of seven solutions on this base,
each closed on its own elemental fluxes, crosses the measurement at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 10.7$ ($10.5$--$10.9$ on the measurement's $1\sigma$
interval), read off the bracket between the $10.31$ and $12.01$ rungs
(Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Neither number is an independent prediction of the model.}
Each is the reservoir the observed line picks out over its own base, and
the escape rate --- which is an output, not a fit --- barely moves along the
way. The flux closure does not change that on the profile branch. It
returns $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09235$ at the match from trial fluxes a factor of
ten apart, single-valued to $5.5\times10^{-6}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}),
but at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ the eddy term homogenizes the column all the way to
the microbar match, so only $2\times10^{-4}$ of that answer comes from the
escape flux: the closure hands back the reservoir it was given. And the
reservoir it was given, $2.09$, is itself the value fitted to this same line
over the earlier metal-free scalar base. What the closure adds is that a
composition is consistent with a photochemical lower atmosphere carrying its
own C, N and O, not that it is measured. Every rung of the ladder satisfies
the closure at residual flux spreads of $0.4$--$2.6\%$; the line is what
selects one.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Why the same helium absorbs differently over the two bases.}
The base metals are a thermostat setting, and helium is the other half of
the same thermostat. The photochemical column's C, N and O cool the wind
--- $T_{\max}$ from $5330$ to $3944$\,K --- and although the metastable peak
itself rises, $42.9$ to $50.2$\,cm$^{-3}$, the cooler wind does not carry
the population outward, so the metastable column above $2\,R_p$ falls by
$2.6$ and the line weakens by the same factor. Raising the reservoir reheats the wind:
at $\mathrm{He/H} = 10.31$ the mean mass per particle at $2\,R_p$ is
$2.53\,m_{\rm H}$ against $1.51$, the same deposited energy is shared among
fewer and heavier particles, the coolant is diluted with the hydrogen it is
referred to, and $T_{\max}$ returns to $5869$\,K --- past the metal-free
$5330$. The ladder is therefore closer to a thermal trade between base metal
content and helium content than to a determination of composition, and this
one line cannot separate the two (Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals},
Fig.~\ref{fig:thermostat}).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The escape rate is flat on every axis, and end to end the
composition spans a factor $180$.} $\log_{10}(\dot M/\mathrm{g\,s^{-1}})
\simeq 7.6$--$7.8$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy across composition, diffusion and
eddy coefficient alike, $7.39$--$7.50$ across the whole closed ladder, and
$8.6$ on the GJ\,699 proxy --- there within $0.04$\,dex of what the paper
itself retrieves on that same proxy (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}). Against that,
the composition the line implies runs from $0.060$ to $10.7$ over choices
this measurement cannot arbitrate: strongly conditional on the assumed
spectrum and on what is placed at the lower boundary, weakly and
monotonically on the assumed mixing. Any composition read off this line ---
the paper's $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much as any value here
--- inherits those conditions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(ii) The width is not reached on any axis, and the deficit
is invariant.} At the equivalent-width crossing the profile is about $3.4$
times too deep and $3.4$ times too narrow, and no composition fixes it,
because composition changes how much helium absorbs, not how the absorption
is spread in velocity. Measured directly --- convolving the solved profile
with a Gaussian and asking which one reproduces the observed line --- the
missing spread is $22.30$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
($\sigma = 9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) in the well-mixed limit,
$22.31$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$,
$22.39$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the flux-closed solution on the solved
photochemical column, and $22.08$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the $5.1\times$ brighter
spectrum (Sects.~\ref{sec:broadening}, \ref{sec:diffbroad},
\ref{sec:closure} and~\ref{sec:gj699}).
Supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth, blue depth and red/blue ratio
inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval at once, with nothing tuned to them;
what produces it is unidentified. It is not thermal --- the solved gas
cools below $1000$\,K over the line-forming region --- and not bulk
outflow, which reaches $1.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $20\,R_p$ on the fainter
spectrum and $3.6$ on the brighter. Read as turbulence it is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.4$--$4.2$ against the sound speed these solutions
themselves carry, supersonic on every convention and well above what the
literature is willing to assume. The one mechanism that would supply width
without a free parameter --- the adiabatic-cooling metastable bump of
Schulik \& Owen (2025) --- is present in these solutions and measured, but
sits at $0.3$--$1.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}). The shortfall is
in the energy and momentum budget, not in the composition, and it is a
published problem rather than a defect of this setup
(Sect.~\ref{sec:width}).

The paper's own p-winds fit does not reproduce the line either: it carries
roughly the right width, because its temperature is imposed at
$5000$--$6000$\,K where the line forms, but overshoots the blue component
threefold. Adding broadening does not rescue it: its absorbing power is
$24\%$ below the measurement, so no convolution can reach the observed
depth and width at once.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{A caveat that attaches to the escape rate alone.} These
solutions stay subsonic out to $30\,R_p$, so their mass flux is set at the
outer boundary rather than at a critical point, and the gas is already
collisionless there ($\mathrm{Kn} = 1.3$--$2.3$): the $\dot M$ quoted
throughout is \emph{unvalidated in the collisional sense}, while the
He\,10830 layer at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$ sits at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.021$ and the
line analysis is untouched (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).

\section{Inputs, and how each was pinned}

\subsection{System}
Planet and star from Cadieux et al.\ (2024) as quoted by C26:
$R_p = 1.730 \pm 0.025\,R_\oplus$, $M_p = 5.60 \pm 0.19\,M_\oplus$,
$T_{\rm eq} = 226$\,K, $a = 0.0946$\,AU, $M_\star = 0.1844\,M_\odot$,
$R_\star = 0.2159\,R_\odot$. One trap: EXHALE converts $R_p$ with its own
internal constants (mean radii, $R_J = 6.9911\times10^{9}$\,cm,
$R_\oplus = 6.3725\times10^{8}$\,cm), so the input must be built with those
and not with IAU equatorial values --- a $2.2\%$ error in radius, $4.4\%$ in
transit depth, if taken from the wrong pair.

\subsection{Lower boundary condition}
\label{sec:base_bc}
EXHALE anchors the lower boundary at the planetary radius $R_p$ using the
standard CETIMB pressure-anchored condition (\texttt{Base BC: pressure 1.0}),
fixing the base pressure at
$P_0 = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{bar} = 1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-3}}$
($0.1$\,Pa). The corresponding base total number density $n_0$ follows
from the ideal gas law at the equilibrium temperature
$T_0 = T_{\rm eq} = 226.0$\,K:
\begin{equation}
  n_0 = \frac{P_0}{k_B T_0 n_{\rm tot,bc}}
      = \frac{1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}}}{(1.380649\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{erg\,K^{-1}}) \times 226.0\,\mathrm{K}}
      = 3.20485\times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}},
\end{equation}
where $n_{\rm tot,bc} = 1.0$ for neutral atomic gas. In logarithmic form,
$\log_{10}(n_0/\mathrm{cm^{-3}}) = 13.506$, which is entered in
\texttt{input.inp} as \texttt{Log10 lower boundary number density [cm\textasciicircum-3]: 13.506}.
When \texttt{Base BC: pressure} is specified, EXHALE dynamically re-evaluates
and enforces this relation internally (\texttt{input\_read.f90}).

\subsection{Stellar spectrum}
\label{sec:sed}
The paper describes its normalization two ways that cannot both hold: the
Supplement's prose says the proxy SED is scaled so its integrated X-ray flux
matches that measured for LHS\,1140, while the quoted constants
($A_{1132} = 0.59$, $A_{699} = 0.056$) are the catalog X-ray flux
\emph{ratios}, a factor $1.8$ apart. The authors' script settles it:
\begin{verbatim}
Lx_ratio_1132 = 0.59
norm_constant_1132 = Lx_ratio_1132*(d_1140/(smax_1140*au2m))**2
\end{verbatim}
applied to the plain \texttt{const-res} v23 product. That combination also
reproduces the fiducial XUV flux the main text quotes, $0.033$\,W\,m$^{-2}$
(we measure $33.6$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ over $10$--$1300$\,\AA{}). All
results here use it. Note the proxy SED and the paper's own APEC fit
disagree in the X-ray band ($4.4$ against $2.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$
at the orbit); that inconsistency is inherited from the paper's procedure,
not introduced here.

\section{Reproducing the paper's p-winds model}

Run at the authors' settings --- their SED normalization, $R_p = 0.154\,R_J$,
$b = 0.23$, \texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True}, five-phase transit
averaging, and their fitted line-of-sight bulk velocity
$v_{\rm wind} = 2.26$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ --- and at the MCMC medians
($\dot M = 2.03\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $T = 5160$\,K,
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 1.01\times10^{-3}$), the vector their Fig.~4 legend
states for the plotted curve, our reproduction gives a red depth of
$1.376\%$ and FWHM $0.575$\,\AA{} against the $1.32\%$ and
$\sim 0.71$\,\AA{} read off their Fig.~4: a $3\%$ match in depth, but a
line about $20\%$ too narrow.

The width residual is not a consequence of which point in parameter space is
drawn. Running the same reproduction at the grid maximum-likelihood point
($1.83\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $5131.6$\,K, $8.338\times10^{-4}$) gives
red $1.366\%$ and FWHM $0.581$\,\AA{}, i.e.\ the two vectors differ by less
than $0.01$\,\AA{} in width. What produces the extra width in the published
curve has not been identified.

Two points are worth recording because both cost time to find.

First, the curves drawn over the spectrum in the paper's Fig.~3 are the
\emph{three-Gaussian empirical fits}, not p-winds; the physical model is
overlaid only in Fig.~4. Fig.~3's apparently perfect agreement is a fit to
the data, and says nothing about the model's line shape.

Second, the paper's plotted model is visibly broader than a plain p-winds
run, which we initially modelled as an undocumented instrumental kernel. It
is not: their script sets \texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True} and averages
over five transit phases. Both widen the line, and together they account for
the difference.

\section{Turbulent broadening: what the literature does}

Since that switch turned out to matter, all 43 papers under
\texttt{references/} were checked: every PDF converted to text, searched for
\texttt{turbulen}, \texttt{microturbulen} and \texttt{non-thermal
broadening}, and each hit read in context. Fifteen matched; most use
``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$ or for flow structure in 3D
simulations, not for line broadening. Section~\ref{sec:lit} tabulates the
ones that bear on broadening.

The convention, stated most explicitly by Yan et al.\ (2024), replaces the
thermal Doppler width by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu_D = \frac{\nu_0}{c}\left(\frac{2kT}{m} + v_{\rm turb}^2\right)^{1/2},
  \qquad v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{\frac{5kT}{3m}} = c_s ,
\end{equation}
the monatomic sound speed, as assumed by Salz et al.\ (2018), Lamp\'on et
al.\ (2020) and Czesla et al.\ (2022). The p-winds implementation uses
$\sqrt{5kT/6m}$ in a $\sigma$-convention, which is the same physics
expressed against a Doppler width that lacks the factor $2$; in EXHALE's
$b$-parameter convention the same term is $b \to \sqrt{11/6}\,v_{\rm th}$,
a factor $1.354$. That is what \texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_TURB=1} applies. It
is opt-in and off by default, because the field does not agree that it
belongs.

\subsection{Paper by paper}
\label{sec:lit}

\paragraph{Adopt it as an assumed value.}
\textbf{Salz et al.\ (2016)}, Eq.~(5): ``$b$ is the Doppler parameter,
$v_{\rm th}$ the thermal Doppler velocity, and for the microturbulence
$v_{\rm micro}$ we use the sound speed'', applied when synthesizing the
transit profile. (Their atmosphere solver separately carries a constant
$1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ microturbulence at the lower boundary, a different
quantity, which they note ``has little impact on the overall atmospheric
structure''.) \textbf{Lamp\'on et al.\ (2020, 2021)} is the reference
implementation, with turbulent broadening as a free or assumed parameter of
1D isothermal Parker fits; \textbf{Czesla et al.\ (2022)} follows it.
\textbf{Cherubim et al.\ (2026)} inherits the choice through p-winds'
\texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True}, so the LHS\,1140\,b retrieval carries
it --- a fact stated nowhere in the paper text.

\paragraph{Test it, then leave it out.}
\textbf{Yan et al.\ (2024)} give the cleanest statement of the convention
and of the doubt. They record that ``these authors assumed that the
turbulence velocity $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$, which corresponds to the
speed of sound of a monatomic gas and, thus, to the transonic case with a
Mach number of 1'', then keep it out of their nominal models and test
$v_{\rm turb} = 0.5$, $1.0$ and $1.5\,c_s$ as a sensitivity. Their finding
for He\,10830 is that turbulence ``decreases the absorption depth slightly
at the line center, but doesn't significantly alter the absorption at the
line wings''. They argue Mach~1 is likely an overestimate, since in the
diffuse ISM turbulent velocity anti-correlates with temperature and the Mach
number is below unity at a few thousand K, and conclude ``the turbulence
effect is likely less significant than the one explored in this paper''.
\textbf{Yan et al.\ (2019)} computes the Doppler width explicitly
``assuming no turbulence''.

\paragraph{Argue against it.}
\textbf{Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009)}, on Garc\'ia Mu\~noz's (2007) proposal
to broaden Ly$\alpha$ by wind turbulence: ``to generate the large velocities
observed, the energy in such turbulence would need to exceed the thermal and
bulk kinetic energies in the mean flow by a factor of $\sim 100$. Such
energy requirements seem insurmountable.'' \textbf{Koskinen et al.\ (2013b)}
find that ``non-thermal broadening such as that proposed by Ben-Jaffel and
Hosseini (2010) does not appear to be necessary to explain the current
observations'', while allowing that stellar-wind interaction may produce
broadening turbulence. \textbf{Schulik \& Owen (2025)} offer a physical
alternative --- adiabatic cooling produces a metastable-helium population
bump at high velocity, widening the line with no free parameter --- and are
pointed about the alternative: the isothermal model ``can only explain the
broadness of the data under physically inconsistent assumptions, i.e.\ those
of even larger mass-loss rates, not supported by the energy input into this
planet, and an empirical free parameter, turbulent broadening (Lamp\'on et
al.\ 2021)''. They add that $T = 10^{4}$\,K already maximizes what thermal
broadening alone can give.

\paragraph{Need it even so.}
\textbf{Taylor et al.\ (2026)} is the closest analogue to the present work:
a self-consistent model (energy balance, multispecies diffusion, non-LTE
H($n=2$)) that reproduces the HD\,189733\,b line core but not its width.
They write that ``several prior analyses have matched the HD\,189733\,b width
by introducing ad hoc (micro)turbulent or Gaussian broadening terms; here, we
show that this step remains necessary even in our self-consistent
framework'', and quantify it as $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of additional
non-thermal broadening. They speculate --- explicitly calling it speculative
--- that it is plasma turbulence from ions trapped on low-latitude planetary
field lines, strengthening with stellar activity.
\textbf{Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022)}, as reported there, instead imposed
reduced hydrogen line cooling and prescribed zonal jets of
$10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in a 3D multifluid model.

\paragraph{Matches that are not about broadening.}
Recorded so the sweep need not be repeated: Wogan (2025), Lavvas (2014,
2019), Robeling (2026) and the second half of Koskinen et al.\ (2013b) use
``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$; \texttt{2607.18193v1} for
flow--flow interaction in a 3D stellar-wind simulation; Jensen (2012) for
photospheric turbulence driving stellar activity; Taylor et al.\ (2025) for
gravity-wave breaking. \texttt{Vidal\_2003\_Nature.pdf} matches only on its
first page, which carries the tail of the preceding \textit{Nature} letter
(on Crab pulsar radio emission); Vidal-Madjar et al.\ themselves do not
discuss turbulence.

\subsection{Empirical baseline: turbulence in Solar System giant planets}
\label{sec:solarsys_turb}

To evaluate whether sonic or supersonic ($v_{\rm turb} \sim c_s$) turbulence
is physically plausible in a planetary atmosphere, it is instructive to compare
with directly measured turbulence and non-thermal velocity fields across the
stratified layers of Jupiter and Saturn:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Troposphere and cloud deck ($\sim 0.1$--$1$\,bar).}
      Cloud-tracking measurements from \textit{Voyager}, \textit{Galileo},
      \textit{Cassini}, and \textit{Juno} (e.g., Salyk et al.\ 2006; Del
      Genio et al.\ 2007; Ingersoll et al.) isolate the fluctuating eddy
      velocities $v' = \sqrt{\overline{u'^2 + v'^2}}$ from the mean zonal
      jets ($100$--$500$\,m\,s$^{-1}$). The resulting RMS turbulent eddy
      velocity is $v' \approx 3$--$10$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Jupiter (locally
      $20$--$30$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the Great Red Spot shear zone) and
      $5$--$15$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Saturn. Compared to the tropospheric sound
      speed ($c_s \approx 800$--$900$\,m\,s$^{-1}$), this is deeply subsonic
      turbulence, with Mach numbers $\mathcal{M} \sim 0.005$--$0.02$.
\item \emph{Stratosphere ($\sim 10\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$--$10\,\mathrm{mbar}$).}
      High-resolution submillimeter spectroscopy of impact-delivered HCN/CO
      molecules with ALMA (e.g., Cavali\'e et al.\ 2021) and Herschel reveals
      stratospheric jet velocities up to $300$--$400$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the
      auroral zone, but the associated non-thermal velocity dispersion
      (turbulence and wave motions) remains limited to tens of m\,s$^{-1}$
      up to $\sim 100$--$200$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ ($\mathcal{M} \lesssim 0.2$).
\item \emph{Thermosphere and auroral ionosphere ($\lesssim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$).}
      Ground-based high-resolution ($R \sim 10^5$) infrared spectroscopy of
      $\mathrm{H}_3^+$ line profiles (e.g., with VLT/CRIRES, Keck, and CFHT)
      disentangles thermal Doppler broadening from non-thermal velocity
      fields. In non-auroral mid-latitudes, the non-thermal velocity is
      $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.2$--$0.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. In the auroral oval,
      where intense magnetosphere--ionosphere coupling, ion drag, and Joule
      heating inject substantial kinetic energy, non-thermal broadening
      reaches $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.8$--$2.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent These empirical benchmarks show that pure hydrodynamic planetary
turbulence is strictly subsonic ($\mathcal{M} \ll 1$), and even violent
magnetospheric auroral driving in upper thermospheres caps non-thermal
broadening at $\lesssim 2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Turbulence and 3D hydrodynamic simulations of exoplanets}
\label{sec:3d_turb}

Beyond 1D parameterizations, 3D numerical simulations explore how turbulent
mixing, non-thermal velocity structures, and line broadening emerge across
diverse exoplanetary regimes:

\paragraph{1. 3D hot Jupiter escape and circulation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{3D atmospheric escape and kinematic line broadening.}
      Hydrodynamic 3D escape simulations (e.g., McCann et al.\ 2019;
      Debrecht et al.\ 2020; Carolan et al.\ 2020, 2021; Wang \& Dai 2021;
      MacLeod \& Oklop\v{c}i\'c 2022) demonstrate that apparent transmission
      line broadening arises primarily from large-scale 3D kinematic velocity
      gradients ($10$--$50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) across the day-to-night outflow,
      Coriolis-deflected tails, and tidal distortion, rather than unresolved
      isotropic microscale turbulence. Furthermore, the collision between the
      expanding planetary wind and the incoming stellar wind creates a shear
      layer prone to Kelvin--Helmholtz instabilities and turbulent mixing at
      the contact discontinuity. In 3D multifluid models of HD\,189733\,b,
      Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022, 2023) show that upper-atmospheric zonal jets
      of $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ combined with 3D outflow geometry reproduce
      the observed broad transmission profiles without invoking ad hoc
      microturbulence.
\item \emph{3D general circulation models (GCMs) and macroturbulent mixing.}
      In the observable lower-to-middle atmosphere ($1\,\mathrm{bar}$ to
      $\sim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$), 3D GCMs (e.g., Showman \& Polvani 2011;
      Showman et al.\ 2013, 2015; Parmentier et al.\ 2013; Charnay et al.\
      2015; Zhang \& Showman 2018) demonstrate that equatorial superrotation
      ($1$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) and vertical mixing ($K_{zz} \sim
      10^7$--$10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$) are driven by planetary-scale
      macroturbulence (non-linear interactions between planetary-scale Kelvin
      and Rossby waves and the mean flow) and overturning circulation. The
      sub-grid parameterization of small-scale turbulent dissipation
      (hyperdiffusion, Smagorinsky closures, or Shapiro filters) remains a
      known source of numerical sensitivity in GCM wind speeds and jet
      stability (Heng et al.\ 2011; Polichtchouk et al.\ 2014; Skinner \& Cho
      2021).
\item \emph{Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in ultra-hot atmospheres.}
      For planets with $T \gtrsim 1500$\,K, thermal ionization of alkali
      metals (Na, K) couples the gas to the planetary magnetic field. 3D MHD
      simulations (e.g., Perna et al.\ 2010, 2012; Rauscher \& Menou 2013;
      Rogers \& Showman 2014; Rogers \& Komacek 2014; Beltz et al.\ 2022)
      show that magnetic winding of the field by supersonic zonal winds
      generates Lorentz forces (magnetic drag) that brake the flow, generating
      3D MHD turbulence, Ohmic dissipation, and time-dependent variability in
      wind patterns and hotspot offsets.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. 3D simulations across other planetary classes.}
The reach of 3D fluid simulations extends far beyond hot Jupiters to other
major categories of exoplanets and substellar objects:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Warm Neptunes and sub-Neptunes.}
      For warm Neptunes such as GJ\,436\,b and GJ\,3470\,b, 3D particle-mesh
      and hydrodynamic escape simulations (Bourrier et al.\ 2015, 2016;
      Villarreal D'Angelo et al.\ 2021; Carolan et al.\ 2021) reveal gigantic,
      asymmetric cometary hydrogen tails spanning tens of planetary radii,
      directly explaining the $56\%$ Ly$\alpha$ transit obscuration and the
      He\,10830\,\AA{} absorption through 3D stellar radiation pressure and
      charge-exchange interactions. In metal- and water-rich sub-Neptune
      atmospheres (e.g., GJ\,1214\,b, K2-18\,b), 3D GCMs model cloud/haze
      circulation, photochemical transport, and vertical eddy mixing
      ($K_{zz} \propto P^{-0.4}$; Charnay et al.\ 2015; Innes \&
      Pierrehumbert 2022).
\item \emph{M-dwarf habitable zone terrestrial planets.}
      Tidally locked rocky worlds (such as TRAPPIST-1\,e/f/g, Proxima\,b, and
      LHS\,1140\,b) have been extensively modelled with 3D climate GCMs
      (Turbet et al.\ 2016, 2018; Wolf et al.\ 2017; Sergeev et al.\ 2020,
      2022; the THAI intercomparison, Fauchez et al.\ 2020, 2022). These
      simulations demonstrate how thick dayside convective cloud decks
      elevate planetary albedo to prevent runaway greenhouse states, while
      stationary Rossby--Kelvin wave patterns redistribute heat to the nightside
      to avoid atmospheric freeze-out. Complementary 3D multi-species MHD
      simulations (Dong et al.\ 2017, 2018; Cohen et al.\ 2014) assess $\mathrm{O}^+$
      and $\mathrm{H}^+$ ion escape driven by extreme stellar coronal mass
      ejections and flaring winds.
\item \emph{Lava worlds and ultra-short-period rocky planets.}
      For scorched rocky planets with dayside magma oceans (e.g., 55\,Cancri\,e,
      K2-141\,b, CoRoT-7\,b), 3D hydrodynamic models of mineral-vapor
      atmospheres (Castan \& Menou 2011; Nguyen et al.\ 2020; Zilinskas et
      al.\ 2022; Kang et al.\ 2021) reveal supersonic dayside-to-nightside
      winds ($\mathcal{M} > 1$--$3$, $v \sim 1$--$3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), standing
      hydraulic jumps/shocks, and mineral vapor condensation leading to rock
      rain on the nightside.
\item \emph{Brown dwarfs and directly imaged giant planets.}
      In isolated brown dwarfs and young, directly imaged giants (e.g.,
      HR\,8799\,b/c/d/e, VHS\,1256\,b), 3D convective-radiative GCMs (Tan \&
      Showman 2019, 2021a,b; Showman \& Kaspi 2013; Zhang \& Showman 2014)
      couple rapid planetary rotation (strong Coriolis forces) with deep
      geothermal convection and silicate/iron cloud radiative feedbacks. The
      resulting turbulent vortices, patchy cloud bands, and storm dynamics
      provide the physical explanation for observed rotational photometric
      variability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where that leaves this comparison}
\label{sec:turb_conclusion}

There is no settled treatment. Turbulence at $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ is a
common convention in 1D isothermal Parker-wind fits --- and hence in the
paper being reproduced --- while Solar System observations and self-consistent
or 3D models demonstrate that isotropic microscale turbulence cannot physically
reach the required supersonic levels. 3D models instead supply line width
through kinematic flow gradients, zonal jets, and magnetospheric/plasma
interactions. Enabling the $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ term in EXHALE is therefore
right for a like-for-like comparison with C26 and wrong as a physical default,
which is how it is implemented.

\section{Searching for a match: the composition scan}
\label{sec:scan}

Once the star, the planet and the spectrum are fixed, composition is the
free parameter a match has to be sought in first. This section takes it in
the well-mixed limit --- element diffusion off, so the reservoir ratio is
also the ratio everywhere in the wind. That is the simpler of the two
mixing assumptions available, and Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} repeats the
whole exercise with binary H/He diffusion active, where the answer depends
on the eddy coefficient as well.

\section{Why the two models disagree}

At the same composition the two models are not small perturbations of each
other. The \emph{total} metastable column is comparable --- at
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 10^{-3}$ EXHALE's is about half of p-winds' --- but its
distribution is not, and transit depth is absorbing \emph{area}:

Both radii are read from the local-equilibrium metastable profile, which is

the transit integral consumes is more concentrated, and for this
helium-dominated composition it collapses onto the base once the advection
gate is formed on the metastable's own clock rather than on hydrogen's
(\texttt{Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~72). This section is therefore a
statement about the two wind solutions; every transit number quoted
elsewhere in the memo is measured on the advection-corrected profiles.

The star is $13.6\,R_p$ across, so metastable helium spread to $10\,R_p$
covers most of the disk while a column confined to $1.6\,R_p$ can darken
only a narrow ring. The distribution difference is the wind structure ---
the one thing p-winds imposes and EXHALE solves. At $r = 10\,R_p$, same
composition and spectrum: p-winds is isothermal at $6100$\,K by
construction, EXHALE's energy balance gives $1392$\,K; p-winds flows at
$4.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, EXHALE at $0.53$; and the cold, slow, denser gas keeps
helium neutral and the metastable level populated far out,
$n(2\,^3S) = 40$\,cm$^{-3}$ against $2\times10^{-4}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:structure}).

\section{What can be matched: the equivalent width}
\label{sec:ew}

Depth and width trade against each other under any broadening, but the
equivalent width does not: it is invariant under the instrument profile, the
turbulence term and the phase averaging. It is therefore the quantity on
which a self-consistent model can be matched without first settling the
broadening question.

Over the blended red pair (vacuum $10832.6$--$10834.2$\,\AA), the released
spectrum gives $EW = 1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA. EXHALE crosses it at

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} \simeq 0.55 \quad (0.53\text{--}0.57 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} \simeq 1.8 ,
\end{equation}

that is $6.6\times$ solar, a helium mass fraction of $0.69$. Enabling
turbulence moves this to $0.541$, a $1.6\%$ shift --- a direct demonstration
that the estimate does not depend on the broadening treatment. The value
sits three orders of magnitude away from the paper's
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$, and the reason is the structural
difference of the previous section: a wind that spreads metastable helium
over most of the stellar disk needs far less helium to absorb as much.

Two assumptions are carried inside that number and are taken up later. It
is the well-mixed crossing, and it is the crossing on the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum. Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} rescans it with element diffusion
active at every decade of the eddy coefficient, and Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}
rescans it on the brighter proxy; both move it, the spectrum by much the
larger factor.

\section{What cannot be matched: the profile}
\label{sec:width}

Matching the equivalent width is not matching the line. At
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ the profile is $3.4$ times too deep ($4.29\%$ against
$1.254\%$) and $3.4$ times too narrow ($0.244$ against $0.841$\,\AA). The
equivalent width states how much absorbing power the model has; the profile
states how that power is spread in velocity, and composition does not act on
the second. Every solved composition gives $0.22$--$0.30$\,\AA{}, rising
only to $0.24$--$0.38$\,\AA{} with the turbulence term.

\subsection{How much is missing, in velocity}

The measured FWHM is $0.841$\,\AA, that is $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
$10833$\,\AA. Three ways to supply it, and what each would require:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Thermal alone.} A Doppler width of $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM in
      helium needs $T = 4.7\times10^{4}$\,K. Helium is fully ionized far
      below that, so there would be no metastable population left to
      absorb. This is excluded.
\item \emph{Bulk outflow.} A line-of-sight velocity spread of order
      $\pm 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across the line-forming region would do it.
      The EW-matched EXHALE solution reaches $1.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
      $20\,R_p$; the paper's p-winds model, with its imposed temperature,
      reaches $4$--$7$. Both are an order of magnitude short.
\item \emph{Non-thermal (turbulent) broadening.} Added in quadrature to
      what EXHALE already has, closing the gap takes
      $22.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM --- $9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of turbulent
      velocity ($\sigma$), against the $2.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ that the
      sound-speed convention of Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies for
      helium at $5000$\,K. This is the one of the three that can be measured
      directly, and Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} measures it.
\end{itemize}

\noindent So the model is not short by a detail. It is short by an amount
of velocity comparable to the escape speed, and no choice of composition
supplies it.

\subsection{The broadening the data demand, measured}
\label{sec:broadening}

The three routes above can be replaced by one measurement. Take the
EW-matched EXHALE profile as solved, convolve it with a Gaussian of FWHM
$f$ in velocity, and ask which $f$ reproduces the measured line. The
convolution is applied to the excess absorption
$(\max T - T)/\max T$ built from the instrument-convolved column of
\texttt{tpm\_He10830.txt}; depth, width and the red/blue ratio are then read
off with the same three-Gaussian extractor applied to the data, and the
equivalent width by the same integral over the vacuum window
$10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA. So $f$ is the only quantity varied, and every
number in Table~\ref{tab:broadening} is measured the way the corresponding
observed number was.

The width is reached at $f = 22.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM, that is
$\sigma = 9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. At that one value the depth is $1.237\%$
against the measured $1.24^{+0.22}_{-0.23}$, the blue component $0.162\%$
against $0.25^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$, and the ratio $7.62$ against
$6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$: depth, blue depth and ratio all land inside the
observed $1\sigma$ interval at the value fixed by the width alone. Nothing
was tuned to them. Figure~\ref{fig:broadened} shows the profile and the two
crossings.

The number does not depend on which solution is broadened. The same
crossing is at $21.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ run with
the turbulence term already on, and at $22.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for solar
composition --- although the solar run is a quarter of the observed
absorption ($0.311\%$ red, EW $0.270$) and so matches the shape while
missing the amount. The required broadening is therefore set by the
velocity field, not by composition or by the broadening treatment already
in place: the direct measurement of what Sect.~\ref{sec:width} inferred.
The equivalent width falls by $3\%$ between $f = 0$ and $f = 22.3$ because
the Gaussian wings carry absorption out of the red-pair integration
window, not because absorbing power is lost.

This is descriptive, not explanatory. It states that one free parameter,
applied to a solved profile, reproduces the measured line; it does not say
what produces $22.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The candidates are the ones listed
below, and the measurement sharpens them by fixing the number each has to
deliver.

One conversion narrows the list before it is read. If the missing
$22.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is called turbulence, it has to be turbulence in gas
whose sound speed the solution itself fixes. Evaluating
$c_s = (\gamma kT/\mu m_H)^{1/2}$ with $\gamma = 5/3$ on the
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ output, taking $\mu$ from the solved
H\,\textsc{i}/H\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{i}/He\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{iii}
and electron densities, gives $c_s = 5.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $2\,R_p$
($T = 4311$\,K, $\mu = 1.94$), $3.8$ at the metastable peak $5.4\,R_p$
($1609$\,K, $1.57$) and $3.0$ at $10\,R_p$ ($845$\,K, $1.32$); weighted over
the line-forming region by $n(2\,^3S)\,r$, $T = 2022$\,K, $\mu = 1.59$ and
$c_s = 3.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. Note this is the bulk-gas sound speed, the
quantity a Mach number is defined against, and not the helium thermal speed
that enters the line profile.

Against that, the kernel is supersonic on any convention. Read as a
line-of-sight velocity dispersion, $\sigma = 9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.4$. Read in the $b$-parameter convention of Lamp\'on et
al.\ and p-winds, where $v_{\rm turb}$ is the quantity added to
$b^2 = 2kT/m + v_{\rm turb}^2$, it is $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma =
13.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\mathcal{M} = 3.4$; as an isotropic three-dimensional
turbulent speed, $\sqrt{3}\,\sigma = 16.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
$\mathcal{M} = 4.2$. The $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ convention of
Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies only $3$--$4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in this gas, about
a third of what the data demand. That is the opposite corner of the
literature reviewed there: Yan et al.\ (2024) argue $\mathcal{M} = 1$ is
already an overestimate at a few thousand K, and Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009)
call the energy requirement of supersonic turbulence ``insurmountable''.
Supplying the shortfall under the name of turbulence therefore looks
physically hard to sustain, and the measurement points to the candidates of
the next section --- an ordered velocity field, three-dimensional flow, or
heating the 1D energy equation does not carry --- rather than to a missing
thermal width.

\subsection{The same demand, under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$}
\label{sec:diffbroad}

Everything above was solved in the well-mixed limit. With binary H/He
element diffusion active, helium settles out of the base unless an eddy term
lifts it, and how much of it reaches the line-forming region is set by the
eddy coefficient $K_{zz}$, which is not measured for this system. The
diffusion operator is described in \texttt{binary\_diffusion\_design.md};
the literature range at the $1\,\mu$bar base of these runs, the ladder of
solved cases, and the adoption of
$K_{zz} = 1.0\times10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ are in
\texttt{LHS1140b/kzz\_decision.md}.

\subsubsection*{The composition, and how much of it $K_{zz}$ carries}

Turning the operator on moves the equivalent-width crossing of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ew} away from $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$, and by how much depends
on $K_{zz}$. The composition was therefore rescanned at every decade of the
eddy coefficient from $K_{zz} = 0$ to $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, by the
same route in every case: the same control input, the same direct-steady
JFNK solve at $\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 5.0\times10^{-3}$, the same single
post-processing pass and transit synthesis, and the same red-pair equivalent
width on the vacuum window $10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA.
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross} is the result; every crossing is interpolated
inside a bracket of two solved runs, none extrapolated and none against a
saturated line --- at $K_{zz} = 10^{6}$, where the crossing sits highest,
the equivalent width is still climbing at $\mathrm{He/H} = 10$.

$10^{7}$ the curve rolls over ($-0.071$ across $10^{6}$--$10^{7}$, $-0.022$
across $10^{5}$--$10^{6}$) onto a plateau at $\mathrm{He/H} = 4.68$, the
value the line implies when the eddy term does nothing. That plateau is
where it is for a reason that is measured rather than assumed: the operator
adds $K_{zz}$ to the molecular coefficient, and these helium-rich solutions
carry $D_{\rm eff} = 1.24$--$1.32\times10^{6}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at the
base, so $K_{zz} = 10^{6}$ raises the base coefficient by $77\%$, $10^{5}$
by $8\%$ and $10^{4}$ by $0.8\%$. At a fixed $\mathrm{He/H} = 5$ the
resulting equivalent width departs from the $K_{zz} = 0$ one by $0.005$ of
the measurement error at $10^{4}$, $0.18$ at $10^{5}$ and $1.76$ at
$10^{6}$: \emph{no eddy coefficient at or below
$10^{4}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ is distinguishable from none at all by this
line.} At the other end the operator never reaches the well-mixed limit
either --- even at $10^{11}$ the inferred reservoir, $1.11$, is twice the
well-mixed $0.55$.

Over the span the literature leaves open at the base pressure of these runs
the inferred reservoir ratio moves only from $1.5$ to $2.9$, a factor $1.9$,
against a factor $9$ in equivalent width across the same two decades at
fixed composition: the line responds to composition much faster than it
responds to $K_{zz}$, so a large change in the eddy coefficient is bought
back by a small change in the reservoir. \emph{The composition inferred
from this line under diffusion is therefore only weakly conditional on $K_{zz}$, as
$K_{zz}^{-0.14}$, and strongly conditional on the assumed spectrum} --- the
SED moves the well-mixed crossing by a factor $9$, from $0.550$ to $0.060$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), which is five times the entire $K_{zz}$ lever.

\subsection{Broadening cannot repair a deficit in equivalent width}

unchanged, apart from the few percent that the wings carry out of the
integration window. Whether one kernel can reproduce depth and width
together is therefore decided before the scan starts, by the equivalent
width the curve already has at $f = 0$.

The p-winds best fit carries $0.837$\,\%\,\AA{} against the measured
$1.108 \pm 0.030$, $24\%$ short. At the kernel that reproduces the width,
$f = 17.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, the red depth is $0.926\%$ against
$1.24^{+0.22}_{-0.23}$, about $1.4\sigma$ low. What the broadening does is
move the disagreement rather than remove it: unbroadened, the curve is too
deep in the blue component ($0.534\%$ against $0.25^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$, some
$2\sigma$ high) and too narrow; broadened to the measured width, the blue
component falls to $0.339\%$, inside $1\sigma$, and the red component drops
below the measurement. The blue side is genuinely improved; the red side is
not, and the total is fixed.

The intensity ratio barely moves, $2.57$ at $f = 0$ against $2.73$ at the
matched kernel. It is the amplitude ratio of a saturated pair, set by how
the two components share the optical depth, and a common convolution acts
on both alike. It stays well below the measured $6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$,
though the observed interval is wide enough that this alone is not a
discriminator.

Matching the depth as well would require $24\%$ more absorbing power, and
that is not something a kernel supplies: it is a change in $\dot M$,
temperature or composition. Fitting the broadening as a free parameter
alongside those --- which is what the measurement asks for --- would
presumably shift the C26 parameter estimates, since the composition is
constrained through the same absorbing power that would have to rise.

The asymmetry with EXHALE should not be read as one code outperforming the
other. The EXHALE curve entered the scan with its equivalent width already
set to the observed value by the choice of $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening}), so only the shape remained free, and one
kernel settled it. The p-winds curve entered at the composition C26 report,
whose equivalent width falls short, so no kernel could settle both. The two
scans test the same physical statement --- broadening redistributes, it
does not create --- from two different starting points.

\subsection{The adiabatic-cooling population bump, looked for}
\label{sec:bump}

Schulik \& Owen (2025) propose that the missing width is already contained
in the metastable population itself. Writing the steady state of their
Eq.~(1) as
%
\begin{equation}
n_{2^3S}(T_e) = \frac{\alpha(T_e)}{q(T_e) + t_{\rm ion}/n_e}\,n_{\rm He^+},
\end{equation}
%
they note that as adiabatic expansion drops the temperature ``from $\simeq
10^4$\,K to $\simeq 10^3$\,K'', ``a change in slope in the population occurs
as the dominant triplet loss rate $q(T_e)$ drops significantly at lower
temperatures''---the metastable fraction climbs above the curve computed
with the rates frozen at the peak temperature. The rise is ``quenched'' where
the advection timescale overtakes the excitation to $2^1S$, after which
$\dot m_{2^3S} = 4\pi r^2 v\,n_{2^3S}\,m_{\rm He}$ falls through
ionization and the $2^3S \rightarrow 1^1S$ decay. They credit Yan et al.\
(2022) and Biassoni et al.\ (2024) with first identifying the effect, and
their conclusion states the condition it must satisfy to be observable:
``The helium triplet population bump must be situated at high velocities to
help explain anomalous line widths.''

EXHALE carries the temperature dependence the mechanism needs, and carries it
in exactly the balance above. The metastable row
(\texttt{tr\_triplet\_row}, \texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}:72) is
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
n_e\bigl(n_{\rm He^+}\alpha_{2^3S} + n_{\rm He\,I(1^1S)}\,q_{13}\bigr)
 = \; & n_{2^3S}\bigl[\Gamma_{2^3S} + A_{31} + n_{\rm H\,I}Q_{31} \\
      & + n_e(q_{31a}+q_{31b}+b_{2^3S})\bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
%
with every coefficient a function of $T$: $\alpha_{2^3S} = 2.10\times
10^{-13}(T/10^4)^{-0.778}$ (\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:970), the collisional
losses $q_{31a}$ ($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1S$, $0.80$\,eV) and $q_{31b}$
($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1P$, $1.40$\,eV) as Bray (2000) collision strengths
folded with $\exp(-\Delta E/kT)$ (\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:905 and 928), the
Penning term $Q_{31}$ as the Taylor et al.\ (2025) two-branch fit
(\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:988), collisional ionization of the metastable
(\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:1030), and the radiative decay $A_{31} =
1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$ (\texttt{util\_ion\_eq.f90}:1192). Between
$5000$ and $1000$\,K the loss $q_{31a}+q_{31b}$ falls by a factor $1.3\times
10^{3}$ while $\alpha_{2^3S}$ rises by a factor $3.5$, so the
ingredients of the bump are present and are not an imposed
parameterization.

\texttt{LHS1140b/bump\_analysis.py} measures the result. It reconstructs the
balance above in Python from the solved profiles---reproducing the solved
$n_{2^3S}$ to a relative $3\times10^{-16}$, which confirms the rate
transcription and lets the one unresolved term, the $2^3S$ photoionization
rate $\Gamma_{2^3S}$, be recovered from the solution itself---and then
recomputes the reference curve with $\alpha$, $q_{13}$, $q_{31a}$, $q_{31b}$
and $Q_{31}$ frozen at the peak temperature.

The bump is there. In every case the metastable fraction $f_3 =
n_{2^3S}/n_{\rm He}$ rises monotonically outward across the whole cooling
leg, from the temperature maximum to a maximum of $f_3$ at
$1.1$--$16.2\,R_p$---a rise of $1.4$--$4.2\times10^{3}$ in the equilibrium
profiles and $22$--$236$ in the advection-corrected ones---and the rise is
attributable to the temperature dependence in the way Schulik \& Owen
describe: against the same balance with the rates frozen at the peak
temperature, the solved population stands a factor $1.6$--$31$ high at the
density maximum and up to $8.8$--$156$ high further out. On the GJ\,1132
proxy at the equivalent-width-matched composition the maximum sits at
$5.42\,R_p$ where $T = 1608$\,K, against $T_{\rm max} = 5043$\,K at
$1.45\,R_p$: the temperature has fallen to $0.32$ of its peak, which is the
regime the mechanism asks for. The quench is visible too---in the
advection-corrected profiles the density maximum has moved inward to
$1.05$--$1.64\,R_p$ and $n_{2^3S}$ then falls monotonically, so the outflow
appears to be sweeping the metastable out at least as fast as recombination
resupplies it, and $\dot m_{2^3S}$ drops past the peak by $31\%$ at the
GJ\,1132 equivalent-width-matched composition and by $98$--$99.9\%$ on the
helium-dominated He/H\,$=1000$ runs, while the remaining cases carry it
outward without loss.

The velocity condition, however, is not met, and not narrowly. At the bump
the outflow is moving at $0.27$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy and
$1.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,699 proxy. Weighting by absorbers over the
whole $2^3S$ column, the median $|v_r|$ is at most $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and
$2.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the two spectra, and the largest $|v_r|$ anywhere in
the column is $1.6$ and $3.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. No part of the column on
either spectrum reaches $5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, against the $\sigma
= 9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of extra broadening the measurement demands
(Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening}). Radial velocity is the generous choice here:
the line-of-sight projection of a radial field over the transit chord is
smaller still. Figure~\ref{fig:bump} shows the three statements together.

So the bump exists in these solutions and contributes nothing measurable to
the width. That is consistent with what Schulik \& Owen themselves report
for the coolest hosts: for their M2 case they find that ``the transit
signal probes the same velocity region for both treatments of
thermodynamics, making the line insensitive to velocity'', and they conclude
that K stars, not M stars, are the favorable testbeds---G-type hosts
suppress the bump by photoionizing the triplet, and M-type hosts have weaker
tidal fields and so rarely drive fast enough outflows. LHS\,1140 is an M4.5
dwarf, cooler than the M2 they model, and its planet is a temperate
super-Earth rather than a hot giant. The mechanism appears to be present and
correctly captured; the host is the wrong one for it to help.

\subsection{This is a published shortfall}

Taylor et al.\ (2026), with a model of the same class, reproduce the
HD\,189733\,b line core but need $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of imposed
non-thermal broadening for the width. Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022), in 3D,
instead imposed reduced hydrogen cooling and $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ zonal
jets. Schulik \& Owen (2025) argue the width can come from a metastable
population bump produced by adiabatic cooling, with no free parameter. The
present result reproduces that pattern on a different, much cooler planet:
a self-consistent energy balance produces a line far too narrow, and the
literature's responses are all ways of adding velocity that the 1D
energy equation does not generate.

\subsection{What would have to change}

The measured line wants gas that is fast enough to be broad but not so
extended that it absorbs too much. EXHALE at this XUV flux delivers
neither. The candidates, in the order they should be tested:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{More energy} --- tested (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), and it does
      not do it. The GJ\,699 proxy carries five times the XUV of the
      GJ\,1132 proxy used here, and the paper itself runs both. Solving the
      scan again on it raises $\dot M$ sevenfold and moves the composition
      estimate by an order of magnitude, but leaves the required extra
      broadening at $22.08$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ against $22.30$: the width gap is
      not an energy-input problem.
\item \emph{A different helium distribution} --- tested
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}), and it does not do it either. Letting
      helium settle against binary diffusion and lifting it back with an
      eddy term redistributes the absorber by orders of magnitude and moves
      the inferred composition with it, but the required broadening stays
      at $22.31$\,km\,s$^{-1}$: it changes how much helium is there, not
      how fast it moves.
\item \emph{Heating the code does not carry.} Taylor et al.'s trapped-ion
      turbulence, or any magnetically mediated dissipation, is outside a
      1D hydrodynamic energy equation by construction.
\item \emph{Geometry.} A line-of-sight velocity field from day--night or
      zonal flow is a 3D effect; Rumenskikh et al.\ reach the observed width
      with $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ jets, which is the right order.
\item \emph{Adiabatic-cooling population bump.} Looked for
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}): the bump is present in these solutions, but
      it sits at $0.3$--$1.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, so it does not supply width.
\end{enumerate}

The paper's composition constraint should be read in the same light: it
inherits the isothermal assumption. Impose a $6100$\,K structure and only
helium-rich gas absorbs enough; solve the energy balance on this spectrum
and, in the well-mixed limit, the same absorption is reached near
$\mathrm{He/H} \sim 0.5$ --- and near $0.06$ on the other proxy spectrum,
or near $2$ once helium is allowed to settle against the adopted eddy
coefficient. No answer on that list is secure while the width is
unexplained, because the width is what tells us whether the line-forming
gas is the gas either model thinks it is.

\section{The best each model can do}

Figure~\ref{fig:best} puts only the two best models on the measurement: the
paper's p-winds fit at its own best fit (the MCMC medians), and the EXHALE
solution whose
equivalent width matches. They fail in opposite directions. p-winds carries
roughly the right width because its temperature is imposed where the line
forms, but overshoots the blue component by a factor of about three. EXHALE
carries the right total absorption but concentrates it into a line a third
as wide as observed, so its core is too deep. Only the second failure can be
undone by broadening alone (Sects.~\ref{sec:broadening}

\section{The other proxy: five times the XUV}
\label{sec:gj699}

The first item on the list above --- more energy --- can be tested without
adding any physics, because the paper itself runs two proxy spectra. Both
stand in for LHS\,1140's unobserved XUV: GJ\,1132 (used everywhere above)
and GJ\,699, scaled to the orbit by the catalog X-ray flux ratios
$A_{1132} = 0.59$ and $A_{699} = 0.056$ following the authors' script, the
same convention adopted in Sect.~\ref{sec:sed}. Integrated over
$10$--$1300$\,\AA{} at the orbit of the planet, the GJ\,699 file carries
$169.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ against $33.5$ for the GJ\,1132 one: a
factor $5.1$ in XUV input.

On the EXHALE side nothing else changes. Each GJ\,699 case is the matching
GJ\,1132 input with one key replaced, \texttt{Spectrum file:}; the
luminosity keys in the file are irrelevant because \texttt{sed\_read}
overwrites them with the integral of the spectrum it reads. Each case is
started from the converged solution of the same composition on the GJ\,1132
spectrum and finished with JFNK, and all seven reach \texttt{info}~$= 0$
with $\|R\| < 10^{-3}$. One case needed a different seed:
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ stalled when started from its GJ\,1132 counterpart
and converged when restarted from the already-solved
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.25$ GJ\,699 solution.

estimate the paper quotes for this proxy, $2.90\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$.

retrieval. Whether a solved wind and an imposed one agree on $\dot M$
therefore depends on which proxy is assumed, and the disagreement reported
in Sect.~\ref{sec:width} is not a fixed property of the code.

\subsection{The composition estimate moves by an order of magnitude}

Interpolating the equivalent width in $\log$--$\log$ across the seven
solutions and solving for the measured value gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 0.060 \quad (0.059\text{--}0.062 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 16.6 ,
\end{equation}
with the turbulence term on, $0.060$ --- again a shift below the percent
level, so the crossing is set by the absorbing column and not by the
broadening treatment. The bracketing runs are what fix this: the two
compositions straddle the measured band directly ($EW = 0.761$ and $1.104$
against $1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA), so the crossing is measured rather than
extrapolated from the solar case.

This is a shift of one order of magnitude from the $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$
the same code gives on the GJ\,1132 proxy, and it crosses the solar value on
the way: the brighter spectrum wants a helium-\emph{poor} atmosphere,
$0.72\times$ solar, where the fainter one wanted a helium-rich one,
$6.6\times$ solar. The measurement cannot arbitrate, because the two proxies
are two guesses at the same unobserved spectrum and both reproduce the
observed equivalent width once composition is free. The conclusion to draw
is therefore about the inference, not about the planet: what the helium line
constrains is the product of composition and XUV input, and any composition
quoted from it --- the paper's $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much
as the values here --- is conditional on the assumed SED. On the present
evidence the composition inference is SED-limited.

\subsection{The width does not move}

The width is the one thing the extra energy does not fix. Across the whole
GJ\,699 scan the red-pair FWHM is $0.270$--$0.342$\,\AA{}
($0.304$--$0.427$ with the turbulence term), against the measured
$0.841$\,\AA. Measuring the deficit the way Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} does
--- convolving with a Gaussian and asking which width reproduces the
observation --- the composition at the crossing needs
$22.08$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of added velocity spread, against
$22.30$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the equivalent-width-matched GJ\,1132 solution and
$22.31$ for the diffusive one of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}. A
factor $5.1$ in XUV input, a factor $7$ in $\dot M$ and a factor $3$ in
outflow velocity at $20\,R_p$ change the required broadening by $1\%$.

The reason is visible in the table: the outflow velocity that grows is
radial and small compared with the missing spread, while the line-forming
region stays cold. The candidate ``more energy'' is therefore tested and
rejected as an explanation of the width. It moves $\dot M$ and it moves the
composition estimate; it does not move the profile. The remaining candidates
--- heating outside a 1D hydrodynamic energy equation, 3D velocity fields,
and an adiabatic-cooling metastable bump --- are unaffected by this test;
the last of them has since been looked for on both spectra
(Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) and does not help either.

\section{Closing the loop: the composition as an output}
\label{sec:closure}

Every number above took the helium abundance as an input. The scans of
Sects.~\ref{sec:scan} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} chose it, solved the wind, and
asked what the line did; the reservoir at the base was a parameter set by
hand, and the lower atmosphere below the $1\,\mu$bar matching level was a
handful of scalars. This section removes that freedom on one axis: the
lower atmosphere is solved as a photochemical column, handed over as a
profile, and the elemental fluxes are iterated until they are continuous
across the matching level. The composition at the match is then a solution
of the coupled problem rather than a number chosen for it. The
implementation and its tests are recorded in
\texttt{docs/phase\_e\_flux\_closure\_design.md} and
\texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md}, Sects.~76--79; the runs are
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/flux\_closure/}.

\subsection{The setup}

The column below the match is solved with Photochem (Wogan 2025) on the
Zahnle reaction set restricted to H/He/N/O/C. The temperature is not
prescribed: \texttt{clima}'s \texttt{AdiabatClimate} is run with
\texttt{solve\_for\_T\_trop} on, so the stratospheric temperature is the
skin temperature of the solution, and the background gas is chosen by
abundance rather than by name --- on this atmosphere that is helium, not
H$_2$. The climate solve is taken from a deep boundary at $20$\,bar, which
is the shallowest one at which the photochemical model bottom fits inside
the climate column. Chemistry and climate are run on the same elemental
vector, He/H starting at $2.09$ --- the $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossing of
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross} --- with Lodders C, N and O, on the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum at the planet's orbit and at the adopted constant
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$.

The result is handed to EXHALE as a profile of $102$ levels from
$16.73$\,bar to $1.10\times10^{-8}$\,bar, carrying $p$, $r$, $T$, $n$,
$\rho$, $K_{zz}$, the molecular fractions and the elemental reservoirs
He/H, C/H, N/H and O/H, matched to the wind at $1\,\mu$bar;
Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem} reads that solution column by column, and says
which of the columns the wind is actually built from. The loop is then
closed in the other direction: the elemental escape fluxes EXHALE computes
are imposed back on the photochemical model at its top ---
$\phi_{\rm El} = \Phi_{\rm El}/(m_{\rm El}\,4\pi r_{\rm top}^{2})$, helium on
He and hydrogen on H$_2$ at $\phi_{\rm H}/2$, the dominant carriers, the
atomic-H share at that level being $10^{-4}$ --- and the pair is iterated by
damped Picard ($\omega = 0.5$) on the residual
$|F_{\rm measured} - \Phi_{\rm trial}|/|F_{\rm measured}|$ to a tolerance of
$5\%$.

Two things about that measurement are worth stating, because they decide
whether it means anything. The elemental flux is read in the \emph{steady}
window $r \ge r_{\rm esc}$, not in the interval the two models share: at a
steady state $4\pi r^2(\rho X v + J)$ does not depend on radius, so a flux
measured there is the flux through the matching level. The overlap window
cannot carry it --- from the match to the profile top the column spans
$1.96$ decades of pressure in $0.0098\,R_p$, entirely inside the region where
the wind's mass flux is not yet its own, and the flux across it has a radial
spread of $13$ in $F_{\rm H}$ and $35$ in $F_{\rm He}$. In the steady window
the same solution holds $191$ faces at spreads of $0.44\%$ and $0.46\%$. And
that flatness is a statement about how far the wind was converged, which was
measured rather than assumed: at $\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 10^{-3}$ the same
solution spreads by $7.4\%$, which would fail the closure's own validity
test, and continuing it to $10^{-4}$ brings it to $0.50\%$ and moves
$\log_{10}\dot M$ from $7.47$ to $7.50$. The closure runs the wind at
$10^{-4}$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Not verified: the cross-check with a second chemistry code.}
The same handoff was set up with VULCAN (Tsai et al.\ 2017) on the
production profile's own $T(p)$ and $K_{zz}$, and VULCAN did not converge on
this cold helium-rich column: its convergence measure rose from $2.65$ to
$2.75$ against a criterion of $0.01$ with the timestep pinned, the limiting
cell being H$_2$O at the cold trap, and the run exited on its step limit. No
VULCAN composition at the match was produced and no wind was run on one, so
the cross-code spread is not measured here. The record is
\texttt{LHS1140b/lower\_profile\_vulcan/vulcan\_work/STOPPED.txt}.

\subsection{The column the chemistry returns}
\label{sec:photochem}

The settings above produce one file, and what EXHALE does on the profile
branch rests entirely on it, so this section reads the solution itself.
Figs.~\ref{fig:pccol} and~\ref{fig:pccol2} draw every column of the schema
except the two elemental-flux columns, which are constants carrying the
trial fluxes the header states. The file measured here is
\texttt{LHS1140b/lower\_profile/lower\_atmosphere\_profile.dat}, the one-way
handoff at iteration $0$; the closed arms of Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}
carry their own copies and are compared at the end of this section.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The grid and the thermal structure.} $102$ levels from
$16.73$\,bar to $1.098\times10^{-8}$\,bar --- the photochemical model's own
domain, sitting inside the $20$\,bar column the climate step was taken from.
Temperature runs from $520.3$\,K at the deep boundary to a floor of
$185.05$\,K, which it reaches at $0.94$\,bar and holds at every level above:
the stratosphere is isothermal at the skin temperature, and the tropopause
the climate solve reports, $1.031$\,bar, is one level below the first
isothermal level. $K_{zz}$ is $10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at all $102$
levels, so the eddy coefficient the wind runs at and the one the column was
mixed at are the same constant. The column spans $553.6$\,km in radius,
$0.15608$ to $0.16400\,R_{\rm J}$, with the $1\,\mu$bar match at
$0.16240\,R_{\rm J} = 1.782\,R_\oplus$ --- which is what fixes the base
radius of the wind. Density falls from $n = 2.33\times10^{20}$\,cm$^{-3}$
($\rho = 1.40\times10^{-3}$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$) to
$4.30\times10^{11}$\,cm$^{-3}$ ($2.58\times10^{-12}$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The column is chemically almost frozen.} Between the deep
boundary and the match, $17$ decades of pressure, $q_{\rm H_2}$ moves from
$0.19258$ to $0.19264$, $q_{\rm CH_4}$ from $1.072\times10^{-4}$ to
$1.025\times10^{-4}$ and $q_{\rm NH_3}$ from $3.158\times10^{-5}$ to
$2.919\times10^{-5}$. What changes at all is water, which condenses, and a
set of trace photochemical products at the very top. In an eddy-mixed column
at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ that is what one expects, and it is the same statement
as Sect.~\ref{sec:closure} makes from the other side --- the eddy term
homogenizes the column all the way to the match.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hydrogen stays molecular to the top.} $q_{\rm H}$ rises
from $5.9\times10^{-21}$ at the deep boundary to $6.5\times10^{-6}$ at the
match and $4.0\times10^{-5}$ at the model top, four to five decades below
$q_{\rm H_2}$ throughout: there is no level in this column at which atomic
hydrogen overtakes H$_2$. The base EXHALE is handed is an H$_2$ base in that
sense. It does not enter the wind that way in these runs --- EXHALE's
molecular chemistry is off, and $q_{\rm H_2}$ is read but inert (below) ---
which is worth stating rather than leaving implicit.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The composition is cold and reducing.} At $520$\,K and
$17$\,bar CO is already negligible, $q_{\rm CO} = 8.1\times10^{-18}$, and
CO$_2$ smaller still: carbon is CH$_4$ and nitrogen is NH$_3$, the opposite
of the CO/N$_2$-dominated deep chemistry of a hot Jupiter. Counting carriers
at the match against the hydrogen-nucleus fraction of the gas,
$2q_{\rm H_2} + q_{\rm H} = 0.38532$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2ex
\item carbon: CH$_4$ gives $2.661\times10^{-4}$ per H, $99.7\%$ of the
      carbon the profile's carrier columns hold, with C$_2$H$_2$ and HCN
      supplying the rest;
\item nitrogen: NH$_3$ gives $7.576\times10^{-5}$ per H, $96.3\%$, with
      N$_2$ at $3.4\%$;
\item oxygen: the water that survived the cold trap gives
      $4.831\times10^{-7}$ per H, $99.9\%$.
\end{itemize}
Those carrier sums come to $96.0$, $96.0$ and $97.6$ per cent of the
elemental columns ${\rm C/H} = 2.778\times10^{-4}$,
${\rm N/H} = 8.187\times10^{-5}$ and ${\rm O/H} = 4.957\times10^{-7}$; the
remainder sits in species the profile does not carry as columns, so the
carrier accounting here is a decomposition of the elemental ratios and not
an independent measurement of them.

This is the direct link to Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}. The C and N that cool
the wind and take its line down by $2.6\times$ are held in the column as
CH$_4$ and NH$_3$, both of which the solution keeps in the gas phase across the trap, so they
cross the tropopause intact and arrive at the match at essentially their deep
values.
Oxygen, whose carrier is water, does not.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Photochemistry acts only in the top decades.} From the
tropopause to the match, HCN goes $1.3\times10^{-13} \rightarrow
6.5\times10^{-8}$, N$_2$ $9.2\times10^{-9} \rightarrow 5.2\times10^{-7}$,
C$_2$H$_2$ $2.5\times10^{-13} \rightarrow 1.1\times10^{-7}$, CO
$5.2\times10^{-16} \rightarrow 1.8\times10^{-10}$ and OH
$4.5\times10^{-27} \rightarrow 7.1\times10^{-11}$ --- several decades each,
and all of them still trace. They do not move the elemental budget: the
carrier accounting above puts every one of them below $4\%$ of its element.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What EXHALE actually consumes from this file.} Read in the
code rather than inferred: the reader in
\texttt{files\_IO/lower\_} \texttt{atmosphere\_profile.f90} requires the
columns \texttt{p}, \texttt{r}, \texttt{T}, \texttt{n\_tot}, \texttt{rho},
\texttt{Kzz}, \texttt{q\_H2}, \texttt{q\_H} and \texttt{X\_He} to be
present, and \texttt{apply\_lower\_atmosphere\_profile}
(\texttt{input\_read.f90}) then takes, \emph{at the matching level only},
\texttt{T} into $T_0$, \texttt{r} into $R_0$, \texttt{q\_H2} into
\texttt{q\_h2\_base}, \texttt{X\_He} into \texttt{HeH}, and \texttt{X\_C},
\texttt{X\_N}, \texttt{X\_O} (and any other \texttt{X\_<El>} the file
carries) into the metal abundances, through the same door \texttt{metals.inp}
uses. \texttt{p\_match\_bar} comes from the header. The \texttt{Kzz} column
is the one thing read over the whole profile, interpolated onto the radial
grid through the file's own \texttt{r} column
(\texttt{eddy\_diffusion\_on\_grid}); a scalar \texttt{He\_Kzz:} in
\texttt{input.inp} is then announced as inert. Everything else is
\emph{not} read: \texttt{n\_tot} and \texttt{rho} are required by the schema
but never consumed --- the base density is $n_0$ of \texttt{input.inp} and
the base particle count follows from $T_0$, He/H and the elemental ratios ---
\texttt{q\_H} is required and never consumed, the two flux columns
\texttt{F\_H} and \texttt{F\_He} are not read by the Fortran at all (the
closure driver reads them on the Python side), and none of the molecular
columns beyond \texttt{q\_H2} is read. And \texttt{q\_H2} itself only acts
when a molecular base is switched on: it reaches the base EOS through
\texttt{h2\_mixing\_ratio\_base}, which \texttt{comp\_ntot\_bc} calls only
under \texttt{molecular\_base}. In the runs of this memo that flag is off,
and the resolved report confirms it --- \texttt{mass\_per\_H} $= 9.37393$
and \texttt{ntot\_bc} $= 1.000116$ are exactly
$1 + 4\,{\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El}A_{\rm El}$ and
$(1 + {\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El})/(1 + {\rm He/H})$ with no H$_2$ term.
So of the $23$ columns, six numbers at one level plus one full column are
what the wind is actually built from.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the reservoir does to the column.} The ladder arms of
Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder} each carry their own solved column, and raising
the reservoir changes it in three measurable ways
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pccol2}d--f). The deep boundary cools, $520.3 \rightarrow
439.4 \rightarrow 387.9$\,K at $16.73$\,bar for ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, $5.01$
and $10.31$, while the stratosphere stays at $185$\,K. H$_2$ is diluted by
number, $q_{\rm H_2} = 0.1926 \rightarrow 0.0907 \rightarrow 0.0462$ at the
match, with C/H and N/H unchanged to four digits because they are stated per
hydrogen nucleus. And the cold trap loosens: O/H at the match goes
$4.96\times10^{-7} \rightarrow 6.43\times10^{-7} \rightarrow
9.09\times10^{-7}$, the column of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder}. Oxygen is
the only element the reservoir moves here, and it is the one whose carrier
condenses.

handoff, and the trap is where the climate solution says it is rather than
where a closed form assumes it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Against the published estimate.} Cherubim et al.\ (2026)
quote $f_{\rm H2O} \sim 7$\,ppm from a closed-form cold trap at a $0.1$\,bar
tropopause with $T_{\rm skin} = 194$\,K. The climate solution gives
$0.13$--$0.33$\,ppm at a tropopause of $0.5$--$1.0$\,bar: a factor $20$--$50$
less water at a tropopause $5$--$10\times$ deeper. The saturation physics
agrees --- $p_{\rm sat}(185\,{\rm K})/1\,{\rm bar}$ is $1.3\times10^{-7}$ and
$p_{\rm sat}(194\,{\rm K})/0.1\,{\rm bar}$ about $5\times10^{-6}$, so each
number follows from its own tropopause --- and what differs is where the
tropopause is put. Ours is a solved level in a helium-dominated column
($\mu = 3.6$ at ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$), theirs an assumed one. This is
reported as a result about the climate model and was not tuned toward the
published number.

What the trap does to the handoff is a depletion with height, not a
condensate sitting at the base: gas-phase oxygen falls from
${\rm O/H} = 6.062\times10^{-4}$ at the deep boundary to
$4.957\times10^{-7}$ at the match, a factor $1223$, while C/H
($2.778\times10^{-4}$), N/H ($8.187\times10^{-5}$) and He/H cross the
tropopause unchanged --- the carriers of carbon and nitrogen in this column
are CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem}), and this solution keeps
both of them in the gas phase across the trap
(Figs.~\ref{fig:closure}c and~\ref{fig:pccol2}a--c).

starting from the same ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$ reservoir.

\noindent The three arms agree on He/H to $5.5\times10^{-6}$, on

the residual fell on every iteration --- but the $3\times$ arm used six of
its eight allowed steps, so a start further than an order of magnitude out
would need a larger iteration budget.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The composition is an output for the first time, and at
this eddy coefficient it is the well-mixed value.} He/H at the match comes
back at $2.09235$ against the $2.09$ the reservoir was started from, a change
of $1.1\times10^{-3}$, of which only $2\times10^{-4}$ comes from the escape
flux itself. The reason is measured: at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ eddy mixing homogenizes the column all
the way to $1\,\mu$bar, so a $1.8\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ hydrogen escape
imposed at the model top moves He/H by $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at that top and by
$2\times10^{-4}$ at the match. \emph{It is the eddy coefficient, not the
escape flux, that sets the composition at the matching level.} Closing the
loop therefore does not retire the conditionality of
Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}; it relocates it, onto the same adopted $K_{zz}$.

Above the match the wind separates the two elements as strongly as before.
The elemental fluxes carry $F_{\rm He}/F_{\rm H} = 0.73$ by mass against the
base reservoir's $8.31$, and He/H falls from $2.09$ at the base to $0.183$ at
$30\,R_p$. The closure does not weaken that: it says the fractionation
happens in the wind and not below the match. One caveat on the budget, stated
rather than corrected: the diffusion operator's mass fraction is that of the
H\,$+$\,He mixture and the trace metals ride inside $\rho$, so the measured
$F_{\rm H}$ is hydrogen plus metals and overstates elemental hydrogen by
$4.8\times10^{-4}$, two decades below the tolerance.

\subsection{What the base metals do, and what the closed solution does not
reproduce}
\label{sec:basemetals}

The profile brings something the scalar base never had: the carbon, nitrogen
and oxygen of the photochemical column. Isolating it takes a ladder of
controls in which the scalar-base run is moved one key at a time onto the
profile's own base state, every rung a converged solution
(\texttt{Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~78). Setting the base helium ratio to the
profile's $2.0921$ moves $\log_{10}\dot M$ by $0.01$; setting the base
temperature to the solved $185.05$\,K, from the $226$\,K the scalar runs
used, moves it by less than $0.01$ more --- the base temperature is not the
lever. Adding the base radius and molecular fraction moves it to $7.86$.
Adding the profile's C/H, N/H and O/H takes it to
$\mathbf{7.46}$, and the red-pair depth from $4.40\%$ to $\mathbf{1.52\%}$:
$-0.40$\,dex in $\dot M$ and a $3.3\times$ shallower helium line, from
metal-line cooling of a $185$\,K base at ${\rm C/H} = 2.8\times10^{-4}$. The
profile route reproduces that rung to every digit printed, which is the
consistency check the two routes exist to give.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The consequence for the line is that the flux-closed
solution does not reproduce the measured equivalent width.} At
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and a metal-free base, ${\rm He/H} = 2.13$ gives
$EW = 1.129$\,\%\,\AA, which is why Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross} puts the
crossing at $2.09$. The flux-closed solution sits at the same eddy
coefficient and essentially the same reservoir, $2.09235$, and gives
$0.421$\,\%\,\AA{} --- a factor $2.6$ below the observed
$1.108 \pm 0.030$, and $23\sigma$ from it. The difference is entirely the
base metals beneath it.

So closing the elemental fluxes and matching the equivalent width are two
different constraints, and the solution presented at $2.09$ satisfies the
first only. Fixing the reservoir by the line, as Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}
and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} do, was done above a base carrying no metals; with
the photochemical column beneath it the same reservoir absorbs $2.6\times$
less, so the composition that satisfies both conditions at once is larger
than $2.09$. Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder} solves for it, at the adopted
$K_{zz}$, and returns $\mathrm{He/H} = 10.7$. So the lower boundary is an
axis of its own, of the same standing as the assumed spectrum, the assumed
mixing and composition itself: what is placed beneath the wind is not a free
parameter once the lower atmosphere is solved, but is set by the elemental
vector and the cold trap of the column below.

\subsubsection*{The mechanism: helium as a thermostat}

Why the same reservoir absorbs differently over the two bases can be read
off the solved structures directly. Table~\ref{tab:thermostat} measures the
three solutions that bracket the question: the metal-free scalar base at
${\rm He/H} = 2.13$, which reproduces the line
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/heh2p13\_diff\_kzz1e9/}); the flux-closed solution
on the photochemical column at $2.09$, which does not
(\texttt{.../flux\_closure/hi/k06/}); and the closed solution at $10.31$,
the rung just below the crossing (\texttt{.../flux\_closure/heh10p3/k01/}).
All three run with H/He element diffusion active at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^{2}$\,s$^{-1}$ --- constant through the profile in
the two profile runs, the scalar value in the third --- and with EXHALE's
molecular chemistry off, so no molecular carrier appears in the wind. Mean
mass per particle is $(n_{\rm H} + 4n_{\rm He})/(n_{\rm H} + n_{\rm He} +
n_e)$; nuclei are counted as H\,\textsc{i}\,$+$\,H\,\textsc{ii} and
He\,\textsc{i}\,$+$\,He\,\textsc{ii}\,$+$\,He\,\textsc{iii}. The values are
from the advection-corrected profiles; the reference arm of the closure
(\texttt{.../flux\_closure/ref/k00/}) reproduces the $2.09$ column to under
$1\%$ in every entry.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Adding the base metals cools the wind, and the line loses
its outer column.} With no metals, radiative cooling is $6$--$9\%$ of the
heating over $1.2$--$2\,R_p$ and the wind is limited by expansion rather
than by radiation. With the column's C, N and O beneath it, cooling reaches
$71\%$ of the heating at $1.2\,R_p$, of which $95\%$ is metal-line cooling
and the largest single contributor is neutral C\,\textsc{i}; $T_{\max}$
falls from $5330$ to $3944$\,K. The metastable peak does not fall with it
--- it rises, $42.9 \rightarrow 50.2$\,cm$^{-3}$ --- and neither does the
column inside $2\,R_p$, which is the same to $0.4\%$ ($24.9$ against
$25.0$). What is lost is entirely outside $2\,R_p$: $40.5 \rightarrow
15.5$\,cm$^{-3}R_p$, a factor $2.6$, matching the factor the equivalent
width falls by ($1.129 \rightarrow 0.421$\,\%\,\AA). The cooler wind has the
smaller scale height, so it does not carry the metastable population out to
where it adds absorbing area.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Raising the reservoir reheats it, by two terms that are
both measured.} From $2.09$ to $10.31$ on the same base, $T_{\max}$ goes
$3944 \rightarrow 5869$\,K. Two things change together. First, there are
fewer particles to share the deposited energy: the mean mass per particle at
$2\,R_p$ rises from $1.51$ to $2.53\,m_{\rm H}$, a factor $1.68$, and the
heating rate per particle at $2\,R_p$ rises by $1.88$, while $T(2\,R_p)$
rises by $1.71$. The three factors agreeing to within $10\%$ is a
consistency check on that reading, not a proof of it --- the cooling term is
not held fixed between the two runs. Second, the coolant is diluted along
with the hydrogen it is referred to: the profile states C, N and O per
hydrogen nucleus (schema column \texttt{X\_*[El/H nuclei]}, and in the code
$n_X = \mathtt{melem\_ab}(e)\,n_{\rm H}$), and the two profiles carry the
same ${\rm C/H} = 2.778\times10^{-4}$ to four digits, so when
$n({\rm H\,\textsc{i}})$ at $2\,R_p$ falls by $4.1$ the carbon density falls
with it by the same factor. The volumetric metal cooling nonetheless
\emph{rises}, $3.79 \rightarrow 6.05\times10^{-10}$\,erg\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$
at $1.5\,R_p$, because the cooling function is steeply temperature
dependent; what the dilution does is let the gas settle at the higher
temperature at which that happens.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the metastable balance does with that.} The 2\,$^3S$
population in these solutions is fed almost entirely by recombination and
drained by Penning ionization on neutral hydrogen and by electron collisional
de-excitation. Reconstructing the residual EXHALE solves --- source
$n_e n_{\rm He\,\textsc{ii}}\alpha_{2^3S}$ plus collisional excitation from
the singlet ground state, against radiative decay $A_{31}$, photoionization,
Penning on H\,\textsc{i} at the Taylor et al.\ (2025) rate, electron
collisional de-excitation to $2\,^1S$ and $2\,^1P$, and electron collisional
ionization (\texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}, \texttt{tr\_triplet\_row})
reproduces the tabulated $n(2\,^3S)$ to five digits, so the budget can be
apportioned. At the metastable peak, collisional excitation supplies under
$0.05\%$ of the source, and radiative decay, photoionization and collisional
ionization together take under $0.1\%$ of the sink. What is left is: at
${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, Penning $89\%$ and electron de-excitation $11\%$ of the
loss; at $10.31$, Penning $60\%$ and electron de-excitation $40\%$. Between
the two, the source rate rises by $1.55$ (more He\,\textsc{ii} and more
electrons in the hotter gas, against a recombination coefficient that falls
with temperature) and the metastable lifetime lengthens by $1.57$, from
$0.47$ to $0.74$\,s: hydrogen has been thinned by $3.2$ at the peak, so
Penning removes the level more slowly, and the faster electron
de-excitation of the hotter gas takes back only part of it. The product,
$2.44$, is the peak ratio $50.2 \rightarrow 122.5$. \emph{There is no
charge-exchange term on the metastable in this code} --- the He/H charge
exchange acts on ground-state He and He$^+$ --- so it is not part of this
balance.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Read as an interpretation}, the thermal structure of the
$10.31$ solution ($T_{\max} = 5869$\,K, column $96.9$) has recovered and
slightly passed that of the metal-free $2.09$ solution ($5330$\,K, $65.4$),
which is why the equivalent width comes back to the measured value there.
The ladder therefore looks less like a determination of composition than
like a thermal trade between the metal content of the base and the helium
content above it, and one line cannot separate the two. Helium in these
solutions is not only the absorber; it is also the thermostat that sets how
far the absorber is carried.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the wind is made of, and how ionized it is.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} carries the elemental fractions and the ionization
state of the same three solutions, on the same advection-corrected profiles
as Table~\ref{tab:thermostat}. Two things follow from it that the thermostat
argument rests on.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Diffusion keeps the wind far less helium-rich than the
reservoir.} On both branches the helium fraction falls steeply through the
line-forming region and then flattens: ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$ at the base gives
$0.231$ by nuclei at $2\,R_p$ and $0.186$ beyond $5\,R_p$ on this arm
($0.183$ on the reference arm quoted in Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}); $10.31$
gives $1.421$ and $1.156$. That is a factor $9.1$ and $8.9$ between the reservoir
and the wind, in a solution where the reservoir is an output of the closure
rather than a choice. The consequence is worth stating in the composition's
own terms: the flux-closed solution at $2.09$ --- a reservoir that is $68$
per cent helium by number --- has a \emph{hydrogen-dominated} line-forming
region, $n_{\rm H}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.81$ against
$n_{\rm He}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.19$ at $2\,R_p$. At $10.31$ helium still leads
there, $0.59$ against $0.41$, but by a factor $7$ less than the reservoir
states. A composition quoted for this planet therefore has to say at which
level it is quoted; the reservoir and the absorbing layer are not the same
number, and the memo's crossings are reservoir values.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The hotter solution is also the more ionized one, and it is
not shielding.} At $2\,R_p$, $x({\rm H\,\textsc{ii}}) = 0.031$ on the
photochemical base at $2.09$ and $0.106$ at $10.31$ --- a factor $3.4$, in
the run whose neutral hydrogen density is $4.1$ times lower. The obvious
reading, that thinner hydrogen shields itself less, is contradicted by the
outputs: the H\,\textsc{i} column above $2\,R_p$ gives
$\tau(13.6\,{\rm eV}) = 0.23$ and $0.055$, so both are optically thin there,
and the H\,\textsc{i} photoelectric heating per neutral hydrogen atom is the
same in the two, $1.23$ against $1.26\times10^{-18}$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$ per atom.
The recombination sink barely moves either: $\alpha_B(T)\,n_e$ is
$2.35$ against $2.13\times10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$. What grows is the ionization of
hydrogen \emph{by helium}. Measured on the equilibrium state at $2\,R_p$, the
two channels that can be computed in closed form from the outputs are Penning
ionization He($2\,^3S$)$+$H, which supplies $31\%$ of what case-B
recombination removes at $2.09$ and $24\%$ at $10.31$ --- a falling share but
a rate per neutral hydrogen atom $2.8$ times higher --- and the charge
exchange He$^+ +$H, at $2.3\%$ and $1.5\%$. The rest, $67\%$ and $75\%$, is
photoionization, and inside it the helium-fed parts are what scale: the
He-recombination-radiation channel (He\,\textsc{ii} recombining to ground,
whose $\ge 24.6$\,eV continuum ionizes H) carries $6.4$ times more heating per
neutral hydrogen atom in the $10.31$ solution, and the energy deposited by
He\,\textsc{i} photoionization per neutral hydrogen atom, part of which
secondary ionization turns into hydrogen ionizations, is $7.0$ times higher.
\emph{What could not be settled from the outputs} is the split inside that
$67$--$75\%$: the individual rates --- direct $\Gamma_{\rm H\,\textsc{i}}$,
the He-recombination-driven addition to it, and the secondary-ionization
yield --- are not written to any output file, and the factors quoted here are
read off the heating columns on the assumption that the mean photoelectron
energy is similar between the two runs. The one channel that can be excluded
outright is $n=2$ photoionization: \texttt{heat\_Hpe[excitedH]} is exactly
zero at every cell of all three runs. Helium ionization itself tracks the
temperature more simply --- $x({\rm He\,\textsc{ii}})$ is $0.053$, $0.065$
and $0.079$ at $2\,R_p$ across the three, and
$x({\rm He\,\textsc{iii}})$ stays below $2\times10^{-4}$ everywhere inside
$3\,R_p$.

single well-mixed solution that reproduces the line with no diffusion and no
base metals; Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} is the set the lower-boundary axis is
built on, so the two are complementary rather than alternative views.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The crossing.} The arms at $10.31$ and $12.01$ straddle the
measured equivalent width, so the answer is interpolated inside a bracket
rather than extrapolated off the end of a scan:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 10.7 \quad (10.5\text{--}10.9 \text{ on the } EW\

\end{equation}
by log--log interpolation of the seven scanned points solved with Brent's
method; the range carries the measurement error alone and none of the model
uncertainty. \emph{No run was made at $10.7$ itself} --- the value is read
off the bracket, and the bracket has a real width: the equivalent width
grows sublinearly with the reservoir, at a local power-law slope of $0.69$ at
the bottom of the ladder and $0.51$ between $5$ and $10$, but the two
rungs that straddle the measurement do not follow that trend, giving $0.25$

settle is that the equivalent width does \emph{not} saturate before the
measurement is reached, which the helium-rich end of the metal-free ladder
of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} had made a live possibility.

$2.5$--$2.6\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor $8$ below the
$2.03^{+0.58}_{-0.67}\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ the p-winds retrieval
imposes, and the elemental fluxes are $F_{\rm H} \simeq 4.3\times10^{6}$ and
$F_{\rm He} \simeq 2.1\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$.

\medskip
\noindent Two statements follow, and they are worth separating.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The closure by itself does not select a reservoir on this
      planet.} Every rung of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} satisfies it, at
      residual flux spreads of $0.4$--$2.6\%$ against the $5\%$ tolerance.
      What selects $10.7$ is the line, exactly as in
      Sects.~\ref{sec:ew} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad}. What the closure
      contributes is that the value is now consistent with a photochemical
      lower atmosphere carrying its own C, N and O, rather than resting on a
      bare H/He base --- and the price of that consistency is a helium
      fraction of $91$ per cent by number. Read against the $2.09$ the same
      line returns above a metal-free base, the pair measures the thermal
      trade of Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals} rather than two attempts at one
      number.
\item \textbf{The escape rate is flat along the whole ladder}, $7.39$ to
      $7.50$ in $\log_{10}\dot M$ over a factor $5.7$ in reservoir. So here
      too the composition is fixed by the line and not by the wind, and the
      $\dot M$ this memo quotes does not depend on where in the ladder the
      crossing lands.
\end{itemize}

The wind residual the JFNK finish reaches degrades as the reservoir grows
--- the line search stalls on the base contact mode and returns its best
iterate --- so the target was raised arm by arm and the achieved values
recorded rather than smoothed over: $\|R\| = 9.8\times10^{-5}$ at $2.09$
rising to $2.4$--$3.4\times10^{-4}$ at $8$ and above, all at
\texttt{info} $=0$. The test the closure actually applies is not that
residual but the radial spread of the elemental flux over the steady window,
which is what the $0.4$--$2.6\%$ above are.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the crossing does not fix, and what it inherits.} The
line is still too narrow: FWHM $0.267$\,\AA{} at the crossing against the
measured $0.841$, unchanged from Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} and from the
diffusion-off solutions, so the modelled red depth of $3.7$ per cent
overshoots the measured $1.254$ per cent by the same factor the width falls
short --- which is why the composition here is inferred from the equivalent
width and not from the depth. And the winds these numbers come from carry
the caveat of Sect.~\ref{sec:validity} in full: they are subsonic to the
$30\,R_p$ domain edge, their mass flux is set at the outer boundary rather
than at a critical point, and the critical region is transitional, so the
$\log_{10}\dot M = 7.40$--$7.42$ quoted above is \emph{unvalidated as a
continuum solution}. The He\,10830 layer that fixes $10.7$ is not: it sits
at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.021$.

\section{Is the wind collisional where it is set?}
\label{sec:validity}

Everything above reads the solutions as continuum solutions. A hydrodynamic
wind is one only where the gas is collisional on the scale over which the
flow varies, and above all through its critical point, which is where the
topology --- and with it the mass flux --- is fixed. This section measures
that. The tool is
\texttt{src/utils/collisional\_validity.py}; it reads an existing run
directory and changes nothing, and the definitions and full tables are in
\texttt{docs/collisional\_validity.md} (record:
\texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~81). The numbers below were measured on
2026-08-27 on the outputs the rest of this memo uses; nothing was re-run.

\subsection{What is measured}

\paragraph{The collisions.} The four momentum-transfer limits are the ones
the binary element-diffusion operator of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} already
uses, in the same Chapman--Enskog first approximation, so a mean free path
quoted here and a diffusion coefficient used by the wind cannot drift apart:
rigid-sphere neutral--neutral, non-resonant ion--neutral with the
induced-dipole and rigid-core frictions added, and screened Coulomb with the
Spitzer $\ln\Lambda$ for ion--ion and ion--electron. The step from a binary
diffusion coefficient to a collision frequency is the definition of that
coefficient itself, $\nu_{st} = n_t k T/(\mu_{st}\hat D_{st})$ with
$\hat D = n D$, in which the total carrier density cancels. Species are
H\,\textsc{i}, H\,\textsc{ii}, He\,\textsc{i}, He\,\textsc{ii},
He\,\textsc{iii} and $e$, plus the molecular carriers where a run tracks
them; He\,\textsc{i} $2^3S$ is a level inside the He\,\textsc{i} column and
is not counted twice, and metals are dropped as trace ($<10^{-3}$ of the
particles) except in the electron budget. Two channels are deliberately
absent --- resonant charge exchange and electron--neutral momentum transfer
--- and both lengthen the mean free path, so the verdict they produce is the
conservative one.

\paragraph{The numbers.} $\lambda_s = \bar v_s/\nu_s$ with the harmonic sum
over partners; the local structure scale is
$L = \min(H_p, L_v, r)$ with $H_p = |\mathrm{d}\ln p/\mathrm{d}r|^{-1}$
measured from the solution and $L_v = |v/(\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r)|$
admitted only above Mach $0.01$, below which it is the distance to a
stagnation point rather than a structure scale; and
$\mathrm{Kn}_s = \lambda_s/L$. The \emph{exobase} is the first crossing of
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 1$ from below, never extrapolated beyond the
domain. The \emph{critical point} is the sonic point taken with the code's
own sound speed, $c_s = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$ at
$\gamma = 5/3$, and not a re-definition. The \emph{critical region} runs
from the peak of volumetric heating to the sonic point. ``Collisional''
means $\max \mathrm{Kn} < 0.1$ across that region, over the bulk and over
every species carrying at least $10^{-3}$ of the mass; where it fails the
solution is reported \textbf{unvalidated}, not overestimated, because which
way a collisionless critical region moves the answer is not something the
continuum solution that assumed it away can decide.

\noindent\textbf{LHS\,1140\,b's wind never reaches its critical point inside
the computational domain.} All three solutions stay subsonic out to
$29\,R_p$, at $\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.54$--$0.57$. A subsonic solution is
not fixed by a critical point: its mass flux is set by the outer boundary
condition --- and at that boundary $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is already
$1.3$--$2.3$, with the exobase at $19.8$--$26.7\,R_p$, inside the domain.
The continuum equations were therefore integrated through, and closed at, a
region in which they do not hold. The escape rates quoted throughout this
memo, $\log_{10}\dot M \simeq 7.5$--$7.8$, are \textbf{unvalidated in the
collisional sense}.

Against the p-winds retrieval, whose isothermal Parker solution places its
sonic point at $8$--$9.5\,R_p$: that radius lies \emph{inside} the exobase
measured here, but $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ there is $0.14$--$0.30$, two to
three times the threshold. So the finding is neither ``the sonic point is
above the exobase'' nor ``the critical region is collisional'': the critical
region of LHS\,1140\,b is \emph{transitional}. The scale separation the
continuum equations need has been lost by the radius at which either code
sets the mass flux, while the gas is not yet free molecular.

\paragraph{It is the neutral hydrogen that decides it, and the line does not
care.} In the critical region of the well-mixed solution the species split
is H\,\textsc{i} $2.2$, He\,\textsc{i} $1.6$, H\,\textsc{ii}
$3.6\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{ii} $1.8\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{iii}
$4.7\times10^{-6}$, $e$ $6.5\times10^{-4}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:knudsen}b). The
neutrals carry the mass and the long mean free path; the charged species are
Coulomb-held. That matters for reading the rest of this memo, because the
He\,10830 metastable population forms at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$, where
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is $1.2\times10^{-4}$ at $1.1\,R_p$,
$0.004$--$0.006$ at $2\,R_p$ and $0.013$--$0.021$ at $3\,R_p$ in every case
here. \textbf{The line-forming layer is collisional by two orders of
magnitude.} What is unvalidated is the wind solution's mass flux, not the
absorption Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}--\ref{sec:width} measure, and not the
$22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of missing width, which is asked for at radii where the
continuum description is secure.

\paragraph{The single-temperature energy equation is not what fails.}
Electron--ion energy coupling is fast wherever it matters: the largest
$\tau^E_{ei}/\tau_{\rm heat}$ over the critical region is $0.030$, $0.023$
and $0.018$ in the three LHS\,1140\,b solutions and $8.3\times10^{-5}$ in
the control. Momentum coupling times are likewise short compared with the
flow time --- $\tau_{\rm H\,I} = 924$\,s at $8\,R_p$ against a flow time of
$1.9\times10^{5}$\,s --- which is not a contradiction but a different test:
a strongly subsonic flow gives hundreds of collisions per flow time even at
$\mathrm{Kn}\sim 1$, and it is the Knudsen number that decides continuum
validity, because the terms the closure drops (viscous stress, heat flux,
the non-Maxwellian tail) scale with the gradient over a mean free path
rather than with the collision count.

\paragraph{The kinetic scale, and why it is not a bound.} Jeans escape
through the measured exobase (Chamberlain \& Hunten 1987, eq.~7.2.5) gives
$1.0$--$2.0\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor about three below the
continuum $\dot M$. That is a \emph{scale}, not a bound. Jeans escape bounds
a continuum rate from below only when the exobase is well bound, and here
$\lambda_J(\mathrm{H}) = 0.78$--$1.45$: the exobase is barely gravitationally
bound, the Jeans integral is most of the Maxwellian rather than its
escaping tail, and the atmosphere is in blow-off. What the number does say
is that the continuum result sits within a factor of a few of what a
collisionless outer atmosphere at the same density and temperature would
lose, so the failure of the assumption is not hiding an order of magnitude
--- it is hiding a factor of a few, in an undetermined direction. On
HD\,209458\,b, where $\lambda_J = 10.85$ and the solution is validated
through its critical point, the hydrodynamic rate exceeds Jeans by
$10^{4}$: the signature of a driven wind rather than an evaporating
atmosphere.

\paragraph{Which profile pair is read moves the exobase, not the verdict.}
On the equilibrium profiles instead of the advection-corrected ones the
LHS\,1140\,b solutions are still subsonic across the domain
($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.82$), still reach $\mathrm{Kn}\sim 1$ where the
outer boundary sets the flux, and the control is still collisional through
its critical point. The exobase, however, moves above $30\,R_p$, because the
equilibrium far field is more ionized and Coulomb collisions there are far
stronger than neutral ones. An exobase radius quoted from this diagnostic
has to state which pair it came from; the numbers above are the
advection-corrected pair throughout.

\subsection{What this changes, and what it leaves open}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The outer boundary condition closes this wind, not a critical
      point.} For a transonic solution the critical point fixes the mass
      flux and the outer boundary is passive. Here it is the other way
      round, and the boundary sits in gas that is already collisionless. Any
      $\dot M$ quoted for LHS\,1140\,b from these solutions --- including the
      $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.500$ of the flux closure (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure})
      and the comparison with the rate the retrieval imposes --- has to carry
      that warning. The same applies to the p-winds number, whose sonic point
      sits at $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.14$--$0.30$.
\item \textbf{The rate itself is not settled here, and cannot be.} What it
      would take is a kinetic or transitional-flow calculation --- DSMC, or a
      13-moment closure carried through the transitional region --- and this
      repository contains neither. It is recorded as open, not estimated.
\item \textbf{The line analysis stands.} The He\,10830 layer is collisional
      by two orders of magnitude, so the equivalent-width matching, the
      composition inference conditional on it, and the invariant
      $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ width deficit are unaffected by this section. It
      constrains what the escape rate may be quoted as, not what the line
      says.
\end{itemize}

\section{Status against the goal}

The line is not reproduced. What stands:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Amount of absorption: matched, and the composition it takes
      is a pair set by the lower boundary} --- $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$ above
      a prescribed metal-free scalar base, $10.7$ above a lower atmosphere
      solved as a photochemical column and handed over as a profile
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}). Both carry H/He element diffusion at
      the same adopted $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, so the base is
      what separates them, not the mixing. Inside the prescribed-base branch
      the answer is further conditional: $0.060$ well mixed on the GJ\,699
      proxy, $0.55$ well mixed on the GJ\,1132 proxy, $4.68$ wherever
      $K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$ leaves the molecular coefficient alone. In each
      case the crossing is insensitive to the broadening treatment at the
      $2\%$ level. The match is always available, so it constrains the
      composition only as far as the assumptions behind it are secure.
\item \textbf{Line shape: not matched}, by a factor $3.4$ in both depth and
      width, and not fixable by composition. The missing ingredient is
      $22.30$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of velocity spread ($\sigma =
      9.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} and
      Fig.~\ref{fig:broadened}; supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth,
      blue depth and red/blue ratio inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval
      at once, but its physical origin is unidentified. The demand is
      unchanged under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$: measured the same
      way on the EW-matched diffusive solution it is $22.31$\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
      $0.01$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}); it is
      $22.39$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the flux-closed solution
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}); and it is
      $22.08$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the $5.1\times$ brighter proxy spectrum
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}). The adiabatic
      metastable bump, which would have supplied it without a free
      parameter, is present and measured (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) but sits
      too slow to contribute.
\item \textbf{Escape rate: an output, not a fit, and SED-set.}
      $\dot M \simeq 6\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy, a
      third of the value the retrieval imposes, and nearly independent of
      composition. On the GJ\,699 proxy, $5.1\times$ brighter in XUV, it is
      $4.0\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ --- a factor $7$ higher and within
      $0.04$\,dex of the value the paper retrieves on that same proxy
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}).
\item \textbf{That escape rate is unvalidated in the collisional sense; the
      line is not.} All three representative solutions are subsonic to
      $29\,R_p$ ($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.54$--$0.57$), so the mass flux is
      set at the outer boundary, where $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 1.3$--$2.3$;
      the exobase is at $19.8$--$26.7\,R_p$ and the p-winds sonic point at
      $8$--$9.5\,R_p$ sits at $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.14$--$0.30$, so the critical
      region of this planet is transitional for both codes. The He\,10830
      layer at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$ is collisional by two orders of magnitude
      ($\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.021$), and an HD\,209458\,b control validates
      cleanly at a factor $100$ lower $\mathrm{Kn}$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}). What the true rate is needs a kinetic or
      transitional-flow calculation, which this repository does not contain.
\item \textbf{Composition: base-limited and SED-limited, and weakly
      $K_{zz}$-limited.} In the
      well-mixed limit the equivalent-width crossing is
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.550$ on one proxy spectrum and $0.060$ on the
      other, helium-rich against helium-poor, with the measurement unable to
      separate them. With binary H/He diffusion active the crossing rises,
      and the whole $K_{zz}$ axis has now been walked: $\mathrm{He/H} =
      4.68$ on the plateau at $K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$, then $4.45$, $3.78$,
      $2.87$, $2.09$, $1.54$ and $1.11$ at $10^{6}$ through
      $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ (Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:hehkzz}) --- a factor $1.9$ across the range the
      literature leaves open, $4.2$ across the entire axis, against the
      factor $9$ the SED carries. Those are all conditions inside the
      prescribed-base branch. Replacing that base with the solved
      photochemical column moves the crossing to $10.7$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}), so end to end the inference spans
      $0.060$ to $10.7$, a factor $180$. So any composition read off this
      line, the paper's included, is strongly conditional on the assumed XUV
      spectrum and on what is placed at the lower boundary, and weakly
      conditional on the eddy coefficient, as $K_{zz}^{-0.14}$ wherever the
      eddy term acts at all.
\item \textbf{Composition as an output, on one axis, and the lower
      boundary raised to an axis of its own in its place.} With the lower
      atmosphere solved as a
      photochemical column and the elemental fluxes iterated to continuity
      across the $1\,\mu$bar match, He/H at the match is a solution:
      $2.09235$, single-valued to $5.5\times10^{-6}$ from trial fluxes a
      factor of ten apart, at $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.500$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}). At the adopted
      $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ that output is the well-mixed
      value --- only $2\times10^{-4}$ of it comes from the escape flux --- so
      the conditionality moves onto $K_{zz}$ rather than away. Above the
      match the wind still fractionates strongly, $F_{\rm He}/F_{\rm H} =
      0.73$ by mass against the base reservoir's $8.31$. The handoff also
      brings the column's C, N and O, which take $\log_{10}\dot M$ from
      $7.80$ to $7.46$ and the red-pair depth from $4.40\%$ to $1.52\%$;
      \textbf{at that base the flux-closed solution at $2.09$ no longer
      reproduces the equivalent width}, $0.421$ against the measured
      $1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA.
\item \textbf{The composition the line needs above a photochemical base:
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 10.7$.} A ladder of seven flux-closed solutions,
      reservoirs $2.09$ to $12.0$ and each arm converged to its own fixed
      point, crosses the measured equivalent width between the $10.31$ and
      $12.01$ rungs: $10.7$, with $10.5$--$10.9$ for the measurement's
      $1\sigma$ interval, interpolated inside that bracket and not run at
      the crossing itself (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder},
      Table~\ref{tab:closureladder}). The bracket is wide because the two
      rungs straddling the measurement have local power-law slopes of $0.25$
      and $1.42$ against the $0.5$--$0.7$ of the rest of the ladder, and
      that is not resolved. Two things follow: the closure condition alone
      does not select a reservoir here --- every rung satisfies it, so the
      line selects, and what the closure adds is consistency with a
      photochemical lower atmosphere carrying its own C, N and O --- and
      $\dot M$ is flat across the whole ladder,
      $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.39$--$7.50$, i.e.\ an eighth of the retrieval
      value at the crossing. Both numbers carry the width shortfall
      (FWHM $0.267$ against $0.841$\,\AA) and the collisional caveat above.
      This is the answer on the solved-column branch; $2.09$ is the answer
      to the same question above a prescribed metal-free base, and neither
      is an independent prediction of the model.
\item \textbf{Why the same helium absorbs differently over the two bases,
      measured.} Adding the column's C, N and O at fixed reservoir takes
      radiative cooling from $6$--$9\%$ of the heating to $71\%$ at
      $1.2\,R_p$ ($95\%$ of it metal-line cooling, largest single
      contributor neutral C\,\textsc{i}) and $T_{\max}$ from $5330$ to
      $3944$\,K; the metastable peak rises and the column inside $2\,R_p$
      is unchanged to $0.4\%$, but the column outside $2\,R_p$ falls by
      $2.6$, which is the factor the equivalent width falls by. Raising the
      reservoir to $10.31$ on that same base returns $T_{\max}$ to $5869$\,K
      --- past the metal-free $5330$ --- with the mean mass per particle at
      $2\,R_p$ up by $1.68$ and the heating per particle up by $1.88$, and
      with the C/N/O density diluted along with the hydrogen it is referred
      to. In the metastable balance the peak ratio $50.2 \rightarrow 122.5$
      splits as a source up by $1.55$ against a lifetime up by $1.57$, the
      latter because Penning ionization on H\,\textsc{i} --- $89\%$ of the
      sink at $2.09$, $60\%$ at $10.31$ --- slows as hydrogen is thinned
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}, Table~\ref{tab:thermostat},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:thermostat}). \emph{Read as an interpretation}, the
      ladder is a thermal trade between the metal content of the base and
      the helium content above it, and one line cannot separate them.
\item \textbf{The 2025 non-detection sits at about $0.3$ of the fiducial
      XUV.} Scaling the irradiation with the lower atmosphere held fixed,
      the red-pair depth falls below the $0.6$ per cent limit of the 2025
      epoch at $\simeq\!0.30$ of fiducial in the scalar-base model and just
      under $0.33$ in the flux-closed one (Sect.~\ref{sec:xuvgrid},
      Table~\ref{tab:xuvgrid}) --- about
      $10$\,erg\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ or less. The two models agree there
      despite differing by $4.6$ in reservoir and $2.5$ in escape rate; they
      do not agree on the slope with which they get there, which is a
      property of each model and not a result.
\item \textbf{Not verified: the second-chemistry-code cross-check.} VULCAN
      did not converge on this cold helium-rich column --- its convergence
      measure rose against the criterion with the timestep pinned, the
      limiting cell being H$_2$O at the cold trap --- so no composition from
      a second code, and no cross-code spread, is quoted
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}).
\item \textbf{One solution is not converged to tolerance.}
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 100$ stagnates at $\|R\| = 1.18\times10^{-3}$ from
      every seed tried; neighbours bracket it smoothly, so it is retained
      with the residual recorded.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Four axes have now been walked --- the assumed XUV spectrum, the
mixing assumption with its eddy coefficient, composition itself, and what is
placed at the lower boundary --- and each separates the two questions rather
than answering them together. The last of them is the one that splits the
answer in two rather than shifting it: $2.09$ above a prescribed metal-free
base, $10.7$ above a solved photochemical column. Every
one of them moves the composition the equivalent width implies, two of them
by a large factor; none of them moves the width. What is left as a possible
source of the missing $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is therefore what it was: velocity
fields that are three-dimensional, or heating a 1D hydrodynamic energy
equation does not carry. The adiabatic-cooling metastable bump, the one
candidate that would have needed no free parameter, has been looked for on
both spectra and does not supply it for this host (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}).
