\section{Goal, and the answer up front}

The question is whether a wind that is solved, rather than assumed,
reproduces the measured He\,\textsc{i} $10830$\,\AA{} transit line of
LHS\,1140\,b. The model is EXHALE, a one-dimensional calculation of
photoionization-driven escape in which the temperature, the velocity and the
composition all come out of the solution instead of being imposed. The answer
is that the strength of the absorption can be matched, but only by choosing a
helium abundance, and that the shape of the line cannot be matched at all. At
the abundance that matches the strength the modeled line is $3.3$ times too
deep and $3.3$ times too narrow, and it lacks $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of velocity
spread, the same number for every solution tried. To match the observation
something would have to supply that spread without adding absorption, and
neither the gas temperature, nor the outflow speed, nor any composition in
these solutions supplies it.

\medskip
\noindent Everything below was re-solved on 2026-09-16, with one build of the
code; the build and the files are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. The
results of 2026-08-30 that earlier revisions of this memo carried are
superseded twice over. Those states were never stationary below
$1.1\,R_p$ once the conservation equations are measured the way the code now
measures them. The campaign that replaced them on 2026-09-14 was itself
solved with a lower boundary that decided its entropy branch on the velocity
in the first cell rather than on the mass flux through its face, so that a
run labeled ``$226$\,K base'' in fact sat at $418$\,K in that cell. Both are
recorded in Sect.~\ref{sec:whatchanged}, with how far each number moved.
Every EXHALE number below is read from the set of models kept as the record
for this study. A number that is still the archived one is marked
\emph{archived 2026-08-30} where it appears, and one from the superseded
campaign \emph{preL21}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Result.} The \emph{amount} of absorption can be matched over
a prescribed metal-free base whose composition is a single number, at a
helium abundance that depends on what is assumed about the stellar spectrum
and about mixing. Over a lower atmosphere solved as a photochemical column
and handed over as a full profile, with C, N and O included, it can no longer
be matched from above. The smallest helium reservoir that the base-flux
iteration returns, $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$, already makes an equivalent width
$33$ per cent larger than the measured one, and the sequence of solutions
rises from there. The composition such a base would need lies below every
solution obtained and has not been solved. The \emph{width} is not matched
over either base, and the velocity it asks for is the same number every time.

\medskip
\noindent Two of the changes of Sect.~\ref{sec:whatchanged} act together on
the branch with the solved column. The line cooling by C, N and O, in the six
ions that the photochemical column brings into the base, used to be evaluated
in the coronal limit, that is, above the critical density of the levels it
excites. It is now taken from CHIANTI statistical equilibrium at the electron
density of each cell. The closed-form fits it replaces also mixed theoretical
with observed level energies, which at $2000$\,K is a factor $14$ in
$\exp(-\Delta E/kT)$ for O\,\textsc{ii}. The metals in the base therefore no
longer act as a thermostat. We measured this at one composition,
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$ over the scalar base, with and without the column's C,
N and O reservoirs: $T_{\max}$ went $5730 \to 5566$\,K and the red-pair
equivalent width $1.3749 \to 1.3721$\,\%\,\AA{}, a change of $0.20$ per cent.
The factor $2.6$ that the 2026-08-30 draft carried for this step is gone.

\medskip
\noindent (i) The amount is always matchable, so what the line returns is a
pair of conditional numbers rather than a measurement. Take the prescribed
base first. When that base is a single state carrying no metals, every
combination of assumed spectrum and assumed mixing reaches the observed
red-pair equivalent width at some helium abundance, and the conditions stack
on top of each other. In the well-mixed limit the crossing is
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044$ ($0.3877$--$0.4237$ on the measurement's $1\sigma$
interval) on the GJ\,1132 proxy spectrum and $0.0526$ ($0.0510$--$0.0543$),
below solar, on the GJ\,699 one: the second stand-in for LHS\,1140's
unobserved XUV, $5.1$ times brighter, which the paper also uses. When helium
and hydrogen are allowed to separate by diffusion, helium settles out of the
base unless an eddy term lifts it, and the crossing rises again. It is $3.10$
wherever $K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ does nothing against the
molecular coefficient, $1.516$ at the adopted $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and $0.893$
at $10^{11}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}). The dependence on the eddy
coefficient is weak and monotonic, as $K_{zz}^{-0.115}$ over the four decades
where the eddy term acts at all (a least-squares fit to the four crossings at
$10^{8}$ to $10^{11}$); the dependence on the assumed spectrum is a factor
$7.7$.

The branch with the solved column no longer brackets the measurement.
Replacing the simple base with a photochemical column (Photochem, an external
photochemistry code, run with its climate step solved and handed to EXHALE as
a profile matched at $1\,\mu$bar, at the same $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and with the
same diffusion active) brings C, N and O into the base. Nine reservoirs were
made consistent with the elemental fluxes the wind carries, and the
equivalent width they return runs from $1.4765$\,\%\,\AA{} at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.0924$ to $2.2289$\,\%\,\AA{} at $12.0137$. Every one of
them stands above the measured $1.108 \pm 0.030$, the lowest by $33$ per cent
(Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}). There is no crossing in this sequence. Read
against its own local slope, $\mathrm{d}\ln
EW/\mathrm{d}\ln(\mathrm{He/H}) = 0.49$ between the two lowest solutions, the
measurement would be reached near $\mathrm{He/H} \approx 1.1$, which is below
every solution obtained and is an extrapolation, not a solution. The earlier
crossing at $8.212$ was a consequence of the base metals cooling the wind;
they no longer do.

Neither branch is an independent prediction of the model. The number from the
prescribed base is the reservoir that the observed line picks out over that
base, and the escape rate, which is an output and not a fit, barely moves
along the way. Making the base consistent with the elemental fluxes does not
change that on the profile branch. That iteration returns the same
$\mathrm{He/H}$ at the match from trial fluxes a factor of ten apart,
single-valued to a few parts in $10^{6}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}), but at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ the eddy term mixes the column uniformly all the way to the
microbar matching level, so it hands back very nearly the reservoir it was
given. What it adds is that a composition is consistent with a photochemical
lower atmosphere carrying its own C, N and O, not that the composition is
measured.

The same helium now absorbs almost equally over the two bases. On the new set
of models, at essentially one composition, the metal-free scalar base at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$ and the flux-consistent solution on the photochemical
column at $2.0924$ give: $T_{\max}$ $5730$ against $5415$\,K, the metastable
peak $34.6$ against $35.8$\,cm$^{-3}$, the metastable column over
$2$--$20\,R_p$ $45.2$ against $45.7$\,cm$^{-2}$ in units of $R_p$, and the
equivalent width $1.3749$ against $1.4765$\,\%\,\AA{}. The photochemical
column now makes the \emph{stronger} line, by $7.4$ per cent, where the
2026-08-30 solutions made it weaker by a factor $2.6$. Raising the reservoir
still reheats the wind, and far more strongly than before: at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 10.01$ the mean mass per particle at $2\,R_p$ is
$3.17\,m_{\rm H}$ against $2.15$, the same deposited energy is shared among
fewer and heavier particles, and $T_{\max}$ rises to $7682$\,K
(Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}, Fig.~\ref{fig:thermostat}).

The escape rate is flat on every axis, and the crossings span a factor $59$.
$\log_{10}(\dot M/\mathrm{g\,s^{-1}}) = 7.84$--$7.87$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy
across composition, diffusion and eddy coefficient alike, $7.90$--$7.91$
across the whole flux-consistent sequence, and $8.42$--$8.57$ on the GJ\,699
proxy. Against that, the composition the line implies runs from $0.0526$ to
$3.10$ over choices this measurement cannot arbitrate. It is strongly
conditional on the assumed spectrum and on what is placed at the lower
boundary, and weakly and monotonically on the assumed mixing. Any composition
read off this line (the paper's $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much
as any value here) inherits those conditions.

\medskip
\noindent (ii) The width is not reached on any axis, and the deficit is the
same everywhere. At the equivalent-width crossing the profile is $3.3$ times
too deep and $3.3$ times too narrow (red depth $4.085$ against the measured
$1.254$ per cent, FWHM $0.2533$ against $0.841$\,\AA), and no composition
fixes it. Composition changes how much helium absorbs, not how the absorption
is spread in velocity. Measured directly, by convolving the solved profile
with a Gaussian and asking which Gaussian reproduces the observed line, the
missing spread is $22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
($\sigma = 9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) in the well-mixed limit,
$22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$,
$22.15$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the flux-consistent solution on the solved
photochemical column, and $21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the $5.1\times$ brighter
spectrum. That is a spread of $1.1$ per cent over four solutions that differ
by a factor $40$ in composition (Sects.~\ref{sec:broadening},
\ref{sec:diffbroad}, \ref{sec:closureladder} and~\ref{sec:gj699}).

Supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth, blue depth and red/blue ratio inside
the observed $1\sigma$ interval at once, with nothing tuned to them. What
produces it is unidentified. It is not thermal, since the solved gas cools
below $1000$\,K over the region where the line forms, and it is not bulk
outflow, which reaches $1.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $20\,R_p$ on the fainter
spectrum and $3.6$ on the brighter. Read as turbulence it is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.3$--$4.0$ against the sound speed these solutions themselves
carry, supersonic on every convention and well above what the literature is
willing to assume. One mechanism would supply width without a free parameter,
the metastable bump produced by adiabatic cooling of Schulik \& Owen (2025).
It is present in these solutions and was measured, but it sits at
$0.4$--$2.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}). The shortfall is in the
energy and momentum budget, not in the composition, and it is a published
problem rather than a defect of this setup (Sect.~\ref{sec:width}).

The paper's own p-winds fit does not reproduce the line either. It is broader
than the wind solved here, because its temperature is imposed at
$5000$--$6000$\,K where the line forms, but at FWHM $0.575$\,\AA{} with its
turbulence term on ($0.458$ without it) it is still $1.5$ times narrower than
the measured $0.841$, and it overshoots the blue component threefold. Adding
broadening does not rescue it. Its absorbing power is $24\%$ below the
measurement, so no convolution can reach the observed depth and width at
once. The width is a shortfall the two codes share. That is also what the
audit of the measurement side leaves (Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit}): the
measurement is not at fault, and neither one-dimensional steady-state wind
reproduces it.

\medskip
\noindent Two caveats attach to the outer wind rather than to the line. The
first is that the domain stops inside the critical point. These solutions are
the subsonic part of a transonic escape whose critical point sits at
$40.06\,R_p$, outside the $30\,R_p$ domain they are solved on, so what closes
them is the rule applied at the outer boundary and not a critical point. It
costs the mass flux almost nothing: $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.8737$, $7.8740$,
$7.8735$ at $30$, $45$ and $60\,R_p$, a spread of $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex over
a factor two in domain size, and the He\,\textsc{i} $10830$ equivalent width
moves by at most $1.7$ per cent. What it does cost is the outer profile: at
$28\,R_p$ the $30\,R_p$ solution is $25$ per cent too hot, $29$ per cent too
dense and $22$ per cent too slow against the converged answer, so nothing
above about $10\,R_p$ in these profiles should be quoted
(Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).

The second is that the gas is not collisional out there either:
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ passes $0.1$ at $7.30$--$7.44\,R_p$ and reaches
$0.68$--$0.71$ at $29\,R_p$, while the He\,$10830$ layer at
$1.1$--$3\,R_p$ sits at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.012$ and the line analysis is
untouched.

\medskip
\noindent One further caveat attaches to the composition. Above about
$4\,R_p$ the ionization state of this wind is frozen into the flow. The
recombination and photoionization Damk\"ohler numbers fall to $10^{-2}$ and
below, so the photoionization equilibrium the wind is \emph{solved} with is
not the composition that gas actually carries. The advection-corrected
ionization profiles say so. These are computed after the wind is solved: they
integrate the ionization along the flow instead of assuming it balances
locally, and they are what the transit tool consumes. The difference is not
small. The neutral hydrogen fraction differs by a factor $4.5$ at the outer
boundary, and the He\,\textsc{i} $10830$ equivalent width read from the
solution's own composition is $15.51$\,\%\,\AA{} against $1.17$ from the
corrected one, a factor $13.3$. Every line number in this memo is read from
the advection-corrected profiles. The solution's own composition is not
usable above $4\,R_p$ and is not quoted. Carrying the three ionization
fractions as transported variables is designed but not implemented
(Sect.~\ref{sec:whatchanged}).

\medskip
\noindent Three systematic effects ride on the absolute numbers, and no
convergence test catches any of them. They are stated here because they bound
how far any single crossing should be read.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0.2ex
\item The outer region is inherited from the starting state and is not
      resolved by the domain. A converged wind takes its $20$--$30\,R_p$
      state from the solution it was started from, and the size of the
      residual $\|R\|$ cannot tell two such states apart.
      Sect.~\ref{sec:validity} bounds what that costs the line: moving the
      outer boundary to $45$ and $60\,R_p$ moves the equivalent width by at
      most $1.7$ per cent and the mass flux by $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex, while
      the profiles above $10\,R_p$ move by $22$--$29$ per cent.
\item The tolerance of the base-flux iteration is visible in the answer. It
      stops when the undamped elemental imbalance falls to $0.05$, and the
      solutions end at $0.045$ for helium and $0.018$ for hydrogen. Tightening
      it further is refused, because the radial spread of the flux over the
      measurement window, $3.7\times10^{-3}$ in hydrogen and
      $4.9\times10^{-3}$ in helium, would then approach the imbalance being
      decided on it.
\item Which chemistry produced the column. Every column here is
      Photochem 0.9.0 with the local corrections listed in
      Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What changed since 2026-08-30}
\label{sec:whatchanged}

Every EXHALE number in this memo was re-solved between 2026-09-13 and
2026-09-16. This section says why the older answers were discarded, what was
corrected, and how far the answers moved. The case-by-case record and the
working notes behind each item are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

Throughout this section and the rest of the memo, a state is called
\emph{converged} when it satisfies the discretized conservation equations for
mass, momentum and energy, and the equations that set the composition, to a
stated tolerance. Because the flow is a wind only above about $1.2\,R_p$,
that check is applied above $1.2\,R_p$. The solution proceeds in cycles: the
wind is solved at fixed composition, then the composition is allowed to
adjust at fixed wind, and so on.

The set of models was solved twice. The campaign of 2026-09-14 was superseded
three days later by the correction to the lower boundary described in
Sect.~\ref{sec:l21}, and every case was solved again. The set of record is
the one solved on 2026-09-16, on a single build. Of the 86 cases in it that
have no molecules in the wind, 79 converge, each in one cycle started from
its own previous state; the seven that do not are the gaps in
Table~\ref{tab:campaign}. The results of 2026-09-14 are kept unchanged, and
the two generations are compared case by case in the working notes of
2026-09-15 on the re-solve. A number labeled \emph{preL21} below is from
2026-09-14; everything else is from the final pass.

\subsection{Why the 2026-08-30 solutions were discarded}

They were not stationary. Measured equation by equation the way the code now
measures them, the archived state carries an imbalance of order unity
everywhere between the base and about $1.1\,R_p$. In every cell below
$1.049\,R_p$ the energy equation is dominated by the divergence of its own
flux rather than by heating and cooling ($|\mathrm{d}F_3|/|\Gamma - \Lambda|
= 7\times10^{5}$ at cell 1, $4.9\times10^{1}$ at cell 16), and the momentum
equation is out by a constant $2.8$ per cent of the local weight over the
whole column. The imbalance belongs to the state and not to the way it is
measured. The velocity sawtooth in the base layer contributes nothing to the
energy equation, and putting the archived heating back into the same
hydrodynamic state lowers the worst cell only from $1.979$ to $1.930$. The
old runs reported convergence because they looked only above $1.2\,R_p$ and
never below it.

Interpolating such a state onto the current grid and re-solving does not
repair it. All four routes tried stall: the Newton solve at
$\|R\| = 1.6$--$2.0$ for 56 iterations; a time-marching restart whose
fractional radial spread of the mass flux never falls below the $10^{-2}$
handoff in 5600 steps; a cold two-stage march still at a spread of $9.5$
after 954 steps; and the stationary route, which does not converge and whose
composition step finds no admissible advance.

Separately, the code has moved in ways that reach this wind directly: the
quadrature over the photon grid (a $9.5$ per cent overcount of the
H\,\textsc{i} photoionization rate removed), the cell width
$\mathrm{d}r_j$, the Jupiter radius ($+2.26$ per cent in $R_0$), the helium
mass, the CODATA constants, the spectrum below $13.6$\,eV on which the
He($2\,^3S$) population depends, and a fix to an extra row the transit reader
was picking up. The new Jupiter radius also changes the grid, so no archived
state can be reloaded without interpolation.

\subsection{The well-balanced reconstruction}

No case in this campaign converges unless the discretization is the one that
keeps a hydrostatic atmosphere exactly hydrostatic. At the base of this
planet the Mach number of the flow is $2.7\times10^{-7}$. The contact speed
that the numerical flux puts through a base face is then set by the
third-order truncation error of the reconstruction in the face pressure
rather than by the velocity, which that error exceeds by one to three
decades. The energy equation of every base cell therefore measures its own
largest term against itself, and no starting state can lower it. A relative
density jump $d$ at a face enters the interface flux through its acoustic
part as $d\,\rho c_s$, which is $d/\mathcal{M}$ times $\rho v$. At
$\mathcal{M} \sim 10^{-6}$ a jump of one per cent is a six-decade error in
the flux.

The remedy is the reconstruction of K\"appeli \& Mishra (2016). Every cell
carries its own hydrostatic equilibrium, the reconstruction and the Riemann
solver act on the \emph{departure} from it, and the momentum source is the
face-pressure difference of that same equilibrium. At a discrete hydrostatic
equilibrium the flux difference and the source then cancel in the algebra
instead of in floating point. The term-by-term statement, the mapping onto
the code and the reason this is not the default are in the working notes of
2026-09-13 on hydrostatic discretization. The table below shows what it does
here, from the same starting state, the same input and the same build.

Low-Mach damping does nothing to the energy equation, because it is a fourth
difference of the \emph{velocity} and does not touch the truncation error in
the pressure. Three starting states whose base velocities differ by a factor
$10^{4}$ reach one solution ($\rho$ to $3.4\times10^{-10}$, $v$ to
$2.5\times10^{-8}$, $\dot M$ to seven digits), so the answers of this
campaign do not depend on which solved state they were started from.

\subsection{The secondary-ionization coupling, applied from the first evaluation}

By default the code switches on the Shull \& van Steenberg (1985) channel, in
which a fast photoelectron ionizes further atoms, only once a time-marching run
has first converged. The stationary route takes no time step, so a state solved
under the default is written with that channel off. The advection-corrected
profiles apply it unconditionally, and every number quoted here comes from
those, so they report the same wind with the coupling on. On the
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$, $\mathrm{He/H} = 1.6261$ case we measure
$\log_{10}\dot M = 8.29$ under the default against $7.87$ with the channel on
from the first evaluation, and $n(\mathrm{He}\,2\,^3S)$ differs by $74$ per
cent beyond $1.5\,R_p$, where the line forms. Every case of the campaign
turns the channel on from the start.

\subsection{The C, N and O line cooling at the local electron density}
\label{sec:cnocooling}

Six coolants that the photochemical column brings into the base
(C\,\textsc{i}, C\,\textsc{ii}, N\,\textsc{i}, N\,\textsc{ii}, O\,\textsc{i}
and O\,\textsc{ii}) were evaluated in the coronal limit, that is, above the
critical density of the levels they excite. Against the CHIANTI statistical
equilibrium of the same ion, the coefficient used stood at $0.02$ to
$5.4\times10^{4}$ times the right value over the range of conditions the wind
spans. The replacement is a density-resolved table read at the electron
density of each cell, and it stands at $0.969$ to $1.009$ at every point of a
$7\times6$ grid spanning $1000$ to $20\,000$\,K and $10^{4}$ to
$10^{9}$\,cm$^{-3}$.

A second error sat in the closed-form fits those six terms used, and is
removed along with them. The fits were built from CHIANTI \emph{theoretical}
transition energies, while the level populations that decide the emission use
the \emph{observed} level energies. The two differ by up to $14$ per cent in
$\Delta E$ for the O\,\textsc{ii} $^4S^{\circ}$--$^2D^{\circ}$ excitation,
which at $2000$\,K is a factor $14$ in $\exp(-\Delta E/kT)$.

This is the change that reshaped the branch of this memo with the solved
column. The metal content of the base is no longer a thermostat. At
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$ over the scalar base, adding the column's C, N and O
reservoirs moves $T_{\max}$ from $5730$ to $5566$\,K and the red-pair
equivalent width from $1.3749$ to $1.3721$\,\%\,\AA{}.

\subsection{The outer boundary}

The LHS\,1140\,b wind is subsonic at $30\,R_p$, so continuing the flow freely
across the outer boundary is not a passive extrapolation: one characteristic
points inward, and the rule applied there is an external condition. Solving
the same case on $45$ and $60\,R_p$ domains settles what that costs. The wind
is transonic, with its critical point at $40.06$ and $40.08\,R_p$ in the two
extended runs, and once the boundary is outside that point the two solutions
agree with each other to better than $0.1$ per cent everywhere.

The $30\,R_p$ answer holds for the quantities taken from it and not for the
rest: $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.8737$, $7.8740$, $7.8735$ at $30$, $45$ and
$60\,R_p$ and the He\,\textsc{i} $10830$ equivalent width within $1.7$ per
cent, against $25$ per cent in temperature, $29$ in density and $22$ in
velocity at $28\,R_p$. No profile above about $10\,R_p$ is quoted from these
runs. The exobase of the extended solutions sits at $29.0\,R_p$, below the
critical point, so the continuum model fixes its own topology in gas it
cannot describe. The insensitivity of the mass flux is a statement about the
discrete problem and not a physical validation.

\subsection{The composition above 4\,$R_p$}
\label{sec:frozencomp}

The wind is solved with a composition in photoionization equilibrium, and
above about $4\,R_p$ that composition is not the one the gas carries. Over a
flow time $r/v$ the hydrogen recombines $1.7$ times at $2\,R_p$ and
$5\times10^{-3}$ times at $29\,R_p$, and is photoionized $7\times10^{-2}$ and
$10^{-2}$ times at the same radii. The ionization state is frozen into the
parcel and carried outward. The equilibrium neutral fraction falls from
$0.963$ at $2\,R_p$ to $0.346$ at $29\,R_p$ while the advection-corrected one
goes from $0.974$ to $0.941$, and the photoheating that follows the neutral
density stands a factor $5.9$ apart at $44\,R_p$.

The consequence for the line is a choice of which profile to read rather than
a correction. The He\,\textsc{i} $10830$ equivalent width read from the
solution's own composition is $15.5116$\,\%\,\AA{} against $1.1702$ from the
advection-corrected one, a factor $13.3$. Feeding the transit tool the
solution's \emph{temperature} together with the corrected composition instead
moves it by $0.15$ per cent. The line depends on the composition and hardly
at all on anything else in those two files, and every line number here is
read from the advection-corrected profiles. Carrying
$x(\mathrm{H\,II})$, $x(\mathrm{He\,II})$ and $x(\mathrm{He\,III})$ as
transported fractions of each element's nuclei is designed but not
implemented.

One defect was found and fixed on the way. The advection correction discarded
roots returned by the nonlinear solver with a warning flag even where the
iterate satisfied the energy equation to $10^{-17}$ of the largest term the
equation holds, and because the correction marches outward cell by cell, a
discard restarted the temperature profile at every cell above it. With the
roots accepted on their residual, the $45$ and $60\,R_p$ corrected
temperatures agree to $0.19$ per cent, which is the $0.28$ per cent at which
the two \emph{solutions} agree.

\subsection{The convergence procedure at low XUV}

Five cases of the XUV grid did not converge in the first campaign, and the
reason lies in the cycle rather than in the wind. Each cycle solves the wind
at fixed composition and then relaxes the composition at fixed wind. The
error of that alternation is the undamped distance the composition step
returns, which the under-relaxation contracts by $1-\omega$ per cycle. The
control that decides whether the cycles are making progress reads a different
quantity, the distance of the state from being fully converged. Where that
distance fails to fall it halves $\omega$, taking the contraction from $2.00$
to $1.14$ per cycle, and after three such cycles it ends the loop. On these
cases the two quantities disagree from the first cycle: the composition
distance falls every cycle while the imbalance in the equation for the
helium-to-hydrogen ratio rises. The case at $0.30$ of the same spectrum,
which does converge, is the control: it entered at the same composition
distance, its helium-to-hydrogen imbalance also failed to fall over its first
cycle, and it converged at cycle 13 with $\omega$ at $0.500$ throughout. What
refused the five was not the energy equation in the first cell. At the last
cycle of the worst of them the three hydrodynamic equations read mass
$2.30\times10^{-8}$, momentum $3.39\times10^{-14}$ and energy
$8.49\times10^{-7}$ against a tolerance of $10^{-6}$, while the
helium-to-hydrogen equation read $2.12\times10^{-2}$ against $10^{-5}$.

On the corrected lower boundary these cases start further still from their
fixed point, and the remedy is to start the Newton solver's artificial time
step high, at $10^{8}$, instead of letting it collapse from $1$. MEASURED on
five cases with a scalar base, that turns hours of refusing into minutes of
converging: the case at $0.20$ of the fiducial spectrum and
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$ went from four cycles in $6$\,h\,$22$, still refusing,
to converged in $17$ minutes. Nine of the thirteen cases treated this way
converged within the hour. \emph{Four did not}, and they are the gaps in
Table~\ref{tab:xuvgrid}: the run over the scalar base at $0.10$ of the
fiducial with $\mathrm{He/H} = 9.7$, and the photochemical cases at $0.15$, $0.20$ and
$0.25$, all keep the same behavior at $\mathrm{d}\tau_0 = 10^{8}$, the step
falling back to order ten while the mass equation in the first cell holds
$\|R\|$ near $1.5$. Starting the step higher is not what they need.
Separately, the three cases at $0.01$ of the fiducial have never solved on
any boundary. That is attributed to the Newton model of a wind subsonic to
Mach $10^{-8}$ over its whole lower atmosphere; the finite-difference
Jacobian and the convergence criterion were both measured and both cleared.

\subsection{The base boundary, which chose its branch on the wrong quantity}
\label{sec:l21}

This is the change that forced the second campaign, and it is the largest
single movement in the set of models. The base face of a converged
LHS\,1140\,b state is an \emph{inflow} face carrying the wind's own mass
flux. The boundary condition decided whether to treat it as inflow or as
reversal using the cell-centered product $\rho_1 v_1 r_1^2$ of the first
interior cell. At this base that product is not a flux: it is a two-cell
oscillation in the velocity, sitting on the cell centers. MEASURED on the
fiducial case, the face mass flux from the Riemann solver is
$+1.00000\,F_{\rm wind}$ at every one of the $500$ faces including the base
face (largest departure $1.6\times10^{-9}$), while the cell-centered product
reads $-2.00\,F_{\rm wind}$ at cell 1 and $-2.27$ at cell 2. The boundary was
therefore running $97$ per cent in the reversal branch, where the face
density is the interior's isentrope rather than the reservoir's, and the two
differ by $\rho_{\rm rev}/\rho_{\rm res} = 0.531$ at the common face
pressure. The $226$\,K reservoir prescribed for the lower atmosphere was
entering the state with a weight of $0.026$, and the first cell of a
``$226$\,K base'' run sat at $417.6$\,K.

No choice of blending width repairs that, because the discriminant has the
wrong sign: a narrower window takes the reversal branch more completely.
Deciding the branch on the mass flux the state actually carries is the fix. A
second error sat beside it. The blending window was $10^{-6}$ in face Mach
number, chosen against a much hotter planet, while LHS\,1140\,b's own base
runs at Mach $4.6\times10^{-7}$, \emph{below} the window, so even with the
sign repaired a fifth of the reservoir's entropy would be blended away on a
clean inflow. The window is now $10^{-8}$. On the same state the branch reads
$M_{\rm branch} = +4.02\times10^{-7}$ and a reversal weight of zero: the
reservoir sets the entropy of the gas that enters, which is what the boundary
exists to do.

What it did to the set of models, over the 79 cases that converge both ways:
the first physical cell falls by a median $46$ per cent in temperature and
rises by a median factor $1.82$ in density, $\dot M$ falls by a median $2.0$
per cent with no case rising, and the red-pair equivalent width falls by a
median $2.0$ per cent. On the fiducial case the first cell goes from $417.6$
to $238.0$\,K. The profiles cross near cell 21 and agree to a fraction of a
per cent in the outer wind. Four solutions of the GJ\,699 sequence are
identical across the correction, because on that weak spectrum the interior
isentrope at the base already \emph{was} the reservoir, so the branch the old
boundary took wrongly happened to give the right state. The correction
changes what was wrong and nothing else.

\subsection{What a written state means when it is read back}

Two further items tightened the relation between a state and the files that
hold it, and neither changes physics.

A converged state did not reproduce its own convergence when it was read
back. The pair of output files states the density twice, once directly and
once as the sum over species, and the two agree only while the composition
closes its own mass. It does not close exactly, and the loader took the
species as the authority, so the state handed to the check stood some $2000$
units in the last place from the one that had been solved. At this base the
hydrodynamic equations cancel their largest term by $6.2\times10^{5}$, and at
that cancellation $2000$ units in the last place of the density is the whole
imbalance. The loader now takes the density from its own column and scales
the loaded species onto it, one factor per cell, which leaves every element
ratio and every ionization split where the file put them. One unit in the
last place of the density still moves the largest cellwise imbalance of the
energy equation by about $11$ per cent, so identical reproduction of a
convergence check is not available, and the stated contract says so.

The composition a sweep returns is now put on the density the sweep was
given, and the mass closure $\max_j |\sum_i f_i A_i - 1|$ is reported with
the convergence check. A companion question, whether the written cooling
column is the cooling of the written state, was raised as a defect and is
answered in the negative: on every state the current code produces, that
column and the total of the cooling breakdown agree to between
$3.4\times10^{-15}$ and $3.0\times10^{-10}$. The factor $1.8$ that raised the
question was measured on a regression case pinned from an earlier generation
of the code. What was missing was the check itself, which is now there.

\subsection{The molecular rate network, and the artificial time step}

One item asked which chemistry is right about the base layer, where the state
handed over from the lower atmosphere stands $7$ to $21$ times above the root
of the wind network's own equation for the transported H$_2$. It retired one
reaction, added the channel of Black (1978), and rewrote another. Those
repairs are in the build every number here stands on. They move the molecular
test cases and leave an atomic wind alone, so nothing in Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}
to~\ref{sec:validity} depends on them.

A second item took both ends of the schedule for the Newton solver's
artificial time step: a shrink on damped steps that could pin the step at its
floor, and a growth that could climb to $10^{14}$ while the residual stood at
$1.9$. The rule now in place clips the cut of an accepted step at ten and
floors it a fixed span below the step the solve has already earned at a whole
step, with a cap on how many damped steps may run in a row. Turning the guard
off reproduces the earlier arithmetic exactly. What it does to the XUV grid
of this planet is measured below.

\section{Inputs, and how each was pinned}
\subsection{System}
Planet and star from Cadieux et al.\ (2024) as quoted by C26:
$R_p = 1.730 \pm 0.025\,R_\oplus$, $M_p = 5.60 \pm 0.19\,M_\oplus$,
$T_{\rm eq} = 226$\,K, $a = 0.0946$\,AU, $M_\star = 0.1844\,M_\odot$,
$R_\star = 0.2159\,R_\odot$. One trap: EXHALE converts $R_p$ with its own
internal constants (mean radii, $R_J = 6.9911\times10^{9}$\,cm,
$R_\oplus = 6.3725\times10^{8}$\,cm), so the input must be built with those
rather than with IAU equatorial values. The wrong pair costs $2.2\%$ in
radius and $4.4\%$ in transit depth.

\subsection{Lower boundary condition}
\label{sec:base_bc}
EXHALE anchors the lower boundary at the planetary radius $R_p$ by fixing the
pressure there, which is the standard CETIMB condition, at
$P_0 = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{bar} = 1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-3}}$
($0.1$\,Pa). The corresponding total number density $n_0$ at the base follows
from the ideal gas law at the equilibrium temperature
$T_0 = T_{\rm eq} = 226.0$\,K:
\begin{equation}
  n_0 = \frac{P_0}{k_B T_0 n_{\rm tot,bc}}
      = \frac{1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}}}{(1.380649\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{erg\,K^{-1}}) \times 226.0\,\mathrm{K}}
      = 3.20485\times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}},
\end{equation}
where $n_{\rm tot,bc} = 1.0$ for neutral atomic gas. In logarithmic form,
$\log_{10}(n_0/\mathrm{cm^{-3}}) = 13.506$, which is what the input file
carries. When the boundary is anchored on pressure, EXHALE re-evaluates and
enforces this relation internally, so the density entered in the input is
consistent with the pressure by construction.

\subsection{Stellar spectrum}
\label{sec:sed}
The paper describes its normalization two ways that cannot both hold. The
Supplement's prose says the proxy spectrum is scaled so that its integrated
X-ray flux matches the one measured for LHS\,1140, while the quoted constants
($A_{1132} = 0.59$, $A_{699} = 0.056$) are the catalog X-ray flux
\emph{ratios}, a factor $1.8$ apart. The authors' script settles it:
\begin{verbatim}
Lx_ratio_1132 = 0.59
norm_constant_1132 = Lx_ratio_1132*(d_1140/(smax_1140*au2m))**2
\end{verbatim}
applied to the plain constant-resolution v23 product. That combination also
reproduces the fiducial XUV flux the main text quotes,
$0.033$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ (we measure $33.6$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ over
$10$--$1300$\,\AA{}). All results here use it. The proxy spectrum and the
paper's own APEC fit disagree in the X-ray band ($4.4$ against
$2.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ at the orbit). That inconsistency comes from
the paper's procedure and is not introduced here.

\subsection{The atomic input that sets the line: He($2\,^3S$) destruction on
atomic hydrogen}
\label{sec:penningrate}

Every composition this memo quotes is read off the metastable helium
population, and in this wind that population is drained almost entirely by
one reaction, He($2\,^3S$)\,$+$\,H. Its rate coefficient is therefore an
input of the same standing as the spectrum, and it has been changed since the
earlier draft. The grounds are given here because every number below moved
with it.

\medskip
\noindent The code carried the fit of Taylor et al.\ (2025), Table~2,
transcribed correctly: $1.9\times10^{-9}(300/T)^{0.07}$ below $4000$\,K and
$9.1\times10^{-9}(300/T)^{0.50}$ above it. Those two branches do not meet: at
$4000$\,K they give $1.5849$ and $2.4921\times10^{-9}$, a jump of $1.5724$. A
rate coefficient is a continuous function of temperature, and ``it is the
published value'' is not a defense of an expression that is discontinuous in
a state variable. In these solutions the seam falls at $1.11$--$1.18\,R_p$,
inside the region where the line forms. In the figures of the earlier draft it
showed up as a one-cell step of $33$ per cent in $n(2\,^3S)$.

\medskip
\noindent The fit also disagrees with its own paper. Taylor et al.'s Fig.~19
(bottom right) plots the quantity Table~2 is meant to represent, their
Maxwell--Boltzmann average of the Cohen \& Lane (1971) and Morgner \& Niehaus
(1979) cross sections, and that curve has a maximum near $1400$\,K. A
monotonically decreasing power law cannot produce a maximum, so the tabulated
low branch cannot be the same quantity; digitized, the two differ by
$1.13$--$1.62$ over $500$--$4000$\,K. Above the seam the high branch is
worse: $2.23\times10^{-9}$ at $5000$\,K, above every cross-section
determination collected in Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025), Fig.~5, which lie between
about $1.3$ and $1.7\times10^{-9}$ there. Their appendix records how this
happened: an equation already implemented in their own code, fitted in two
pieces by least squares, with nothing tying the pieces together at the join.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What is used now.} Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025), A\&A 698, A199,
publishes in the caption of its Fig.~5 a closed form for the
Maxwell--Boltzmann average of the Movre \& Meyer (1997) total ionization
cross sections,
\begin{equation}
  k = 10^{-9}\exp\!\left(\frac{c}{T} + d_1\ln T + d_2(\ln T)^2
      + d_3(\ln T)^3\right)\ \mathrm{cm^{3}\,s^{-1}},
  \label{eq:gm25}
\end{equation}
with $c = -8.64804\times10^{1}$, $d_1 = -2.86766\times10^{-1}$,
$d_2 = +8.68445\times10^{-2}$ and $d_3 = -5.73001\times10^{-3}$, stated there
as accurate to better than $2\%$ over $200$--$10^{4}$\,K. Measured here, it
reproduces that paper's own Table~A.5 to $0.4\%$, and it is continuous at
$4000$\,K to the eighth digit ($1.35630953\times10^{-9}$ at $3999.9$\,K
against $1.35630905$ at $4000.1$).

The change is not one paper's fit traded for another's. Taylor's own Fig.~19
and the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz curve agree to $10$--$20\%$. The outlier is the
tabulated fit, and that is what the code was using.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The $0.9\!:\!0.1$ branching.} Equation~(\ref{eq:gm25}) is
the \emph{total} ionization rate: Penning ionization,
He($2\,^3S$)\,$+$\,H\,$\rightarrow$\,He($1\,^1S$)\,$+$\,H$^+ + e^-$, and the
associative channel that leaves HeH$^+$, sharing one cross section. Garc\'ia
Mu\~noz splits them ``an average $0.9\!:\!0.1$'', and the machine-readable
network file deposited with that paper (Zenodo 15453858) applies that split
through two amplitudes, one per channel. EXHALE now does the same. The split
is more than a scale factor. The total rate removes the metastable atom, so
every sink must keep the full sum; using $0.9$ there would make the
observable, $n(2\,^3S)$, $10$ per cent too high. The $0.9$ scales only the
terms that leave a lasting proton behind and deposit the Penning
exothermicity, and the remaining $0.1$ becomes a source of HeH$^+$ in the
molecular system.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Effect on the numbers.} The new coefficient is lower than
the old one everywhere in this wind (new over old: $0.69$ at $1000$\,K,
$0.86$ just below the seam, $0.54$ just above, $0.81$ at $10^{4}$\,K), so the
metastable level lives longer and the line is stronger at fixed composition.
Re-solved, the case at $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ goes from $EW = 1.109$ to
$1.310$\,\%\,\AA{} and the one at $2.13$ with diffusion at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ from $1.129$ to $1.338$, with $\log_{10}\dot M$ unchanged
in both. Every crossing therefore moves down, and the composition sequences
were re-solved rather than scaled by a local slope, because a $+18$ per cent
shift is well outside the range over which such a slope is valid. A detector
that looks for a one-cell step in the metastable population flags no cell at
the seam in any of the runs quoted here.

\section{Reproducing the paper's p-winds model}

We ran p-winds at the authors' settings: their spectrum normalization,
$R_p = 0.154\,R_J$, $b = 0.23$, five-phase transit averaging, their
turbulent broadening term on, and their fitted line-of-sight bulk velocity
$v_{\rm wind} = 2.26$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The composition and outflow are the MCMC
medians ($\dot M = 2.03\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $T = 5160$\,K,
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 1.01\times10^{-3}$), the set of values their Fig.~4
legend states for the plotted curve. Our reproduction gives a red depth of
$1.376\%$ and FWHM $0.575$\,\AA{}, against the $1.32\%$ and
$\sim 0.71$\,\AA{} read off their Fig.~4. The depth matches to $3\%$, but the
line is about $20\%$ too narrow.

The width residual does not depend on which point in parameter space is
drawn. Running the same reproduction at the grid maximum-likelihood point
($1.83\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $5131.6$\,K, $8.338\times10^{-4}$) gives
red $1.366\%$ and FWHM $0.581$\,\AA{}, so the two choices differ by less than
$0.01$\,\AA{} in width. What produces the extra width in the published curve
has not been identified.

Two things here cost time to find.

First, the curves drawn over the spectrum in the paper's Fig.~3 are the
three-Gaussian empirical fits, not p-winds; the physical model is overlaid
only in Fig.~4. The close agreement in Fig.~3 is a fit to the data, and says
nothing about the model's line shape.

Second, the paper's plotted model is visibly broader than a plain p-winds
run. We first read that as an undocumented instrumental kernel, and it is not
one. Their script turns the turbulent broadening term on and averages over
five transit phases. Both widen the line, and together they account for the
difference.

\section{Turbulent broadening: what the literature does}

Since that switch turned out to matter, all 43 papers held with this study
were checked: every PDF converted to text, searched for ``turbulen'',
``microturbulen'' and ``non-thermal broadening'', and each hit read in
context. Fifteen matched; most use ``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$
or for flow structure in 3D simulations, not for line broadening.
Section~\ref{sec:lit} tabulates the ones that bear on broadening.

The convention, stated most explicitly by Yan et al.\ (2024), replaces the
thermal Doppler width by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu_D = \frac{\nu_0}{c}\left(\frac{2kT}{m} + v_{\rm turb}^2\right)^{1/2},
  \qquad v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{\frac{5kT}{3m}} = c_s ,
\end{equation}
the monatomic sound speed, as assumed by Salz et al.\ (2018), Lamp\'on et
al.\ (2020) and Czesla et al.\ (2022). The p-winds implementation uses
$\sqrt{5kT/6m}$ against a Doppler width that lacks the factor $2$, which is
the same physics; in the convention EXHALE uses for the Doppler parameter the
same term is $b \to \sqrt{11/6}\,v_{\rm th}$, a factor $1.354$. That is what
the optional turbulence term in our transit tool applies. It is opt-in and
off by default, since the field does not agree that it belongs.

\subsection{Paper by paper}
\label{sec:lit}

\paragraph{Adopt it as an assumed value.}
Salz et al.\ (2016), Eq.~(5): ``$b$ is the Doppler parameter,
$v_{\rm th}$ the thermal Doppler velocity, and for the microturbulence
$v_{\rm micro}$ we use the sound speed'', applied when synthesizing the
transit profile. Their atmosphere solver separately carries a constant
$1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ microturbulence at the lower boundary, a different
quantity, which they note ``has little impact on the overall atmospheric
structure''. Lamp\'on et al.\ (2020, 2021) is the reference implementation,
with turbulent broadening a free or assumed parameter of 1D isothermal Parker
fits, and Czesla et al.\ (2022) follows it. Cherubim et al.\ (2026) inherits
the choice by leaving the p-winds turbulence term on, so the LHS\,1140\,b
retrieval carries it. The paper text does not say so anywhere.

\paragraph{Test it, then leave it out.}
Yan et al.\ (2024) give the cleanest statement of the convention and of the
doubt. They record that ``these authors assumed that the turbulence velocity
$v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$, which corresponds to the speed of sound of a
monatomic gas and, thus, to the transonic case with a Mach number of 1'',
then keep it out of their nominal models and test $v_{\rm turb} = 0.5$, $1.0$
and $1.5\,c_s$ as a sensitivity. Their finding for He\,10830 is that
turbulence ``decreases the absorption depth slightly at the line center, but
doesn't significantly alter the absorption at the line wings''. They argue
Mach~1 is likely an overestimate, since in the diffuse ISM turbulent velocity
anti-correlates with temperature and the Mach number is below unity at a few
thousand K. They conclude that ``the turbulence effect is likely less
significant than the one explored in this paper''. Yan et al.\ (2019)
computes the Doppler width explicitly ``assuming no turbulence''.
\paragraph{Argue against it.}
Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009), on Garc\'ia Mu\~noz's (2007) proposal to
broaden Ly$\alpha$ by wind turbulence: ``to generate the large velocities
observed, the energy in such turbulence would need to exceed the thermal and
bulk kinetic energies in the mean flow by a factor of $\sim 100$. Such
energy requirements seem insurmountable.'' Koskinen et al.\ (2013b) find
that ``non-thermal broadening such as that proposed by Ben-Jaffel and
Hosseini (2010) does not appear to be necessary to explain the current
observations'', while allowing that stellar-wind interaction may produce
broadening turbulence. Schulik \& Owen (2025) offer a physical alternative,
in which adiabatic cooling produces a metastable-helium population bump at
high velocity and widens the line with no free parameter, and they are
direct about the isothermal case: that model ``can only explain the
broadness of the data under physically inconsistent assumptions, i.e.\ those
of even larger mass-loss rates, not supported by the energy input into this
planet, and an empirical free parameter, turbulent broadening (Lamp\'on et
al.\ 2021)''. They add that $T = 10^{4}$\,K already maximizes what thermal
broadening alone can give.

\paragraph{Need it even so.}
Taylor et al.\ (2026) is the closest analogue to the present work: a
self-consistent model (energy balance, multispecies diffusion, non-LTE
H($n=2$)) that reproduces the HD\,189733\,b line core but not its width.
They write that ``several prior analyses have matched the HD\,189733\,b width
by introducing ad hoc (micro)turbulent or Gaussian broadening terms; here, we
show that this step remains necessary even in our self-consistent
framework'', and quantify it as $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of additional
non-thermal broadening. They suggest, calling the suggestion speculative,
that it is plasma turbulence from ions trapped on low-latitude planetary
field lines, strengthening with stellar activity. Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022),
as reported there, instead imposed reduced hydrogen line cooling and
prescribed zonal jets of $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in a 3D multifluid
model.

\paragraph{Matches that are not about broadening.}
Recorded so the sweep need not be repeated: Wogan (2025), Lavvas (2014,
2019), Robeling (2026) and the second half of Koskinen et al.\ (2013b) use
``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$; the 2026 preprint
arXiv:2607.18193 uses it for flow-flow interaction in a 3D stellar-wind
simulation; Jensen (2012) for
photospheric turbulence driving stellar activity; Taylor et al.\ (2025) for
gravity-wave breaking. The Vidal-Madjar et al.\ (2003) \textit{Nature} letter
matches only on its first page, which carries the tail of the preceding
letter, on Crab pulsar radio emission; the authors themselves do not discuss
turbulence.

\subsection{Empirical baseline: turbulence in Solar System giant planets}
\label{sec:solarsys_turb}

Whether sonic or supersonic ($v_{\rm turb} \sim c_s$) turbulence is
physically plausible in a planetary atmosphere can be checked against the
measured turbulence and non-thermal velocity fields in the stratified layers
of Jupiter and Saturn:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Troposphere and cloud deck ($\sim 0.1$--$1$\,bar).}
      Cloud-tracking measurements from \textit{Voyager}, \textit{Galileo},
      \textit{Cassini}, and \textit{Juno} (e.g., Salyk et al.\ 2006; Del
      Genio et al.\ 2007; Ingersoll et al.) isolate the fluctuating eddy
      velocities $v' = \sqrt{\overline{u'^2 + v'^2}}$ from the mean zonal
      jets ($100$--$500$\,m\,s$^{-1}$). The resulting RMS turbulent eddy
      velocity is $v' \approx 3$--$10$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Jupiter (locally
      $20$--$30$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the Great Red Spot shear zone) and
      $5$--$15$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Saturn. Compared to the tropospheric sound
      speed ($c_s \approx 800$--$900$\,m\,s$^{-1}$), this is deeply subsonic
      turbulence, with Mach numbers $\mathcal{M} \sim 0.005$--$0.02$.
\item \emph{Stratosphere ($\sim 10\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$--$10\,\mathrm{mbar}$).}
      High-resolution submillimeter spectroscopy of impact-delivered HCN/CO
      molecules with ALMA (e.g., Cavali\'e et al.\ 2021) and Herschel reveals
      stratospheric jet velocities up to $300$--$400$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the
      auroral zone, but the associated non-thermal velocity dispersion
      (turbulence and wave motions) remains limited to tens of m\,s$^{-1}$
      up to $\sim 100$--$200$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ ($\mathcal{M} \lesssim 0.2$).
\item \emph{Thermosphere and auroral ionosphere ($\lesssim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$).}
      Ground-based high-resolution ($R \sim 10^5$) infrared spectroscopy of
      $\mathrm{H}_3^+$ line profiles (e.g., with VLT/CRIRES, Keck, and CFHT)
      disentangles thermal Doppler broadening from non-thermal velocity
      fields. In non-auroral mid-latitudes, the non-thermal velocity is
      $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.2$--$0.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. In the auroral oval,
      where intense magnetosphere--ionosphere coupling, ion drag, and Joule
      heating inject substantial kinetic energy, non-thermal broadening
      reaches $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.8$--$2.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent These measurements show that pure hydrodynamic planetary
turbulence is strictly subsonic ($\mathcal{M} \ll 1$), and that even strong
magnetospheric auroral driving in upper thermospheres caps non-thermal
broadening at $\lesssim 2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Turbulence and 3D hydrodynamic simulations of exoplanets}
\label{sec:3d_turb}

Beyond 1D parameterizations, 3D simulations show how turbulent mixing,
non-thermal velocity structure and line broadening arise in several classes
of exoplanet:

\paragraph{1. 3D hot Jupiter escape and circulation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{3D atmospheric escape and kinematic line broadening.}
      Hydrodynamic 3D escape simulations (e.g., McCann et al.\ 2019;
      Debrecht et al.\ 2020; Carolan et al.\ 2020, 2021; Wang \& Dai 2021;
      MacLeod \& Oklop\v{c}i\'c 2022) show that apparent transmission
      line broadening comes mainly from large-scale 3D kinematic velocity
      gradients ($10$--$50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) across the day-to-night outflow,
      Coriolis-deflected tails, and tidal distortion, rather than from
      unresolved isotropic microscale turbulence. The collision between the
      expanding planetary wind and the incoming stellar wind creates a shear
      layer prone to Kelvin--Helmholtz instabilities and turbulent mixing at
      the contact discontinuity. In 3D multifluid models of HD\,189733\,b,
      Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022, 2023) show that upper-atmospheric zonal jets
      of $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ combined with 3D outflow geometry reproduce
      the observed broad transmission profiles without invoking ad hoc
      microturbulence.
\item \emph{3D general circulation models (GCMs) and macroturbulent mixing.}
      In the observable lower-to-middle atmosphere ($1\,\mathrm{bar}$ to
      $\sim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$), 3D GCMs (e.g., Showman \& Polvani 2011;
      Showman et al.\ 2013, 2015; Parmentier et al.\ 2013; Charnay et al.\
      2015; Zhang \& Showman 2018) show that equatorial superrotation
      ($1$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) and vertical mixing ($K_{zz} \sim
      10^7$--$10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$) are driven by planetary-scale
      macroturbulence (non-linear interactions between planetary-scale Kelvin
      and Rossby waves and the mean flow) and overturning circulation. The
      sub-grid parameterization of small-scale turbulent dissipation
      (hyperdiffusion, Smagorinsky closures, or Shapiro filters) remains a
      known source of numerical sensitivity in GCM wind speeds and jet
      stability (Heng et al.\ 2011; Polichtchouk et al.\ 2014; Skinner \& Cho
      2021).
\item \emph{Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in ultra-hot atmospheres.}
      For planets with $T \gtrsim 1500$\,K, thermal ionization of alkali
      metals (Na, K) couples the gas to the planetary magnetic field. 3D MHD
      simulations (e.g., Perna et al.\ 2010, 2012; Rauscher \& Menou 2013;
      Rogers \& Showman 2014; Rogers \& Komacek 2014; Beltz et al.\ 2022)
      show that magnetic winding of the field by supersonic zonal winds
      generates Lorentz forces (magnetic drag) that brake the flow, producing
      3D MHD turbulence, Ohmic dissipation, and time-dependent variability in
      wind patterns and hotspot offsets.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. 3D simulations across other planetary classes.}
3D fluid simulations reach well beyond hot Jupiters, into the other main
classes of exoplanet and substellar object:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Warm Neptunes and sub-Neptunes.}
      For warm Neptunes such as GJ\,436\,b and GJ\,3470\,b, 3D particle-mesh
      and hydrodynamic escape simulations (Bourrier et al.\ 2015, 2016;
      Villarreal D'Angelo et al.\ 2021; Carolan et al.\ 2021) give large
      asymmetric cometary hydrogen tails spanning tens of planetary radii,
      which account for the $56\%$ Ly$\alpha$ transit obscuration and the
      He\,10830\,\AA{} absorption through 3D stellar radiation pressure and
      charge-exchange interactions. In metal- and water-rich sub-Neptune
      atmospheres (e.g., GJ\,1214\,b, K2-18\,b), 3D GCMs model cloud/haze
      circulation, photochemical transport, and vertical eddy mixing
      ($K_{zz} \propto P^{-0.4}$; Charnay et al.\ 2015; Innes \&
      Pierrehumbert 2022).
\item \emph{M-dwarf habitable zone terrestrial planets.}
      Tidally locked rocky worlds (such as TRAPPIST-1\,e/f/g, Proxima\,b, and
      LHS\,1140\,b) have been modeled extensively with 3D climate GCMs
      (Turbet et al.\ 2016, 2018; Wolf et al.\ 2017; Sergeev et al.\ 2020,
      2022; the THAI intercomparison, Fauchez et al.\ 2020, 2022). These
      simulations show how thick dayside convective cloud decks raise the
      planetary albedo and prevent runaway greenhouse states, while
      stationary Rossby--Kelvin wave patterns redistribute heat to the
      nightside and keep the atmosphere from freezing out. Complementary
      3D multi-species MHD simulations (Dong et al.\ 2017, 2018; Cohen et
      al.\ 2014) assess $\mathrm{O}^+$ and $\mathrm{H}^+$ ion escape driven
      by extreme stellar coronal mass ejections and flaring winds.
\item \emph{Lava worlds and ultra-short-period rocky planets.}
      For scorched rocky planets with dayside magma oceans (e.g., 55\,Cancri\,e,
      K2-141\,b, CoRoT-7\,b), 3D hydrodynamic models of mineral-vapor
      atmospheres (Castan \& Menou 2011; Nguyen et al.\ 2020; Zilinskas et
      al.\ 2022; Kang et al.\ 2021) show supersonic dayside-to-nightside
      winds ($\mathcal{M} > 1$--$3$, $v \sim 1$--$3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), standing
      hydraulic jumps/shocks, and mineral vapor condensation leading to rock
      rain on the nightside.
\item \emph{Brown dwarfs and directly imaged giant planets.}
      In isolated brown dwarfs and young, directly imaged giants (e.g.,
      HR\,8799\,b/c/d/e, VHS\,1256\,b), 3D convective-radiative GCMs (Tan \&
      Showman 2019, 2021a,b; Showman \& Kaspi 2013; Zhang \& Showman 2014)
      couple rapid planetary rotation (strong Coriolis forces) with deep
      geothermal convection and silicate/iron cloud radiative feedbacks. The
      resulting turbulent vortices, patchy cloud bands, and storm dynamics
      explain the observed rotational photometric variability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where that leaves this comparison}
\label{sec:turb_conclusion}

There is no settled treatment. Turbulence at $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ is a
common convention in 1D isothermal Parker-wind fits, and hence in the
paper being reproduced, while Solar System observations and self-consistent
or 3D models show that isotropic microscale turbulence cannot reach the
required supersonic levels. 3D models instead supply line width
through kinematic flow gradients, zonal jets, and magnetospheric or plasma
interactions. Turning the $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ term on in EXHALE is
therefore right for a like-for-like comparison with C26 and wrong as a
physical default, which is how it is implemented.

absorbing \emph{area}:

\subsection{Density and ionization, the same pair}
\label{sec:rhoion}

Figure~\ref{fig:structure} draws three of the five profiles the comparison
was set up to make; the other two, mass density and hydrogen ionization, are
in Fig.~\ref{fig:structrhoion}, from the same pair of solutions.

The densities are the part that agrees. Outside the base the ratio
EXHALE\,/\,p-winds runs from $0.15$ at $1.2\,R_p$ through unity near
$2\,R_p$ to $2.7$ at $5\,R_p$, and stays inside a factor $2.3$ from
$3\,R_p$ out. At the base cell itself EXHALE sits $176\times$ higher, which
is the two lower boundary conditions and not a profile difference: p-winds
sets its base density from the imposed $\dot M$ through the Parker solution,
EXHALE from its own base state.

The ionization does not agree at all. EXHALE has hydrogen completely ionized
above $1.03\,R_p$, with a neutral hydrogen density of exactly zero in the
output, while p-winds keeps it $91$--$94\%$ neutral throughout,
$x({\rm H\,II})$ rising only from $0.056$ at $1.2\,R_p$ to $0.086$ at
$20\,R_p$. Neither end of that is the other's error, and both need an
explanation.

p-winds' hydrogen is frozen far below its own photoionization equilibrium.
Integrating the same spectrum against the Verner et al.\ (1996) ground-state
cross section gives an unattenuated rate
$\phi = 5.17\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$ (p-winds' internal value for this file
agrees to $0.1\%$), an ionization time of $224$\,d, while the flow time
$r/v$ through the imposed $\dot M = 2.03\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ falls to
$2.6\times10^{5}$\,s at $2\,R_p$ and $2.6\times10^{4}$\,s at $10\,R_p$.
Equilibrium on p-winds' own density and temperature would be
$x({\rm H\,II}) = 0.76$ at $2\,R_p$ and $0.997$ at $5\,R_p$ (dotted in
Fig.~\ref{fig:structrhoion}b); the wind carries the gas out before it gets
there. Shielding is not what holds it down: hydrogen is a trace species here
and the neutral hydrogen column above $1.2\,R_p$ is optically thin at every
energy.

EXHALE's hydrogen goes the other way: it is more ionized than any
photoionization balance on its own structure allows. With the same $\phi$,
its own electron density at $3\,R_p$ ($2.0\times10^{6}$\,cm$^{-3}$, set by
helium) and its own temperature there ($5247$\,K), primary photoionization
alone gives $x({\rm H\,II}) = 0.059$: close to what p-winds carries, and
nowhere near $1$. An exact zero can only come from a hydrogen ionization
source that does not vanish with the neutral hydrogen density, and the code
has one. The Shull \& van Steenberg (1985) secondary channel adds to the
hydrogen photoionization rate a term
$R_{\rm sec,HI}/n({\rm H\,I})$, where $R_{\rm sec,HI}$ is accumulated over
the photoelectrons of every absorber. In a helium-dominated gas that
numerator is helium's. Evaluating it at $3\,R_p$ on this solution's own
radiation field (the He\,\textsc{i} photoelectrons above the code's $40$\,eV
secondary threshold, with $f_{\rm ion,HI} = 0.091$ at the local ionized
fraction $x = 0.246$) gives
$R_{\rm sec,HI} = 4.9\times10^{-2}$\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$ against a hydrogen
recombination rate of $6.6\times10^{-3}$, a factor $7.5$. Complete ionization
follows.

That is an approximation used outside the composition it was made at. The
Shull \& van Steenberg fits are functions of the ionized fraction alone; the
helium abundance of the gas their Monte Carlo followed is fixed inside them
and is not the $\mathrm{He/H} = 10^{3}$ of this run. Their two branches hand
hydrogen $7\times$ helium's share of the secondary ionizations
($f_{\rm ion,HI}/f_{\rm ion,HeI} = 0.3908/0.0554$ at $x \to 0$), where the
abundances say the opposite by three orders of magnitude. It is recorded here
rather than changed, because the reach was measured and is bounded. The
neutral hydrogen density reaches an exact zero only at
$\mathrm{He/H} \ge 10$ over a prescribed metal-free base: above $6.6\,R_p$ at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 10$, above $1.06$ at $100$ and above $1.03$ at $1000$. It
does not happen in any of the solutions this memo quotes science from:
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ well mixed, $2.13$ with diffusion at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$, and every flux-consistent solution out to
$\mathrm{He/H} = 12$, where $x({\rm H\,II})$ at $2\,R_p$ runs $0.060$ to
$0.178$ and no cell is fully ionized. The $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 10^{-3}$
comparison of this section is the only place in the memo that sits inside it,
and the quantity it distorts is hydrogen rather than the metastable helium
the line is made of.

\section{What can be matched: the equivalent width}
\label{sec:ew}

Depth and width trade against each other under any broadening, but the
equivalent width does not: it is unchanged by the instrument profile, by the
turbulence term and by the phase averaging. It is therefore the quantity on
which a self-consistent model can be matched without first settling the
broadening question.

Over the blended red pair (vacuum $10832.6$--$10834.2$\,\AA), the released
spectrum gives $EW = 1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA. EXHALE crosses it at

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044 \quad (0.3851\text{--}0.4201 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 2.5 ,
\end{equation}

that is $4.8\times$ solar, a helium mass fraction of $0.62$. The crossing is
read between two solved compositions, $0.40$ at $EW = 1.1011$ and $0.42$ at
$1.1324$\,\%\,\AA{} against the observed $1.1084 \pm 0.0295$, in the
well-mixed group; the case at $0.40$ is $0.07\sigma$ from the measurement.
Turning the turbulence term on moves the crossing to $0.3985$, $1.3$ per cent
away, so the estimate does not depend on the broadening treatment. The value
sits three orders of magnitude away from the paper's
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$, and the reason is the structural
difference of the previous section: a wind that spreads metastable helium
over most of the stellar disk needs far less helium to absorb as much.

Two assumptions are carried inside that number and are taken up later: the
gas is well mixed, and the spectrum is the GJ\,1132 proxy.
Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} rescans it with element diffusion active at every
decade of the eddy coefficient, and Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699} rescans it on the
brighter proxy. Both move it, the spectrum by much the larger factor.

\section{What cannot be matched: the profile}
\label{sec:width}

Matching the equivalent width is not matching the line. At the crossing
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044$ the profile is $3.3$ times too deep ($4.09\%$
against $1.254\%$) and $3.3$ times too narrow ($0.253$ against
$0.841$\,\AA). The equivalent width states how much absorbing power the model
has. The profile states how that power is spread in velocity, and composition
does not act on the second: every solved composition gives
$0.23$--$0.32$\,\AA{}, rising only to $0.26$--$0.39$\,\AA{} with the
turbulence term.

\subsection{How much is missing, in velocity}

The measured FWHM is $0.841$\,\AA, that is $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
$10833$\,\AA. Three ways to supply it, and what each would require:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Thermal alone.} A Doppler width of $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM in
      helium needs $T = 4.7\times10^{4}$\,K. Helium is fully ionized far
      below that, so there would be no metastable population left to
      absorb. This is excluded.
\item \emph{Bulk outflow.} A line-of-sight velocity spread of order
      $\pm 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across the line-forming region would do it.
      The EXHALE solution matched on equivalent width reaches
      $1.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $20\,R_p$; the paper's p-winds model, with its
      imposed temperature, reaches $4$--$7$. Both are an order of magnitude
      short.
\item \emph{Non-thermal (turbulent) broadening.} Added in quadrature to
      what EXHALE already has, closing the gap takes
      $22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM: $9.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of turbulent
      velocity ($\sigma$), against the $2.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ that the
      sound-speed convention of Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies for
      helium at $5000$\,K. Of the three, this is the one that can be
      measured directly, and Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} measures it.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The model is short by an amount of velocity comparable to the
escape speed, and no choice of composition supplies it.

\subsection{The broadening the data demand, measured}
\label{sec:broadening}

The three routes above can be replaced by one measurement. Take the EXHALE
profile matched on equivalent width as solved, convolve it with a Gaussian of
FWHM $f$ in velocity, and ask which $f$ reproduces the measured line. The
convolution is applied to the excess absorption $(\max T - T)/\max T$ built
from the instrument-convolved synthetic spectrum. Depth, width and the
red/blue ratio are then read off with the same three-Gaussian extractor used
on the data, and the equivalent width by the same integral over the vacuum
window $10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA. Only $f$ is varied, and every number in
Table~\ref{tab:broadening} is measured the way the corresponding observed
number was.

The width is reached at $f = 22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM, that is
$\sigma = 9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. At that value the depth is $1.229\%$ against
the measured $1.24^{+0.22}_{-0.23}$, the blue component $0.161\%$ against
$0.25^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$, and the ratio $7.65$ against $6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$, so
all three fall inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval at the value fixed by
the width alone. None of the three was tuned. Figure~\ref{fig:broadened}
shows the profile and the two crossings.

The number does not depend on which solution is broadened. The neighboring
compositions of the same group, $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.42$ and $0.44$, ask for
$22.21$ and $22.20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The $K_{zz} = 0$ run at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 3.0$ asks for $22.23$ and the $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ run at
$1.50$ for $22.22$. Solar composition asks for $22.40$, although that run
carries a third of the observed absorption ($1.533\%$ red, EW $0.380$) and so
matches the shape while missing the amount. All six are solved cases, and the
spread over them is $0.9$ per cent. The required broadening is therefore set
by the velocity field rather than by composition or by the broadening
treatment already in place, which is what Sect.~\ref{sec:width} inferred. The
equivalent width falls by $3\%$ between $f = 0$ and $f = 22.2$ because the
Gaussian wings carry absorption out of the red-pair integration window, not
because absorbing power is lost.

This is a description, not an explanation. One free parameter applied to a
solved profile reproduces the measured line, and nothing in that says what
produces $22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The candidates are listed below, and the
measurement sharpens them by fixing the number each has to deliver.

One conversion narrows that list. If the missing $22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is
called turbulence, it has to be turbulence in gas whose sound speed the
solution itself fixes. Evaluating $c_s = (\gamma kT/\mu m_H)^{1/2}$ with
$\gamma = 5/3$ on the advection-corrected profiles of the well-mixed
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ solution, taking $\mu$ from the solved
H\,\textsc{i}/H\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{i}/He\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{iii}
and electron densities, gives $c_s = 5.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $2\,R_p$
($T = 4108$\,K, $\mu = 2.00$), $5.6$ at the metastable peak $1.53\,R_p$
($4607$\,K, $2.03$) and $3.2$ at $10\,R_p$ ($1437$\,K, $1.90$); weighted over
the line-forming region by $n(2\,^3S)\,r$, $T = 2499$\,K, $\mu = 1.94$ and
$c_s = 4.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. This is the sound speed of the bulk gas, the
quantity a Mach number is defined against, not the helium thermal speed that
enters the line profile.

Against that, the Gaussian is supersonic on any convention. Read as a
line-of-sight velocity dispersion, $\sigma = 9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.3$. Read in the convention of Lamp\'on et al.\ and p-winds,
where $v_{\rm turb}$ is the quantity added to
$b^2 = 2kT/m + v_{\rm turb}^2$, it is $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma =
13.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\mathcal{M} = 3.3$; as an isotropic three-dimensional
turbulent speed, $\sqrt{3}\,\sigma = 16.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
$\mathcal{M} = 4.0$. The $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ convention of
Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies only $3$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in this gas, about a
third of what the data demand. The literature reviewed there points the other
way: Yan et al.\ (2024) argue $\mathcal{M} = 1$ is already an overestimate at
a few thousand K, and Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009) call the energy requirement
of supersonic turbulence ``insurmountable''. Supplying the shortfall under
the name of turbulence therefore looks physically hard to sustain, and the
measurement points to the candidates of the next section (an ordered velocity
field, three-dimensional flow, or heating the 1D energy equation does not
carry) rather than to a missing thermal width.

\subsection{Is the demand an artifact of the measurement?}
\label{sec:widthaudit}

The number above is a difference between two widths, so it is worth no more
than the two definitions behind it. Before the missing velocity is treated as
physics, the observing and fitting side was audited on its own. In that audit
every number was recomputed from the released spectrum, the released MCMC
chain and the code in this repository.

\medskip
\noindent Nothing on the measurement side supplies the width. Term by term,
in the units the requirement is stated in:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The instrument profile.} The WINERED HIRES-Y kernel the
      Supplement states, and validates against nearby telluric features, is
      $10833\,\text{\AA}/68{,}000 = 0.1593$\,\AA{} $= 4.41$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
      FWHM. The observed line is $5.4$ times wider, so it is resolved, and
      removing the kernel in quadrature moves the intrinsic width from
      $23.9$ to $23.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\item \emph{The triplet splitting.} The $0.0897$\,\AA{} separation of the
      red pair is present on both sides of the comparison. It is $0.7$ per
      cent of the observed blend width but $8$ per cent of the model's,
      because at $\sigma = 0.35$\,\AA{} the pair is a single hump; the
      asymmetry nearly cancels, moving the requirement by less than
      $0.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and in the direction that makes the gap larger,
      since the extractor already credits the model with width it does not
      get from gas motion.
\item \emph{Stellar rotation.} With $P_{\rm rot} = 131$\,d and
      $R_\star = 0.216\,R_\odot$, $v_{\rm eq} = 0.083$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the
      rotational broadening is $0.14$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The
      Rossiter--McLaughlin distortion and the center-to-limb residual scale
      with the same $v\sin i$ and are correspondingly irrelevant. Neither
      is mentioned in the paper or its Supplement, and on this star there
      is nothing to mention.
\item \emph{Planetary rotation.} Tidally locked: $0.032$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
      $R_p$ and $0.097$ at $3\,R_p$. It is already inside the model curves,
      which carry the solid-body spin.
\item \emph{Orbital motion.} $0.93$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across the full transit if
      left uncorrected, but the spectra are shifted into the planetary rest
      frame before averaging, which leaves the $0.022$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the
      ephemeris uncertainty; a $300$\,s exposure smears
      $0.037$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Together these come to at most about $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and most
of that is removed as soon as each side is deconvolved from its own kernel.
Done that way, component width against component width and both intrinsic,
the requirement is $22.00$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
($\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), against the $22.22$ the convolution scan
above returns without deconvolving either side. The audit moves the number by
$0.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, not by a factor. The audit itself was run on the earlier
crossing; what is carried here is the difference it measured,
$-0.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, applied to the present scan.

\medskip
\noindent One defect was on our side, and it is corrected here. The transit
synthesis convolves the He\,10830 line with a Gaussian of FWHM $\lambda/R$,
and until this revision every LHS\,1140\,b run in this memo took $R$ from the
transit tool's built-in default, the CARMENES $8\times10^{4}$, whereas this
observation was made at WINERED's $68{,}000$. Every model curve quoted here
has been re-synthesized at the WINERED value, which the runs now read from
their own setting, and the built-in default is left alone because the other
planets in this repository were observed through other instruments. The
correction widens the modeled blend by about $0.013$\,\AA{} (at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$, $0.244 \rightarrow 0.257$\,\AA{}), lowers the modeled
peak depth by about $5$ per cent, and leaves every equivalent width in this
memo unchanged to $10^{-4}$\,\%\,\AA, because a convolution conserves
absorbing power. So it moves no crossing and no composition, only the widths
and the broadening they demand, the latter by $0.12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The
p-winds curves were already computed at $R = 68{,}000$, so the comparison of
Sect.~\ref{sec:pwbroad} is now through one instrument on both sides.

\medskip
\noindent The paper's own model does not reach the width either, and that
changes what the shortfall means. Reproducing the authors' p-winds setting at
their retrieved medians and measuring it with the same extractor gives FWHM
$0.458$\,\AA{} with turbulence off and $0.575$\,\AA{} with it on, against the
measured $0.841$. The retrieval that produced the published parameters
therefore lands $1.5$ times short of the width printed in the same paper.
Taken with the accounting above, the reading this supports is that the
observation is not described by a one-dimensional steady-state wind of either
code, rather than that the wind solved here carries a defect the comparison
model does not. That is an interpretation. What is measured is that both
models are too narrow and that the measurement side does not account for the
difference.

A second difference runs the same way. The p-winds curve reaches part of its
width through saturation: its red-to-blue amplitude ratio is $2.36$ with
turbulence off and $2.57$ with it on, against the optically thin limit of
$8$, whereas the measurement sits at $6.35$ and the solutions here at
$6.9$--$7.0$. The premise that this line is optically thin, so that column
density cannot be traded against width, holds for the data and for these
solutions but not for the comparison model.

\medskip
\noindent Two remarks on the published width, offered as observations rather
than conclusions. First, the three widths the Supplement's
stellar-variability test reports as FWHM ($0.22176$\,\AA{} for the stellar
helium line, $0.323$ for the in-transit feature and $0.392$ for the
pre-ingress feature) appear to be Gaussian standard deviations rather than
full widths. Fitting the same three-Gaussian model to the same spectrum
before its Gaussian-process treatment, with our extractor, returns
$\sigma = 0.3241$\,\AA{} against their $0.323$, a $0.3$ per cent match, where
the corresponding FWHM is $0.763$\,\AA{} and matches nothing on that list.
The $\sim 9\sigma$ separation the paragraph concludes with is a difference
between two widths and is unaffected; the absolute values are affected.
Second, the asymmetric uncertainties on the headline FWHM appear to be
transposed. The deposited MCMC chain (identified by its shift column
reproducing the published $0.072^{+0.080}_{-0.073}$\,\AA{} exactly) gives
$2.3548\,\sigma = 0.865^{+0.261}_{-0.155}$\,\AA{}
($23.93^{+7.24}_{-4.30}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), where the paper prints
$0.86^{+0.15}_{-0.27}$\,\AA{} and $23.9^{+4.2}_{-7.5}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$: the
magnitudes agree once the two are swapped. Neither remark can be settled from
outside the collaboration, since the printed bars may come from a chain other
than the deposited one, and neither changes the comparison except in one
direction: on the deposited posterior the $1\sigma$ lower bound on the
measured width is $0.709$\,\AA{} rather than $0.59$, so narrow models are
excluded somewhat more firmly than the printed interval suggests.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the audit could not exclude.} The star's own metastable
helium line is broad: taking the Supplement's $0.22176$\,\AA{} as a standard
deviation, it is $0.522$\,\AA{} $= 14.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM as observed and
$13.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with the instrument kernel removed. An imperfectly
divided stellar line would therefore inject a residual much wider than the
modeled planetary line, though still short of the observed feature on its
own. The released data do not include the stellar template, so a mixture of a
narrow planetary line with a displaced stellar residual could not be tested
here and is not excluded. Whether the published $\sigma$ is itself
deconvolved from the instrument profile was also left unsettled; it is worth
$1.7$ per cent in width either way.

\subsection{The same demand, under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$}
\label{sec:diffbroad}

Everything above was solved in the well-mixed limit. When helium and hydrogen
are allowed to separate by diffusion, helium settles out of the base unless
an eddy term lifts it, and how much of it reaches the line-forming region is
set by the eddy coefficient $K_{zz}$, which is not measured for this system.
The range the literature leaves open at the $1\,\mu$bar base of these runs,
the set of cases solved, and the adoption of
$K_{zz} = 1.0\times10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ are recorded in the notes listed
in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsubsection*{The composition, and how much of it $K_{zz}$ carries}

Letting helium and hydrogen separate moves the equivalent-width crossing of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ew} away from $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044$, and by how much depends
on $K_{zz}$. The composition was therefore rescanned at every decade of the
eddy coefficient from $K_{zz} = 0$ to $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, by the
same route in every case: the same stationary solve, the same
advection-corrected profiles, the same transit synthesis, and the same
red-pair equivalent width on the vacuum window
$10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA. Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross} is the result, with
four to seven solved compositions in each group. Every entry is interpolated
between two solved compositions, none extrapolated, but the brackets are not
all equally tight: at $10^{7}$, $10^{8}$ and $10^{9}$ the case below the
crossing is the $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ one that each group carries for the
sensitivity scan, so the reading spans a factor $2.7$--$4.9$ in composition
rather than the $12$--$17$ per cent the other decades give.

The full curve is three regimes rather than one power law
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hehkzz}). Above $10^{7}$ the degeneracy is shallow and very
nearly a power law,
$\mathrm{d}\log(\mathrm{He/H})/\mathrm{d}\log K_{zz} = -0.115$ (a
least-squares fit over $10^{8}$--$10^{11}$; $-0.109$, $-0.114$, $-0.117$ and
$-0.113$ decade by decade), so $\mathrm{He/H} \propto K_{zz}^{-0.115}$. Below
$10^{7}$ the curve rolls over ($-0.057$ across $10^{6}$--$10^{7}$, $-0.026$
across $10^{5}$--$10^{6}$) onto a plateau at $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.10$, the
value the line implies when the eddy term does nothing.

The shape has survived every revision; the level has not. Decade by decade
the exponent was $-0.021$, $-0.071$, $-0.130$, $-0.150$, $-0.151$ and
$-0.133$ on the 2026-08-30 solutions, against $-0.026$, $-0.057$, $-0.109$,
$-0.114$, $-0.117$ and $-0.113$ here: the three regimes and their ordering
are the same, the shallow branch is $24$ per cent shallower and the roll-off
below $10^{7}$ is steeper. What moved is the level, and not by a uniform
factor: the plateau falls to $0.81$ of its archived value while the $10^{11}$
end rises to $1.07$ (last column of Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross}). That is the
shallow branch flattening, not a rescaling.

The plateau sits where it does because the eddy term is added to the
molecular diffusion coefficient. The base coefficient of the $K_{zz} = 0$ run
is $D_{\rm eff} = 9.276\times10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ (measured at the
$r = 1.00029\,R_p$ face, dominant pair He\,\textsc{i}--H\,\textsc{i}), so
$K_{zz} = 10^{6}$ raises it by $108\%$, $10^{5}$ by $11\%$ and $10^{4}$ by
$1.1\%$. The crossings in the table say the same from the other side:
$10^{5}$ moves the inferred reservoir by $1.0$ per cent against the $6.9$ per
cent the measurement error alone allows, and $10^{6}$ is the first decade
that moves it by more than that error. At the other end the eddy term never
reaches the well-mixed limit either: even at $10^{11}$ the inferred
reservoir, $0.8927$, is $2.2$ times the well-mixed $0.4044$.

Over the span the literature leaves open at the base pressure of these runs
the inferred reservoir ratio moves only from $1.16$ to $1.97$, a factor
$1.7$, against a factor $7$ in equivalent width across the same two decades
at fixed composition: the line responds to composition much faster than it
responds to $K_{zz}$, so a large change in the eddy coefficient is bought
back by a small change in the reservoir. The composition inferred from this
line under diffusion is therefore only weakly conditional on $K_{zz}$, as
$K_{zz}^{-0.115}$, and strongly conditional on the assumed spectrum. The
spectrum moves the well-mixed crossing by a factor $7.7$, from $0.4044$ to
$0.0526$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), which is more than twice the entire
$K_{zz}$ lever.

\subsection{Broadening cannot repair a deficit in equivalent width}
\label{sec:pwbroad}

and with the same three-Gaussian extractor and the same integration window
(Table~\ref{tab:pwbroad}). The convolution conserves absorbing power: it
redistributes the excess in velocity and leaves the equivalent width
unchanged, apart from the few percent that the wings carry out of the
integration window. Whether one Gaussian can reproduce depth and width
together is therefore decided before the scan starts, by the equivalent width
the curve already has at $f = 0$.

falls to $0.339\%$, inside $1\sigma$, and the red component drops below the
measurement. The blue side improves, the red side does not, and the total is
fixed.

the two components share the optical depth, and a common convolution acts on
both alike. It stays well below the measured $6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$, though the
observed interval is wide enough that this alone is not a discriminator.

Matching the depth as well would require $24\%$ more absorbing power, and
that is not something a convolution supplies: it is a change in $\dot M$,
temperature or composition. Fitting the broadening as a free parameter
alongside those, which is what the measurement asks for, would presumably
shift the C26 parameter estimates, since the composition is constrained
through the same absorbing power that would have to rise.

The asymmetry with EXHALE is not a statement about one code outperforming the
other. The EXHALE curve entered the scan with its equivalent width already
set to the observed value by the choice of $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.40$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening}), so only the shape remained free, and one
Gaussian settled it. The p-winds curve entered at the composition C26 report,
whose equivalent width falls short, so no Gaussian could settle both. The two
scans test the same physical statement, that broadening redistributes and
does not create, from two different starting points.

\subsection{The adiabatic-cooling population bump, looked for}
\label{sec:bump}

Schulik \& Owen (2025) propose that the missing width is already contained in
the metastable population itself. Writing the steady state of their Eq.~(1)
as
%
\begin{equation}
n_{2^3S}(T_e) = \frac{\alpha(T_e)}{q(T_e) + t_{\rm ion}/n_e}\,n_{\rm He^+},
\end{equation}
%
they note that as adiabatic expansion drops the temperature ``from $\simeq
10^4$\,K to $\simeq 10^3$\,K'', ``a change in slope in the population occurs
as the dominant triplet loss rate $q(T_e)$ drops significantly at lower
temperatures'': the metastable fraction climbs above the curve computed with
the rates frozen at the peak temperature. The rise is ``quenched'' where the
advection timescale overtakes the excitation to $2^1S$, after which
$\dot m_{2^3S} = 4\pi r^2 v\,n_{2^3S}\,m_{\rm He}$ falls through ionization
and the $2^3S \rightarrow 1^1S$ decay. They credit Yan et al.\ (2022) and
Biassoni et al.\ (2024) with first identifying the effect, and their
conclusion states the condition it must satisfy to be observable: ``The
helium triplet population bump must be situated at high velocities to help
explain anomalous line widths.''

EXHALE carries the temperature dependence the mechanism needs, and it enters
in the balance written above. The equation the code solves for the metastable
population is
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
n_e\bigl(n_{\rm He^+}\alpha_{2^3S} + n_{\rm He\,I(1^1S)}\,q_{13}\bigr)
 = \; & n_{2^3S}\bigl[\Gamma_{2^3S} + A_{31} + n_{\rm H\,I}Q_{31} \\
      & + n_e(q_{31a}+q_{31b}+b_{2^3S})\bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
%
with every coefficient a function of $T$: the recombination
$\alpha_{2^3S} = 2.10\times 10^{-13}(T/10^4)^{-0.778}$, the collisional
losses $q_{31a}$ ($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1S$, $0.80$\,eV) and $q_{31b}$
($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1P$, $1.40$\,eV) as Bray (2000) collision strengths
folded with $\exp(-\Delta E/kT)$, the destruction term $Q_{31}$ on atomic
hydrogen as the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025) closed form of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gm25}), collisional ionization of the metastable, and the
radiative decay $A_{31} = 1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$. Between $5000$ and
$1000$\,K the loss $q_{31a}+q_{31b}$ falls by a factor $1.3\times 10^{3}$
while $\alpha_{2^3S}$ rises by a factor $3.5$, so the ingredients of the bump
are present and are not an imposed parameterization.

The result was measured by reconstructing that balance in Python from the
solved profiles, recovering from the balance itself the one term not written
to file, the $2^3S$ photoionization rate $\Gamma_{2^3S}$, and then
recomputing the reference curve with $\alpha$, $q_{13}$, $q_{31a}$, $q_{31b}$
and $Q_{31}$ frozen at the peak temperature. Each transcribed coefficient was
checked against the compiled Fortran over $300$--$10^4$\,K: $Q_{31}$ agrees
to $8\times10^{-16}$ and the other five to $1.2\times10^{-5}$ or better, the
residue being the single-precision literals the Fortran carries.

The bump is there. In every case the metastable fraction
$f_3 = n_{2^3S}/n_{\rm He}$ rises monotonically outward across the whole
cooling leg, from the temperature maximum to a maximum of $f_3$ at
$9$--$15\,R_p$ (a rise of $1.2$--$4.4\times10^{3}$ in the equilibrium
profiles and $20$--$140$ in the advection-corrected ones), and the rise
follows from the temperature dependence in the way Schulik \& Owen describe.
On the GJ\,1132 proxy at the well-mixed $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ case the
equilibrium maximum sits at $5.57\,R_p$ where $T = 1616$\,K, against
$T_{\rm max} = 4789$\,K at the inner peak: the temperature has fallen to
$0.34$ of its peak, which is the regime the mechanism asks for.

The quench is visible too: in the advection-corrected profiles the density
maximum has moved inward to $1.05$--$1.8\,R_p$ and $n_{2^3S}$ then falls
monotonically, although $\dot m_{2^3S}$ itself does not drop past the peak on
the GJ\,1132 cases and falls by $33$ per cent only on the helium-dominated
He/H\,$=1000$ run of the brighter spectrum.

The velocity condition, however, is not met, and the shortfall is large. At
the bump the outflow is moving at $0.28$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy
and $1.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,699 proxy. Weighting by absorbers over the
whole $2^3S$ column, the median $|v_r|$ is at most $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and
$2.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the two spectra, and the largest $|v_r|$ anywhere in
the column is $1.2$ and $3.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. No part of the column on either
spectrum reaches $5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, against the
$\sigma = 9.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of extra broadening the measurement demands
(Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening}). Radial velocity is the generous choice here:
the line-of-sight projection of a radial field over the transit chord is
smaller still. Figure~\ref{fig:bump} draws the three statements together.

So the bump exists in these solutions and contributes nothing measurable to
the width. That is consistent with what Schulik \& Owen themselves report for
the coolest hosts: for their M2 case they find that ``the transit signal
probes the same velocity region for both treatments of thermodynamics, making
the line insensitive to velocity'', and they conclude that K stars, not M
stars, are the favorable testbeds: G-type hosts suppress the bump by
photoionizing the triplet, and M-type hosts have weaker tidal fields and so
rarely drive fast enough outflows. LHS\,1140 is an M4.5 dwarf, cooler than
the M2 they model, and its planet is a temperate super-Earth rather than a
hot giant. The mechanism appears to be present and correctly captured; the
host is the wrong one for it to help.

\subsection{The width in the wider He\,10830 literature}
\label{sec:widthlit}

The comparison so far has been between this model and this measurement.
Against the published population, the measured width is ordinary and it is
the model that is anomalous.

\paragraph{The measured width is typical.}
Few He\,10830 analyses fit the width freely rather than predicting it; where
they do, the answer is $19$--$33$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM.
Table~\ref{tab:widthcensus} collects them. LHS\,1140\,b's
$23.9^{+7.2}_{-4.3}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is an unremarkable member of that set.
Guilluy et al.\ (2024) is the only survey that tabulates a free FWHM for
several planets at once; their GJ\,3470\,b entry should not be taken at face
value, since they write that ``The larger FWHM for GJ\,3470\,b is believed to
be attributed to residuals from OH line contamination''. The entries are
quoted as published (different instruments, different extractors, no common
deconvolution of the instrumental profile), so the clustering is a statement
about published numbers rather than a uniformly reduced sample.

Zhang et al.\ (2023) treat that width as \emph{evidence for}
photoevaporation: ``The width is the most consistent metric and is within 10\%
of 1.1 A for all planets'', because ``For a Parker outflow, the sound, bulk
outflow, and thermal speeds are all close to sqrt(2kB T/mu mH) = 9.4 km s-1
for T = 7000 K and mu = 1.3, leading to an FWHM of 2.355 sigma $\sim$ 22 km
s-1.'' That argument needs a $7000$\,K hydrogen-dominated transonic outflow.
This solution has $5200$\,K at $2\,R_p$, a mean mass of $2.6$--$3.5$\,amu
because of the helium enrichment, and an outflow of $0.024$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
there; the same construction returns the model's $2.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, not
$9.4$.

\paragraph{The turbulence term the field offers.}
There is a single turbulence formula in this literature,
$v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$ with $m$ the mass of a helium atom
(Lamp\'on et al.\ 2020, Eq.~16), adopted unchanged by p-winds as its
Eq.~(21) (Dos Santos et al.\ 2022). It is the adiabatic sound speed of a
monatomic gas, that is, a Mach number of one, and it is a fixed function of
$T$, not a free parameter. Evaluated on this solution it gives $4.2$, $4.2$,
$3.6$, $3.0$ and $2.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $1.2$, $2$, $3$, $5$ and $8\,R_p$,
with a metastable-weighted mean of $3.8$--$4.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ over
$1$--$8\,R_p$. Against the required $\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ that is
short by a factor $2.1$--$3.7$ across the absorbing region and $2.3$--$2.5$
on the absorber-weighted average. The measurement asks for Mach $2$--$4$ and
the term on offer is Mach $1$. It cannot absorb the requirement, whatever one
concludes about its physical justification. Three published analyses report
the same failure in their own systems: Pall\'e et al.\ (2020) on GJ\,3470\,b
(``The inclusion of the broadening of the lines due to turbulence (vturb =
5kT/3m, where m is the mass of a helium atom), in addition to the standard
Doppler broadening, was not enough to explain the measured broadening in the
observations''), Lamp\'on et al.\ (2021) on HD\,189733\,b (``Including a
turbulence broadening component (see Sect.~3.3), the profile broadens, but it
is still narrower than the measured absorption. \ldots\ Hence, our
hydrodynamic model alone is not able to explain the width of the absorption
profile.''), and Czesla et al.\ (2022) on HAT-P-32\,b (``Thermal broadening
alone remains insufficient to explain the observed line width, even if the
turbulence term is included.'').

Where a width is instead fitted freely, it comes back two to three times
larger: Salz et al.\ (2018) obtain $b = 15.1 \pm 0.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (extended
model, $f = 20\%$) and $11.3 \pm 0.5$ (compact) at mid-transit for
HD\,189733\,b, and Czesla et al.\ (2022) obtain $v_{t,a} = 15.2 \pm 3.1$,
$11.2 \pm 3.1$ and $11.7 \pm 1.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for HAT-P-32\,b. Our own
$9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ sits at the low end of that set.

\paragraph{An independently written code on another planet.}
Taylor et al.\ (2026) apply a 1D self-consistent model of the same class
(energy balance, lower-atmosphere coupling, multispecies diffusion, non-LTE
H($n=2$)) to HD\,189733\,b and report the same shortfall: ``several prior
analyses have matched the HD\,189733\,b width by introducing ad hoc
(micro)turbulent or Gaussian broadening terms; here, we show that this step
remains necessary even in our self-consistent framework'', at ``a factor of
$\sim 2$--$3$'' in width. The axes they varied are the axes varied here: ``We
tested variations in the free parameters (heating efficiency, $K_{zz}$,
stellar activity level/XUV input, base composition, and lower-boundary
placement): configurations that increase the width either deepen the core
transit depth too much or violate the H$\alpha$ constraints, and none
simultaneously reproduce both core depth and width.'' Their proposed
mechanism is stated by them as speculative, ``Given that excited He is mostly
produced by recombination of helium ions, this broadening can be driven by
plasma turbulence \ldots\ While these ideas are speculative, we encourage
further exploration of plasma effects and magnetic field interactions'', and
its first clause is the same physics Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale} derives
independently below. Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022), as reported there, reached
the observed width in 3D only by prescribing zonal jets of
$10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ together with reduced hydrogen line cooling; Schulik
\& Owen (2025) offer the adiabatic-cooling population bump as the one route
with no free parameter, which Sect.~\ref{sec:bump} shows is present here and
worth $0.25$--$1.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\paragraph{What follows.} The width shortfall is not specific to EXHALE and
not specific to LHS\,1140\,b. It is an unresolved problem of the 1D
steady-state model class. The evidence for that statement is threefold: the
measured width is a typical member of the published population
(Table~\ref{tab:widthcensus}); the deficit is reproduced at the same factor
of $2$--$3$ by an independently written code, on a different planet, around a
star at the opposite end of the activity range, after testing the same axes
(Taylor et al.\ 2026); and the field's own remedy is a term that is Mach 1 by
construction and that its users report in print to be insufficient. Every
published route to the observed width adds velocity by hand (a sonic
turbulence term, a free Gaussian, or prescribed jets), and none of them
follows from the 1D energy equation.

One citation needs a note here, as an observation rather than a correction to
anyone's result. Lamp\'on et al.\ (2023) and Yan et al.\ (2024) both cite
Salz et al.\ (2018) as a source of the $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$
convention, but ``turbulen'' does not occur anywhere in the published Salz et
al.\ (2018), which fits a free Doppler parameter instead. The convention
itself is stated in Salz et al.\ (2016), Eq.~(5); the 2018 paper appears to
be intended as the source of the measured \emph{width}. As written, the
attribution suggests that a Mach-1 turbulence term reproduced the
HD\,189733\,b width, when in that paper it was a free $15.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
Doppler parameter.

\subsection{The size of the non-thermal candidates}
\label{sec:candscale}

If the missing velocity is not thermal and not in the 1D energy equation, the
next question is which non-thermal mechanism could supply
$\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The estimates below are order-of-magnitude
requirements computed on the reference solution, one of the solutions made
consistent with the elemental fluxes. The full set, including the
stellar-wind standoff, the turbulent and kinetic power budgets, charge
exchange and radiation pressure, is in the working notes on the non-thermal
candidates.

\paragraph{Magnetic.}
Setting the Alfv\'en speed equal to the required dispersion,
$v_A = B/\sqrt{4\pi\rho} = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and reading $\rho$ off the
advection-corrected profile, the field needed in the line-forming region is
$B = 0.423$, $0.118$, $0.043$ and $0.016$\,G at $1.2$, $1.5$, $2$ and
$3\,R_p$. Continued inward as a dipole, $B_s = B\,(r/R_p)^3$, those are
surface fields of $0.73$, $0.40$, $0.34$ and $0.43$\,G: a shallow minimum
near $2\,R_p$, so a dipole of a few tenths of a gauss would put $v_A$ near
the required value over the whole region at once. As a scale comparison that
is unremarkable: it lies between Earth's surface dipole ($\approx 0.3$\,G)
and Jupiter's ($\approx 4$\,G). The difficulty is what such a field would do
rather than whether a planet can have it: the plasma $\beta$ at that strength
is $0.28$--$0.38$ over $1.2$--$3\,R_p$, so the field would be dynamically
dominant and would restructure the outflow rather than decorate it, and this
solution does not model that. The wave energy flux required to sustain
$\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\rho(3\sigma^2)v_A$ through a sphere, is
$12$--$870$ times the solution's total XUV heating of
$1.22\times 10^{20}$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$ over the same radii, which only a
star-planet interaction driven by a wind of at least solar strength at this
orbit could feed. The field strength does not exclude this route. The power
the field would have to be given does constrain it, as does the fact that the
comparison is being made for a $131$\,d rotator with
$L_X/L_{\rm bol} = 6.5\times 10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Where a metastable atom can be made.}
This is the constraint that bears hardest on any mechanism that manufactures
fast helium somewhere else (a stellar-wind interaction region, a
charge-exchange or energetic-neutral population), and brings it into the
beam. He\,$2^3$S is fed by He$^+$ recombination and destroyed by Penning
ionization with atomic hydrogen. The collisional channel from the singlet
ground state supplies a fraction $1\times 10^{-13}$ of the source at
$1.2\,R_p$ and $2\times 10^{-14}$ at $2\,R_p$, falling to $10^{-33}$ by
$5\,R_p$: the metastable population is a recombination product without
qualification. Table~\ref{tab:metatimes} evaluates the two timescales on the
solution, with $\alpha_{2^3S} = 2.10\times 10^{-13}(T/10^4)^{-0.778}$ and the
Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025) destruction rate of Eq.~(\ref{eq:gm25}). Both columns
are recomputed here from the solved profiles. The replacement of the rate
coefficient leaves alone the wind the table stands on: between the last
solution of this case that predates the replacement and the re-solved one,
$T$ agrees to $1.2$ per cent and $t_{\rm rec}$ to $1.8$ at every radius,
because the metastable is a trace population and not an energy-budget term.
The last three columns are not preserved that way and should not be: they
\emph{are} the destruction rate, and it is the quantity
Sect.~\ref{sec:penningrate} replaced, so they move by factors of $1.0$--$1.8$
between the two fits. A reconstruction that reproduced them would be evidence
that the replacement had not been applied.

Two requirements follow, and they are severe. First, recombination is slower
than destruction by $7.5\times10^{3}$--$4.8\times10^{4}$ everywhere, so only
a tiny standing fraction of the helium is ever metastable and the population
is set locally and continuously. Second, and more restrictive, within its own
lifetime an atom moving at the required speed travels $0.05\,R_p$ at
$2\,R_p$ and $0.30\,R_p$ at $3\,R_p$ (Table~\ref{tab:metatimes}, last
column). The inner metastable helium, which carries roughly a third of the
absorption, cannot have been manufactured anywhere but where it is seen. A
stellar-wind or energetic-neutral population would therefore have to be
re-ionized and recombine \emph{inside} the absorbing region: the
recombination time there is $10^{6}$--$10^{7}$\,s against a crossing time of
$5\,R_p$ at $9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of $5.9\times 10^{3}$\,s, short by three to
four orders of magnitude. The candidate is not excluded as a source of
\emph{some} broad outer emission, but it cannot be the source of the bulk of
the line.

\paragraph{Three-dimensional flow.}
With $P = 24.737$\,d and tidal locking,
$\Omega = 2.94\times 10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$ and the corotation speed $\Omega r$
is $0.032$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at the surface, $0.098$ at $3\,R_p$ and $0.94$ at
the $29\,R_p$ domain edge: already measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit} and
two orders below the requirement. A pressure-driven day-night flow is bounded
above by the thermal energy available, that is, by a few times the
$4$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ sound speed in the complete-conversion limit, and
independently by the measured line shift, whose posterior caps any coherent
line-of-sight flow of the whole absorbing column at $4.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. Both
are small here for the same reason: the driver of 3D circulation is the
day-night temperature contrast, and LHS\,1140\,b is a temperate planet at
$T_{\rm eq} = 226$\,K around an M4.5 dwarf, with an irradiation and a
rotation rate one to two orders of magnitude below the hot Jupiters for which
$10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ jets are prescribed. Taylor et al.\ (2026) reach the
same conclusion for HD\,189733\,b, where the case is far more favorable:
``typical GCM wind speeds (a few kilometers per second) are unlikely to
supply the additional $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ required to match the observed
width.''

\paragraph{What this does not settle.} These are requirement estimates
evaluated on \emph{this} solution's density, temperature and ionization
structure. They say what each mechanism would have to be given in order to
produce $9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ here, and on that basis none of them is
comfortable: the magnetic route needs a power it has no obvious source for
around this star, the fast-neutral route cannot make metastables where the
absorption is, and the 3D route is short by two orders of magnitude. They do
not identify which mechanism is in fact responsible, and they do not exclude
the possibility that the absorbing gas is not this solution's gas at all: the
measured pre-ingress signal, at least as broad as the in-transit one at a
phase when the planet occults nothing, is direct evidence that some of the
signal comes from a structure no 1D radial model contains. Which mechanism
supplies the width is not determined by this work, and that is also the state
of the literature.

\subsection{What would have to change}

The measured line wants gas that is fast enough to be broad but not so
extended that it absorbs too much. EXHALE at this XUV flux delivers neither.
The candidates, in the order they should be tested:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{More energy}: tested (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), and it does
      not do it. The GJ\,699 proxy carries five times the XUV of the
      GJ\,1132 proxy used here, and the paper itself runs both. Solving the
      scan again on it raises $\dot M$ sevenfold and moves the composition
      estimate by an order of magnitude, but leaves the required extra
      broadening at $21.95$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ against $22.18$: the width gap is
      not an energy-input problem.
\item \emph{A different helium distribution}: tested
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}), and it does not do it either. Letting
      helium settle by diffusion and lifting it back with an eddy term
      redistributes the absorber by orders of magnitude and moves the
      inferred composition with it, but the required broadening stays at
      $22.19$\,km\,s$^{-1}$: it changes how much helium is there, not how
      fast it moves.
\item \emph{Heating the code does not carry.} Taylor et al.'s trapped-ion
      turbulence, or any magnetically mediated dissipation, is outside a
      1D hydrodynamic energy equation by construction. Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale}
      sizes it: the field needed to make $v_A = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is a
      few tenths of a gauss, which is ordinary, but the wave power that field
      must carry is $12$--$870$ times the XUV heating.
\item \emph{Geometry.} A line-of-sight velocity field from day-night or
      zonal flow is a 3D effect; Rumenskikh et al.\ reach the observed width
      with $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ jets, which is the right order. For this
      planet, though, rotation gives $0.03$--$0.10$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the
      measured line shift caps any coherent line-of-sight flow at
      $4.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale}).
\item \emph{Adiabatic-cooling population bump.} Looked for
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}): the bump is present in these solutions, but
      it sits at $0.28$--$1.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, so it does not supply width.
\end{enumerate}

The paper's composition constraint should be read in the same light: it
inherits the isothermal assumption. Impose a $6100$\,K structure and only
helium-rich gas absorbs enough; solve the energy balance on this spectrum
and, in the well-mixed limit, the same absorption is reached near
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.42$, and near $0.048$ on the other proxy spectrum, or near
$1.6$ once helium is allowed to settle against the adopted eddy coefficient.
No answer on that list is secure while the width is unexplained, because the
width is what tells us whether the line-forming gas is the gas either model
thinks it is.

\section{The best each model can do}

Figure~\ref{fig:best} puts only the two best models on the measurement: the
paper's p-winds model at its own best fit (the MCMC medians), and the EXHALE

neither reaches the observed width. p-winds is the broader of the two,

\section{The other proxy: five times the XUV}
\label{sec:gj699}

The first item on the list above, more energy, can be tested without adding
any physics, because the paper itself runs two proxy spectra. Both stand in
for LHS\,1140's unobserved XUV: GJ\,1132 (used everywhere above) and
GJ\,699, scaled to the orbit by the catalog X-ray flux ratios
$A_{1132} = 0.59$ and $A_{699} = 0.056$ following the authors' script, the
same convention adopted in Sect.~\ref{sec:sed}. Integrated over
$10$--$1300$\,\AA{} at the orbit of the planet, the GJ\,699 file carries
$169.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ against $33.5$ for the GJ\,1132 one: a
factor $5.1$ in XUV input.

On the EXHALE side only the spectrum changes. Each GJ\,699 case is the
matching GJ\,1132 input with the spectrum file replaced. The luminosities
stated in the input are irrelevant, because the code overwrites them with the
integral of the spectrum it reads. Six reservoirs were solved, all of them
converged.

Six compositions were solved on this spectrum, three of them

\subsection{The composition estimate moves by an order of magnitude}

Interpolating the equivalent width in $\log$--$\log$ across the solved
compositions and solving for the measured value gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 0.0526 \quad (0.0510\text{--}0.0543 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 19.0 ,
\end{equation}
read between the solved cases at $0.050$ ($EW = 1.0609$) and $0.083$
($1.6271$\,\%\,\AA), the first of which is $1.6\sigma$ below the measurement
and the second far above it, so the crossing is measured rather than
extrapolated from the solar case.

The same code gives $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy, an order
of magnitude away, and the crossing passes the solar value on the way: the
brighter spectrum wants a helium-\emph{poor} atmosphere, $0.63\times$ solar,
where the fainter one wanted a helium-rich one, $4.8\times$ solar. The
measurement cannot arbitrate, because the two proxies are two guesses at the
same unobserved spectrum and both reproduce the observed equivalent width
once composition is free. The conclusion is therefore about the inference
rather than about the planet: what the helium line constrains is the product
of composition and XUV input, and any composition quoted from it (the paper's
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much as the values here) is
conditional on the assumed spectrum. On the present evidence the composition
inference is limited by the assumed spectrum.

\subsection{The width does not move}

On this spectrum too the width does not move. Across the whole GJ\,699 set
the red-pair FWHM is $0.282$--$0.359$\,\AA{} against the measured
$0.841$\,\AA. Measuring the deficit the way Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} does,
by convolving with a Gaussian and asking which width reproduces the
observation, the solution at the crossing needs $21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of
added velocity spread, against $22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the GJ\,1132
solution matched on equivalent width and the same $22.22$ for the diffusive
one of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}. Raising the XUV input by $5.1$, $\dot M$ by
$5$ and the outflow velocity at $20\,R_p$ by $3$ changes the required
broadening by $1$ per cent.

The reason is visible in the table: the outflow velocity that grows is radial
and small compared with the missing spread, while the line-forming region
stays cold. More energy is therefore tested and rejected as an explanation of
the width. It moves $\dot M$ and the composition estimate, but not the
profile. The remaining candidates, heating outside a 1D hydrodynamic energy
equation, 3D velocity fields, and an adiabatic-cooling metastable bump, are
unaffected by this test, and the last of them has since been looked for on
both spectra (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) and does not help either.

\section{Closing the loop: the composition as an output}
\label{sec:closure}

Every number above took the helium abundance as an input. The scans of
Sects.~\ref{sec:scan} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} chose it, solved the wind, and
asked what the line did. The reservoir at the base was a parameter set by
hand, and the lower atmosphere below the $1\,\mu$bar matching level was a
handful of single numbers. This section removes that freedom on one axis: the
lower atmosphere is solved as a photochemical column, handed over as a
profile, and the elemental fluxes are iterated until they are continuous
across the matching level. The composition at the match is then a solution of
the coupled problem rather than a number chosen for it.

The three runs of this section have since been re-run end to end (chemistry,
wind, measurement of the elemental fluxes and damped update) on Photochem
$0.9.0$, with the interface to it re-pinned, and on the current build, which
is what Table~\ref{tab:closure} reports. What they establish is that the
iteration is single-valued and that at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ it hands back the
reservoir it was given, which is a statement about the iteration rather than
about the line. The sequence of solutions of
Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}, which is what the composition is read from,
was re-solved on both. The design, its tests and the runs are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{The setup}

The column below the match is solved with Photochem (Wogan 2025) on the
Zahnle reaction set restricted to H/He/N/O/C. The temperature is not
prescribed: the accompanying climate solver is run with the tropospheric
temperature solved for rather than imposed, so the stratospheric temperature
is the skin temperature of the solution. The background gas is chosen by
abundance rather than by name, and on this atmosphere that is helium rather
than H$_2$. The climate solve is taken from a deep boundary at $20$\,bar, the
shallowest one at which the bottom of the photochemical model fits inside the
climate column. Chemistry and climate are run on the same set of elemental
abundances, He/H starting at $2.09$ (the $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossing of
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross}) with Lodders C, N and O, on the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum at the planet's orbit and at the adopted constant
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$.

The result is handed to EXHALE as a profile of $102$ levels from $16.73$\,bar
to $1.10\times10^{-8}$\,bar, carrying $p$, $r$, $T$, $n$, $\rho$, $K_{zz}$,
the molecular fractions and the elemental reservoirs He/H, C/H, N/H and O/H,
matched to the wind at $1\,\mu$bar. Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem} reads that
solution column by column, and says which of the columns the wind is actually
built from. The loop is then closed in the other direction. The elemental
escape fluxes EXHALE computes are imposed back on the photochemical model at
its top, $\phi_{\rm El} = \Phi_{\rm El}/(m_{\rm El}\,4\pi r_{\rm top}^{2})$,
helium on He and hydrogen on H$_2$ at $\phi_{\rm H}/2$, which are the
dominant carriers; the share carried by atomic hydrogen at that level is
$10^{-4}$. The pair is iterated by a damped fixed-point step
($\omega = 0.5$) on the mismatch
$|F_{\rm measured} - \Phi_{\rm trial}|/|F_{\rm measured}|$ to a tolerance of
$5\%$.

Two things about that measurement decide whether it means anything. The
elemental flux is read in the \emph{steady} window $r \ge r_{\rm esc}$ rather
than in the interval the two models share: at a steady state
$4\pi r^2(\rho X v + J)$ does not depend on radius, so a flux measured there
is the flux through the matching level. The overlap window cannot carry it.
From the match to the top of the profile the column spans $1.96$ decades of
pressure in $0.0098\,R_p$, entirely inside the region where the wind's mass
flux is not yet its own, and the flux across it has a radial spread of $13$
in $F_{\rm H}$ and $35$ in $F_{\rm He}$. In the steady window the same
solution holds $191$ faces at spreads of $0.44\%$ and $0.46\%$.

That flatness depends on how far the wind was converged, and we measured it:
at a residual tolerance of $10^{-3}$ the same solution spreads by $7.4\%$,
which would fail the validity test of the iteration itself, and continuing it
to $10^{-4}$ brings it to $0.50\%$ and moves $\log_{10}\dot M$ from $7.47$ to
$7.50$. The iteration runs the wind at $10^{-4}$.

\medskip
\noindent The cross-check with a second chemistry code is not verified. The
same handoff was set up with VULCAN (Tsai et al.\ 2017) on the production
profile's own $T(p)$ and $K_{zz}$, and VULCAN did not converge on this cold
helium-rich column: its convergence measure rose from $2.65$ to $2.75$
against a criterion of $0.01$ with the timestep pinned, the limiting cell
being H$_2$O at the cold trap, and the run exited on its step limit. No
VULCAN composition at the match was produced and no wind was run on one, so
the spread between the two chemistry codes is not measured here.

\subsection{The column the chemistry returns}
\label{sec:photochem}

The settings above produce one file, and everything EXHALE does on the
profile branch rests on it, so the solution itself is read here column by
column. Figs.~\ref{fig:pccol} and~\ref{fig:pccol2} draw every column of the
file except the two elemental-flux columns, which are constants carrying the
trial fluxes the header states. The file measured here is the one-way handoff
at iteration $0$; the flux-consistent solutions of
Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder} carry their own copies and are compared at the
end of this section.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Grid and thermal structure.} $102$ levels from $16.73$\,bar
to $1.098\times10^{-8}$\,bar: the photochemical model's own domain, sitting
inside the $20$\,bar column the climate step was taken from. Temperature runs
from $520.3$\,K at the deep boundary to a floor of $185.05$\,K, which it
reaches at $0.94$\,bar and holds at every level above. The stratosphere is
isothermal at the skin temperature, and the tropopause the climate solve
reports, $1.031$\,bar, is one level below the first isothermal level.
$K_{zz}$ is $10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at all $102$ levels, so the eddy
coefficient the wind runs at and the one the column was mixed at are the same
constant. The column spans $553.6$\,km in radius, $0.15608$ to
$0.16400\,R_{\rm J}$, with the $1\,\mu$bar match at
$0.16240\,R_{\rm J} = 1.782\,R_\oplus$, which fixes the base radius of the
wind. Density falls from $n = 2.33\times10^{20}$\,cm$^{-3}$
($\rho = 1.40\times10^{-3}$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$) to
$4.30\times10^{11}$\,cm$^{-3}$ ($2.58\times10^{-12}$).

\medskip
\noindent The column is chemically almost frozen. Between the deep boundary
and the match, $17$ decades of pressure, $q_{\rm H_2}$ moves from $0.19258$
to $0.19264$, $q_{\rm CH_4}$ from $1.072\times10^{-4}$ to
$1.025\times10^{-4}$ and $q_{\rm NH_3}$ from $3.158\times10^{-5}$ to
$2.919\times10^{-5}$. What changes at all is water, which condenses, and a
set of trace photochemical products at the very top. In an eddy-mixed column
at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ that is what one expects, and Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}
makes the same point from the other side: the eddy term mixes the column
uniformly all the way to the match.

\medskip
\noindent Hydrogen stays molecular to the top. $q_{\rm H}$ rises from
$5.9\times10^{-21}$ at the deep boundary to $6.5\times10^{-6}$ at the match
and $4.0\times10^{-5}$ at the model top, four to five decades below
$q_{\rm H_2}$ throughout: there is no level in this column at which atomic
hydrogen overtakes H$_2$. The base EXHALE is handed is an H$_2$ base in that
sense. It does not enter the wind that way in these runs: EXHALE's molecular
chemistry is off, and $q_{\rm H_2}$ is read but inert (see below).

\medskip
\medskip
\noindent The composition is cold and reducing. At $520$\,K and $17$\,bar CO
is already negligible, $q_{\rm CO} = 8.1\times10^{-18}$, and CO$_2$ smaller
still. Carbon is CH$_4$ and nitrogen is NH$_3$, the opposite of the
CO/N$_2$-dominated deep chemistry of a hot Jupiter. Counting carriers at the
match against the hydrogen-nucleus fraction of the gas,
$2q_{\rm H_2} + q_{\rm H} = 0.38532$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2ex
\item carbon: CH$_4$ gives $2.661\times10^{-4}$ per H, $99.7\%$ of the
      carbon the profile's molecular columns hold, with C$_2$H$_2$ and HCN
      supplying the rest;
\item nitrogen: NH$_3$ gives $7.576\times10^{-5}$ per H, $96.3\%$, with
      N$_2$ at $3.4\%$;
\item oxygen: the water that survived the cold trap gives
      $4.831\times10^{-7}$ per H, $99.9\%$.
\end{itemize}
Those sums come to $96.0$, $96.0$ and $97.6$ per cent of the elemental
columns ${\rm C/H} = 2.778\times10^{-4}$, ${\rm N/H} = 8.187\times10^{-5}$
and ${\rm O/H} = 4.957\times10^{-7}$. The remainder sits in species the
profile does not carry, so this accounting is a decomposition of the
elemental ratios rather than an independent measurement of them.

This is the direct link to Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}. The C and N that cool
the wind and take its line down by $2.6\times$ are held in the column as
CH$_4$ and NH$_3$, both of which the solution keeps in the gas phase across
the trap, so they cross the tropopause intact and arrive at the match at
essentially their deep values. Oxygen, whose carrier is water, does not.

\medskip
\noindent Photochemistry acts only in the top decades. From the tropopause to
the match, HCN goes
$1.3\times10^{-13} \rightarrow 6.5\times10^{-8}$, N$_2$
$9.2\times10^{-9} \rightarrow 5.2\times10^{-7}$, C$_2$H$_2$
$2.5\times10^{-13} \rightarrow 1.1\times10^{-7}$, CO
$5.2\times10^{-16} \rightarrow 1.8\times10^{-10}$ and OH
$4.5\times10^{-27} \rightarrow 7.1\times10^{-11}$: several decades each, and
all of them still trace. They do not move the elemental budget: the
accounting above puts every one of them below $4\%$ of its element.

\medskip
\noindent What EXHALE consumes from this file was read in the code rather
than inferred. The reader requires the pressure, the radius, the temperature,
the total number density, the mass density, the eddy coefficient, the H$_2$
and atomic hydrogen fractions and the helium-to-hydrogen ratio to be present.
It then takes, \emph{at the matching level only}, the temperature as the base
temperature, the radius as the base radius, the H$_2$ fraction as the base
H$_2$ fraction, the helium-to-hydrogen ratio as the base reservoir, and the
elemental ratios of C, N, O and of any other element the file carries as the
metal abundances, through the same door a prescribed metal list uses. The
matching pressure comes from the header. The eddy coefficient is the only
quantity read over the whole profile, interpolated onto the radial grid
through the file's own radius column; a single value of $K_{zz}$ given in the
input is then announced as inert.

Everything else goes unread. The number density and the mass density are
required by the format but never used, since the base density follows from
the input pressure and the base particle count from the base temperature, the
helium-to-hydrogen ratio and the elemental ratios; the atomic hydrogen
fraction is required and never used; the two elemental-flux columns are not
read by the Fortran at all, since the iteration reads them on the Python
side; and none of the molecular fractions beyond H$_2$ is read. The H$_2$
fraction itself acts only when a molecular base is switched on, in which case
it enters the equation of state at the base. In the runs of this memo that
option is off, and the configuration report the code prints at startup
confirms it: the mass per hydrogen nucleus, $9.37393$, and the particles per
hydrogen nucleus at the boundary, $1.000116$, are
$1 + 4\,{\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El}A_{\rm El}$ and
$(1 + {\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El})/(1 + {\rm He/H})$ with no H$_2$ term. So
of the $23$ columns, six numbers at one level plus one full column are what
the wind is actually built from.

\medskip
\noindent The flux-consistent solutions each carry their own solved column,
and raising the reservoir changes that column in three measurable ways
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pccol2}d--f). The deep boundary cools,
$520.3 \rightarrow 439.4 \rightarrow 387.9$\,K at $16.73$\,bar for
${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, $5.01$ and $10.31$, while the stratosphere stays at
$185$\,K. H$_2$ is diluted by number,
$q_{\rm H_2} = 0.1926 \rightarrow 0.0907 \rightarrow 0.0462$ at the match,
with C/H and N/H unchanged to four digits because they are stated per
hydrogen nucleus. The cold trap loosens: O/H at the match goes
$4.96\times10^{-7} \rightarrow 6.43\times10^{-7} \rightarrow
9.09\times10^{-7}$, the trend the O/H column of
Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} continues over $8.45$--$11.0$. Oxygen is the
only element the reservoir moves here, and it is the one whose carrier
condenses.

\subsection{The pressure form of $K_{zz}$}
\label{sec:kzzform}

Every number in this subsection is the 2026-08-30 measurement. The mechanism
it establishes does not depend on the physics that has changed since
(Sect.~\ref{sec:whatchanged}), but its magnitudes have not been re-measured
on the current code.

The eddy coefficient of the last section is a stated number rather than a
solved one, and it is stated in a particular way: constant,
$1.0\times10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at every one of the $102$ levels. The
form is an assumption laid on top of the value. What the \emph{value} carries
is already measured: the equivalent width moves by a factor $\simeq 9$
between $K_{zz} = 10^{8}$ and $10^{10}$, so the open question is whether the
\emph{shape} carries anything as well. We tested that against the constant,
and the shape does not carry the conclusions of this memo. The reason is
short: of the profile, EXHALE consumes elemental ratios and thermodynamics,
and both are the same at the match to better than $1.2$ per cent under either
form.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What was tested.} The saturated gravity-wave form,
%
\begin{equation}
  K_{zz}(p) = 1.0\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{cm^2\,s^{-1}}
  \left(\frac{p}{1.1\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{bar}}\right)^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:kzzpow}
\end{equation}
%
anchored at the top of the profile and not at $1$\,bar. The anchor is the
point of the test rather than a detail of it: EXHALE gives every cell above
the shallowest tabulated level that level's value
(Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem}), so anchoring at the top leaves the number the
wind inherits above the file unchanged and confines the change to the
interval the file describes. Equation~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) then implies
$1.05\times10^{8}$ at the $1\,\mu$bar match, $1.05\times10^{5}$ at $1$\,bar
and $2.56\times10^{4}$ at the $16.73$\,bar deep boundary: a factor $9.5$
below the constant at the match, and four decades below it at the bottom.

Two cases separate the chemistry from the wind, because the profile hands
over both:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2ex
\item \emph{A, chemistry only.} The column is solved on
      Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) and then its eddy coefficient alone is written
      back to the constant $10^{9}$ before the handoff, so the wind sees the
      eddy coefficient it saw before and only the composition it is handed
      changes.
\item \emph{B, chemistry and wind.} The profile as produced; the wind reads
      $K_{zz}(p)$ as well, but only where the file speaks. The column
      reaches $1.0091\,R_{\rm p}$ above the match, and every cell above that
      takes the top level's value, so the change on the wind side is a shell
      nine thousandths of a radius deep in which the coefficient is $9.5$
      times lower at the base and equal at the top. The homopause under the
      constant sits at $1.056\,R_{\rm p}$, outside that shell. Case B is
      therefore a bounded test of the wind side rather than a test of
      lowering $K_{zz}$ where helium settles; the unbounded version of that
      is the scan over magnitude, which is already measured.
\end{itemize}
%
The reference is the constant-$K_{zz}$ profile re-solved from the same
starting state and with the same build, so the comparison is not against a
number recorded on an earlier occasion; the recorded number is carried in the
tables anyway, because the difference between it and the reference is the
size of the spread the procedure itself carries from one starting state to
another. Two reservoirs of the earlier flux-consistent sequence were run, one
at each end of it: $\mathrm{He/H} = 11.11$ and $2.09$, the latter on the
branch started from a high trial flux. The test has since been repeated run
by run on the current build, and the reasoning it was first reported under is
confirmed: the coefficient is common to both sides of every entry, so a ratio
between two chemistries at fixed everything else does not turn on it, and
every ratio the test reports moves by less than the spread the procedure
itself carries. The table below is the repeated version.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The crossing.} Converted with the local slope
$\mathrm{d}\ln EW/\mathrm{d}\ln(\mathrm{He/H}) = 0.382$ measured over the
$11.11$--$12.01$ interval of the sequence these two belong to, the four
equivalent widths move that sequence's crossing from $11.73$ to $11.61$ and
$11.63$ at $\mathrm{He/H} = 11.11$ and to $11.48$ and $11.62$ at $2.09$:
$-0.25$ to $-0.10$ in He/H, under a tenth of the $10.92$--$12.58$ interval
the measurement's own $1\sigma$ band allowed there. All four now move it the
same way, downward. In relative terms that is $-2.1$ to $-0.8$ per cent, and
it is the relative figure that carries over to the present crossing. It is
also no larger than the spread of the procedure itself: re-solving the
\emph{same} profile from a different starting state moves the equivalent
width by $1.08$ per cent at $\mathrm{He/H} = 11.11$ and $0.38$ per cent at
$2.09$, against the $0.32$--$0.83$ per cent the eddy form produces. The
signal is buried in the spread of the procedure that would have to measure
it, by a narrower margin than before: $1.08$ against $0.83$ per cent where
the previous branching gave $1.29$ against $0.79$.

\medskip
\noindent One objection to the test itself has to be stated. The deep half of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) is not the right law. The $P^{-1/2}$ growth is the
saturated gravity-wave scaling of a \emph{stratified} region (Lindzen 1981),
and the climate solve puts this planet's tropopause at $2.636$\,bar: below it
the column is convective, where the mixing-length form of Gierasch \& Conrath
applies and $K_{zz}$ should flatten or grow with depth, not collapse. Nor is
the amplitude defensible there. At $1$\,bar Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) gives
$1.05\times10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, which is at or below the floor of the
modeling literature, one and a half to two and a half decades under the
$3\times10^{6}$--$3\times10^{7}$ Charnay et al.\ (2015) use for GJ\,1214\,b,
$3.7$ decades under the $5\times10^{8}$ of Parmentier et al.\ (2013) for
HD\,209458\,b, and equal to the $10^{5}$ floor Ackerman \& Marley (2001)
impose by hand. The physically correct construction is two-branch, a wave law
above the radiative-convective boundary joined to a mixing-length law below
it; this test ran one branch all the way down to $16.73$\,bar. Read as
interpretation, that is very likely why the carriers moved as far as they
did, since the $0.1$--$10$\,bar interval the single branch over-quenches is
the one that fixes where CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ quench. The objection does not
reach the verdict, though: a form this wrong in the deep column still failed
to move an elemental ratio at the match by more than $1.2$ per cent.

At the other end the same equation is on firmer ground than the adopted
constant. Its value at the match, $1.05\times10^{8}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, falls
inside the $2.8\times10^{7}$--$1.4\times10^{8}$ that the Arfaux \& Lavvas
homopause scaling gives when it is evaluated with \emph{this} planet's
gravity and temperature: the one estimate in the survey derived from
LHS\,1140\,b itself, and the direction in which our own notes on the eddy
coefficient already record the adopted $10^{9}$ as having no planet-specific
basis.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Verdict.} The constant assumption was carrying a great deal
of the lower atmosphere's carrier chemistry, up to four decades of it, and
none of this memo's conclusions. What EXHALE consumes from the profile is the
elemental ratios and the thermodynamics, and those agree at the match to
$1.2$ per cent under a form that differs from the constant by a factor $9.5$
there and four decades at the bottom. The match is still well mixed under
either form: $1.05\times10^{8}$ is $200$ times the molecular coefficient of
$\simeq 4.6\times10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at the base. What the results
carry is therefore the magnitude of $K_{zz}$, the $10^{8}$--$10^{10}$ bracket
across which the equivalent width moves by a factor $9$, rather than its
pressure form. One limit: the verdict is tied to this anchor. Anchoring
Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) at $1\,\mu$bar instead would raise by $9.5$ the value
the wind inherits over its whole domain, which is a change of magnitude and
therefore a different test; it was not run.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Against the published estimate.} Cherubim et al.\ (2026)
quote $f_{\rm H2O} \sim 7$\,ppm from a closed-form cold trap at a $0.1$\,bar

agrees: $p_{\rm sat}(185\,{\rm K})/1\,{\rm bar}$ is $1.3\times10^{-7}$ and
$p_{\rm sat}(194\,{\rm K})/0.1\,{\rm bar}$ about $5\times10^{-6}$, so each

tropopause is put. In our case it is a solved level in a helium-dominated
column ($\mu = 3.6$ at ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$); in theirs it is assumed. We
report this as a result of the climate model, and it was not tuned toward the
published number.

condensate sitting at the base. Gas-phase oxygen falls from
${\rm O/H} = 6.062\times10^{-4}$ at the deep boundary to
$4.957\times10^{-7}$ at the match, a factor $1223$, while C/H
($2.778\times10^{-4}$), N/H ($8.187\times10^{-5}$) and He/H cross the
tropopause unchanged. The carriers of carbon and nitrogen in this column are
CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem}), and this solution keeps both
of them in the gas phase across the trap (Figs.~\ref{fig:closure}c
and~\ref{fig:pccol2}a--c).

\noindent The three runs agree on He/H to $7.8\times10^{-6}$, on
$\log_{10}\dot M$ to better than the $0.005$ the log prints, and on the
fluxes to $0.8\%$ and $3.5\%$, over a factor of ten in where they started, so

used six of its eight allowed steps, so a start further than an order of
magnitude out would need more steps.

\medskip
\noindent One systematic effect on the profile route is larger than anything
the iteration resolves, and it is stated here rather than corrected. Solving
the same run again in four cycles (identical input, identical handoff column,
identical build, each cycle started from the previous one and each converging
at $\|R\| \simeq 10^{-4}$) lands on different fixed points depending on which
converged wind it started from: on the $1.0\times$ run, $EW = 0.519$, $0.532$
and $0.542$\,\%\,\AA{} from three starting states, a spread of $4.6$ per
cent. Four further cycles move each line of descent by at most $0.23$ per
cent and leave the flux spread and the base temperature unmoved, so these are
three fixed points of the procedure rather than three truncations of one. The
diffusion step at fixed wind reports three different composition drifts
($5.9$, $6.2$ and $7.3\times10^{-4}$), which is where the history dependence
lives. Every line number taken from the profile route therefore carries a
systematic effect of that order from its starting state, larger than the
$1.8$ per cent the secondary-ionization correction is worth on these runs.
The comparisons here are internally consistent because each holds the
procedure fixed. The history dependence itself is not diagnosed.

\medskip
\noindent The composition is an output for the first time, and at this eddy
coefficient it is the well-mixed value. He/H at the match comes back at
$2.09235$ against the $2.09$ the reservoir was started from, a change of
$1.1\times10^{-3}$, of which only $2\times10^{-4}$ comes from the escape flux
itself. The reason is measured: at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ eddy
mixing makes the column uniform all the way to $1\,\mu$bar, so a
$1.8\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ hydrogen escape imposed at the model top moves
He/H by $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at that top and by $2\times10^{-4}$ at the match.
At the matching level the composition is set by the eddy coefficient rather
than by the escape flux. Closing the loop therefore does not retire the
conditionality of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}, but moves it onto the same
adopted $K_{zz}$.

Above the match the wind separates the two elements as strongly as before.
The elemental fluxes carry $F_{\rm He}/F_{\rm H} = 0.73$ by mass against the
base reservoir's $8.31$, and He/H falls from $2.09$ at the base to $0.183$ at
$30\,R_p$. Making the base consistent with the fluxes does not weaken that;
it places the fractionation in the wind rather than below the match. One
caveat on the budget, stated rather than corrected: the diffusion term uses
the mass fraction of the H\,$+$\,He mixture and the trace metals ride inside
$\rho$, so the measured $F_{\rm H}$ is hydrogen plus metals and overstates
elemental hydrogen by $4.8\times10^{-4}$, two decades below the tolerance.

\subsection{What the base metals do, and what the closed solution does not
reproduce}
\label{sec:basemetals}

The profile brings something the scalar base never had: the carbon, nitrogen
and oxygen of the photochemical column. Isolating it takes a sequence of
controls in which the scalar-base run is moved one setting at a time onto the
profile's own base state, every step a converged solution. That sequence was
run on the 2026-08-30 code and its magnitudes are superseded. The paragraph
after it gives the current measurement, and Sect.~\ref{sec:cnocooling} says
what moved. Setting the base helium ratio to the profile's $2.0921$ moves
$\log_{10}\dot M$ by less than $0.01$. Setting the base temperature to the
solved $185.05$\,K, from the $226$\,K the scalar runs used, moves it by less
than $0.01$ more, so the base temperature is not the lever. Adding the base
radius moves it to $7.85$; the molecular fraction does not enter, since it is
inert unless a molecular base is switched on, which neither route does.
Adding the profile's C/H, N/H and O/H takes it to $\mathbf{7.49}$ and brings
the red-pair depth down by a factor $\mathbf{2.9}$ at that step
($5.59 \rightarrow 1.93$ per cent), that is $-0.36$\,dex in $\dot M$ and a
correspondingly shallower helium line, from metal-line cooling of a $185$\,K
base at ${\rm C/H} = 2.8\times10^{-4}$.

Over the sequence end to end the same pair is $-0.32$\,dex and a factor
$2.7$. The profile route reproduces that step to $0.00$\,dex in $\dot M$ and
$0.5$ per cent in the line, inside the spread the procedure itself carries,
which is the consistency check the two routes exist to give. The six steps
were re-solved at a residual tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-4}$ on the four
without metals and $4.0\times10^{-4}$ and $5.0\times10^{-4}$ on the two with
them. The scalar step and the scalar column of Table~\ref{tab:thermostat} are
now the same configuration at the same tolerance and give $1.420$ and
$1.424$\,\%\,\AA, $0.3$ per cent apart, which is the spread this procedure
carries from one starting state to another.

\medskip
\noindent Every number in the two paragraphs above is the 2026-08-30
measurement, and the C, N and O cooling that carries it has since been
replaced (Sect.~\ref{sec:cnocooling}). The decomposition of the handoff into
its five terms (reservoir, base temperature, base radius, molecular fraction,
elemental abundances) stands as an account of which term does what; the size
of the last term does not. Re-measured on the current code at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$, adding the column's C, N and O
to the scalar base moves $\log_{10}\dot M$ from $7.87$ to $7.87$ and the
red-pair equivalent width from $1.3749$ to $1.3721$\,\%\,\AA{}, $0.12$ per
cent, where the archived pair moved by $-0.36$\,dex and a factor $2.9$ in
depth. The base metals are no longer the lever that separates the two lower
boundaries. The flux-consistent solution at $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$ now gives
$EW = 1.4765$\,\%\,\AA{} against the metal-free base's $1.3749$ at $2.13$,
above the measurement rather than a factor $2.0$ below it.

Making the base consistent with the elemental fluxes and matching the
equivalent width are still two different constraints, but they no longer pull
in opposite directions. Fixing the reservoir by the line, as
Sects.~\ref{sec:ew} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} do, was done above a base
carrying no metals; with the photochemical column beneath it the same
reservoir now absorbs \emph{more}, so the composition that satisfies both
conditions at once is smaller than the metal-free crossing, and smaller than
every reservoir the flux-consistent sequence was run at
(Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}). The lower boundary is still an axis of its
own, of the same standing as the assumed spectrum, the assumed mixing and
composition itself. What changed is its sign and its size.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The cost to the wind.} With no metals, radiative cooling is
$4.1$--$6.9$ per cent of the heating over $1.2$--$2\,R_p$ and the wind is
limited by expansion rather than by radiation. With the column's C, N and O
beneath it, cooling is $5.1$--$7.9$ per cent, of which the metal lines are
$23$ per cent at $1.2\,R_p$ and $3$ per cent at $2\,R_p$, and $T_{\max}$
falls from $5730$ to $5415$\,K. Both the metastable peak and the metastable
column rise slightly ($34.6 \rightarrow 35.8$\,cm$^{-3}$ and
$68.7 \rightarrow 70.2$\,cm$^{-3}R_p$ over $1$--$10\,R_p$), and the line
rises with them. The 2026-08-30 draft stated here that the metals take the
outer column, because the cooler wind has the smaller scale height and does
not carry the metastable population out to where it adds absorbing area. That
is a real mechanism at a cooling rate that large, but it is not what these
solutions do at the cooling rate the density-resolved coefficient gives.

\medskip
\noindent Raising the reservoir reheats the wind, by two terms that are both
measured. From $2.09$ to $10.01$ on the same base, $T_{\max}$ goes
$5415 \rightarrow 7682$\,K. Two things change together. First, there are
fewer particles to share the deposited energy. The mean mass per particle at
$2\,R_p$ rises from $2.15$ to $3.17\,m_{\rm H}$, a factor $1.47$; the heating
rate at $2\,R_p$ divided by the particle density rises by $1.50$; and
$T(2\,R_p)$ rises by $1.57$. Here $n_{\rm tot}$ counts one particle per
species with electrons excluded, as elsewhere in this section. The three
factors agree to within $6$ per cent, which is a consistency check on that
reading rather than a proof of it, since the cooling term is not held fixed
between the two runs. Second, the coolant is diluted along with the hydrogen
it is referred to. The profile states C, N and O per hydrogen nucleus, and
the code carries them that way, so when the neutral hydrogen density at
$2\,R_p$ falls by $3.7$ the carbon density falls with it by the same factor,
the two profiles carrying ${\rm C/H}$ within $0.1$ per cent of each other.
The volumetric metal cooling nonetheless \emph{rises},
$2.36 \rightarrow 4.65\times10^{-11}$\,erg\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$ at
$1.5\,R_p$, because the cooling function is steeply temperature dependent.
What the dilution does is let the gas settle at the higher temperature at
which that happens.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The metastable balance.} The 2\,$^3S$ population in these
solutions is fed almost entirely by recombination and drained by Penning
ionization on neutral hydrogen and by electron collisional de-excitation.
Reconstructing the balance EXHALE solves apportions the budget: the source is
$n_e n_{\rm He\,\textsc{ii}}\alpha_{2^3S}$ plus collisional excitation from
the singlet ground state, against radiative decay $A_{31}$,
photoionization, destruction on H\,\textsc{i}, electron collisional
de-excitation to $2\,^1S$ and $2\,^1P$, and electron collisional ionization.
At the metastable peak of the advection-corrected profiles, collisional
excitation supplies a negligible part of the source and radiative decay plus
collisional ionization take $0.02$--$0.03$ per cent of the sink. The rest of
the loss is Penning ionization and electron de-excitation: $72\%$ and $28\%$
at ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, $54\%$ and $46\%$ at $10.01$. Between the two the
source rate rises by $2.76$ (more He\,\textsc{ii} and more electrons in the
hotter gas) while the metastable lifetime is essentially unchanged, $1.09$
against $0.98$\,s. The neutral hydrogen the Penning term feeds on is thinned
by only $1.20$ at the peak, and the faster electron de-excitation of the
hotter gas takes back what that gains. The product, $2.48$, is the peak ratio
$35.8 \rightarrow 88.8$\,cm$^{-3}$. There is no charge-exchange term on the
metastable in this code, since the He/H charge exchange acts on ground-state
He and He$^+$, so it is not part of this balance.

\medskip
\noindent Read as an interpretation, the thermal structure of the $10.01$
solution ($T_{\max} = 7682$\,K, column $112.0$ over $1$--$10\,R_p$) now
stands well above that of the metal-free $2.13$ solution ($5730$\,K, $68.7$),
and its equivalent width is correspondingly above the measured value rather
than back at it. The sequence therefore still looks like a thermal trade
between the metal content of the base and the helium content above it, but
the metal side of the trade has become small (Sect.~\ref{sec:cnocooling}) and
what is left is the helium. Helium in these solutions is not only the
absorber; it is also the thermostat that sets how far the absorber is
carried.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Composition and ionization.} Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} carries
the elemental fractions and the ionization state of the same three solutions,
on the same advection-corrected profiles as Table~\ref{tab:thermostat}. Two
things follow from it that the thermostat argument rests on.

\smallskip
\noindent Diffusion keeps the wind less helium-rich than the reservoir. In
both solutions the helium fraction falls through the line-forming region and
then flattens: ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$ at the base gives $0.696$ by nuclei at
$2\,R_p$ and $0.554$ at $5\,R_p$; $10.01$ gives $3.587$ and $2.747$. That is
a factor $3.0$ and $2.8$ between the reservoir and the wind at $2\,R_p$. In
the composition's own terms, the flux-consistent solution at $2.09$, a
reservoir that is $68$ per cent helium by number, has a
\emph{hydrogen-dominated} line-forming region,
$n_{\rm H}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.59$ against $n_{\rm He}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.41$ at
$2\,R_p$. At $10.01$ helium leads there, $0.78$ against $0.22$, but by a
factor $2.8$ less than the reservoir states. A composition quoted for this
planet therefore has to say at which level it is quoted; the reservoir and
the absorbing layer are not the same number, and the memo's crossings are
reservoir values.

\smallskip
\noindent The hotter solution is also the more ionized one. At $2\,R_p$,
$x({\rm H\,\textsc{ii}}) = 0.031$ on the photochemical base at $2.09$ and
$0.057$ at $10.01$: a factor $1.9$, in the run whose neutral hydrogen density
is $3.7$ times lower. Both layers are optically thin at $13.6$\,eV, so the
reading that thinner hydrogen shields itself less does not account for it.
What grows is the ionization of hydrogen \emph{by helium}, through Penning
ionization He($2\,^3S$)$+$H, charge exchange He$^+ +$H and the
He-recombination continuum above $24.6$\,eV. The split between those channels
cannot be settled from the outputs: the individual rates (direct
$\Gamma_{\rm H\,\textsc{i}}$, the He-recombination-driven addition to it, and
the secondary-ionization yield) are not written to any output file. One
channel can be excluded, photoionization from $n=2$, since the heating it
would deposit is zero in all cells of all three runs. Helium ionization
itself tracks the temperature more simply: $x({\rm He\,\textsc{ii}})$ is
$0.046$, $0.046$ and $0.055$ at $2\,R_p$ across the three, and
$x({\rm He\,\textsc{iii}})$ is at most $1.3\times10^{-4}$ at $2\,R_p$
($9.1\times10^{-5}$, $9.0\times10^{-5}$ and $1.3\times10^{-4}$).

\smallskip
\noindent Fig.~\ref{fig:beststruct} shows the same kind of structure for the
single well-mixed solution that reproduces the line with no diffusion and no
base metals, while Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} is the set the lower-boundary axis
is built on. The two are complementary rather than alternative views.

\subsection{The composition the line needs above a photochemical base}
\label{sec:closureladder}

consistent with the elemental fluxes \emph{and} reproduces the line. This
section solves the sequence, at the adopted eddy coefficient, and finds that
on the current code there is nothing to solve for: every flux-consistent
solution stands above the measured equivalent width. The iteration is run at
nine starting reservoirs between $2.09$ and $12$, each cycling Photochem and
EXHALE until the elemental fluxes are continuous across the $1\,\mu$bar
match, with the climate step solved, $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$, the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum, a $5\%$ tolerance on the mismatch and the same stationary solve
inside every iteration. Each reservoir is read at its last iterate.

Every run returns a matching-level He/H equal to the reservoir it was started
from to four digits, for the reason Sect.~\ref{sec:closure} measured at
$2.09$: at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ the eddy-mixed column reaches the microbar
match, so the escape flux does not set the composition there. What this
sequence adds is the line.

\medskip
\noindent There is no crossing. The lowest reservoir solved,
$\mathrm{He/H} = 2.0924$, already makes $EW = 1.4765$\,\%\,\AA{} against the
measured $1.108 \pm 0.030$: $33$ per cent, that is $12\sigma$, above it. The
sequence rises from there and flattens near $2.22$\,\%\,\AA{} above
$\mathrm{He/H} \simeq 8$, so the measurement is not reached anywhere on it.
Read against its own local slope
$\mathrm{d}\ln EW/\mathrm{d}\ln(\mathrm{He/H}) = 0.49$ between the two lowest
rows, the measured equivalent width would be reached near
$\mathrm{He/H} \approx 1.1$; that is an extrapolation below every solved
reservoir and is not offered as the answer. What the sequence does say is
that the photochemical lower boundary no longer makes the line harder to
reach than the metal-free one does; it makes it easier. The comparison at one
composition is Table~\ref{tab:thermostat}, and its cause is the
density-resolved C, N and O cooling of Sect.~\ref{sec:cnocooling}.

The sequence is shallow and becomes shallower: the local slope runs $0.49$,
$0.32$, $0.14$, $0.08$, $0.05$, $0.04$, $0.03$ and $0.02$ over the successive
intervals, so above $\mathrm{He/H} \simeq 7$ the line stops responding to
composition. The saturation is a result in its own right: on this base the
equivalent width is a weak lever on the reservoir at the helium-rich end, and
a composition read there would carry a large error bar even if the sequence
did cross.

At the lowest reservoir the escape rate is $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.91$, that is
$8.1\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor $2.5$ below the
$2.03^{+0.58}_{-0.67}\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ the p-winds retrieval
imposes, and the elemental fluxes are $F_{\rm H} = 2.6\times10^{7}$ and
$F_{\rm He} = 5.4\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$.

\medskip
\noindent Two separate statements follow.

\begin{itemize}
\item Consistency with the elemental fluxes by itself does not select a
      reservoir on this planet. Every row of
      Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} satisfies it, at elemental mismatches of
      $2.6$--$4.8\%$ against the $5\%$ tolerance. What used to select one was
      the line, as in Sects.~\ref{sec:ew} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad}; on the
      current code the line selects none of them, and the composition this
      base would need lies below the sequence.
\item The escape rate is flat along the whole sequence, $7.90$ to $7.91$ in
      $\log_{10}\dot M$ over a factor $5.7$ in reservoir, so here too the
      wind does not fix the composition. It agrees with the $7.84$--$7.87$
      of the prescribed-base branch to within $0.05$\,dex, so what is placed
      at the lower boundary is no longer visible in the escape rate either.
\end{itemize}

The elemental mismatch $\varepsilon$ of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} is the
test applied; the radial spread of the flux over the window it is measured on
is $3.2\times10^{-3}$ in hydrogen and $6.5\times10^{-4}$ in helium.

\medskip
\noindent The sequence does not fix the width. The line is still too narrow:
FWHM $0.26$--$0.30$\,\AA{} against the measured $0.841$, unchanged from
Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} and from the solutions without diffusion. The winds
these numbers come from also carry the caveats of Sect.~\ref{sec:validity} in
full: their domain stops inside the critical point, and nothing in their
profiles above about $10\,R_p$ is usable. The He\,10830 layer is not
affected: it sits at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.011$.
\subsection{The outer region a converged wind inherits}
\label{sec:outerinherit}

Every number in this subsection is the 2026-08-30 measurement. The mechanism
it establishes does not depend on the physics that has changed since
(Sect.~\ref{sec:whatchanged}), but its magnitudes have not been re-measured
on the current code.

The effect was found on the earlier sequence, at its $\mathrm{He/H} = 12.01$
step, and it is why the sequence of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} is climbed
from one common parent. That step was first reached in one jump from $8.0$, a
factor $1.50$ in reservoir; a control run reached the same reservoir from
$11.11$, a factor $1.08$. The two solutions agree on everything the iteration
and the chemistry set, and disagree on the line:

\begin{itemize}
\item Same reservoir: He/H at the match $12.013522$ against
      $12.013520$, seven digits.
\item Same lower atmosphere: cold-trap O/H $1.0656\times10^{-6}$
      against $1.0660\times10^{-6}$ nuclei per H.
\item Both converged: $\|R\| = 1.866\times10^{-4}$ for the one reached in
      one jump and $1.287\times10^{-4}$ for the control, both inside the
      $4.0\times10^{-4}$ target the step was run to, and both inside the
      tolerance on the flux spread.
\item Different line: $EW = 1.4293$ against $1.2094$\,\%\,\AA{} and a
      red depth of $4.789$ against $3.956$ per cent, an $18$ per cent
      difference in equivalent width at fixed composition.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The difference sits entirely in the outer wind. Two diagnostics
that do not depend on where a spectral window is placed separate the two
solutions, and both vary monotonically along the sequence except at the
discarded point:

\noindent $r_{\rm drop}$ moves inward monotonically as the reservoir rises,
from $30.0$ to $19.5\,R_p$, and the outer share of the metastable column
stays in a narrow band, $0.0083$--$0.0117$, across the whole sequence. The
discarded solution departs from both: $25.8\,R_p$ where its own reservoir
gives $19.5$, and about twice the outer column share of any other step
($2.05$ times its control's, $1.67$ times the largest any other step
reaches). Its $T(12\,R_p)$ does \emph{not} match its control's ($1846$\,K
against $1959$, $5.8$ per cent apart), so that quantity no longer shows that
the two agree on the base. The agreement rests on the matching-level
composition, identical to seven digits, and on the profile ratio below. The
discarded solution is a hotter, more extended outer atmosphere sitting on an
identical base.

\medskip
\noindent The mechanism appears to be that the outer state is inherited from
the starting state and the Newton finish does not re-solve it. The density
there is low enough that it contributes almost nothing to the norm of the
residual, so $\|R\|$ cannot tell the two states apart. Both converge at
residuals inside the target, and which of the two reports the smaller one
says nothing about which is right: on the current version of the code, as on
the one before it, the discarded solution reports the larger residual, and
only on the retired rate coefficient did it report the smaller. He\,10830 is
optically thick at these columns, so a hot, extended outer region fills the
line wings and raises the equivalent width even though it carries about one
per cent of the metastable column.

\medskip
\noindent Measured on the metastable profiles, the two solutions agree to
$2.3$ per cent out to $11.9\,R_p$ and then separate abruptly (the departure
passes $10$ per cent at $11.95\,R_p$ and $50$ per cent at $12.14$, reaching a
factor $5.1$), so the region in which they differ is $12$--$30\,R_p$ rather
than the $20$--$30\,R_p$ an earlier draft of this paragraph gave. The exobase
of these two runs is also not the $18.8$--$25.9\,R_p$ of
Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}: that is the three-case range of
Table~\ref{tab:validity}, whose cases are two runs with a prescribed base and
the flux-consistent run at $2.09$. For this pair it is $11.81\,R_p$ for the
one reached in one jump and $14.73$ for the control.

Putting the corrected radii together: for the solution being discarded the
whole differing region lies outside its own exobase, where $\mathrm{Kn} > 1$;
for its control the innermost $2.6\,R_p$ of that region is nominally still
collisional. The fluid solution is therefore not valid in most of the region
that makes the difference, and in the discarded solution it is not valid
anywhere in it. The ground for discarding that solution is continuity of the
trend rather than a physical criterion, and by the same token the absolute
equivalent widths of the whole sequence carry a systematic error that a $5\%$
tolerance on the flux spread does not catch. Its size is small, because the
outer region is about one per cent of the metastable column, but it is not
zero. The exobase itself does not move with either correction: across the
sequence it is $11.8$--$18.9\,R_p$ on the current version of the code against
$11.8$--$18.8$ on the one before it.

\medskip
\noindent Independence of the path was checked directly at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 3.0$, from a starting state $3.7\times$ away in composition
and in the downward direction the sequence never takes: it returns the same
$r_{\rm drop}$ to the digit, with everything else inside tolerance. The
$5.0$, $8.0$ and $2.09$ reservoirs were not checked this way. Separately, the
$10.31$ one was re-run from the same starting state in its own directory and
reproduced identically, which fixes determinism but says nothing about the
dependence on the starting state. The rule the episode leaves is that a scan
over reservoirs must be climbed one step at a time.

\subsection{The 2025 non-detection}
\label{sec:xuvgrid}

Every point is solved by the same route as the fiducial.

Measured the way Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} measures it, by convolving the
solved profile with a Gaussian and asking which one reproduces the observed

\section{Is the wind collisional where it is set?}
\label{sec:validity}

Everything above reads the solutions as continuum solutions. A flow is a
hydrodynamic wind only where the gas is collisional on the scale over which
it varies, and in particular through its critical point, where the topology
and with it the mass flux are fixed. This section measures that. The tool
reads an existing run and changes nothing; the definitions, the tables and
the record are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. The numbers below were
measured on the outputs the rest of this memo uses, and nothing was re-run.

\subsection{What is measured}

\paragraph{The collisions.} The four momentum-transfer limits are the ones
the binary element diffusion of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} already uses, in
the same Chapman--Enskog first approximation: rigid-sphere neutral-neutral,
non-resonant ion-neutral with the induced-dipole and rigid-core frictions
added, and screened Coulomb with the Spitzer $\ln\Lambda$ for ion-ion and
ion-electron. Sharing them keeps a mean free path quoted here and a diffusion
coefficient used by the wind from drifting apart. The step from a binary
diffusion coefficient to a collision frequency is the definition of that
coefficient itself, $\nu_{st} = n_t k T/(\mu_{st}\hat D_{st})$ with
$\hat D = n D$, in which the total carrier density cancels. Species are
H\,\textsc{i}, H\,\textsc{ii}, He\,\textsc{i}, He\,\textsc{ii},
He\,\textsc{iii} and $e$, plus the molecules where a run tracks them;
He\,\textsc{i} $2^3S$ is a level inside He\,\textsc{i} and is not counted
twice, and metals are dropped as trace ($<10^{-3}$ of the particles) except
in the electron budget. Two channels are left out, resonant charge exchange
and electron-neutral momentum transfer. Both lengthen the mean free path, so
the verdict here is the conservative one.

\paragraph{The numbers.} $\lambda_s = \bar v_s/\nu_s$ with the harmonic sum
over partners; the local structure scale is the length over which the state
vector of the local Maxwellian changes,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{L^{2}} = \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}\right)^{2}
  + \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln T}{\mathrm{d}r}\right)^{2}
  + \left(\frac{|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|}{c_s}\right)^{2}
  + \frac{1}{r^{2}},
  \qquad L = \max\!\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cdot}},\ \Delta r_{\rm cell}\right),
\end{equation}
and $\mathrm{Kn}_s = \lambda_s/L$. The pressure is deliberately left out of
it. The continuum closure expands about a local Maxwellian, which is fixed by
$(n, T, u)$; $p = nkT$ is a derived field, so including it would count the
density and temperature gradients twice wherever they do not cancel and
contribute nothing where they do. The second half of that is what matters
here: across a heating front $\rho$ falls and $T$ rises with nearly the same
logarithmic slope, so $|\mathrm{d}\ln p/\mathrm{d}r|$ passes through a broad
near-zero while the gas is changing fast, and the earlier definition
$L = \min(H_p, L_v, r)$ read a scale height fifteen times longer than the
density and temperature scales there. The sum is taken in quadrature rather
than as a minimum because a minimum jumps to the next length whenever the
field it is taken from flattens; in quadrature a flat field contributes zero,
and $L$ still cannot exceed the shortest individual scale. The velocity
enters as $|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|/c_s$ and not
$|(1/v)\,\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|$ because the term the closure drops is the
viscous stress, whose size relative to the pressure is
$\sim\lambda|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|/\bar v$: a change in bulk velocity
distorts the distribution in proportion to the thermal speed, not to the
local bulk speed. That removes the Mach-number floor the old definition
needed. The floor at the local cell width says that a gradient claiming
structure below one cell is measuring the mesh; it binds in three cells of
one of the four runs, all inside $1.002\,R_p$, and in no critical region. The
\emph{exobase} is the first crossing of $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 1$ from
below, never extrapolated beyond the domain. The \emph{critical point} is the
sonic point taken with the code's own sound speed,
$c_s = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$ at $\gamma = 5/3$, and not a redefinition. The
\emph{critical region} runs from the peak of volumetric heating to the sonic
point. ``Collisional'' means $\max \mathrm{Kn} < 0.1$ across that region,
over the bulk and over every species carrying at least $10^{-3}$ of the mass.
Where it fails the solution is reported as unvalidated rather than as
overestimated, because a continuum solution that assumed collisionality away
cannot decide which way a collisionless critical region moves the answer.

\noindent The wind of LHS\,1140\,b does reach a critical point, and the
domain stops short of it. All three solutions stay subsonic out to
$29\,R_p$, at $\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.34$--$0.37$, so inside the domain the
mass flux is set by the outer boundary condition and not by a critical point.
Extending the domain settles what that costs: at $45$ and $60\,R_p$ the same
case crosses its critical point at $40.06$ and $40.08\,R_p$ and leaves the
domain supersonic, and $\log_{10}\dot M$ is $7.8737$, $7.8740$ and $7.8735$
at $30$, $45$ and $60\,R_p$, a spread of $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex. The escape
rate is therefore not an artifact of where the boundary was put, but the
profiles above $10\,R_p$ are: they move by $25$ per cent in temperature, $29$
in density and $22$ in velocity at $28\,R_p$, and nothing is quoted from
them.

What remains unvalidated is the \emph{continuum} description out there.
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ passes $0.1$ at $7.30$--$7.44\,R_p$ and reaches
$0.68$--$0.71$ at the top of the $30\,R_p$ domain, and the exobase of the
extended solutions sits at $29.0\,R_p$, \emph{below} the critical point at
$40.06$. The continuum equations therefore fix their own topology in gas they
cannot describe. The escape rates quoted throughout this memo,
$\log_{10}\dot M \simeq 7.8$--$7.9$, are unvalidated in the collisional
sense, and their insensitivity to the domain size is a statement about the
discrete problem rather than a physical validation.

The p-winds retrieval sits in the same regime. Its isothermal Parker solution
places its sonic point at $8$--$9.5\,R_p$, where $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is
$0.12$--$0.17$, past the threshold. The critical region of LHS\,1140\,b is
therefore \emph{transitional}: it is not collisional, and it does not lie
above the exobase either. The scale separation the continuum equations need
has been lost by the radius at which either code sets the mass flux, while
the gas is not yet free molecular.

\paragraph{Which species sets it.} Neutral hydrogen decides the bulk number,
and the line-forming layer is unaffected. In the critical region of the
well-mixed solution the species split is H\,\textsc{i} $1.03$,
He\,\textsc{i} $0.73$, H\,\textsc{ii} $3.6\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{ii}
$1.8\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{iii} $4.7\times10^{-6}$, $e$
$6.5\times10^{-4}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:knudsen}b). The neutrals carry the mass and
the long mean free path, and the charged species are held by Coulomb
collisions. That matters for reading the rest of this memo, because the
He\,10830 metastable population forms at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$, where
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is $1.5$--$2.4\times10^{-4}$ at $1.1\,R_p$,
$0.0033$--$0.0036$ at $2\,R_p$ and $0.0110$--$0.0115$ at $3\,R_p$ in every
case here. The line-forming layer is collisional by two orders of magnitude.
The unvalidated quantity is the mass flux of the wind solution. The
absorption that Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}--\ref{sec:width} measure, and the
$22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of missing width, are asked for at radii where the
continuum description is secure.

\paragraph{The energy equation.} The single-temperature energy equation is
not where the description fails. Electron-ion energy coupling is fast
wherever it matters: the largest $\tau^E_{ei}/\tau_{\rm heat}$ over the
critical region is $0.027$, $0.024$ and $0.026$ in the three LHS\,1140\,b
solutions and $8.3\times10^{-5}$ in the control. Momentum coupling times are
likewise short compared with the flow time. That is a different test, not a
contradiction: a strongly subsonic flow gives hundreds of collisions per flow
time even at $\mathrm{Kn}\sim 1$. The Knudsen number is what decides
continuum validity, because the terms the closure drops (viscous stress, heat
flux, the non-Maxwellian tail) scale with the gradient over a mean free path
rather than with the collision count.

\paragraph{The kinetic scale.} Jeans escape through the top of the domain
(Chamberlain \& Hunten 1987, eq.~7.2.5) gives
$4.5$--$4.7\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor $1.5$--$1.7$ below the
continuum $\dot M$. That is a \emph{scale}, not a bound. Jeans escape bounds
a continuum rate from below only when the exobase is well bound, and here
$\lambda_J = 0.46$--$0.48$: the outer atmosphere is barely gravitationally
bound, the Jeans integral is most of the Maxwellian rather than its escaping
tail, and the atmosphere is in blow-off. What the number does say is that the
continuum result sits within a factor of two of what a collisionless outer
atmosphere at the same density and temperature would lose, so the failure of
the assumption is not hiding an order of magnitude. On HD\,209458\,b, where
$\lambda_J = 10.6$ and the solution is validated through its critical point,
the hydrodynamic rate exceeds Jeans by $10^{4}$: the signature of a driven
wind rather than an evaporating atmosphere.

\subsection{What this changes, and what it leaves open}

\begin{itemize}
\item The domain stops inside the critical point, and the region it stops
      in is not collisional. The extended-domain runs put the
      critical point at $40.06\,R_p$ and the exobase at $29.0$, so the
      topology that sets the mass flux is fixed in gas the continuum
      equations do not describe. That the flux itself moves by
      $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex between $30$, $45$ and $60\,R_p$ bounds the
      discretization error and not the physical one. Any $\dot M$ quoted for
      LHS\,1140\,b from these solutions has to carry that warning, and so
      does the p-winds number, whose sonic point sits at
      $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.12$--$0.16$.
\item The outer profiles are not usable, and the residual cannot say so.
      At $28\,R_p$ the $30\,R_p$ solution stands $25$ per cent too
      hot, $29$ per cent too dense and $22$ per cent too slow against the
      converged answer, at a residual that passes every convergence test.
      Nothing above about $10\,R_p$ in these profiles is quoted.
\item The rate itself is not settled here, and cannot be. Settling it would
      take a kinetic or transitional-flow calculation (DSMC, or a
      13-moment closure carried through the transitional region), and this
      repository contains neither. It is recorded as open, not estimated.
\item The line analysis stands. The He\,10830 layer is collisional
      by two orders of magnitude, so the equivalent-width matching, the
      composition inference conditional on it, and the invariant
      $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ width deficit are unaffected by this section. It
      constrains what the escape rate may be quoted as, not what the line
      says.
\end{itemize}
\section{Status against the goal}

The line is not reproduced. What stands:

\begin{itemize}
\item Amount of absorption: matched over a prescribed metal-free scalar
      base, and no longer matched over a solved photochemical column. Over
      the scalar base the answer is
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 1.5161$ at the adopted
      $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, and inside that branch it is
      further conditional: $0.0526$ well mixed on the GJ\,699 proxy,
      $0.4044$ well mixed on the GJ\,1132 proxy, $3.10$ wherever
      $K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$ leaves the molecular coefficient alone. Every one
      of those is read between two solved compositions. Over the
      photochemical column every flux-consistent solution from
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09$ to $12.01$ stands \emph{above} the measurement,
      the lowest by $33$ per cent, so that branch has no crossing
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}). The match, where it exists, is
      conditional: it constrains the composition only as far as the
      assumptions behind it are secure.
\item Everything here was re-solved twice, and one of the two branches
      reversed. Against 2026-08-30 the crossings over the prescribed base
      moved by $2$ to $19$ per cent and $\dot M$ by about $0.1$\,dex, and
      the branch with the solved column went from making the line
      $2.6\times$ weaker than the metal-free base at the same composition to
      making it $7.4$ per cent stronger; the cause is the C, N and O line
      cooling, which was evaluated in the coronal limit above the critical
      density of the levels it excites and is now the CHIANTI statistical
      equilibrium at the electron density of each cell
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:cnocooling}). The first re-solve was needed because
      the 2026-08-30 states were never stationary below $1.1\,R_p$, and it
      was possible because of the discretization that keeps a hydrostatic
      atmosphere hydrostatic. The second was needed because the lower
      boundary chose its entropy branch on the velocity in the first cell
      rather than on the mass flux through its face, so the campaign of
      2026-09-14 carried a $418$\,K first cell where the prescribed
      reservoir was $226$\,K (Sect.~\ref{sec:l21}). That correction takes
      the base cell down by a median $46$ per cent in temperature, $\dot M$
      down by $2$ per cent everywhere, and the crossings \emph{up} by $1$ to
      $14$ per cent.
\item Line shape: not matched, by a factor $3.3$ in depth and width alike,
      and not fixable by composition. The missing ingredient is
      $22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of velocity spread ($\sigma =
      9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} and
      Fig.~\ref{fig:broadened}; supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth,
      blue depth and red/blue ratio inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval
      at once, but its physical origin is unidentified. The demand is
      unchanged under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$: measured the same
      way on the diffusive solution matched on equivalent width it is the
      same $22.22$ to four digits (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}); it is
      $22.15$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at the lowest flux-consistent solution
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}); and it is $21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on
      the $5.1\times$ brighter proxy spectrum (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), $1.1$
      per cent of spread over four solutions that differ by a factor $40$ in
      composition. The adiabatic metastable bump, which would have supplied
      it without a free parameter, is present and measured
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) but sits too slow to contribute.
\item The width shortfall is not a measurement artifact, and it is not this
      code's alone. Audited term by term, the instrument profile,
      the triplet splitting, stellar rotation, planetary rotation and
      orbital smearing account for at most $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of it;
      deconvolving both sides from their own instrument kernels puts the
      like-for-like requirement at $22.00$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit}). The paper's own p-winds fit, measured
      the same way, is itself $1.5$ times narrower than the width printed in
      the same paper, so the shortfall appears to be a property of
      one-dimensional steady-state wind models rather than of this one.
\item Escape rate: an output, not a fit, and set by the assumed spectrum.
      $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.84$--$7.87$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy, a factor
      $2.7$ below the value the retrieval imposes, and nearly independent of
      composition, of diffusion, of the eddy coefficient and now also of
      what is placed at the lower boundary (the flux-consistent sequence
      gives $7.90$--$7.91$). On the GJ\,699 proxy, $5.1\times$ brighter in
      XUV, it is $8.55$--$8.57$: within $0.06$\,dex of the value the paper
      retrieves on that same proxy (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}).
\item That escape rate is unvalidated in the collisional sense; the line is
      not. The solutions are the subsonic part of a transonic
      escape whose critical point sits at $40.06\,R_p$, outside the
      $30\,R_p$ domain, and whose exobase sits at $29.0\,R_p$, below the
      critical point. Moving the boundary to $45$ and $60\,R_p$ changes
      $\log_{10}\dot M$ by $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex and the equivalent width by
      at most $1.7$ per cent, but changes the profiles at $28\,R_p$ by
      $22$--$29$ per cent, so nothing above about $10\,R_p$ is quoted. The
      p-winds sonic point at $8$--$9.5\,R_p$ sits at
      $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.12$--$0.17$, so the critical region of this planet is
      transitional for both codes. The He\,10830 layer at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$
      is collisional by two orders of magnitude ($\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.012$),
      and an HD\,209458\,b control validates cleanly at a factor $56$ lower
      $\mathrm{Kn}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}). What the true rate is needs
      a kinetic or transitional-flow calculation, which this repository does
      not contain.
\item Composition: limited by the base and by the assumed spectrum, and
      weakly limited by $K_{zz}$. In the well-mixed limit the
      equivalent-width crossing is $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4044$ on one proxy
      spectrum and $0.0526$ on the other, helium-rich against helium-poor,
      with the measurement unable to separate them. When helium and hydrogen
      are allowed to separate by diffusion the crossing rises, and the whole
      $K_{zz}$ axis has been walked: $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.10$ on the plateau
      at $K_{zz} \le 10^{5}$, then $2.89$, $2.54$, $1.97$, $1.52$, $1.16$
      and $0.89$ at $10^{6}$ through $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$
      (Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross}, Fig.~\ref{fig:hehkzz}), a factor $1.7$
      across the range the literature leaves open and $3.5$ across the
      entire axis, against the factor $7.7$ the spectrum carries. So any
      composition read off this line, the paper's included, is strongly
      conditional on the assumed XUV spectrum, and weakly conditional on the
      eddy coefficient, as $K_{zz}^{-0.115}$ wherever the eddy term acts at
      all.
\item The lower boundary is still an axis, and it now points the other way.
      With the lower atmosphere solved as a photochemical column
      and the elemental fluxes iterated to continuity across the
      $1\,\mu$bar match, He/H at the match is a solution rather than an
      input, and at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ that solution is the well-mixed value
      because the eddy term reaches the match (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}).
      What the column brings with it is C, N and O, and on the current
      cooling those elements cost the wind $164$\,K in $T_{\max}$ and the
      line $0.20$ per cent of its equivalent width at fixed composition
      (Table~\ref{tab:thermostat}). The flux-consistent sequence therefore
      sits above the measurement everywhere, and the composition this base
      would need lies below every reservoir solved.
\item The 2025 non-detection sits at about $0.28$ of the fiducial XUV over
      the scalar base; over the photochemical column it is not yet
      bracketed. Scaling the irradiation with the lower atmosphere held
      fixed, the red-pair depth falls below the $0.6$ per cent limit of the
      2025 epoch at $\simeq\!0.279$ of fiducial in the scalar-base model,
      about $9$\,erg\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:xuvgrid}, Table~\ref{tab:xuvgrid}). The
      flux-consistent grid is still at $1.001$ per cent at $0.10$, the
      faintest scaling it solves, so its limit lies below $0.10$ and is not
      bracketed: on the corrected lower boundary the three scalings between
      $0.10$ and $0.30$ no longer solve, and the $0.01$ points of both
      branches have never solved.
\item Not verified: the cross-check with a second chemistry code. VULCAN
      did not converge on this cold helium-rich column, its convergence
      measure rose against the criterion with the timestep pinned, the
      limiting cell being H$_2$O at the cold trap, so no composition from
      a second code, and no spread between the two codes, is quoted
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}).
\item Partly solved: the molecular lower boundary. Three groups of models
      solve the molecular layer in EXHALE itself rather than taking it from
      the column, and two of their cases now converge, both
      with element diffusion at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$:
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.13$ at $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.91$ with a red-pair
      $EW = 1.5758$\,\%\,\AA, and $9.7$ at $7.95$ with $2.4212$\,\%\,\AA.
      Both stand above the measurement, as the flux-consistent solutions do.
      The other cases of those groups have not converged, are a marching
      stop, or are still running at the time of writing, so no crossing is
      read from a molecular group here. What they wait on is the step that
      lets the transported H$_2$ settle.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Four axes have now been walked: the assumed XUV spectrum, the
mixing assumption with its eddy coefficient, composition itself, and what is
placed at the lower boundary. Each separates the two questions rather than
answering them together. All four move the composition the equivalent width
implies, two of them by a large factor, and none of them moves the width. The
possible sources of the missing $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ are therefore unchanged:
velocity fields that are three-dimensional, or heating a 1D hydrodynamic
energy equation does not carry. The adiabatic-cooling metastable bump, the
candidate that would have needed no free parameter, has been looked for on
both spectra and does not supply it for this host (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}).
