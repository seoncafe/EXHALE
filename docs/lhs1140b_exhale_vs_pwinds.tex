\section{Goal, and the answer up front}

The objective is to reproduce the measured He\,\textsc{i} $10830$\,\AA{}
transit line of LHS\,1140\,b with EXHALE, which solves the wind rather than
imposing it, and to establish what (if anything) has to be added to
the physics for the model to match the observation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Result.} The \emph{amount} of absorption can be matched,
but the composition it takes is not one number: it splits in two according
to what is placed at the lower boundary. Above a prescribed metal-free
scalar base the measured equivalent width is reached at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 1.61$; above a lower atmosphere solved as a photochemical
column and handed over as a profile, C, N and O included, it is reached at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 8.21$. Both runs have binary H/He element diffusion active
and both sit at the same constant $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^{2}$\,s$^{-1}$
(a stated number, and stated with no pressure dependence: what that
assumption carries is measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:kzzform}), so what
separates the two values is the base and not the mixing. The \emph{width}
is not matched over either base, and the velocity it asks for is the same
number every time. Solving the lower atmosphere instead of prescribing it
does not remove the match: it moves it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the Penning-coefficient revision changed, and what it
did not.} Every composition quoted here is lower than the value the draft
before it carried, by a nearly uniform factor. The cause is one rate
coefficient: the He($2\,^3S$) destruction rate on atomic hydrogen was the
Taylor et al.\ (2025) two-branch fit, which jumps by $1.5724$ at $4000$\,K,
and it has been replaced by the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025) closed form, which
is continuous (Sect.~\ref{sec:penningrate}). The new coefficient is smaller
everywhere in this wind, so the metastable level lives longer, the line is
stronger at fixed composition, and every crossing moves down. Measured on
re-solved composition ladders rather than scaled from a slope, the five
crossings are $0.74$--$0.81$ of what they were, and the local exponent
$\mathrm{d}\log(\mathrm{He/H})/\mathrm{d}\log K_{zz}$ is the same to within
$0.01$--$0.02$ at every decade. \emph{The structure of the result (that
the composition this line implies is conditional on the assumed spectrum
and on what is placed at the lower boundary, and only weakly on the assumed
mixing) survives unchanged; what moved is the level, by about $20$ per
cent.} That revision left the ratios between the branches where they were:
a factor $5.6$ between the two bases, $3.8$ between the well-mixed and
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossings, and $9$ between the two proxy spectra.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the composition-renormalized secondary ionization
changed.} The Shull \& van Steenberg (1985) secondary-ionization split was
tabulated at one composition, $n({\rm He\,\textsc{i}})/n({\rm H\,\textsc{i}})
= 0.1$, and EXHALE now renormalizes it on the local composition
(\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96). Every crossing in this memo sits far from
that ratio, so the He\,\textsc{i} photoionization rate the split feeds moves
and the line moves with it. At fixed composition the equivalent width rises
by $1.4$, $1.6$, $2.0$ and $0.1$ per cent on the four helium-rich cases and
\emph{falls} by $0.6$ per cent on the helium-poor GJ\,699 one, so the
crossings do not all move the same way: a pattern that reads as the split
being renormalized about the SvS85 value, although nothing here checks that
reading against the rate itself. All five were re-solved on the
corrected binary, each inside a bracket of two run arms and each then solved
at the crossing composition itself
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}). Four of the five move by $0.1$ to
$2.7$ per cent; the fifth, the flux closure, moves by $9.2$ per cent, not
because it is more sensitive to the split but because its ladder is the
shallowest of the five: its equivalent width rises as
$(\mathrm{He/H})^{0.27}$ across the ladder and as $(\mathrm{He/H})^{0.20}$
over the stretch between the old crossing and the new one, where the
well-mixed and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ ladders rise as $^{0.76}$ and the plateau
as $^{0.95}$, so a $2$ per cent move in the line is a $9$ per cent move in
the reservoir. The structure
survives again: the $K_{zz}$ landscape keeps its three regimes and its local
exponents to $0.003$, the plateau is still about nine times the well-mixed
crossing, and the composition still spans a factor $170$ end to end where it
spanned $189$. The ratios between the branches now read: the two bases
differ by a factor $5.1$, the well-mixed and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossings by
$3.9$, and the two proxy spectra by $8.6$. Quantities that were \emph{not}
re-solved on the corrected split are marked where they appear.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(i) The amount is always matchable, so what the line
returns is a conditional pair rather than a measurement.}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The prescribed-base branch.} With the base a scalar state
carrying no metals, every combination of assumed spectrum and assumed mixing
reaches the observed red-pair equivalent width at some helium abundance, and
the conditions stack inside this branch. In the well-mixed limit the
crossing is $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy spectrum and
$0.0483$, below solar, on the GJ\,699 one: the second stand-in for
LHS\,1140's unobserved XUV, $5.1$ times brighter, which the paper also uses.
With binary H/He element diffusion active (helium then settles out of the
base unless an eddy term lifts it) it rises again, to $3.81$ wherever
$K_{zz} \le 10^{4}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ does nothing against the molecular
coefficient, $1.6108$ at the adopted $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and $0.837$ at
$10^{11}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}). The dependence on the eddy
coefficient is weak and monotonic, as $K_{zz}^{-0.14}$ wherever the eddy
term acts at all; the dependence on the assumed spectrum is a factor
$8.6$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The solved-column branch.} Replacing that base with a
photochemical column (Photochem with its climate step solved, handed to
EXHALE as a profile at a $1\,\mu$bar match, at the same
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and with the same diffusion active) brings C, N and O
into the base, and the same reservoir then absorbs about $2.6\times$ less
(Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}).
A ladder of nineteen solutions on this base, each
closed on its own elemental fluxes, has two rungs that straddle the
measurement directly, $8.21$ below it and $8.26$ above, and crosses it at
$\mathrm{He/H} = 8.212$ ($7.474$--$9.395$ on the measurement's $1\sigma$
interval) (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Neither number is an independent prediction of the model.}
Each is the reservoir the observed line picks out over its own base, and
the escape rate (which is an output, not a fit) barely moves along the
way. The flux closure does not change that on the profile branch. It
returns $\mathrm{He/H} = 2.09235$ at the match from trial fluxes a factor of
ten apart, single-valued to $7.8\times10^{-6}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}),
but at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ the eddy term homogenizes the column all the way to
the microbar match, so only $2\times10^{-4}$ of that answer comes from the
escape flux: the closure hands back the reservoir it was given. And the
reservoir it was given, $2.09$, was itself a value fitted to this same line
over the earlier metal-free scalar base. What the closure adds is that a
composition is consistent with a photochemical lower atmosphere carrying its
own C, N and O, not that it is measured. Every rung of the ladder satisfies
the closure at residuals of $2.3$--$4.7\%$ against its $5\%$ tolerance; the
line is what selects one.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Why the same helium absorbs differently over the two bases.}
The base metals are a thermostat setting, and helium is the other half of
the same thermostat. The photochemical column's C, N and O cool the wind
($T_{\max}$ from $5447$ to $3955$\,K), and although the metastable peak
itself rises, $54.0$ to $60.4$\,cm$^{-3}$, the cooler wind does not carry
the population outward, so the metastable column above $2\,R_p$ falls by
$2.6$ and the line weakens by the same factor, $2.6$. Raising the reservoir reheats the wind:
at $\mathrm{He/H} = 10.31$ the mean mass per particle at $2\,R_p$ is
$2.53\,m_{\rm H}$ against $1.52$, the same deposited energy is shared among
fewer and heavier particles, the coolant is diluted with the hydrogen it is
referred to, and $T_{\max}$ returns to $5898$\,K, past the metal-free
$5447$. The ladder is therefore closer to a thermal trade between base metal
content and helium content than to a determination of composition, and this
one line cannot separate the two (Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals},
Fig.~\ref{fig:thermostat}).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The escape rate is flat on every axis, and end to end the
composition spans a factor $170$.} $\log_{10}(\dot M/\mathrm{g\,s^{-1}})
\simeq 7.7$--$7.8$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy across composition, diffusion and
eddy coefficient alike, $7.40$--$7.41$ across the whole closed ladder, and
$8.58$ on the GJ\,699 proxy: there within $0.05$\,dex of what the paper
itself retrieves on that same proxy (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}). Against that,
the composition the line implies runs from $0.0483$ to $8.21$ over choices
this measurement cannot arbitrate: strongly conditional on the assumed
spectrum and on what is placed at the lower boundary, weakly and
monotonically on the assumed mixing. Any composition read off this line
(the paper's $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much as any value here)
inherits those conditions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(ii) The width is not reached on any axis, and the deficit
is invariant.} At the equivalent-width crossing the profile is about $3.3$
times too deep and $3.3$ times too narrow, and no composition fixes it,
because composition changes how much helium absorbs, not how the absorption
is spread in velocity. Measured directly (convolving the solved profile
with a Gaussian and asking which one reproduces the observed line) the
missing spread is $22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
($\sigma = 9.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) in the well-mixed limit,
$22.23$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$,
$21.95$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the flux-closed solution on the solved
photochemical column, and $21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the $5.1\times$ brighter
spectrum: a spread of $1.3$ per cent over four solutions that differ by
a factor $170$ in composition (Sects.~\ref{sec:broadening},
\ref{sec:diffbroad}, \ref{sec:closureladder} and~\ref{sec:gj699}).
Supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth, blue depth and red/blue ratio
inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval at once, with nothing tuned to them;
what produces it is unidentified. It is not thermal (the solved gas
cools below $1000$\,K over the line-forming region), and not bulk
outflow, which reaches $1.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $20\,R_p$ on the fainter
spectrum and $3.6$ on the brighter. Read as turbulence it is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.4$--$4.2$ against the sound speed these solutions
themselves carry, supersonic on every convention and well above what the
literature is willing to assume. The one mechanism that would supply width
without a free parameter (the adiabatic-cooling metastable bump of
Schulik \& Owen (2025)) is present in these solutions and measured, but
sits at $0.25$--$1.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}). The shortfall is
in the energy and momentum budget, not in the composition, and it is a
published problem rather than a defect of this setup
(Sect.~\ref{sec:width}).

The paper's own p-winds fit does not reproduce the line either. It is
broader than the wind solved here, because its temperature is imposed at
$5000$--$6000$\,K where the line forms, but at FWHM $0.575$\,\AA{} with its
turbulence term on ($0.458$ without it) it is still $1.5$ times narrower
than the measured $0.841$, and it overshoots the blue component threefold.
Adding broadening does not rescue it: its absorbing power is
$24\%$ below the measurement, so no convolution can reach the observed
depth and width at once. \emph{The width is a shortfall the two codes
share}, which is what the audit of the measurement side
(Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit}) leaves as the reading: the measurement is not
at fault, and neither one-dimensional steady-state wind reproduces it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{A caveat that attaches to the escape rate alone.} These
solutions stay subsonic out to $30\,R_p$, so their mass flux is set at the
outer boundary rather than at a critical point, and the gas is already
collisionless there ($\mathrm{Kn} = 1.4$--$2.4$): the $\dot M$ quoted
throughout is \emph{unvalidated in the collisional sense}, while the
He\,10830 layer at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$ sits at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.019$ and the
line analysis is untouched (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Four systematics ride on the absolute numbers, and none of
them is caught by a convergence test.} They are stated here because they
bound how far any single crossing should be read.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0.2ex
\item \emph{The outer region is inherited from the seed.} A converged wind
      takes its $20$--$30\,R_p$ state from the solution it was started
      from, the JFNK finish does not re-solve it, and $\|R\|$ cannot
      separate two such states (Sect.~\ref{sec:outerinherit};
      \texttt{Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~83). That region lies outside the
      exobase, where the fluid solution is not valid in any case, so the
      absolute equivalent width of every rung of every ladder carries a
      systematic the closure tolerance does not reach.
\item \emph{The closure crossing carries a seed systematic of order half a
      unit in reservoir.} Re-closing an arm from a neighbour instead of
      from the common parent moves its equivalent width by $0.2$--$1.9$ per
      cent, which at the local slope is a large fraction of a unit in
      $\mathrm{He/H}$, and no test the driver applies sees it
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}).
\item \emph{The closure tolerance itself is visible in the answer.} At
      $\mathrm{tol} = 0.05$ the ladder steps by $0.0122$\,\%\,\AA{} between
      reservoirs $8.45$ and $8.50$ and by $0.0048$ over the three times
      wider interval $8.50$--$8.65$. Tightening it to $0.008$ is refused by
      the driver, because the radial spread of the flux over the steady
      window would then exceed the residual being decided on it.
\item \emph{Which chemistry produced the column.} The earlier results were
      made with Photochem 0.8.4; the closure results here are Photochem
      0.9.0 with the local corrections of
      \texttt{docs/photochem\_solver\_modification\_*.md}. On the seven
      arms that can be compared like for like the equivalent width moves by
      at most $1.2\times10^{-6}$ in relative terms and $\log_{10}\dot M$
      does not move in its fourth decimal, \emph{even though the correction
      takes the deepest-level elemental residual from $2\times10^{-5}$ to
      $10^{-14}$}: nine orders of magnitude in the equilibrium residual,
      and nothing in the observable (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Inputs, and how each was pinned}

\subsection{System}
Planet and star from Cadieux et al.\ (2024) as quoted by C26:
$R_p = 1.730 \pm 0.025\,R_\oplus$, $M_p = 5.60 \pm 0.19\,M_\oplus$,
$T_{\rm eq} = 226$\,K, $a = 0.0946$\,AU, $M_\star = 0.1844\,M_\odot$,
$R_\star = 0.2159\,R_\odot$. One trap: EXHALE converts $R_p$ with its own
internal constants (mean radii, $R_J = 6.9911\times10^{9}$\,cm,
$R_\oplus = 6.3725\times10^{8}$\,cm), so the input must be built with those
and not with IAU equatorial values, a $2.2\%$ error in radius, $4.4\%$ in
transit depth, if taken from the wrong pair.

\subsection{Lower boundary condition}
\label{sec:base_bc}
EXHALE anchors the lower boundary at the planetary radius $R_p$ using the
standard CETIMB pressure-anchored condition (\texttt{Base BC: pressure 1.0}),
fixing the base pressure at
$P_0 = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{bar} = 1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-3}}$
($0.1$\,Pa). The corresponding base total number density $n_0$ follows
from the ideal gas law at the equilibrium temperature
$T_0 = T_{\rm eq} = 226.0$\,K:
\begin{equation}
  n_0 = \frac{P_0}{k_B T_0 n_{\rm tot,bc}}
      = \frac{1.0\,\mathrm{dyn\,cm^{-2}}}{(1.380649\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{erg\,K^{-1}}) \times 226.0\,\mathrm{K}}
      = 3.20485\times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}},
\end{equation}
where $n_{\rm tot,bc} = 1.0$ for neutral atomic gas. In logarithmic form,
$\log_{10}(n_0/\mathrm{cm^{-3}}) = 13.506$, which is entered in
\texttt{input.inp} as \texttt{Log10 lower boundary number density [cm\textasciicircum-3]: 13.506}.
When \texttt{Base BC: pressure} is specified, EXHALE dynamically re-evaluates
and enforces this relation internally (\texttt{input\_read.f90}).

\subsection{Stellar spectrum}
\label{sec:sed}
The paper describes its normalization two ways that cannot both hold: the
Supplement's prose says the proxy SED is scaled so its integrated X-ray flux
matches that measured for LHS\,1140, while the quoted constants
($A_{1132} = 0.59$, $A_{699} = 0.056$) are the catalog X-ray flux
\emph{ratios}, a factor $1.8$ apart. The authors' script settles it:
\begin{verbatim}
Lx_ratio_1132 = 0.59
norm_constant_1132 = Lx_ratio_1132*(d_1140/(smax_1140*au2m))**2
\end{verbatim}
applied to the plain \texttt{const-res} v23 product. That combination also
reproduces the fiducial XUV flux the main text quotes, $0.033$\,W\,m$^{-2}$
(we measure $33.6$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ over $10$--$1300$\,\AA{}). All
results here use it. Note the proxy SED and the paper's own APEC fit
disagree in the X-ray band ($4.4$ against $2.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$
at the orbit); that inconsistency is inherited from the paper's procedure,
not introduced here.

\subsection{The atomic input that sets the line: He($2\,^3S$) destruction on
atomic hydrogen}
\label{sec:penningrate}

Every composition this memo quotes is read off the metastable population,
and in this wind that population is drained almost entirely by one
reaction, He($2\,^3S$)\,$+$\,H. Its rate coefficient is therefore an input
of the same standing as the spectrum, and it has been changed since the
earlier draft. The record is \texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md}, Sect.~87; the
grounds are stated here because every number below moved with it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What was used, and why it cannot be.} The code carried
Taylor et al.\ (2025), Table~2, transcribed correctly:
$1.9\times10^{-9}(300/T)^{0.07}$ below $4000$\,K and
$9.1\times10^{-9}(300/T)^{0.50}$ above it. Those two branches do not meet:
at $4000$\,K they give $1.5849$ and $2.4921\times10^{-9}$, a jump of
$1.5724$. A rate coefficient is a continuous function of temperature, and
``it is the published value'' is not a defence of an expression that is
discontinuous in a state variable. In these solutions the seam falls at
$1.11$--$1.18\,R_p$, inside the line-forming region, and it was visible as
a one-cell step of $33$ per cent in $n(2\,^3S)$ in the figures of the
earlier draft.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The fit also disagrees with its own paper.} Taylor et
al.'s Fig.~19 (bottom right) plots the quantity Table~2 is meant to
represent (their Maxwell--Boltzmann average of the Cohen \& Lane (1971)
and Morgner \& Niehaus (1979) cross sections), and that curve has a
maximum near $1400$\,K. A monotonically decreasing power law cannot produce
a maximum, so the tabulated low branch cannot be the same quantity;
digitized, the two differ by $1.13$--$1.62$ over $500$--$4000$\,K. Above the
seam the high branch is worse: $2.23\times10^{-9}$ at $5000$\,K, above
\emph{every} cross-section determination collected in Garc\'ia Mu\~noz
(2025), Fig.~5, which lie between about $1.3$ and $1.7\times10^{-9}$ there.
Their appendix records how this happened: an equation already
implemented in their KPP routine, fitted in two pieces with
\texttt{curve\_fit} and nothing tying the pieces together at the join.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What is used now.} Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025), A\&A 698,
A199, publishes in the caption of its Fig.~5 a closed form for the
Maxwell--Boltzmann average of the Movre \& Meyer (1997) total ionization
cross sections,
\begin{equation}
  k = 10^{-9}\exp\!\left(\frac{c}{T} + d_1\ln T + d_2(\ln T)^2
      + d_3(\ln T)^3\right)\ \mathrm{cm^{3}\,s^{-1}},
  \label{eq:gm25}
\end{equation}
with $c = -8.64804\times10^{1}$, $d_1 = -2.86766\times10^{-1}$,
$d_2 = +8.68445\times10^{-2}$ and $d_3 = -5.73001\times10^{-3}$, stated
there as accurate to better than $2\%$ over $200$--$10^{4}$\,K. Measured
here, it reproduces that paper's own Table~A.5 to $0.4\%$, and it is
continuous at $4000$\,K to the eighth digit
($1.35630953\times10^{-9}$ at $3999.9$\,K against $1.35630905$ at
$4000.1$).

\emph{This is not one paper's fit traded for another's.} Taylor's own
Fig.~19 and the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz curve agree to $10$--$20\%$; it is the
tabulated fit that is the outlier, and the tabulated fit is what the code
was using.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The $0.9\!:\!0.1$ branching.} Equation~(\ref{eq:gm25}) is
the \emph{total} ionization rate: Penning,
He($2\,^3S$)\,$+$\,H\,$\rightarrow$\,He($1\,^1S$)\,$+$\,H$^+ + e^-$, and the
associative channel that leaves HeH$^+$, sharing one cross section.
Garc\'ia Mu\~noz splits them ``an average $0.9\!:\!0.1$'', and the
machine-readable network file deposited with that paper
(\texttt{references/garcia\_munoz\_2025\_network/}, Zenodo 15453858)
applies exactly that split through two amplitudes per channel. EXHALE now
does the same, and the distinction is not a scale factor: the total removes
the metastable, so every sink keeps the sum (using $0.9$ there would make
the observable, $n(2\,^3S)$, $10$ per cent too high), while the $0.9$
scales only the terms that leave a lasting proton behind and deposit the
Penning exothermicity, and the remaining $0.1$ becomes an HeH$^+$ source in
the molecular system.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What it does to this memo.} The new coefficient is lower
than the old one everywhere in this wind (new/old $0.69$ at $1000$\,K,
$0.86$ just below the seam, $0.54$ just above, $0.81$ at $10^{4}$\,K), so
the metastable level lives longer and the line is stronger at fixed
composition: re-solved, \texttt{heh0p55} goes from $EW = 1.109$ to
$1.310$\,\%\,\AA{} and \texttt{heh2p13\_diff\_kzz1e9} from $1.129$ to
$1.338$, with $\log_{10}\dot M$ unchanged in both. Every crossing therefore
moves down, and the composition ladders were re-solved rather than scaled
by a local slope, because a $+18$ per cent shift is well outside the range
over which such a slope is valid. The step detector of
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/metastable\_step\_diagnosis} flags no cell at the
seam in any of the runs quoted here.

\section{Reproducing the paper's p-winds model}

Run at the authors' settings: their SED normalization, $R_p = 0.154\,R_J$,
$b = 0.23$, \texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True}, five-phase transit
averaging, and their fitted line-of-sight bulk velocity
$v_{\rm wind} = 2.26$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and at the MCMC medians
($\dot M = 2.03\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $T = 5160$\,K,
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 1.01\times10^{-3}$), the vector their Fig.~4 legend
states for the plotted curve, our reproduction gives a red depth of
$1.376\%$ and FWHM $0.575$\,\AA{} against the $1.32\%$ and
$\sim 0.71$\,\AA{} read off their Fig.~4: a $3\%$ match in depth, but a
line about $20\%$ too narrow.

The width residual is not a consequence of which point in parameter space is
drawn. Running the same reproduction at the grid maximum-likelihood point
($1.83\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, $5131.6$\,K, $8.338\times10^{-4}$) gives
red $1.366\%$ and FWHM $0.581$\,\AA{}, i.e.\ the two vectors differ by less
than $0.01$\,\AA{} in width. What produces the extra width in the published
curve has not been identified.

Two points are worth recording because both cost time to find.

First, the curves drawn over the spectrum in the paper's Fig.~3 are the
\emph{three-Gaussian empirical fits}, not p-winds; the physical model is
overlaid only in Fig.~4. Fig.~3's apparently perfect agreement is a fit to
the data, and says nothing about the model's line shape.

Second, the paper's plotted model is visibly broader than a plain p-winds
run, which we initially modelled as an undocumented instrumental kernel. It
is not: their script sets \texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True} and averages
over five transit phases. Both widen the line, and together they account for
the difference.

\section{Turbulent broadening: what the literature does}

Since that switch turned out to matter, all 43 papers under
\texttt{references/} were checked: every PDF converted to text, searched for
\texttt{turbulen}, \texttt{microturbulen} and \texttt{non-thermal
broadening}, and each hit read in context. Fifteen matched; most use
``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$ or for flow structure in 3D
simulations, not for line broadening. Section~\ref{sec:lit} tabulates the
ones that bear on broadening.

The convention, stated most explicitly by Yan et al.\ (2024), replaces the
thermal Doppler width by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu_D = \frac{\nu_0}{c}\left(\frac{2kT}{m} + v_{\rm turb}^2\right)^{1/2},
  \qquad v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{\frac{5kT}{3m}} = c_s ,
\end{equation}
the monatomic sound speed, as assumed by Salz et al.\ (2018), Lamp\'on et
al.\ (2020) and Czesla et al.\ (2022). The p-winds implementation uses
$\sqrt{5kT/6m}$ in a $\sigma$-convention, which is the same physics
expressed against a Doppler width that lacks the factor $2$; in EXHALE's
$b$-parameter convention the same term is $b \to \sqrt{11/6}\,v_{\rm th}$,
a factor $1.354$. That is what \texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_TURB=1} applies. It
is opt-in and off by default, because the field does not agree that it
belongs.

\subsection{Paper by paper}
\label{sec:lit}

\paragraph{Adopt it as an assumed value.}
\textbf{Salz et al.\ (2016)}, Eq.~(5): ``$b$ is the Doppler parameter,
$v_{\rm th}$ the thermal Doppler velocity, and for the microturbulence
$v_{\rm micro}$ we use the sound speed'', applied when synthesizing the
transit profile. (Their atmosphere solver separately carries a constant
$1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ microturbulence at the lower boundary, a different
quantity, which they note ``has little impact on the overall atmospheric
structure''.) \textbf{Lamp\'on et al.\ (2020, 2021)} is the reference
implementation, with turbulent broadening as a free or assumed parameter of
1D isothermal Parker fits; \textbf{Czesla et al.\ (2022)} follows it.
\textbf{Cherubim et al.\ (2026)} inherits the choice through p-winds'
\texttt{turbulence\_broadening=True}, so the LHS\,1140\,b retrieval carries
it: a fact stated nowhere in the paper text.

\paragraph{Test it, then leave it out.}
\textbf{Yan et al.\ (2024)} give the cleanest statement of the convention
and of the doubt. They record that ``these authors assumed that the
turbulence velocity $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$, which corresponds to the
speed of sound of a monatomic gas and, thus, to the transonic case with a
Mach number of 1'', then keep it out of their nominal models and test
$v_{\rm turb} = 0.5$, $1.0$ and $1.5\,c_s$ as a sensitivity. Their finding
for He\,10830 is that turbulence ``decreases the absorption depth slightly
at the line center, but doesn't significantly alter the absorption at the
line wings''. They argue Mach~1 is likely an overestimate, since in the
diffuse ISM turbulent velocity anti-correlates with temperature and the Mach
number is below unity at a few thousand K, and conclude ``the turbulence
effect is likely less significant than the one explored in this paper''.
\textbf{Yan et al.\ (2019)} computes the Doppler width explicitly
``assuming no turbulence''.

\paragraph{Argue against it.}
\textbf{Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009)}, on Garc\'ia Mu\~noz's (2007) proposal
to broaden Ly$\alpha$ by wind turbulence: ``to generate the large velocities
observed, the energy in such turbulence would need to exceed the thermal and
bulk kinetic energies in the mean flow by a factor of $\sim 100$. Such
energy requirements seem insurmountable.'' \textbf{Koskinen et al.\ (2013b)}
find that ``non-thermal broadening such as that proposed by Ben-Jaffel and
Hosseini (2010) does not appear to be necessary to explain the current
observations'', while allowing that stellar-wind interaction may produce
broadening turbulence. \textbf{Schulik \& Owen (2025)} offer a physical
alternative (adiabatic cooling produces a metastable-helium population
bump at high velocity, widening the line with no free parameter), and are
pointed about the alternative: the isothermal model ``can only explain the
broadness of the data under physically inconsistent assumptions, i.e.\ those
of even larger mass-loss rates, not supported by the energy input into this
planet, and an empirical free parameter, turbulent broadening (Lamp\'on et
al.\ 2021)''. They add that $T = 10^{4}$\,K already maximizes what thermal
broadening alone can give.

\paragraph{Need it even so.}
\textbf{Taylor et al.\ (2026)} is the closest analogue to the present work:
a self-consistent model (energy balance, multispecies diffusion, non-LTE
H($n=2$)) that reproduces the HD\,189733\,b line core but not its width.
They write that ``several prior analyses have matched the HD\,189733\,b width
by introducing ad hoc (micro)turbulent or Gaussian broadening terms; here, we
show that this step remains necessary even in our self-consistent
framework'', and quantify it as $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of additional
non-thermal broadening. They speculate (explicitly calling it speculative)
that it is plasma turbulence from ions trapped on low-latitude planetary
field lines, strengthening with stellar activity.
\textbf{Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022)}, as reported there, instead imposed
reduced hydrogen line cooling and prescribed zonal jets of
$10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in a 3D multifluid model.

\paragraph{Matches that are not about broadening.}
Recorded so the sweep need not be repeated: Wogan (2025), Lavvas (2014,
2019), Robeling (2026) and the second half of Koskinen et al.\ (2013b) use
``turbulent'' for eddy diffusion $K_{zz}$; \texttt{2607.18193v1} for
flow--flow interaction in a 3D stellar-wind simulation; Jensen (2012) for
photospheric turbulence driving stellar activity; Taylor et al.\ (2025) for
gravity-wave breaking. \texttt{Vidal\_2003\_Nature.pdf} matches only on its
first page, which carries the tail of the preceding \textit{Nature} letter
(on Crab pulsar radio emission); Vidal-Madjar et al.\ themselves do not
discuss turbulence.

\subsection{Empirical baseline: turbulence in Solar System giant planets}
\label{sec:solarsys_turb}

To evaluate whether sonic or supersonic ($v_{\rm turb} \sim c_s$) turbulence
is physically plausible in a planetary atmosphere, it is instructive to compare
with directly measured turbulence and non-thermal velocity fields across the
stratified layers of Jupiter and Saturn:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Troposphere and cloud deck ($\sim 0.1$--$1$\,bar).}
      Cloud-tracking measurements from \textit{Voyager}, \textit{Galileo},
      \textit{Cassini}, and \textit{Juno} (e.g., Salyk et al.\ 2006; Del
      Genio et al.\ 2007; Ingersoll et al.) isolate the fluctuating eddy
      velocities $v' = \sqrt{\overline{u'^2 + v'^2}}$ from the mean zonal
      jets ($100$--$500$\,m\,s$^{-1}$). The resulting RMS turbulent eddy
      velocity is $v' \approx 3$--$10$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Jupiter (locally
      $20$--$30$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the Great Red Spot shear zone) and
      $5$--$15$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ on Saturn. Compared to the tropospheric sound
      speed ($c_s \approx 800$--$900$\,m\,s$^{-1}$), this is deeply subsonic
      turbulence, with Mach numbers $\mathcal{M} \sim 0.005$--$0.02$.
\item \emph{Stratosphere ($\sim 10\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$--$10\,\mathrm{mbar}$).}
      High-resolution submillimeter spectroscopy of impact-delivered HCN/CO
      molecules with ALMA (e.g., Cavali\'e et al.\ 2021) and Herschel reveals
      stratospheric jet velocities up to $300$--$400$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ in the
      auroral zone, but the associated non-thermal velocity dispersion
      (turbulence and wave motions) remains limited to tens of m\,s$^{-1}$
      up to $\sim 100$--$200$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ ($\mathcal{M} \lesssim 0.2$).
\item \emph{Thermosphere and auroral ionosphere ($\lesssim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$).}
      Ground-based high-resolution ($R \sim 10^5$) infrared spectroscopy of
      $\mathrm{H}_3^+$ line profiles (e.g., with VLT/CRIRES, Keck, and CFHT)
      disentangles thermal Doppler broadening from non-thermal velocity
      fields. In non-auroral mid-latitudes, the non-thermal velocity is
      $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.2$--$0.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. In the auroral oval,
      where intense magnetosphere--ionosphere coupling, ion drag, and Joule
      heating inject substantial kinetic energy, non-thermal broadening
      reaches $v_{\rm nth} \approx 0.8$--$2.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent These empirical benchmarks show that pure hydrodynamic planetary
turbulence is strictly subsonic ($\mathcal{M} \ll 1$), and even violent
magnetospheric auroral driving in upper thermospheres caps non-thermal
broadening at $\lesssim 2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Turbulence and 3D hydrodynamic simulations of exoplanets}
\label{sec:3d_turb}

Beyond 1D parameterizations, 3D numerical simulations explore how turbulent
mixing, non-thermal velocity structures, and line broadening emerge across
diverse exoplanetary regimes:

\paragraph{1. 3D hot Jupiter escape and circulation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{3D atmospheric escape and kinematic line broadening.}
      Hydrodynamic 3D escape simulations (e.g., McCann et al.\ 2019;
      Debrecht et al.\ 2020; Carolan et al.\ 2020, 2021; Wang \& Dai 2021;
      MacLeod \& Oklop\v{c}i\'c 2022) demonstrate that apparent transmission
      line broadening arises primarily from large-scale 3D kinematic velocity
      gradients ($10$--$50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) across the day-to-night outflow,
      Coriolis-deflected tails, and tidal distortion, rather than unresolved
      isotropic microscale turbulence. Furthermore, the collision between the
      expanding planetary wind and the incoming stellar wind creates a shear
      layer prone to Kelvin--Helmholtz instabilities and turbulent mixing at
      the contact discontinuity. In 3D multifluid models of HD\,189733\,b,
      Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022, 2023) show that upper-atmospheric zonal jets
      of $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ combined with 3D outflow geometry reproduce
      the observed broad transmission profiles without invoking ad hoc
      microturbulence.
\item \emph{3D general circulation models (GCMs) and macroturbulent mixing.}
      In the observable lower-to-middle atmosphere ($1\,\mathrm{bar}$ to
      $\sim 1\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$), 3D GCMs (e.g., Showman \& Polvani 2011;
      Showman et al.\ 2013, 2015; Parmentier et al.\ 2013; Charnay et al.\
      2015; Zhang \& Showman 2018) demonstrate that equatorial superrotation
      ($1$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) and vertical mixing ($K_{zz} \sim
      10^7$--$10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$) are driven by planetary-scale
      macroturbulence (non-linear interactions between planetary-scale Kelvin
      and Rossby waves and the mean flow) and overturning circulation. The
      sub-grid parameterization of small-scale turbulent dissipation
      (hyperdiffusion, Smagorinsky closures, or Shapiro filters) remains a
      known source of numerical sensitivity in GCM wind speeds and jet
      stability (Heng et al.\ 2011; Polichtchouk et al.\ 2014; Skinner \& Cho
      2021).
\item \emph{Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in ultra-hot atmospheres.}
      For planets with $T \gtrsim 1500$\,K, thermal ionization of alkali
      metals (Na, K) couples the gas to the planetary magnetic field. 3D MHD
      simulations (e.g., Perna et al.\ 2010, 2012; Rauscher \& Menou 2013;
      Rogers \& Showman 2014; Rogers \& Komacek 2014; Beltz et al.\ 2022)
      show that magnetic winding of the field by supersonic zonal winds
      generates Lorentz forces (magnetic drag) that brake the flow, generating
      3D MHD turbulence, Ohmic dissipation, and time-dependent variability in
      wind patterns and hotspot offsets.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. 3D simulations across other planetary classes.}
The reach of 3D fluid simulations extends far beyond hot Jupiters to other
major categories of exoplanets and substellar objects:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Warm Neptunes and sub-Neptunes.}
      For warm Neptunes such as GJ\,436\,b and GJ\,3470\,b, 3D particle-mesh
      and hydrodynamic escape simulations (Bourrier et al.\ 2015, 2016;
      Villarreal D'Angelo et al.\ 2021; Carolan et al.\ 2021) reveal gigantic,
      asymmetric cometary hydrogen tails spanning tens of planetary radii,
      directly explaining the $56\%$ Ly$\alpha$ transit obscuration and the
      He\,10830\,\AA{} absorption through 3D stellar radiation pressure and
      charge-exchange interactions. In metal- and water-rich sub-Neptune
      atmospheres (e.g., GJ\,1214\,b, K2-18\,b), 3D GCMs model cloud/haze
      circulation, photochemical transport, and vertical eddy mixing
      ($K_{zz} \propto P^{-0.4}$; Charnay et al.\ 2015; Innes \&
      Pierrehumbert 2022).
\item \emph{M-dwarf habitable zone terrestrial planets.}
      Tidally locked rocky worlds (such as TRAPPIST-1\,e/f/g, Proxima\,b, and
      LHS\,1140\,b) have been extensively modelled with 3D climate GCMs
      (Turbet et al.\ 2016, 2018; Wolf et al.\ 2017; Sergeev et al.\ 2020,
      2022; the THAI intercomparison, Fauchez et al.\ 2020, 2022). These
      simulations demonstrate how thick dayside convective cloud decks
      elevate planetary albedo to prevent runaway greenhouse states, while
      stationary Rossby--Kelvin wave patterns redistribute heat to the nightside
      to avoid atmospheric freeze-out. Complementary 3D multi-species MHD
      simulations (Dong et al.\ 2017, 2018; Cohen et al.\ 2014) assess $\mathrm{O}^+$
      and $\mathrm{H}^+$ ion escape driven by extreme stellar coronal mass
      ejections and flaring winds.
\item \emph{Lava worlds and ultra-short-period rocky planets.}
      For scorched rocky planets with dayside magma oceans (e.g., 55\,Cancri\,e,
      K2-141\,b, CoRoT-7\,b), 3D hydrodynamic models of mineral-vapor
      atmospheres (Castan \& Menou 2011; Nguyen et al.\ 2020; Zilinskas et
      al.\ 2022; Kang et al.\ 2021) reveal supersonic dayside-to-nightside
      winds ($\mathcal{M} > 1$--$3$, $v \sim 1$--$3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), standing
      hydraulic jumps/shocks, and mineral vapor condensation leading to rock
      rain on the nightside.
\item \emph{Brown dwarfs and directly imaged giant planets.}
      In isolated brown dwarfs and young, directly imaged giants (e.g.,
      HR\,8799\,b/c/d/e, VHS\,1256\,b), 3D convective-radiative GCMs (Tan \&
      Showman 2019, 2021a,b; Showman \& Kaspi 2013; Zhang \& Showman 2014)
      couple rapid planetary rotation (strong Coriolis forces) with deep
      geothermal convection and silicate/iron cloud radiative feedbacks. The
      resulting turbulent vortices, patchy cloud bands, and storm dynamics
      provide the physical explanation for observed rotational photometric
      variability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where that leaves this comparison}
\label{sec:turb_conclusion}

There is no settled treatment. Turbulence at $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ is a
common convention in 1D isothermal Parker-wind fits, and hence in the
paper being reproduced, while Solar System observations and self-consistent
or 3D models demonstrate that isotropic microscale turbulence cannot physically
reach the required supersonic levels. 3D models instead supply line width
through kinematic flow gradients, zonal jets, and magnetospheric/plasma
interactions. Enabling the $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ term in EXHALE is therefore
right for a like-for-like comparison with C26 and wrong as a physical default,
which is how it is implemented.

\subsection{Density and ionization, the same pair}
\label{sec:rhoion}

Figure~\ref{fig:structure} draws three of the five profiles the comparison
was set up to make; the other two, mass density and hydrogen ionization, are
in Fig.~\ref{fig:structrhoion}, from the same pair of solutions.

The densities are the part that agrees. Outside the base the ratio
EXHALE\,/\,p-winds runs from $0.15$ at $1.2\,R_p$ through unity near
$2\,R_p$ to $2.7$ at $5\,R_p$, and stays inside a factor $2.3$ from
$3\,R_p$ out. At the base cell itself EXHALE sits $176\times$ higher, which
is the two lower boundary conditions and not a profile difference: p-winds
sets its base density from the imposed $\dot M$ through the Parker solution,
EXHALE from its own base state.

The ionization does not agree at all. EXHALE has hydrogen completely
ionized above $1.03\,R_p$ ($n({\rm H\,I})$ is an exact zero in the
output), while p-winds keeps it $91$--$94\%$ neutral throughout,
$x({\rm H\,II})$ rising only from $0.056$ at $1.2\,R_p$ to $0.086$ at
$20\,R_p$. Neither end of that is the other's error, and both are worth
stating.

p-winds' hydrogen is frozen far below its own photoionization equilibrium.
Integrating the same SED against the Verner et al.\ (1996) ground-state
cross section gives an unattenuated rate
$\phi = 5.17\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$ (p-winds' internal value for this file
agrees to $0.1\%$), an ionization time of $224$\,d, while the flow time
$r/v$ through the imposed $\dot M = 2.03\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ falls to
$2.6\times10^{5}$\,s at $2\,R_p$ and $2.6\times10^{4}$\,s at $10\,R_p$.
Equilibrium on p-winds' own density and temperature would be
$x({\rm H\,II}) = 0.76$ at $2\,R_p$ and $0.997$ at $5\,R_p$ (dotted in
Fig.~\ref{fig:structrhoion}b); the wind carries the gas out before it gets
there. Shielding is not what holds it down: hydrogen is a trace species
here and the neutral-H column above $1.2\,R_p$ is optically thin at every
energy.

EXHALE's hydrogen goes the other way: it is more ionized than any
photoionization balance on its own structure allows. With the same $\phi$,
its own electron density at $3\,R_p$ ($2.0\times10^{6}$\,cm$^{-3}$, set by
helium) and its own temperature there ($5247$\,K), primary photoionization
alone gives $x({\rm H\,II}) = 0.059$: close to what p-winds carries, and
nowhere near $1$. An exact zero can only come from a hydrogen ionization
source that does not vanish with $n({\rm H\,I})$, and the code has one: the
Shull \& van Steenberg (1985) secondary channel, added in
\texttt{util\_ion\_eq.f90} as
$P_{\rm HI} \rightarrow P_{\rm HI} + R_{\rm sec,HI}/n({\rm H\,I})$, where
$R_{\rm sec,HI}$ is accumulated over \emph{every} absorber's photoelectrons.
In a helium-dominated gas that numerator is helium's. Evaluating it at
$3\,R_p$ on this solution's own radiation field (the He\,\textsc{i}
photoelectrons above the code's $40$\,eV secondary threshold, with
$f_{\rm ion,HI} = 0.091$ at the local ionized fraction $x = 0.246$) gives
$R_{\rm sec,HI} = 4.9\times10^{-2}$\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$ against a hydrogen
recombination rate of $6.6\times10^{-3}$, a factor $7.5$. Complete
ionization follows.

That is an approximation used outside the composition it was made at. The
SvS85 fits are functions of the ionized fraction alone; the helium abundance
of the gas their Monte Carlo followed is fixed inside them and is not the
$\mathrm{He/H} = 10^{3}$ of this run, and their two branches hand hydrogen
$7\times$ helium's share of the secondary ionizations
($f_{\rm ion,HI}/f_{\rm ion,HeI} = 0.3908/0.0554$ at $x \to 0$) where the
abundances say the opposite by three orders of magnitude. It is recorded
here rather than changed, because the reach was measured and is bounded:
$n({\rm H\,I})$ reaches an exact zero only at $\mathrm{He/H} \ge 10$ over a
prescribed metal-free base (\texttt{exhale/heh10} above $6.6\,R_p$,
\texttt{heh100} above $1.06$, \texttt{heh1000} above $1.03$), and in none of
the solutions this memo quotes science from, \texttt{heh0p55},
\texttt{heh2p13\_diff\_kzz1e9}, and every rung of the flux-closed ladder out
to $\mathrm{He/H} = 12$, where $x({\rm H\,II})$ at $2\,R_p$ runs $0.060$ to
$0.178$ and no cell is fully ionized. The $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 10^{-3}$
comparison of this section is the one place in the memo that sits inside it,
and the quantity it distorts is hydrogen, not the metastable helium the line
is made of.

\section{What can be matched: the equivalent width}
\label{sec:ew}

Depth and width trade against each other under any broadening, but the
equivalent width does not: it is invariant under the instrument profile, the
turbulence term and the phase averaging. It is therefore the quantity on
which a self-consistent model can be matched without first settling the
broadening question.

Over the blended red pair (vacuum $10832.6$--$10834.2$\,\AA), the released
spectrum gives $EW = 1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA. EXHALE crosses it at

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132 \quad (0.401\text{--}0.429 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 2.4 ,
\end{equation}

that is $5.0\times$ solar, a helium mass fraction of $0.62$. The crossing is
read inside a bracket of two solved arms, $0.410$ at $EW = 1.0982$ and
$0.415$ at $1.1133$\,\%\,\AA{} against the observed $1.1084 \pm 0.0295$,
and a chord and a quadratic through the ladder agree on it to $0.07$ per
cent ($0.4132$ against $0.4135$). A run at $0.4132$ itself gives
$EW = 1.1072$\,\%\,\AA, $0.03\sigma$ from the measurement, on the lineage
the crossing was read on
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}); re-seeded from the nearest
neighbouring arm the same composition gives $1.1161$, still inside the band
and about $1$ per cent away in He/H, which is the path spread on this axis.
Two lineages were solved, the second re-seeded arm by arm from the stored
solutions of the same compositions; the seed moves the crossing from
$0.4132$ to $0.4154$, $0.53$ per cent, which is the size of the path spread
on this axis. Enabling the turbulence term was not measured on this ladder
(its transit synthesis was run in its nominal configuration only), but
on the wider scan of Fig.~\ref{fig:ew}, re-solved and synthesized both ways
on the same binary, it moves the crossing by $1.7$ per cent, a direct
demonstration that
the estimate does not depend on the broadening treatment. The value
sits three orders of magnitude away from the paper's
$\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$, and the reason is the structural
difference of the previous section: a wind that spreads metastable helium
over most of the stellar disk needs far less helium to absorb as much.

Two assumptions are carried inside that number and are taken up later. It
is the well-mixed crossing, and it is the crossing on the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum. Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} rescans it with element diffusion
active at every decade of the eddy coefficient, and Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}
rescans it on the brighter proxy; both move it, the spectrum by much the
larger factor.

\section{What cannot be matched: the profile}
\label{sec:width}

Matching the equivalent width is not matching the line. At the crossing
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$ the profile is $3.3$ times too deep ($4.12\%$
against $1.254\%$) and $3.3$ times too narrow ($0.253$ against
$0.841$\,\AA). The
equivalent width states how much absorbing power the model has; the profile
states how that power is spread in velocity, and composition does not act on
the second. Every solved composition gives $0.23$--$0.32$\,\AA{}, rising
only to $0.26$--$0.39$\,\AA{} with the turbulence term.

\subsection{How much is missing, in velocity}

The measured FWHM is $0.841$\,\AA, that is $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
$10833$\,\AA. Three ways to supply it, and what each would require:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Thermal alone.} A Doppler width of $23.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM in
      helium needs $T = 4.7\times10^{4}$\,K. Helium is fully ionized far
      below that, so there would be no metastable population left to
      absorb. This is excluded.
\item \emph{Bulk outflow.} A line-of-sight velocity spread of order
      $\pm 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across the line-forming region would do it.
      The EW-matched EXHALE solution reaches $1.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
      $20\,R_p$; the paper's p-winds model, with its imposed temperature,
      reaches $4$--$7$. Both are an order of magnitude short.
\item \emph{Non-thermal (turbulent) broadening.} Added in quadrature to
      what EXHALE already has, closing the gap takes
      $22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM: $9.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of turbulent
      velocity ($\sigma$), against the $2.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ that the
      sound-speed convention of Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies for
      helium at $5000$\,K. This is the one of the three that can be measured
      directly, and Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} measures it.
\end{itemize}

\noindent So the model is not short by a detail. It is short by an amount
of velocity comparable to the escape speed, and no choice of composition
supplies it.

\subsection{The broadening the data demand, measured}
\label{sec:broadening}

The three routes above can be replaced by one measurement. Take the
EW-matched EXHALE profile as solved, convolve it with a Gaussian of FWHM
$f$ in velocity, and ask which $f$ reproduces the measured line. The
convolution is applied to the excess absorption
$(\max T - T)/\max T$ built from the instrument-convolved column of
\texttt{tpm\_He10830.txt}; depth, width and the red/blue ratio are then read
off with the same three-Gaussian extractor applied to the data, and the
equivalent width by the same integral over the vacuum window
$10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA. So $f$ is the only quantity varied, and every
number in Table~\ref{tab:broadening} is measured the way the corresponding
observed number was.

The width is reached at $f = 22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM, that is
$\sigma = 9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. At that one value the depth is $1.236\%$
against the measured $1.24^{+0.22}_{-0.23}$, the blue component $0.163\%$
against $0.25^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$, and the ratio $7.59$ against
$6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$: depth, blue depth and ratio all land inside the
observed $1\sigma$ interval at the value fixed by the width alone. Nothing
was tuned to them. Figure~\ref{fig:broadened} shows the profile and the two
crossings.

The number does not depend on which solution is broadened. The neighbouring
arm of the same ladder, $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.42$, asks for
$22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the $K_{zz} = 0$ arm at $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.79$
for $22.24$; the $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ run with the turbulence term
already on asks for $21.76$ and solar composition for $22.40$, although the
solar run is a third of the observed absorption ($0.423\%$ red, EW
$0.379$) and so matches the shape while missing the amount. All four arms
are on the current physics: the first two are the ladder runs of
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}, the last two were re-solved for
this comparison in
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/misc/}. The spread over the four
is $2.9$ per cent, and it is that spread, not an assumption, that makes
the point.
The required broadening is therefore set by the velocity field, not by
composition or by the broadening treatment already in place: the direct
measurement of what Sect.~\ref{sec:width} inferred. The equivalent width
falls by $3\%$ between $f = 0$ and $f = 22.2$ because the Gaussian wings
carry absorption out of the red-pair integration window, not because
absorbing power is lost.

This is descriptive, not explanatory. It states that one free parameter,
applied to a solved profile, reproduces the measured line; it does not say
what produces $22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The candidates are the ones listed
below, and the measurement sharpens them by fixing the number each has to
deliver.

One conversion narrows the list before it is read. If the missing
$22.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is called turbulence, it has to be turbulence in gas
whose sound speed the solution itself fixes. Evaluating
$c_s = (\gamma kT/\mu m_H)^{1/2}$ with $\gamma = 5/3$ on the
well-mixed \texttt{heh0p55} output, taking $\mu$ from the solved
H\,\textsc{i}/H\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{i}/He\,\textsc{ii}/He\,\textsc{iii}
and electron densities, gives $c_s = 5.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $2\,R_p$
($T = 4311$\,K, $\mu = 1.94$), $3.8$ at the metastable peak $5.4\,R_p$
($1609$\,K, $1.57$) and $3.0$ at $10\,R_p$ ($845$\,K, $1.32$); weighted over
the line-forming region by $n(2\,^3S)\,r$, $T = 2022$\,K, $\mu = 1.59$ and
$c_s = 3.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. Note this is the bulk-gas sound speed, the
quantity a Mach number is defined against, and not the helium thermal speed
that enters the line profile.

Against that, the kernel is supersonic on any convention. Read as a
line-of-sight velocity dispersion, $\sigma = 9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is
$\mathcal{M} = 2.4$. Read in the $b$-parameter convention of Lamp\'on et
al.\ and p-winds, where $v_{\rm turb}$ is the quantity added to
$b^2 = 2kT/m + v_{\rm turb}^2$, it is $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma =
13.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\mathcal{M} = 3.4$; as an isotropic three-dimensional
turbulent speed, $\sqrt{3}\,\sigma = 16.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
$\mathcal{M} = 4.2$. The $v_{\rm turb} = c_s$ convention of
Sect.~\ref{sec:lit} supplies only $3$--$4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in this gas, about
a third of what the data demand. That is the opposite corner of the
literature reviewed there: Yan et al.\ (2024) argue $\mathcal{M} = 1$ is
already an overestimate at a few thousand K, and Murray-Clay et al.\ (2009)
call the energy requirement of supersonic turbulence ``insurmountable''.
Supplying the shortfall under the name of turbulence therefore looks
physically hard to sustain, and the measurement points to the candidates of
the next section (an ordered velocity field, three-dimensional flow, or
heating the 1D energy equation does not carry) rather than to a missing
thermal width.

\subsection{Is the demand an artifact of the measurement?}
\label{sec:widthaudit}

The number above is a difference between two widths, so it is worth no more
than the two definitions behind it. Before the missing velocity is treated
as physics, the observing and fitting side was audited on its own; the
record, with every number recomputed there from the released spectrum, the
released MCMC chain and the code in this repository, is
\texttt{docs/lhs1140b\_width\_measurement\_audit.md}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Nothing on the measurement side supplies the width.} Term
by term, in the units the requirement is stated in:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The instrument profile.} The WINERED HIRES-Y kernel the
      Supplement states, and validates against nearby telluric features, is
      $10833\,\text{\AA}/68{,}000 = 0.1593$\,\AA{} $= 4.41$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
      FWHM. The observed line is $5.4$ times wider, so it is resolved, and
      removing the kernel in quadrature moves the intrinsic width from
      $23.9$ to $23.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\item \emph{The triplet splitting.} The $0.0897$\,\AA{} separation of the
      red pair is present on both sides of the comparison. It is $0.7$ per
      cent of the observed blend width but $8$ per cent of the model's,
      because at $\sigma = 0.35$\,\AA{} the pair is a single hump; the
      asymmetry nearly cancels, moving the requirement by less than
      $0.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and in the direction that makes the gap larger,
      since the extractor already credits the model with width it does not
      get from gas motion.
\item \emph{Stellar rotation.} With $P_{\rm rot} = 131$\,d and
      $R_\star = 0.216\,R_\odot$, $v_{\rm eq} = 0.083$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the
      rotational broadening is $0.14$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The
      Rossiter--McLaughlin distortion and the center-to-limb residual scale
      with the same $v\sin i$ and are correspondingly irrelevant; neither is
      mentioned anywhere in the paper or its Supplement, and on this star
      there is nothing to mention.
\item \emph{Planetary rotation.} Tidally locked: $0.032$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at
      $R_p$ and $0.097$ at $3\,R_p$. It is already inside the model curves,
      which carry the solid-body spin.
\item \emph{Orbital motion.} $0.93$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across the full transit if
      left uncorrected, but the spectra are shifted into the planetary rest
      frame before averaging, which leaves the $0.022$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the
      ephemeris uncertainty; a $300$\,s exposure smears
      $0.037$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Together these come to at most about $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and most
of that is removed as soon as each side is deconvolved from its own kernel.
Done that way (component width against component width, both intrinsic)
the requirement is $22.00$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM ($\sigma =
9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), against the $22.22$ the convolution scan above returns
without deconvolving either side. The audit moves the number by
$0.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, not by a factor. (The audit itself was run on the
earlier crossing; what is carried here is the differential it measured,
$-0.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, applied to the present scan.)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{One defect was on our side, and is corrected here.} The
transit synthesis convolves the He\,10830 line with a Gaussian of FWHM
$\lambda/R$, and until this revision every LHS\,1140\,b run in this memo
took $R$ from the built-in default of \texttt{EXHALE\_transit.py}, which is
the CARMENES $8\times10^{4}$, where this observation was made at WINERED's
$68{,}000$. Every model curve quoted here has been re-synthesized at the
WINERED value; the runs now read it from
\texttt{LHS1140b/winered\_hires\_y.sh}, and the built-in default is left
alone because the other planets in this repository were observed through
other instruments. The correction widens the modelled blend by about
$0.013$\,\AA{} (at $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$, $0.244 \rightarrow
0.257$\,\AA{}), lowers the modelled peak depth by about $5$ per cent, and
leaves every equivalent width in this memo unchanged to
$10^{-4}$\,\%\,\AA, because a convolution conserves absorbing power. So it
moves no crossing and no composition, only the widths and the broadening
they demand, the latter by $0.12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The p-winds curves were
already computed at $R = 68{,}000$, so the comparison of
Sect.~\ref{sec:pwbroad} is now through one instrument on both sides.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The paper's own model does not reach the width either, and
that changes what the shortfall means.} Reproducing the authors' p-winds
setting at their retrieved medians and measuring it with the same extractor
gives FWHM $0.458$\,\AA{} with turbulence off and $0.575$\,\AA{} with it on,
against the measured $0.841$. The retrieval that produced the published
parameters therefore lands $1.5$ times short of the width printed in the
same paper. Taken with the accounting above, the reading this supports is
that \emph{the observation is not described by a one-dimensional
steady-state wind of either code}, rather than that the wind solved here
carries a defect the comparison model does not. That is an interpretation,
not a measurement; what is measured is that both models are too narrow and
that the measurement side does not account for the difference.

A second difference runs the same way. The p-winds curve reaches part of its
width through saturation: its red-to-blue amplitude ratio is $2.36$ with
turbulence off and $2.57$ with it on, against the optically thin limit of
$8$, whereas the measurement sits at $6.35$ and the solutions here at
$6.9$--$7.0$. The premise that this line is optically thin, so that column
density cannot be traded against width, holds for the data and for these
solutions but not for the comparison model.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Two remarks on the published width, offered as
observations rather than conclusions.} First, the three widths the
Supplement's stellar-variability test reports as FWHM
($0.22176$\,\AA{} for the stellar helium line, $0.323$ for the in-transit
feature and $0.392$ for the pre-ingress feature) appear to be Gaussian
standard deviations rather than full widths. Fitting the same
three-Gaussian model to the same pre-GP spectrum with our extractor returns
$\sigma = 0.3241$\,\AA{} against their $0.323$, a $0.3$ per cent match,
where the corresponding FWHM is $0.763$\,\AA{} and matches nothing on that
list. The $\sim 9\sigma$ separation the paragraph concludes with is a
difference between two widths and is unaffected; the absolute values are.
Second, the asymmetric uncertainties on the headline FWHM appear to be
transposed. The deposited MCMC chain (identified by its shift column
reproducing the published $0.072^{+0.080}_{-0.073}$\,\AA{} exactly) gives
$2.3548\,\sigma = 0.865^{+0.261}_{-0.155}$\,\AA{}
($23.93^{+7.24}_{-4.30}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), where the paper prints
$0.86^{+0.15}_{-0.27}$\,\AA{} and $23.9^{+4.2}_{-7.5}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$: the
magnitudes agree once the two are swapped. Neither remark can be settled
from outside the collaboration, since the printed bars may come from a chain
other than the deposited one, and neither changes the comparison except in
one direction: on the deposited posterior the $1\sigma$ lower bound on
the measured width is $0.709$\,\AA{} rather than $0.59$, so narrow models
are excluded somewhat more firmly than the printed interval suggests.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the audit could not exclude.} The star's own
metastable helium line is broad: taking the Supplement's $0.22176$\,\AA{} as
a standard deviation, it is $0.522$\,\AA{} $= 14.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM as
observed and $13.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with the instrument kernel removed. An
imperfectly divided stellar line would therefore inject a residual much
wider than the modelled planetary line, though still short of the observed
feature on its own. The released data do not include the stellar template,
so a mixture of a narrow planetary line with a displaced stellar residual
could not be tested here and is not excluded. Whether the published $\sigma$
is itself deconvolved from the instrument profile was also left unsettled;
it is worth $1.7$ per cent in width either way.

\subsection{The same demand, under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$}
\label{sec:diffbroad}

Everything above was solved in the well-mixed limit. With binary H/He
element diffusion active, helium settles out of the base unless an eddy term
lifts it, and how much of it reaches the line-forming region is set by the
eddy coefficient $K_{zz}$, which is not measured for this system. The
diffusion operator is described in \texttt{binary\_diffusion\_design.md};
the literature range at the $1\,\mu$bar base of these runs, the ladder of
solved cases, and the adoption of
$K_{zz} = 1.0\times10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ are in
\texttt{LHS1140b/kzz\_decision.md}.

\subsubsection*{The composition, and how much of it $K_{zz}$ carries}

Turning the operator on moves the equivalent-width crossing of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ew} away from $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$, and by how much depends
on $K_{zz}$. The composition was therefore rescanned at every decade of the
eddy coefficient from $K_{zz} = 0$ to $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, by the
same route in every case: the same control input, the same direct-steady
JFNK solve at $\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 5.0\times10^{-3}$, the same single
post-processing pass and transit synthesis, and the same red-pair equivalent
width on the vacuum window $10832.60$--$10834.20$\,\AA.
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross} is the result. Five arms were solved at every
decade, placed so that both ends of the measurement's $1\sigma$ interval as
well as the crossing itself fall inside solved arms: \emph{every entry of
the table is interpolated inside a bracket of two runs, and nothing is
extrapolated}. Two of the entries, $K_{zz} = 0$ and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$, were
afterwards solved at the interpolated composition itself and return
$EW = 1.1068$ and $1.1082$\,\%\,\AA{} against the measured $1.108$
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}), each on the lineage its crossing
was read on: re-seeded from the nearest neighbouring arm they give
$1.1090$ and $1.1019$ instead, both still inside the band and about $1$ per
cent away in He/H. Two readings of each bracket agree: a chord and a
quadratic in $\log$--$\log$ differ by $0.11$ per cent at worst and by
$0.05$ or better at six of the eight decades.

\textbf{The full ladder is three regimes, not one power law}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hehkzz}). Above $10^{7}$ the degeneracy is shallow and very
nearly a power law,
$\mathrm{d}\log(\mathrm{He/H})/\mathrm{d}\log K_{zz} = -0.145$
($-0.150$, $-0.151$ and $-0.133$ across the three decades
$10^{8}$--$10^{11}$), so $\mathrm{He/H} \propto K_{zz}^{-0.14}$. Below
$10^{7}$ the curve rolls over ($-0.071$ across $10^{6}$--$10^{7}$, $-0.021$
across $10^{5}$--$10^{6}$) onto a plateau at $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.81$, the
value the line implies when the eddy term does nothing.

\emph{This shape has now survived two physics revisions unchanged.}
Decade by decade the exponent was $-0.022$, $-0.071$, $-0.120$, $-0.138$,
$-0.133$ and $-0.142$ on the retired Penning coefficient; $-0.020$,
$-0.070$, $-0.130$, $-0.147$, $-0.153$ and $-0.130$ after it was replaced;
and $-0.021$, $-0.071$, $-0.130$, $-0.150$, $-0.151$ and $-0.133$ on the
composition-renormalized secondary-ionization split of
\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96: the same six numbers to $0.003$. What
each revision moved was the level. The Penning replacement took the whole
ladder to $0.74$--$0.81$ of what it was; the split moves it by a further
$0.988$--$1.001$, decade by decade, which is smaller than the seed spread
at every rung except $10^{9}$ (last column of
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross}). What a rate coefficient this wrong moved was the
absolute composition, by about a fifth; the conditionality the ladder is
drawn to show is a property of the diffusion operator and the line, and
neither revision touched it.

That plateau is where it is for a reason that is measured rather than
assumed: the operator adds $K_{zz}$ to the molecular coefficient, and the
base coefficient of the $K_{zz} = 0$ arm at the crossing composition is
$D_{\rm eff} = 1.2379\times10^{6}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ (measured at
$r = 1.00029\,R_p$ in \texttt{output/element\_flux\_profile.txt}, dominant
pair He\,\textsc{i}--H\,\textsc{i}), so $K_{zz} = 10^{6}$ raises it by
$81\%$, $10^{5}$ by $8\%$ and $10^{4}$ by $0.8\%$. The sharper measurement
holds the composition fixed at $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.8075$ and solves one
common seed at every decade: the equivalent width departs from the
$K_{zz} = 0$ one by less than $3\times10^{-13}$ of the measurement error at
$10^{1}$, $10^{2}$, $10^{3}$ and $10^{4}$ (the four arms reproduce the
$K_{zz} = 0$ one to $10^{-14}$\,\%\,\AA{} in absolute equivalent width),
by $0.21$ at $10^{5}$ and by $1.78$ at $10^{6}$: \emph{no eddy coefficient at or below
$10^{4}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ is distinguishable from none at all by this
line, and $10^{6}$ is the first decade that moves it by more than the
measurement error.} At the other end the operator never reaches the
well-mixed limit either, even at $10^{11}$ the inferred reservoir,
$0.837$, is twice the well-mixed $0.4132$.

Over the span the literature leaves open at the base pressure of these runs
the inferred reservoir ratio moves only from $1.14$ to $2.28$, a factor
$2.0$, against a factor $9$ in equivalent width across the same two decades
at fixed composition: the line responds to composition much faster than it
responds to $K_{zz}$, so a large change in the eddy coefficient is bought
back by a small change in the reservoir. \emph{The composition inferred
from this line under diffusion is therefore only weakly conditional on $K_{zz}$, as
$K_{zz}^{-0.14}$, and strongly conditional on the assumed spectrum}: the
SED moves the well-mixed crossing by a factor $8.6$, from $0.4132$ to
$0.0483$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), which is four times the entire $K_{zz}$
lever.

unchanged, apart from the few percent that the wings carry out of the

not, and the total is fixed.

the two components share the optical depth, and a common convolution acts
on both alike. It stays well below the measured $6.7^{+12.7}_{-3.1}$,

discriminator.

that is not something a kernel supplies: it is a change in $\dot M$,
temperature or composition. Fitting the broadening as a free parameter
alongside those (which is what the measurement asks for) would
presumably shift the C26 parameter estimates, since the composition is
constrained through the same absorbing power that would have to rise.

kernel settled it. The p-winds curve entered at the composition C26 report,
whose equivalent width falls short, so no kernel could settle both. The two
scans test the same physical statement (broadening redistributes, it
does not create) from two different starting points.

\subsection{The adiabatic-cooling population bump, looked for}
\label{sec:bump}

Schulik \& Owen (2025) propose that the missing width is already contained
in the metastable population itself. Writing the steady state of their
Eq.~(1) as
%
\begin{equation}
n_{2^3S}(T_e) = \frac{\alpha(T_e)}{q(T_e) + t_{\rm ion}/n_e}\,n_{\rm He^+},
\end{equation}
%
they note that as adiabatic expansion drops the temperature ``from $\simeq
10^4$\,K to $\simeq 10^3$\,K'', ``a change in slope in the population occurs
as the dominant triplet loss rate $q(T_e)$ drops significantly at lower
temperatures'': the metastable fraction climbs above the curve computed
with the rates frozen at the peak temperature. The rise is ``quenched'' where
the advection timescale overtakes the excitation to $2^1S$, after which
$\dot m_{2^3S} = 4\pi r^2 v\,n_{2^3S}\,m_{\rm He}$ falls through
ionization and the $2^3S \rightarrow 1^1S$ decay. They credit Yan et al.\
(2022) and Biassoni et al.\ (2024) with first identifying the effect, and
their conclusion states the condition it must satisfy to be observable:
``The helium triplet population bump must be situated at high velocities to
help explain anomalous line widths.''

EXHALE carries the temperature dependence the mechanism needs, and carries it
in exactly the balance above. The metastable row
(\texttt{tr\_triplet\_row}, \texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}:81) is
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
n_e\bigl(n_{\rm He^+}\alpha_{2^3S} + n_{\rm He\,I(1^1S)}\,q_{13}\bigr)
 = \; & n_{2^3S}\bigl[\Gamma_{2^3S} + A_{31} + n_{\rm H\,I}Q_{31} \\
      & + n_e(q_{31a}+q_{31b}+b_{2^3S})\bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
%
with every coefficient a function of $T$: $\alpha_{2^3S} = 2.10\times
10^{-13}(T/10^4)^{-0.778}$ (\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:985), the collisional
losses $q_{31a}$ ($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1S$, $0.80$\,eV) and $q_{31b}$
($2^3S \rightarrow 2^1P$, $1.40$\,eV) as Bray (2000) collision strengths
folded with $\exp(-\Delta E/kT)$ (\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:920 and 943), the
destruction term $Q_{31}$ on atomic hydrogen as the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025)
closed form of Eq.~(\ref{eq:gm25}) (\texttt{ioniz\_HeI23S\_H} in
\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}), collisional ionization of the metastable
(\texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}:1091), and the radiative decay $A_{31} =
1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$ (\texttt{util\_ion\_eq.f90}:1235). Between
$5000$ and $1000$\,K the loss $q_{31a}+q_{31b}$ falls by a factor $1.3\times
10^{3}$ while $\alpha_{2^3S}$ rises by a factor $3.5$, so the
ingredients of the bump are present and are not an imposed
parameterization.

\texttt{LHS1140b/bump\_analysis.py} measures the result. It reconstructs the
balance above in Python from the solved profiles, recovering from the row
itself the one term not written to file (the $2^3S$ photoionization rate
$\Gamma_{2^3S}$), and then recomputes the reference curve with $\alpha$,
$q_{13}$, $q_{31a}$, $q_{31b}$ and $Q_{31}$ frozen at the peak temperature.
Each transcribed coefficient was checked against the compiled Fortran over
$300$--$10^4$\,K: $Q_{31}$ agrees to $8\times10^{-16}$ and the other five to
$1.2\times10^{-5}$ or better, the residue being the single-precision
literals the Fortran carries.

The bump is there. In every case the metastable fraction $f_3 =
n_{2^3S}/n_{\rm He}$ rises monotonically outward across the whole cooling
leg, from the temperature maximum to a maximum of $f_3$ at
$1.1$--$14.5\,R_p$ (a rise of $1.3$--$4.2\times10^{3}$ in the equilibrium
profiles and $22$--$256$ in the advection-corrected ones), and the rise is
attributable to the temperature dependence in the way Schulik \& Owen
describe: against the same balance with the rates frozen at the peak
temperature, the solved population stands a factor $2.4$--$30$ high at the
density maximum and up to $9.8$--$156$ high further out. On the GJ\,1132
proxy at the equivalent-width-matched composition
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$, on the nearest solved arm
(\texttt{crossings\_j96/wm\_heh0p415}), the maximum sits at
$5.269\,R_p$ where $T = 1546$\,K, against $T_{\rm max} = 4591$\,K at
$1.449\,R_p$: the temperature has fallen to $0.34$ of its peak, which is the
regime the mechanism asks for. The quench is visible too: in the
advection-corrected profiles the density maximum has moved inward to
$1.05$--$1.65\,R_p$ and $n_{2^3S}$ then falls monotonically, so the outflow
appears to be sweeping the metastable out at least as fast as recombination
resupplies it, and $\dot m_{2^3S}$ drops past the peak by $97.9$--$99.9\%$ on
the helium-dominated He/H\,$=1000$ runs, while every other case, the
equivalent-width-matched ones included, carries it outward without loss.

The velocity condition, however, is not met, and not narrowly. At the bump
the outflow is moving at $0.25$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy and
$1.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,699 proxy. Weighting by absorbers over the
whole $2^3S$ column, the median $|v_r|$ is at most $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and
$2.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the two spectra, and the largest $|v_r|$ anywhere in
the column is $1.6$ and $3.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. No part of the column on
either spectrum reaches $5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, against the $\sigma
= 9.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of extra broadening the measurement demands
(Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening}). Radial velocity is the generous choice here:
the line-of-sight projection of a radial field over the transit chord is
smaller still. Figure~\ref{fig:bump} shows the three statements together.

So the bump exists in these solutions and contributes nothing measurable to
the width. That is consistent with what Schulik \& Owen themselves report
for the coolest hosts: for their M2 case they find that ``the transit
signal probes the same velocity region for both treatments of
thermodynamics, making the line insensitive to velocity'', and they conclude
that K stars, not M stars, are the favorable testbeds: G-type hosts
suppress the bump by photoionizing the triplet, and M-type hosts have weaker
tidal fields and so rarely drive fast enough outflows. LHS\,1140 is an M4.5
dwarf, cooler than the M2 they model, and its planet is a temperate
super-Earth rather than a hot giant. The mechanism appears to be present and
correctly captured; the host is the wrong one for it to help.

\subsection{The width in the wider He\,10830 literature}
\label{sec:widthlit}

The comparison so far has been between this model and this measurement. Put
against the published population, the roles change: \textbf{the measured
width is ordinary, and it is the model that is anomalous.}

\paragraph{The measured width is typical.}
Few He\,10830 analyses fit the width freely rather than predicting it; where
they do, the answer is $19$--$33$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM.
Table~\ref{tab:widthcensus} collects them. LHS\,1140\,b's
$23.9^{+7.2}_{-4.3}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is an unremarkable member of that set.
Guilluy et al.\ (2024) is the only survey that tabulates a free FWHM for
several planets at once; their GJ\,3470\,b entry should not be taken at face
value, since they write that ``The larger FWHM for GJ\,3470\,b is believed to
be attributed to residuals from OH line contamination''. The entries are
quoted as published (different instruments, different extractors, no
common deconvolution of the instrumental profile), so the clustering is a
statement about published numbers rather than a uniformly reduced sample.

Zhang et al.\ (2023) treat that width as \emph{evidence for} photoevaporation:
``The width is the most consistent metric and is within 10\% of 1.1 A for all
planets'', because ``For a Parker outflow, the sound, bulk outflow, and
thermal speeds are all close to sqrt(2kB T/mu mH) = 9.4 km s-1 for T = 7000 K
and mu = 1.3, leading to an FWHM of 2.355 sigma $\sim$ 22 km s-1.'' That
argument needs a $7000$\,K hydrogen-dominated transonic outflow. This solution
has $5200$\,K at $2\,R_p$, a mean mass of $2.6$--$3.5$\,amu because of the
helium enrichment, and an outflow of $0.024$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ there; the same
construction returns the model's $2.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, not $9.4$.

\paragraph{The one broadening term the field offers is Mach 1 by definition.}
There is a single turbulence formula in this literature,
$v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$ with $m$ the mass of a helium atom
(Lamp\'on et al.\ 2020, Eq.~16), adopted unchanged by p-winds as its
Eq.~(21) (Dos Santos et al.\ 2022). It is the adiabatic sound speed of a
monatomic gas (a Mach number of exactly one), and it is a fixed function
of $T$, not a free parameter. Evaluated on this solution it gives $4.2$,
$4.2$, $3.6$, $3.0$ and $2.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at $1.2$, $2$, $3$, $5$ and
$8\,R_p$, with a metastable-weighted mean of $3.8$--$4.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ over
$1$--$8\,R_p$. Against the required $\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ that is
short by a factor $2.1$--$3.7$ across the absorbing region and $2.3$--$2.5$ on
the absorber-weighted average. \textbf{The measurement asks for Mach $2$--$4$
and the term on offer is Mach $1$; it cannot absorb the requirement, whatever
one concludes about its physical justification.} Three published analyses
report the same failure in their own systems: Pall\'e et al.\ (2020) on
GJ\,3470\,b (``The inclusion of the broadening of the lines due to turbulence
(vturb = 5kT/3m, where m is the mass of a helium atom), in addition to the
standard Doppler broadening, was not enough to explain the measured broadening
in the observations''), Lamp\'on et al.\ (2021) on HD\,189733\,b (``Including
a turbulence broadening component (see Sect.~3.3), the profile broadens, but
it is still narrower than the measured absorption. \ldots\ Hence, our
hydrodynamic model alone is not able to explain the width of the absorption
profile.''), and Czesla et al.\ (2022) on HAT-P-32\,b (``Thermal broadening
alone remains insufficient to explain the observed line width, even if the
turbulence term is included.'').

Where a width is instead fitted freely, it comes back two to three times
larger: Salz et al.\ (2018) obtain $b = 15.1 \pm 0.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (extended
model, $f = 20\%$) and $11.3 \pm 0.5$ (compact) at mid-transit for
HD\,189733\,b, and Czesla et al.\ (2022) obtain $v_{t,a} = 15.2 \pm 3.1$,
$11.2 \pm 3.1$ and $11.7 \pm 1.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for HAT-P-32\,b. Our own
$9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ sits at the low end of that set.

\paragraph{An independent code reports the same shortfall, on the same axes.}
Taylor et al.\ (2026) apply a 1D self-consistent model of the same class
(energy balance, lower-atmosphere coupling, multispecies diffusion, non-LTE
H($n=2$)) to HD\,189733\,b and find that ``several prior analyses have
matched the HD\,189733\,b width by introducing ad hoc (micro)turbulent or
Gaussian broadening terms; here, we show that this step remains necessary even
in our self-consistent framework'', at ``a factor of $\sim 2$--$3$'' in width.
Decisively for the present work, the axes they varied are the axes varied
here: ``We tested variations in the free parameters (heating efficiency,
$K_{zz}$, stellar activity level/XUV input, base composition, and
lower-boundary placement): configurations that increase the width either
deepen the core transit depth too much or violate the H$\alpha$ constraints,
and none simultaneously reproduce both core depth and width.'' Their proposed
mechanism is stated by them as speculative, ``Given that excited He is
mostly produced by recombination of helium ions, this broadening can be driven
by plasma turbulence \ldots\ While these ideas are speculative, we encourage
further exploration of plasma effects and magnetic field interactions'',
and its first clause is the same physics Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale} derives
independently below. Rumenskikh et al.\ (2022), as reported there, reached the
observed width in 3D only by prescribing zonal jets of $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
together with reduced hydrogen line cooling; Schulik \& Owen (2025) offer the
adiabatic-cooling population bump as the one route with no free parameter,
which Sect.~\ref{sec:bump} shows is present here and worth
$0.25$--$1.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\paragraph{What follows.} \textbf{The width shortfall is not specific to
EXHALE and not specific to LHS\,1140\,b: it is an unresolved problem of the
1D steady-state model class.} The evidence for that statement is threefold:
the measured width is a typical member of the published population
(Table~\ref{tab:widthcensus}); the deficit is reproduced at the same factor of
$2$--$3$ by an independently written code, on a different planet, around a star
at the opposite end of the activity range, after testing the same axes
(Taylor et al.\ 2026); and the field's own remedy is a term that is Mach 1 by
construction and that its users report in print to be insufficient. Every
published route to the observed width adds velocity by hand (a sonic
turbulence term, a free Gaussian, or prescribed jets), and none of them
follows from the 1D energy equation.

One citation is worth flagging, as an observation and not as a correction to
anyone's result. Lamp\'on et al.\ (2023) and Yan et al.\ (2024) both cite Salz
et al.\ (2018) as a source of the $v_{\rm turb} = \sqrt{5kT/3m}$ convention,
but ``turbulen'' does not occur anywhere in the published Salz et al.\ (2018),
which fits a free Doppler parameter instead. The convention itself is stated
in Salz et al.\ (2016), Eq.~(5); the 2018 paper appears to be intended as the
source of the measured \emph{width}. As written, the attribution invites the
reader to believe that a Mach-1 turbulence term reproduced the HD\,189733\,b
width, when in that paper it was a free $15.1$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ Doppler
parameter.

\subsection{The size of the non-thermal candidates}
\label{sec:candscale}

If the missing velocity is not thermal and not in the 1D energy equation, the
next question is which non-thermal mechanism could supply
$\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The estimates below are order-of-magnitude
requirements computed on the reference solution
(\texttt{flux\_closure/heh11p1/k01}); the full set, including the
stellar-wind standoff, the turbulent and kinetic power budgets, charge
exchange and radiation pressure, is in
\texttt{docs/lhs1140b\_width\_nonthermal\_candidates.md}.

\paragraph{Magnetic.}
Setting the Alfv\'en speed equal to the required dispersion,
$v_A = B/\sqrt{4\pi\rho} = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and reading $\rho$ off the
advection-corrected profile, the field needed in the line-forming region is
$B = 0.423$, $0.118$, $0.043$ and $0.016$\,G at $1.2$, $1.5$, $2$ and
$3\,R_p$. Continued inward as a dipole, $B_s = B\,(r/R_p)^3$, those are
surface fields of $0.73$, $0.40$, $0.34$ and $0.43$\,G: a shallow minimum
near $2\,R_p$, so a dipole of a few tenths of a gauss would put $v_A$ near the
required value over the whole region at once. \emph{As a scale comparison}
that is unremarkable: it lies between Earth's surface dipole
($\approx 0.3$\,G) and Jupiter's ($\approx 4$\,G). The difficulty is what such
a field would do rather than whether a planet can have it: the plasma $\beta$
at that strength is $0.28$--$0.38$ over $1.2$--$3\,R_p$, so the field would be
dynamically dominant and would restructure the outflow rather than decorate
it, and this solution does not model that. The wave energy flux required to
sustain $\sigma = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\rho(3\sigma^2)v_A$ through a sphere,
is $12$--$870$ times the solution's total XUV heating of
$1.22\times 10^{20}$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$ over the same radii, which only a
star--planet interaction driven by a wind of at least solar strength at this
orbit could feed. \textbf{Not excluded by field strength; constrained by the
power it has to be given, and by the fact that this comparison is being made
for a $131$\,d rotator with $L_X/L_{\rm bol} = 6.5\times 10^{-6}$.}

\paragraph{Where a metastable atom can be made.}
This is the constraint that bears hardest on any mechanism that manufactures
fast helium somewhere else (a stellar-wind interaction region, a
charge-exchange or energetic-neutral population), and brings it into the
beam. He\,$2^3$S is fed by He$^+$ recombination and destroyed by Penning
ionization with atomic hydrogen (\texttt{tr\_triplet\_row} in
\texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}). The collisional channel from the singlet
ground state supplies a fraction $1\times 10^{-13}$ of the source at
$1.2\,R_p$ and $2\times 10^{-14}$ at $2\,R_p$, falling to $10^{-33}$ by
$5\,R_p$: the metastable population is a recombination product without
qualification. Table~\ref{tab:metatimes} evaluates the two timescales on the
solution, with $\alpha_{2^3S} = 2.10\times 10^{-13}(T/10^4)^{-0.778}$ and the
Garc\'ia Mu\~noz (2025) destruction rate of Eq.~(\ref{eq:gm25}). Both
columns are recomputed here from the solved profiles. What the coefficient
replacement leaves alone is the wind the table stands on: between the last
solution of this arm that predates the replacement
(\texttt{flux\_closure/heh11p1/k00}) and the re-solved one $T$ agrees to
$1.2$ per cent and $t_{\rm rec}$ to $1.8$ at every radius, because the
metastable is a trace population and not an energy-budget term. The last three columns are
not preserved that way and should not be: they \emph{are} the destruction
rate, and it is the quantity Sect.~\ref{sec:penningrate} replaced, so they
move by factors of $1.0$--$1.8$ between the two fits. A reconstruction that
reproduced them would be evidence that the replacement had not been
applied.

Two requirements follow, and they are severe. First, recombination is slower
than destruction by $7.5\times10^{3}$--$4.8\times10^{4}$ everywhere, so only
a tiny standing fraction of the helium is ever metastable and the population
is set locally and continuously. Second, and more restrictive, within its own
lifetime an atom moving at the required speed travels $0.05\,R_p$ at
$2\,R_p$ and $0.30\,R_p$ at $3\,R_p$ (Table~\ref{tab:metatimes}, last
column). \textbf{The
inner metastable helium, which carries roughly a third of the absorption,
cannot have been manufactured anywhere but where it is seen.} A stellar-wind
or energetic-neutral population would therefore have to be re-ionized and
recombine \emph{inside} the absorbing region: the recombination time there is
$10^{6}$--$10^{7}$\,s against a crossing time of $5\,R_p$ at
$9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of $5.9\times 10^{3}$\,s, short by three to four orders
of magnitude. The candidate is not excluded as a source of \emph{some} broad
outer emission, but it cannot be the source of the bulk of the line.

\paragraph{Three-dimensional flow.}
With $P = 24.737$\,d and tidal locking, $\Omega = 2.94\times 10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$
and the corotation speed $\Omega r$ is $0.032$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at the surface,
$0.098$ at $3\,R_p$ and $0.94$ at the $29\,R_p$ domain edge: already
measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit} and two orders below the requirement. A
pressure-driven day--night flow is bounded above by the thermal energy
available, i.e.\ by a few times the $4$--$5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ sound speed in the
complete-conversion limit, and independently by the measured line shift, whose
posterior caps any coherent line-of-sight flow of the whole absorbing column
at $4.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. Both are small here for the same reason: the driver of
3D circulation is the day--night temperature contrast, and LHS\,1140\,b is a
temperate planet at $T_{\rm eq} = 226$\,K around an M4.5 dwarf, with an
irradiation and a rotation rate one to two orders of magnitude below the hot
Jupiters for which $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ jets are prescribed. Taylor et
al.\ (2026) reach the same conclusion for HD\,189733\,b, where the case is far
more favorable: ``typical GCM wind speeds (a few kilometers per second) are
unlikely to supply the additional $\sim 12$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ required to match
the observed width.''

\paragraph{What this does not settle.} These are requirement estimates
evaluated on \emph{this} solution's density, temperature and ionization
structure. They say what each mechanism would have to be given in order to
produce $9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ here, and on that basis none of them is
comfortable: the magnetic route needs a power it has no obvious source for
around this star, the fast-neutral route cannot make metastables where the
absorption is, and the 3D route is short by two orders of magnitude. They do
not identify which mechanism is in fact responsible, and they do not exclude
the possibility that the absorbing gas is not this solution's gas at all:
the measured pre-ingress signal, at least as broad as the in-transit one at a
phase when the planet occults nothing, is direct evidence that some of the
signal comes from a structure no 1D radial model contains.
\textbf{Which mechanism supplies the width is not determined by this work, and
that is also the state of the literature.}

\subsection{What would have to change}

The measured line wants gas that is fast enough to be broad but not so
extended that it absorbs too much. EXHALE at this XUV flux delivers
neither. The candidates, in the order they should be tested:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{More energy}: tested (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), and it does
      not do it. The GJ\,699 proxy carries five times the XUV of the
      GJ\,1132 proxy used here, and the paper itself runs both. Solving the
      scan again on it raises $\dot M$ sevenfold and moves the composition
      estimate by an order of magnitude, but leaves the required extra
      broadening at $21.95$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ against $22.18$: the width gap is
      not an energy-input problem.
\item \emph{A different helium distribution}: tested
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}), and it does not do it either. Letting
      helium settle against binary diffusion and lifting it back with an
      eddy term redistributes the absorber by orders of magnitude and moves
      the inferred composition with it, but the required broadening stays
      at $22.19$\,km\,s$^{-1}$: it changes how much helium is there, not
      how fast it moves.
\item \emph{Heating the code does not carry.} Taylor et al.'s trapped-ion
      turbulence, or any magnetically mediated dissipation, is outside a
      1D hydrodynamic energy equation by construction. Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale}
      sizes it: the field needed to make $v_A = 9.34$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is a
      few tenths of a gauss, which is ordinary, but the wave power that field
      must carry is $12$--$870$ times the XUV heating.
\item \emph{Geometry.} A line-of-sight velocity field from day--night or
      zonal flow is a 3D effect; Rumenskikh et al.\ reach the observed width
      with $10$--$20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ jets, which is the right order. For this
      planet, though, rotation gives $0.03$--$0.10$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the
      measured line shift caps any coherent line-of-sight flow at
      $4.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:candscale}).
\item \emph{Adiabatic-cooling population bump.} Looked for
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}): the bump is present in these solutions, but
      it sits at $0.25$--$1.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, so it does not supply width.
\end{enumerate}

The paper's composition constraint should be read in the same light: it
inherits the isothermal assumption. Impose a $6100$\,K structure and only
helium-rich gas absorbs enough; solve the energy balance on this spectrum
and, in the well-mixed limit, the same absorption is reached near
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.42$, and near $0.048$ on the other proxy spectrum,
or near $1.6$ once helium is allowed to settle against the adopted eddy
coefficient. No answer on that list is secure while the width is
unexplained, because the width is what tells us whether the line-forming
gas is the gas either model thinks it is.

\section{The best each model can do}

Figure~\ref{fig:best} puts only the two best models on the measurement: the
paper's p-winds fit at its own best fit (the MCMC medians), and the EXHALE
solution whose
equivalent width matches. They fail in different ways, and neither reaches
the observed width. p-winds is the broader of the two, because its
temperature is imposed where the line forms, but at $0.575$\,\AA{} it is
still $1.5$ times narrower than the measurement, and it overshoots the blue
component by a factor of about three. EXHALE
carries the right total absorption but concentrates it into a line a third
as wide as observed, so its core is too deep. Only the second failure can be
undone by broadening alone (Sects.~\ref{sec:broadening}
and~\ref{sec:pwbroad}).

Figure~\ref{fig:beststruct} shows what that EXHALE solution actually is. It
is not a fast, hot outflow: the temperature peaks at $4583$\,K near
$1.45\,R_p$ (comparable to the isothermal value p-winds imposes), but

region and helium is the \emph{more} ionized of the two, not the less:
$x(\mathrm{H\,\textsc{ii}}) = 0.05$, $0.10$ and $0.22$ at $2$, $3$ and

photons. The metastable population peaks well out, at $5.2\,R_p$ with

large, cold, slow, metastable-rich envelope) is precisely what produces
the right equivalent width with the wrong profile: plenty of absorbers
spread over most of the stellar disk, but almost no velocity spread to
distribute the absorption in wavelength.

\section{The other proxy: five times the XUV}
\label{sec:gj699}

The first item on the list above (more energy) can be tested without
adding any physics, because the paper itself runs two proxy spectra. Both
stand in for LHS\,1140's unobserved XUV: GJ\,1132 (used everywhere above)
and GJ\,699, scaled to the orbit by the catalog X-ray flux ratios
$A_{1132} = 0.59$ and $A_{699} = 0.056$ following the authors' script, the
same convention adopted in Sect.~\ref{sec:sed}. Integrated over
$10$--$1300$\,\AA{} at the orbit of the planet, the GJ\,699 file carries
$169.9$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$ against $33.5$ for the GJ\,1132 one: a
factor $5.1$ in XUV input.

On the EXHALE side nothing else changes. Each GJ\,699 case is the matching
GJ\,1132 input with one key replaced, \texttt{Spectrum file:}; the
luminosity keys in the file are irrelevant because \texttt{sed\_read}
overwrites them with the integral of the spectrum it reads. Each case is
started from the converged solution of the same composition on the GJ\,1132
spectrum and finished with JFNK, and all seven reach \texttt{info}~$= 0$
with $\|R\| < 10^{-3}$. One case needed a different seed:
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.55$ stalled when started from its GJ\,1132 counterpart
and converged when restarted from the already-solved
$\mathrm{He/H} = 0.25$ GJ\,699 solution.

estimate the paper quotes for this proxy, $2.90\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$.

retrieval. Whether a solved wind and an imposed one agree on $\dot M$
therefore depends on which proxy is assumed, and the disagreement reported
in Sect.~\ref{sec:width} is not a fixed property of the code.

\subsection{The composition estimate moves by an order of magnitude}

Interpolating the equivalent width in $\log$--$\log$ across the re-solved
ladder and solving for the measured value gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 0.0483 \quad (0.0469\text{--}0.0497 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma), \qquad \mathrm{H\!:\!He} = 20.7 ,
\end{equation}
chord and quadratic agreeing to $0.00$ per cent. A run at $0.0483$ itself
gives $EW = 1.1079$\,\%\,\AA, $0.00\sigma$ from the measurement, solved on
the lineage the crossing was read on
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}). The bracketing runs are
what fix this: the arms at $0.048$ and $0.049$ straddle the measurement
directly ($EW = 1.1012$ and $1.1225$ against
$1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA), so the crossing is measured rather than
extrapolated from the solar case. The ladder is monotone throughout and
evenly spaced in $EW$ to four decimals, and a second lineage seeded arm by
arm from the stored solutions of the same compositions gives $0.0482$, $0.2$
per cent away. The turbulence-on crossing was not measured on this ladder;
on the seven-row scan above, re-solved and synthesized both ways on the
current binary, the turbulence term moves the crossing from $0.0484$ to
$0.0482$, $0.6$ per cent.

This is a shift of an order of magnitude from the $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$
the same code gives on the GJ\,1132 proxy, and it crosses the solar value on
the way: the brighter spectrum wants a helium-\emph{poor} atmosphere,
$0.58\times$ solar, where the fainter one wanted a helium-rich one,
$5.0\times$ solar. The measurement cannot arbitrate, because the two proxies
are two guesses at the same unobserved spectrum and both reproduce the
observed equivalent width once composition is free. The conclusion to draw
is therefore about the inference, not about the planet: what the helium line
constrains is the product of composition and XUV input, and any composition
quoted from it (the paper's $\mathrm{H\!:\!He} \lesssim 10^{-3}$ as much
as the values here) is conditional on the assumed SED. On the present
evidence the composition inference is SED-limited.

\subsection{The width does not move}

The width is the one thing the extra energy does not fix. Across the whole
GJ\,699 scan the red-pair FWHM is $0.281$--$0.352$\,\AA{}
($0.313$--$0.435$ with the turbulence term, and the ladder of record spans
$0.281$--$0.282$\,\AA), against
the measured $0.841$\,\AA. Measuring the deficit the way
Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} does (convolving with a Gaussian and asking
which width reproduces the observation) the arm at the crossing needs
$21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of added velocity spread, against
$22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ for the equivalent-width-matched GJ\,1132 solution and
$22.23$ for the diffusive one of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}. A
factor $5.1$ in XUV input, a factor $7$ in $\dot M$ and a factor $3$ in
outflow velocity at $20\,R_p$ change the required broadening by $1\%$.

The reason is visible in the table: the outflow velocity that grows is
radial and small compared with the missing spread, while the line-forming
region stays cold. The candidate ``more energy'' is therefore tested and
rejected as an explanation of the width. It moves $\dot M$ and it moves the
composition estimate; it does not move the profile. The remaining candidates
(heating outside a 1D hydrodynamic energy equation, 3D velocity fields,
and an adiabatic-cooling metastable bump) are unaffected by this test;
the last of them has since been looked for on both spectra
(Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) and does not help either.

\section{Closing the loop: the composition as an output}
\label{sec:closure}

Every number above took the helium abundance as an input. The scans of
Sects.~\ref{sec:scan} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} chose it, solved the wind, and
asked what the line did; the reservoir at the base was a parameter set by
hand, and the lower atmosphere below the $1\,\mu$bar matching level was a
handful of scalars. This section removes that freedom on one axis: the
lower atmosphere is solved as a photochemical column, handed over as a
profile, and the elemental fluxes are iterated until they are continuous
across the matching level. The composition at the match is then a solution
of the coupled problem rather than a number chosen for it. The
implementation and its tests are recorded in
\texttt{docs/phase\_e\_flux\_closure\_design.md} and
\texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md}, Sects.~76--79; the design runs are
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/flux\_closure/}. The three arms of this section have
since been re-run end to end (chemistry, wind, elemental-flux measurement
and damped update) on Photochem $0.9.0$ with the re-pinned adapter of
\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~99 and on the current binary
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/closure/}), which is what
Table~\ref{tab:closure} reports. What they establish (that the closure is
single-valued and that at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ it hands back the reservoir it
was given) is a statement about the iteration and not about the line, and
the ladder of Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}, which is what the composition
is read from, was re-solved on both.

\subsection{The setup}

The column below the match is solved with Photochem (Wogan 2025) on the
Zahnle reaction set restricted to H/He/N/O/C. The temperature is not
prescribed: \texttt{clima}'s \texttt{AdiabatClimate} is run with
\texttt{solve\_for\_T\_trop} on, so the stratospheric temperature is the
skin temperature of the solution, and the background gas is chosen by
abundance rather than by name: on this atmosphere that is helium, not
H$_2$. The climate solve is taken from a deep boundary at $20$\,bar, which
is the shallowest one at which the photochemical model bottom fits inside
the climate column. Chemistry and climate are run on the same elemental
vector, He/H starting at $2.09$ (the $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossing of
Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross}) with Lodders C, N and O, on the GJ\,1132 proxy
spectrum at the planet's orbit and at the adopted constant
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$.

The result is handed to EXHALE as a profile of $102$ levels from
$16.73$\,bar to $1.10\times10^{-8}$\,bar, carrying $p$, $r$, $T$, $n$,
$\rho$, $K_{zz}$, the molecular fractions and the elemental reservoirs
He/H, C/H, N/H and O/H, matched to the wind at $1\,\mu$bar;
Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem} reads that solution column by column, and says
which of the columns the wind is actually built from. The loop is then
closed in the other direction: the elemental escape fluxes EXHALE computes
are imposed back on the photochemical model at its top,
$\phi_{\rm El} = \Phi_{\rm El}/(m_{\rm El}\,4\pi r_{\rm top}^{2})$, helium on
He and hydrogen on H$_2$ at $\phi_{\rm H}/2$, the dominant carriers, the
atomic-H share at that level being $10^{-4}$, and the pair is iterated by
damped Picard ($\omega = 0.5$) on the residual
$|F_{\rm measured} - \Phi_{\rm trial}|/|F_{\rm measured}|$ to a tolerance of
$5\%$.

Two things about that measurement are worth stating, because they decide
whether it means anything. The elemental flux is read in the \emph{steady}
window $r \ge r_{\rm esc}$, not in the interval the two models share: at a
steady state $4\pi r^2(\rho X v + J)$ does not depend on radius, so a flux
measured there is the flux through the matching level. The overlap window
cannot carry it: from the match to the profile top the column spans
$1.96$ decades of pressure in $0.0098\,R_p$, entirely inside the region where
the wind's mass flux is not yet its own, and the flux across it has a radial
spread of $13$ in $F_{\rm H}$ and $35$ in $F_{\rm He}$. In the steady window
the same solution holds $191$ faces at spreads of $0.44\%$ and $0.46\%$. And
that flatness is a statement about how far the wind was converged, which was
measured rather than assumed: at $\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 10^{-3}$ the same
solution spreads by $7.4\%$, which would fail the closure's own validity
test, and continuing it to $10^{-4}$ brings it to $0.50\%$ and moves
$\log_{10}\dot M$ from $7.47$ to $7.50$. The closure runs the wind at
$10^{-4}$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Not verified: the cross-check with a second chemistry code.}
The same handoff was set up with VULCAN (Tsai et al.\ 2017) on the
production profile's own $T(p)$ and $K_{zz}$, and VULCAN did not converge on
this cold helium-rich column: its convergence measure rose from $2.65$ to
$2.75$ against a criterion of $0.01$ with the timestep pinned, the limiting
cell being H$_2$O at the cold trap, and the run exited on its step limit. No
VULCAN composition at the match was produced and no wind was run on one, so
the cross-code spread is not measured here. The record is
\texttt{LHS1140b/lower\_profile\_vulcan/vulcan\_work/STOPPED.txt}.

\subsection{The column the chemistry returns}
\label{sec:photochem}

The settings above produce one file, and what EXHALE does on the profile
branch rests entirely on it, so this section reads the solution itself.
Figs.~\ref{fig:pccol} and~\ref{fig:pccol2} draw every column of the schema
except the two elemental-flux columns, which are constants carrying the
trial fluxes the header states. The file measured here is
\texttt{LHS1140b/lower\_profile/lower\_atmosphere\_profile.dat}, the one-way
handoff at iteration $0$; the closed arms of Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}
carry their own copies and are compared at the end of this section.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The grid and the thermal structure.} $102$ levels from
$16.73$\,bar to $1.098\times10^{-8}$\,bar: the photochemical model's own
domain, sitting inside the $20$\,bar column the climate step was taken from.
Temperature runs from $520.3$\,K at the deep boundary to a floor of
$185.05$\,K, which it reaches at $0.94$\,bar and holds at every level above:
the stratosphere is isothermal at the skin temperature, and the tropopause
the climate solve reports, $1.031$\,bar, is one level below the first
isothermal level. $K_{zz}$ is $10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at all $102$
levels, so the eddy coefficient the wind runs at and the one the column was
mixed at are the same constant. The column spans $553.6$\,km in radius,
$0.15608$ to $0.16400\,R_{\rm J}$, with the $1\,\mu$bar match at
$0.16240\,R_{\rm J} = 1.782\,R_\oplus$, which is what fixes the base
radius of the wind. Density falls from $n = 2.33\times10^{20}$\,cm$^{-3}$
($\rho = 1.40\times10^{-3}$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$) to
$4.30\times10^{11}$\,cm$^{-3}$ ($2.58\times10^{-12}$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The column is chemically almost frozen.} Between the deep
boundary and the match, $17$ decades of pressure, $q_{\rm H_2}$ moves from
$0.19258$ to $0.19264$, $q_{\rm CH_4}$ from $1.072\times10^{-4}$ to
$1.025\times10^{-4}$ and $q_{\rm NH_3}$ from $3.158\times10^{-5}$ to
$2.919\times10^{-5}$. What changes at all is water, which condenses, and a
set of trace photochemical products at the very top. In an eddy-mixed column
at $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ that is what one expects, and it is the same statement
as Sect.~\ref{sec:closure} makes from the other side: the eddy term
homogenizes the column all the way to the match.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hydrogen stays molecular to the top.} $q_{\rm H}$ rises
from $5.9\times10^{-21}$ at the deep boundary to $6.5\times10^{-6}$ at the
match and $4.0\times10^{-5}$ at the model top, four to five decades below
$q_{\rm H_2}$ throughout: there is no level in this column at which atomic
hydrogen overtakes H$_2$. The base EXHALE is handed is an H$_2$ base in that
sense. It does not enter the wind that way in these runs (EXHALE's
molecular chemistry is off, and $q_{\rm H_2}$ is read but inert; see below),
which is worth stating rather than leaving implicit.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The composition is cold and reducing.} At $520$\,K and
$17$\,bar CO is already negligible, $q_{\rm CO} = 8.1\times10^{-18}$, and
CO$_2$ smaller still: carbon is CH$_4$ and nitrogen is NH$_3$, the opposite
of the CO/N$_2$-dominated deep chemistry of a hot Jupiter. Counting carriers
at the match against the hydrogen-nucleus fraction of the gas,
$2q_{\rm H_2} + q_{\rm H} = 0.38532$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2ex
\item carbon: CH$_4$ gives $2.661\times10^{-4}$ per H, $99.7\%$ of the
      carbon the profile's carrier columns hold, with C$_2$H$_2$ and HCN
      supplying the rest;
\item nitrogen: NH$_3$ gives $7.576\times10^{-5}$ per H, $96.3\%$, with
      N$_2$ at $3.4\%$;
\item oxygen: the water that survived the cold trap gives
      $4.831\times10^{-7}$ per H, $99.9\%$.
\end{itemize}
Those carrier sums come to $96.0$, $96.0$ and $97.6$ per cent of the
elemental columns ${\rm C/H} = 2.778\times10^{-4}$,
${\rm N/H} = 8.187\times10^{-5}$ and ${\rm O/H} = 4.957\times10^{-7}$; the
remainder sits in species the profile does not carry as columns, so the
carrier accounting here is a decomposition of the elemental ratios and not
an independent measurement of them.

This is the direct link to Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}. The C and N that cool
the wind and take its line down by $2.6\times$ are held in the column as
CH$_4$ and NH$_3$, both of which the solution keeps in the gas phase across the trap, so they
cross the tropopause intact and arrive at the match at essentially their deep
values.
Oxygen, whose carrier is water, does not.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Photochemistry acts only in the top decades.} From the
tropopause to the match, HCN goes $1.3\times10^{-13} \rightarrow
6.5\times10^{-8}$, N$_2$ $9.2\times10^{-9} \rightarrow 5.2\times10^{-7}$,
C$_2$H$_2$ $2.5\times10^{-13} \rightarrow 1.1\times10^{-7}$, CO
$5.2\times10^{-16} \rightarrow 1.8\times10^{-10}$ and OH
$4.5\times10^{-27} \rightarrow 7.1\times10^{-11}$: several decades each,
and all of them still trace. They do not move the elemental budget: the
carrier accounting above puts every one of them below $4\%$ of its element.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What EXHALE actually consumes from this file.} Read in the
code rather than inferred: the reader in
\texttt{files\_IO/lower\_} \texttt{atmosphere\_profile.f90} requires the
columns \texttt{p}, \texttt{r}, \texttt{T}, \texttt{n\_tot}, \texttt{rho},
\texttt{Kzz}, \texttt{q\_H2}, \texttt{q\_H} and \texttt{X\_He} to be
present, and \texttt{apply\_lower\_atmosphere\_profile}
(\texttt{input\_read.f90}) then takes, \emph{at the matching level only},
\texttt{T} into $T_0$, \texttt{r} into $R_0$, \texttt{q\_H2} into
\texttt{q\_h2\_base}, \texttt{X\_He} into \texttt{HeH}, and \texttt{X\_C},
\texttt{X\_N}, \texttt{X\_O} (and any other \texttt{X\_<El>} the file
carries) into the metal abundances, through the same door \texttt{metals.inp}
uses. \texttt{p\_match\_bar} comes from the header. The \texttt{Kzz} column
is the one thing read over the whole profile, interpolated onto the radial
grid through the file's own \texttt{r} column
(\texttt{eddy\_diffusion\_on\_grid}); a scalar \texttt{He\_Kzz:} in
\texttt{input.inp} is then announced as inert. Everything else is
\emph{not} read: \texttt{n\_tot} and \texttt{rho} are required by the schema
but never consumed (the base density is $n_0$ of \texttt{input.inp} and
the base particle count follows from $T_0$, He/H and the elemental ratios),
\texttt{q\_H} is required and never consumed, the two flux columns
\texttt{F\_H} and \texttt{F\_He} are not read by the Fortran at all (the
closure driver reads them on the Python side), and none of the molecular
columns beyond \texttt{q\_H2} is read. And \texttt{q\_H2} itself only acts
when a molecular base is switched on: it reaches the base EOS through
\texttt{h2\_mixing\_ratio\_base}, which \texttt{comp\_ntot\_bc} calls only
under \texttt{molecular\_base}. In the runs of this memo that flag is off,
and the resolved report confirms it: \texttt{mass\_per\_H} $= 9.37393$
and \texttt{ntot\_bc} $= 1.000116$ are exactly
$1 + 4\,{\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El}A_{\rm El}$ and
$(1 + {\rm He/H} + \sum X_{\rm El})/(1 + {\rm He/H})$ with no H$_2$ term.
So of the $23$ columns, six numbers at one level plus one full column are
what the wind is actually built from.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the reservoir does to the column.} The closure arms
each carry their own solved column, and raising
the reservoir changes it in three measurable ways
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pccol2}d--f). The deep boundary cools, $520.3 \rightarrow
439.4 \rightarrow 387.9$\,K at $16.73$\,bar for ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, $5.01$
and $10.31$, while the stratosphere stays at $185$\,K. H$_2$ is diluted by
number, $q_{\rm H_2} = 0.1926 \rightarrow 0.0907 \rightarrow 0.0462$ at the
match, with C/H and N/H unchanged to four digits because they are stated per
hydrogen nucleus. And the cold trap loosens: O/H at the match goes
$4.96\times10^{-7} \rightarrow 6.43\times10^{-7} \rightarrow
9.09\times10^{-7}$, the trend the O/H column of
Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} continues over $8.45$--$11.0$. Oxygen is
the only element the reservoir moves here, and it is the one whose carrier
condenses.

\subsection{What the constant $K_{zz}$ carries: the pressure form, tested}
\label{sec:kzzform}

The eddy coefficient of the last section is a stated number rather than a
solved one, and it is stated in a particular way: constant,
$1.0\times10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at every one of the $102$ levels, which is
what the adapter's \texttt{-{}-kzz-const 1.0e9} writes into the \texttt{Kzz}
column. The form is an assumption laid on top of the value. What the
\emph{value} carries is already measured: the equivalent width moves by a
factor $\simeq 9$ between $K_{zz} = 10^{8}$ and $10^{10}$
(\texttt{LHS1140b/kzz\_decision.md}, Sect.~3), so the open question is the
different one of whether the \emph{shape} carries anything. This section
measures that, and the answer is that it does not carry the conclusions of
this memo, for a reason that can be stated in one line: of the profile,
EXHALE consumes elemental ratios and thermodynamics, and both are the same at
the match to better than $1.2$ per cent under either form.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What was tested.} The saturated gravity-wave form,
%
\begin{equation}
  K_{zz}(p) = 1.0\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{cm^2\,s^{-1}}
  \left(\frac{p}{1.1\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{bar}}\right)^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:kzzpow}
\end{equation}
%
anchored at the top of the profile and not at $1$\,bar. The anchor is the
point of the test rather than a detail of it: EXHALE gives every cell above
the shallowest tabulated level that level's value
(\texttt{eddy\_diffusion\_on\_grid}, Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem}), so anchoring
at the top leaves the number the wind inherits above the file exactly what it
was and confines the change to the interval the file describes.
Equation~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) then implies $1.05\times10^{8}$ at the $1\,\mu$bar
match, $1.05\times10^{5}$ at $1$\,bar and $2.56\times10^{4}$ at the
$16.73$\,bar deep boundary: a factor $9.5$ below the constant at the match,
and four decades below it at the bottom.

Two arms separate the chemistry from the wind, because the profile hands over
both:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2ex
\item \emph{A, chemistry only.} The column is solved on
      Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) and then its \texttt{Kzz} column alone is written
      back to the constant $10^{9}$ before the handoff, so the wind sees the
      eddy coefficient it saw before and only the composition it is handed
      changes.
\item \emph{B, chemistry and wind.} The profile as produced; the wind reads
      $K_{zz}(p)$ as well, but only where the file speaks. The column
      reaches $1.0091\,R_{\rm p}$ above the match, and every cell above that
      takes the top level's value, so the wind-side change is a shell nine
      thousandths of a radius deep in which the coefficient is $9.5$ times
      lower at the base and equal at the top. The homopause under the
      constant sits at $1.056\,R_{\rm p}$
      (\texttt{kzz\_decision.md}, Sect.~3), outside that shell. \emph{Arm B
      is therefore a bounded test of the wind side and not a test of
      lowering $K_{zz}$ where helium settles}; the unbounded version of that
      is the magnitude scan, which is already measured.
\end{itemize}
%
The reference is the constant-$K_{zz}$ profile re-solved with the same seed
and the same binary, so the comparison is not against a number recorded on an
earlier occasion; the recorded number is carried in the tables anyway,
because the difference between it and the reference is the size of the
workflow's own path spread. Two reservoirs of the earlier closed ladder were
run, one at each end of it: the $11.11$ rung
(\texttt{flux\_closure/heh11p1}, $k=1$) and the $2.09$ one on its
high-trial-flux closure branch (\texttt{flux\_closure/hi}, $k=6$). Runs and
the measurement script: \texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/kzz\_power/}. The test has
since been repeated run by run on the current binary
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/xuvkzz/}, the two ``recorded, other
seed'' arms in \texttt{L11p1} and \texttt{L2p09}), and the reasoning it was first
reported under (that the coefficient is common to both sides of every
entry, so a ratio between two chemistries at fixed everything else does not
turn on it) is confirmed rather than overturned: every ratio the test
reports moves by less than the workflow's own path spread. The table below
is the repeated version.

\noindent\textbf{What did move: the carriers, by up to four decades.} The
lower part of Table~\ref{tab:kzzformmatch} is the real effect of the eddy
form, and it is large. Nitrogen changes carrier outright: on the $11.11$ arm
$q_{\rm NH_3}$ at the match falls from $6.32\times10^{-6}$ to
$2.43\times10^{-8}$ while $q_{\rm N_2}$ rises from $1.21\times10^{-7}$ to
$3.46\times10^{-6}$, so a column that was NH$_3$-dominated becomes
N$_2$-dominated. Carbon partly follows, CH$_4$ down by $2.3$ and CO up by
$55$; CO$_2$ rises by nearly four decades from a negligible base. Deeper the
redistribution is more extreme still (at $1$\,bar the same ratios are
$1.3\times10^{5}$ for CO, $3.0\times10^{7}$ for CO$_2$ and
$8.8\times10^{3}$ for N$_2$) because that is where the two forms differ
most. \emph{Elemental} nitrogen at the match nevertheless moves by $-1.2$ per
cent, because a carrier swap conserves nuclei, and elemental nuclei are what
the handoff transmits.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The crossing, against the path spread.} Converted with the
local slope $\mathrm{d}\ln EW/\mathrm{d}\ln(\mathrm{He/H}) = 0.382$ measured
over the $11.11$--$12.01$ interval of the ladder these arms belong to, the
four equivalent widths move that ladder's crossing from $11.73$ to $11.61$
and $11.63$ on
the $11.11$ arm and to $11.48$ and $11.62$ on the $2.09$ one: $-0.25$ to
$-0.10$ in He/H, under a tenth of the $10.92$--$12.58$ interval the
measurement's own $1\sigma$ band allowed there. All four now move it the
same way, downward. In relative terms that is
$-2.1$ to $-0.8$ per cent, and it is the relative figure that carries over
to the present crossing. It is also no larger than the
workflow's path spread: re-solving the \emph{same} profile from a different
seed moves the equivalent width by $1.08$ per cent on the $11.11$ arm and
$0.38$ per cent on the $2.09$ one, against the $0.32$--$0.83$ per cent the
eddy form produces. The signal is buried in the spread of the procedure that
would have to measure it: by a narrower margin than before, $1.08$ against
$0.83$ per cent where the previous branching gave $1.29$ against $0.79$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{An objection to the test itself, which has to be stated.}
The deep half of Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) is not the right law. The $P^{-1/2}$
growth is the saturated gravity-wave scaling of a \emph{stratified} region
(Lindzen 1981), and the climate solve puts this planet's tropopause at
$2.636$\,bar: below it the column is convective, where the mixing-length form
of Gierasch \& Conrath applies and $K_{zz}$ should flatten or grow with
depth, not collapse. Nor is the amplitude defensible there. At $1$\,bar
Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) gives $1.05\times10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, which is at
or below the floor of the modelling literature, one and a half to two and
a half decades under the $3\times10^{6}$--$3\times10^{7}$ Charnay et al.\
(2015) use for GJ\,1214\,b, $3.7$ decades under the $5\times10^{8}$ of
Parmentier et al.\ (2013) for HD\,209458\,b, and equal to the $10^{5}$ floor
Ackerman \& Marley (2001) impose by hand. The physically correct
construction is two-branch, a wave law above the radiative--convective
boundary joined to a mixing-length law below it; this test ran one branch all
the way down to $16.73$\,bar. \emph{Read as interpretation}: that is very
likely why the carriers moved as far as they did, since the $0.1$--$10$\,bar
interval the single branch over-quenches is the one that fixes where CH$_4$
and NH$_3$ quench. But the objection does not reach the verdict: a form
this wrong in the deep column still failed to move an elemental ratio at the
match by more than $1.2$ per cent.

At the other end the same equation is on firmer ground than the adopted
constant. Its value at the match, $1.05\times10^{8}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, falls
inside the $2.8\times10^{7}$--$1.4\times10^{8}$ that the Arfaux \& Lavvas
homopause scaling gives when it is evaluated with \emph{this} planet's
gravity and temperature: the one estimate in the survey derived from
LHS\,1140\,b itself, and the direction in which
\texttt{docs/eddy\_diffusion\_kzz.tex} already records the adopted $10^{9}$
as having no planet-specific basis.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Verdict.} The constant assumption was carrying a great deal
of the lower atmosphere's carrier chemistry (up to four decades), and
none of this memo's conclusions. What EXHALE consumes from the profile is the
elemental ratios and the thermodynamics; those agree at the match to $1.2$
per cent under a form that differs from the constant by a factor $9.5$ there
and four decades at the bottom, because the match is still well mixed under
either ($1.05\times10^{8}$ is $200$ times the molecular coefficient of
$\simeq 4.6\times10^{5}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ at the base,
\texttt{kzz\_decision.md} Sect.~1). \emph{What the results carry is the
magnitude of $K_{zz}$ (the $10^{8}$--$10^{10}$ bracket, across which the
equivalent width moves by a factor $9$), and not its pressure form.} One
limit on the statement: it is a statement about this anchor. Anchoring
Eq.~(\ref{eq:kzzpow}) at $1\,\mu$bar instead would raise by $9.5$ the value
the wind inherits over its whole domain, which is a change of magnitude and
therefore a different test; it was not run.

where a closed form assumes it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Against the published estimate.} Cherubim et al.\ (2026)
quote $f_{\rm H2O} \sim 7$\,ppm from a closed-form cold trap at a $0.1$\,bar
tropopause with $T_{\rm skin} = 194$\,K. The climate solution gives
$0.13$--$0.33$\,ppm at a tropopause of $0.5$--$1.0$\,bar: a factor $20$--$50$
less water at a tropopause $5$--$10\times$ deeper. The saturation physics
agrees: $p_{\rm sat}(185\,{\rm K})/1\,{\rm bar}$ is $1.3\times10^{-7}$ and
$p_{\rm sat}(194\,{\rm K})/0.1\,{\rm bar}$ about $5\times10^{-6}$, so each
number follows from its own tropopause, and what differs is where the
tropopause is put. Ours is a solved level in a helium-dominated column
($\mu = 3.6$ at ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$), theirs an assumed one. This is
reported as a result about the climate model and was not tuned toward the
published number.

What the trap does to the handoff is a depletion with height, not a
condensate sitting at the base: gas-phase oxygen falls from
${\rm O/H} = 6.062\times10^{-4}$ at the deep boundary to
$4.957\times10^{-7}$ at the match, a factor $1223$, while C/H
($2.778\times10^{-4}$), N/H ($8.187\times10^{-5}$) and He/H cross the
tropopause unchanged, the carriers of carbon and nitrogen in this column
are CH$_4$ and NH$_3$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:photochem}), and this solution keeps
both of them in the gas phase across the trap
(Figs.~\ref{fig:closure}c and~\ref{fig:pccol2}a--c).

the closure is single-valued on this planet. The damping was never exercised
(the residual fell on every iteration), but the $3\times$ arm used six of
its eight allowed steps, so a start further than an order of magnitude out
would need a larger iteration budget.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{One systematic on this branch is larger than anything the
closure resolves, and it is stated rather than corrected.} A four-pass JFNK
re-solve of one and the same arm (identical \texttt{input.inp}, identical
handoff column, identical binary, each pass seeded from the previous one and
each reaching \texttt{info}\,$=0$ at $\|R\| \simeq 10^{-4}$) lands on
different fixed points depending on which converged wind it started from:
on the $1.0\times$ arm, $EW = 0.519$, $0.532$ and $0.542$\,\%\,\AA{} from
three seeds, a spread of $4.6$ per cent. Four further passes move each
lineage by at most $0.23$ per cent and leave the flux spread and the base
temperature unmoved, so these are three fixed points of the protocol and not
three truncations of one; the steady-wind diffusion outer pass reports three
different composition drifts ($5.9$, $6.2$ and $7.3\times10^{-4}$), which is
where the hysteresis lives. Every profile-branch line number in this memo
therefore carries a seed systematic of that order, which is larger than the
$1.8$ per cent the secondary-ionization correction of
\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96 is worth on these arms. The comparisons here
are internally consistent because each holds the seed protocol fixed; the
hysteresis itself is not diagnosed
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/closure/results.txt}, Sect.~3).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The composition is an output for the first time, and at
this eddy coefficient it is the well-mixed value.} He/H at the match comes
back at $2.09235$ against the $2.09$ the reservoir was started from, a change
of $1.1\times10^{-3}$, of which only $2\times10^{-4}$ comes from the escape
flux itself. The reason is measured: at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ eddy mixing homogenizes the column all
the way to $1\,\mu$bar, so a $1.8\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ hydrogen escape
imposed at the model top moves He/H by $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at that top and by
$2\times10^{-4}$ at the match. \emph{It is the eddy coefficient, not the
escape flux, that sets the composition at the matching level.} Closing the
loop therefore does not retire the conditionality of
Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}; it relocates it, onto the same adopted $K_{zz}$.

Above the match the wind separates the two elements as strongly as before.
The elemental fluxes carry $F_{\rm He}/F_{\rm H} = 0.73$ by mass against the
base reservoir's $8.31$, and He/H falls from $2.09$ at the base to $0.183$ at
$30\,R_p$. The closure does not weaken that: it says the fractionation
happens in the wind and not below the match. One caveat on the budget, stated
rather than corrected: the diffusion operator's mass fraction is that of the
H\,$+$\,He mixture and the trace metals ride inside $\rho$, so the measured
$F_{\rm H}$ is hydrogen plus metals and overstates elemental hydrogen by
$4.8\times10^{-4}$, two decades below the tolerance.

\subsection{What the base metals do, and what the closed solution does not
reproduce}
\label{sec:basemetals}

The profile brings something the scalar base never had: the carbon, nitrogen
and oxygen of the photochemical column. Isolating it takes a ladder of
controls in which the scalar-base run is moved one key at a time onto the
profile's own base state, every rung a converged solution
(\texttt{Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~78). Setting the base helium ratio to the
profile's $2.0921$ moves $\log_{10}\dot M$ by less than $0.01$; setting the
base temperature to the solved $185.05$\,K, from the $226$\,K the scalar runs
used, moves it by less than $0.01$ more: the base temperature is not the
lever. Adding the base radius moves it to $7.85$ (the molecular fraction
does not enter: \texttt{q\_H2\_base} is inert unless \texttt{Molecular base}
is set, which neither route sets). Adding the profile's C/H, N/H and O/H
takes it to $\mathbf{7.49}$, and the red-pair depth down by a factor
$\mathbf{2.9}$ on that rung ($5.59 \rightarrow 1.93$ per cent):
$-0.36$\,dex in $\dot M$ and that much of a shallower helium line, from
metal-line cooling of a $185$\,K base at ${\rm C/H} = 2.8\times10^{-4}$.
Over the ladder end to end the same pair is $-0.32$\,dex and a factor $2.7$.
The profile route reproduces that rung to $0.00$\,dex in $\dot M$ and $0.5$
per cent in the line, inside the workflow's own path spread, which is the
consistency check the two routes exist to give. The six rungs are re-solved
in \texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/basemetals/}, at
$\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 1.0\times10^{-4}$ on the four scalar rungs and
$4.0\times10^{-4}$ and $5.0\times10^{-4}$ on the two metal ones. The scalar
rung and the scalar column of Table~\ref{tab:thermostat} are now the same
configuration at the same tolerance and give $1.420$ and $1.424$\,\%\,\AA,
$0.3$ per cent apart, which is the seed-path spread of this workflow.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The consequence for the line is that the flux-closed
solution does not reproduce the measured equivalent width.} At
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ and a metal-free base, ${\rm He/H} = 2.13$ gives
$EW = 1.424$\,\%\,\AA. The flux-closed solution sits at the same eddy
coefficient and essentially the same reservoir, $2.09235$, and gives
$0.545$\,\%\,\AA{}: a factor $2.0$ below the observed
$1.108 \pm 0.030$, and $18.8\sigma$ from it. The difference is entirely the
base metals beneath it. Both equivalent widths are re-solved on the current
binary (\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/refresh\_j96/thermostat/}), and the
metal-free-to-closed ratio between them, $2.6$, has now survived two
successive corrections to the physics: the Penning replacement raised the
pair by $19$ and $22$ per cent, and the composition-renormalized
secondary-ionization split of \emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96 by a further
$4.9$ and $4.4$, moving the ratio by $0.5$ per cent. What the comparison
establishes (that at one reservoir
the photochemical base takes the line down by a large factor) is a
comparison at fixed reservoir and does not turn on either coefficient.

So closing the elemental fluxes and matching the equivalent width are two
different constraints, and the solution presented at $2.09$ satisfies the
first only. Fixing the reservoir by the line, as Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}
and~\ref{sec:diffbroad} do, was done above a base carrying no metals; with
the photochemical column beneath it the same reservoir absorbs several times
less, so the composition that satisfies both conditions at once is larger
than the metal-free crossing. Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder} solves for it,
at the adopted $K_{zz}$, and returns $\mathrm{He/H} = 8.21$ against the
$1.61$ the same line returns above the metal-free base, a factor $5.1$,
the same factor the earlier draft measured between $11.7$ and $2.09$. So the
lower boundary is an
axis of its own, of the same standing as the assumed spectrum, the assumed
mixing and composition itself: what is placed beneath the wind is not a free
parameter once the lower atmosphere is solved, but is set by the elemental
vector and the cold trap of the column below.

\subsubsection*{The mechanism: helium as a thermostat}

Why the same reservoir absorbs differently over the two bases can be read
off the solved structures directly. Table~\ref{tab:thermostat} measures the
three solutions that bracket the question: the metal-free scalar base at
${\rm He/H} = 2.13$, which reproduces the line
(seeded from \texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/heh2p13\_diff\_kzz1e9/}); the
flux-closed solution
on the photochemical column at $2.09$, which does not
(\texttt{.../flux\_closure/hi/k06/}); and the closed solution at $10.31$,
the rung just below the crossing (\texttt{.../flux\_closure/heh10p3/k01/}).
All three run with H/He element diffusion active at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^{2}$\,s$^{-1}$ (constant through the profile in
the two profile runs, the scalar value in the third), and with EXHALE's
molecular chemistry off, so no molecular carrier appears in the wind. Mean
mass per particle is $(n_{\rm H} + 4n_{\rm He})/(n_{\rm H} + n_{\rm He} +
n_e)$; nuclei are counted as H\,\textsc{i}\,$+$\,H\,\textsc{ii} and
He\,\textsc{i}\,$+$\,He\,\textsc{ii}\,$+$\,He\,\textsc{iii}. The values are
from the advection-corrected profiles; the reference arm of the closure
(\texttt{.../flux\_closure/ref/k00/}) reproduces the $2.09$ column to $0.6\%$
or better in every entry.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Adding the base metals cools the wind, and the line loses
its outer column.} With no metals, radiative cooling is $7.3$--$10.4\%$ of the
heating over $1.2$--$2\,R_p$ and the wind is limited by expansion rather
than by radiation. With the column's C, N and O beneath it, cooling reaches
$75\%$ of the heating at $1.2\,R_p$, of which $95\%$ is metal-line cooling
and the largest single contributor is neutral C\,\textsc{i}; $T_{\max}$
falls from $5447$ to $3955$\,K. The metastable peak does not fall with it:
it rises, $54.0 \rightarrow 60.4$\,cm$^{-3}$. The column inside
$2\,R_p$ does fall, but only by $18\%$ ($37.4$ against $30.7$), against the
$2.6$ the column outside $2\,R_p$ loses: $50.3 \rightarrow
19.7$\,cm$^{-3}R_p$, the same factor the equivalent width falls
by ($1.424 \rightarrow 0.545$\,\%\,\AA). What the metals take is therefore
overwhelmingly the outer column: the cooler wind has the smaller scale
height, so it does not carry the metastable population out to where it adds
absorbing area.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Raising the reservoir reheats it, by two terms that are
both measured.} From $2.09$ to $10.31$ on the same base, $T_{\max}$ goes
$3955 \rightarrow 5898$\,K. Two things change together. First, there are
fewer particles to share the deposited energy: the mean mass per particle at
$2\,R_p$ rises from $1.52$ to $2.53\,m_{\rm H}$, a factor $1.67$, and the
heating rate at $2\,R_p$ divided by the particle density rises by $1.98$,
with $n_{\rm tot}$ on the code's \texttt{calc\_ntot} convention, one
particle per species and electrons excluded, as elsewhere in this section,
while $T(2\,R_p)$ rises by $1.71$. The three factors agreeing to within
$20\%$ is a
consistency check on that reading, not a proof of it: the cooling term is
not held fixed between the two runs. Second, the coolant is diluted along
with the hydrogen it is referred to: the profile states C, N and O per
hydrogen nucleus (schema column \texttt{X\_*[El/H nuclei]}, and in the code
$n_X = \mathtt{melem\_ab}(e)\,n_{\rm H}$), and the two profiles carry the
same ${\rm C/H} = 2.778\times10^{-4}$ to four digits, so when
$n({\rm H\,\textsc{i}})$ at $2\,R_p$ falls by $4.1$ the carbon density falls
with it by the same factor. The volumetric metal cooling nonetheless
\emph{rises}, $4.05 \rightarrow 6.11\times10^{-10}$\,erg\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$
at $1.5\,R_p$, because the cooling function is steeply temperature
dependent; what the dilution does is let the gas settle at the higher
temperature at which that happens.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the metastable balance does with that.} The 2\,$^3S$
population in these solutions is fed almost entirely by recombination and
drained by Penning ionization on neutral hydrogen and by electron collisional
de-excitation. Reconstructing the residual EXHALE solves: source
$n_e n_{\rm He\,\textsc{ii}}\alpha_{2^3S}$ plus collisional excitation from
the singlet ground state, against radiative decay $A_{31}$, photoionization,
destruction on H\,\textsc{i}, electron
collisional de-excitation to $2\,^1S$ and $2\,^1P$, and electron collisional
ionization (\texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}, \texttt{tr\_triplet\_row})
reproduces the tabulated $n(2\,^3S)$ to five digits, so the budget can be
apportioned. The apportionment below is measured on the three re-solved
solutions, i.e.\ on the Garc\'ia Mu\~noz rate. The direction the retired
rate implied is confirmed: relative to the Taylor et al.\ (2025) fit both
lifetimes lengthen and both Penning shares fall.
At the metastable peak, collisional excitation supplies less than
$10^{-13}$ of the source, and radiative decay, photoionization and collisional
ionization together take $0.01\%$ of the sink. What is left is: at
${\rm He/H} = 2.09$, Penning $88\%$ and electron de-excitation $12\%$ of the
loss; at $10.31$, Penning $57\%$ and electron de-excitation $43\%$. Between
the two, the source rate rises by $1.92$ (more He\,\textsc{ii} and more
electrons in the hotter gas, against a recombination coefficient that falls
with temperature) and the metastable lifetime lengthens by $1.54$, from
$0.53$ to $0.82$\,s: hydrogen has been thinned by $2.3$ at the peak, so
Penning removes the level more slowly, and the faster electron
de-excitation of the hotter gas takes back only part of it. The product,
$2.95$, is the peak ratio $60.4 \rightarrow 178.5$. \emph{There is no
charge-exchange term on the metastable in this code} (the He/H charge
exchange acts on ground-state He and He$^+$), so it is not part of this
balance.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Read as an interpretation}, the thermal structure of the
$10.31$ solution ($T_{\max} = 5898$\,K, column $122.1$) has recovered and
slightly passed that of the metal-free $2.13$ solution ($5447$\,K, $87.7$),
which is why the equivalent width comes back to the measured value there.
The ladder therefore looks less like a determination of composition than
like a thermal trade between the metal content of the base and the helium
content above it, and one line cannot separate the two. Helium in these
solutions is not only the absorber; it is also the thermostat that sets how
far the absorber is carried.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the wind is made of, and how ionized it is.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} carries the elemental fractions and the ionization
state of the same three solutions, on the same advection-corrected profiles
as Table~\ref{tab:thermostat}. Two things follow from it that the thermostat
argument rests on.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Diffusion keeps the wind far less helium-rich than the
reservoir.} On both branches the helium fraction falls steeply through the
line-forming region and then flattens: ${\rm He/H} = 2.09$ at the base gives
$0.236$ by nuclei at $2\,R_p$ and $0.189$ beyond $5\,R_p$ on this arm
($0.189$ on the reference arm quoted in Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}); $10.31$
gives $1.413$ and $1.148$. That is a factor $8.9$ and $7.3$ between the
reservoir and the wind at $2\,R_p$, and $11.0$ and $9.0$ beyond $5\,R_p$, in
a solution where the reservoir is an output of the closure
rather than a choice. The consequence is worth stating in the composition's
own terms: the flux-closed solution at $2.09$ (a reservoir that is $68$
per cent helium by number) has a \emph{hydrogen-dominated} line-forming
region, $n_{\rm H}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.81$ against
$n_{\rm He}/n_{\rm tot} = 0.19$ at $2\,R_p$. At $10.31$ helium still leads
there, $0.59$ against $0.41$, but by a factor $7$ less than the reservoir
states. A composition quoted for this planet therefore has to say at which
level it is quoted; the reservoir and the absorbing layer are not the same
number, and the memo's crossings are reservoir values.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The hotter solution is also the more ionized one, and it is
not shielding.} At $2\,R_p$, $x({\rm H\,\textsc{ii}}) = 0.030$ on the
photochemical base at $2.09$ and $0.104$ at $10.31$: a factor $3.4$, in
the run whose neutral hydrogen density is $4.1$ times lower. The obvious
reading, that thinner hydrogen shields itself less, is contradicted by the
outputs: the H\,\textsc{i} column above $2\,R_p$ gives
$\tau(13.6\,{\rm eV}) = 0.23$ and $0.055$, so both are optically thin there,
and the H\,\textsc{i} photoelectric heating per neutral hydrogen atom is the
same in the two, $1.21$ against $1.19\times10^{-18}$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$ per atom.
The recombination sink moves little: with $\alpha_B$ as the code defines it
(\texttt{rec\_HII\_B}, the Badnell radiative rate less the Mao \& Kaastra
$n=1$ term), $\alpha_B(T)\,n_e$ is
$1.72$ against $1.41\times10^{-6}$\,s$^{-1}$, a factor $1.22$. What grows is the ionization of
hydrogen \emph{by helium}. Measured on the equilibrium state at $2\,R_p$, the
two channels that can be computed in closed form from the outputs are Penning
ionization He($2\,^3S$)$+$H, which supplies $32\%$ of what case-B
recombination removes at $2.09$ and $22\%$ at $10.31$ (a falling share but
a rate per neutral hydrogen atom $2.0$ times higher), and the charge
exchange He$^+ +$H, at $3.6\%$ and $2.9\%$. The rest, $65\%$ and $75\%$, is
photoionization, and inside it the helium-fed parts are what scale: the
He-recombination-radiation channel (He\,\textsc{ii} recombining to ground,
whose $\ge 24.6$\,eV continuum ionizes H) carries $6.2$ times more heating per
neutral hydrogen atom in the $10.31$ solution, and the energy deposited by
He\,\textsc{i} photoionization per neutral hydrogen atom, part of which
secondary ionization turns into hydrogen ionizations, is $6.5$ times higher.
\emph{What could not be settled from the outputs} is the split inside that
$66$--$77\%$: the individual rates (direct $\Gamma_{\rm H\,\textsc{i}}$,
the He-recombination-driven addition to it, and the secondary-ionization
yield) are not written to any output file, and the factors quoted here are
read off the heating columns on the assumption that the mean photoelectron
energy is similar between the two runs. The one channel that can be excluded
outright is $n=2$ photoionization: \texttt{heat\_Hpe[excitedH]} is exactly
zero at every cell of all three runs. Helium ionization itself tracks the
temperature more simply: $x({\rm He\,\textsc{ii}})$ is $0.055$, $0.065$
and $0.081$ at $2\,R_p$ across the three, and
$x({\rm He\,\textsc{iii}})$ is at most $2.1\times10^{-4}$ at $2\,R_p$
($9.5\times10^{-5}$, $1.4\times10^{-4}$ and $2.1\times10^{-4}$), rising to
at most $2.9$, $4.4$ and $6.4\times10^{-4}$ by $2.98\,R_p$.

\noindent Fig.~\ref{fig:beststruct} shows the same kind of structure for the

base metals; Fig.~\ref{fig:compprof} is the set the lower-boundary axis is

\subsection{The composition the line needs above a photochemical base}
\label{sec:closureladder}

Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals} left the composition that closes the fluxes
\emph{and} reproduces the line unsolved. This section solves for it on the
composition axis, at the adopted eddy coefficient. What comes out is the
answer on the solved-column branch alone: it is the counterpart of the
$1.61$ that the same line returns above a prescribed metal-free base
(Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}), not a replacement for it, and the two differ
by the base and not by the mixing, which is $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ with
diffusion active in both. The setup: the closure driver is run at nineteen
reservoirs between $7.0$ and $9.5$. Fifteen of them, everything at $8.0$ and
above, come \emph{from one common parent} (the converged $9.71$ arm of the
earlier ladder, so no reservoir step exceeds $1.21\times$); that parent is
$1.39\times$ away from $7.0$, past the $1.35\times$ this series holds to, so
the four arms below $8.0$ take the new $8.0$ arm as their parent instead, a
step of at most $1.14\times$. The crossing and the $-1\sigma$ end lie in the
single-parent region. Each converged iterate's wind is then re-solved on its
own output with the profile held fixed, so that arms which stopped at
different $k$ are read at the same place. Everything else is the
configuration of Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}, Photochem with the
\texttt{clima} step solved, $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$, a $1\,\mu$bar match, the
GJ\,1132 proxy spectrum, a $5\%$ residual tolerance and $k_{\max} = 8$. The
runs and the measurement scripts are
\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96/}. An earlier version of this ladder
was solved twice on the pre-Sect.~96 branching, once with Photochem 0.8.4
(\texttt{crossings\_gm25}) and once with 0.9.0 and the local solver
corrections (\texttt{crossings\_pc090}), which is what the
chemistry-version caveat of Sect.~1 is measured on.

The earlier ladder of nine arms between $2.09$ and $12.0$, seeded one step
at a time, is superseded by this one and is not re-tabulated. What it
established and this ladder confirms is recorded where it belongs:
Sect.~\ref{sec:outerinherit} keeps the discarded arm that showed a
converged wind inheriting its outer region from its seed.

Every arm returns a matching-level He/H equal to its reservoir to four
digits, for the reason Sect.~\ref{sec:closure} measured at $2.09$: at
$K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ the eddy-mixed column reaches the microbar match, so the
escape flux does not set the composition there. What the ladder adds is the
line.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The crossing.} The arms at $8.21$ and $8.26$ straddle the
measured equivalent width directly, $1.1075$ below it and $1.1098$ above
against $1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA, and every rung from $7.51$ to $9.31$
is itself inside the measurement's $1\sigma$ band. The crossing is therefore
read inside a bracket the runs measured, not off the end of a scan:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{He/H} = 8.212 \quad (7.474\text{--}9.395 \text{ on the } EW\
  1\sigma\ \text{interval}),
\end{equation}
where the range carries the measurement error alone and none of the model
uncertainty, and both of its edges are bracketed by solved arms as well.
Two readings of the same ladder agree on the center: chord interpolation in
$\log$--$\log$ between the two straddling rungs gives $8.2117$ and a
quadratic in $\log$--$\log$ gives $8.2115$, $0.00$ per cent apart.

This is the crossing of the five that moved most when the
secondary-ionization split was renormalized on the composition
(\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96), from $9.048$ to $8.212$. It is not because
the closed solution is more sensitive to the split than the others: at
reservoir $9.0$ the split raises the equivalent width by $1.2$ per cent at a
frozen lower-atmosphere profile and by $2.0$ per cent once the closure is
re-run, which is the same size as the $1.4$ and $1.6$ per cent the
well-mixed and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ arms move. It is that this ladder is the
shallowest of the five in composition: its equivalent width rises as
$(\mathrm{He/H})^{0.27}$ across the whole ladder, and as
$(\mathrm{He/H})^{0.20}$ over the stretch between the old crossing and the
new one, where the well-mixed and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ ladders rise as
$^{0.76}$, the plateau as $^{0.95}$ and the GJ\,699 one as $^{0.93}$,
so the same move in the line is the largest move in the reservoir. The chemistry correction that came before it
did \emph{not} move the crossing: on the pre-Sect.~96 branching the
Photochem 0.8.4 ladder and the 0.9.0 one gave $9.0484$ and $9.0461$ to
every digit either printed.

\emph{The center is read as an interpolation between $8.21$ and $8.26$, a
bracket $0.05$ wide in reservoir, and has also been solved directly}: the
closure run at reservoir $\mathrm{He/H} = 8.2117$, chemistry, wind,
elemental-flux measurement and under-relaxed update, then its converged
iterate's wind re-solved to its own fixed point as every arm of record was,
gives $EW = 1.107775$\,\%\,\AA{} against the $1.107999$ the chord
asserts, a difference of $2.2\times10^{-4}$\,\%\,\AA{} or $0.008\sigma$
(\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}). \emph{And the ladder is not
smooth at this resolution.} The local power-law slope
$\mathrm{d}\ln EW/\mathrm{d}\ln(\mathrm{He/H})$ over the successive
intervals runs from $0.05$ to $0.40$ about a mean of $0.27$: the equivalent
width rises by $0.0023$\,\%\,\AA{} between $8.21$ and $8.26$ and by only
$0.0004$ over the equally wide interval $8.26$--$8.31$. That step is not a
lineage artifact: re-closing the $8.3$ arm from its $8.2$ neighbour
instead of from the common parent reproduces it to $0.01$ per cent, and the
$9.0$ arm to $0.12$ per cent, and unlike the earlier ladder's it does not
track the iteration count, since both arms of the flat interval stop at
$k = 2$ at residuals $0.034$ and $0.033$. \emph{It is the closure's own
$\mathrm{tol} = 0.05$, made visible.} What the sublinearity settles is that
the equivalent width does \emph{not} saturate before the measurement is
reached, which the helium-rich end of the metal-free ladder of
Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} had made a live possibility.

At the crossing the escape rate is $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.40$, i.e.\
$2.5\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor $8$ below the
$2.03^{+0.58}_{-0.67}\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ the p-winds retrieval
imposes, and the elemental fluxes are $F_{\rm H} = 5.5\times10^{6}$ and
$F_{\rm He} = 2.0\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$.

\medskip
\noindent Two statements follow, and they are worth separating.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The closure by itself does not select a reservoir on this
      planet.} Every rung of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} satisfies it, at
      elemental residuals of $2.3$--$4.7\%$ against the $5\%$ tolerance.
      What selects $8.21$ is the line, exactly as in
      Sects.~\ref{sec:ew} and~\ref{sec:diffbroad}. What the closure
      contributes is that the value is now consistent with a photochemical
      lower atmosphere carrying its own C, N and O, rather than resting on a
      bare H/He base, and the price of that consistency is a helium
      fraction of $89$ per cent by number. Read against the $1.61$ the same
      line returns above a metal-free base, the pair measures the thermal
      trade of Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals} rather than two attempts at one
      number.
\item \textbf{The escape rate is flat along the whole ladder}, $7.39$ to
      $7.40$ in $\log_{10}\dot M$ over a factor $1.3$ in reservoir, and
      $7.39$--$7.50$ once the earlier arms down to $2.09$ are included. So
      here too the composition is fixed by the line and not by the wind, and
      the $\dot M$ this memo quotes does not depend on where in the ladder
      the crossing lands.
\item \textbf{A seed systematic sits under all of it.} Three arms
      re-closed from the neighbour below instead of from the common parent
      move by $0.2$--$1.9$ per cent in equivalent width, which at the local
      slope is of order half a unit in reservoir: larger than the
      $0.10$-wide bracket the crossing is read in, and invisible to every
      test the driver applies. The crossing quoted here is the crossing of
      the single-parent ladder.
\end{itemize}

The wind residual the JFNK finish reaches degrades as the reservoir grows
(the line search stalls on the base contact mode and returns its best
iterate), so the target was raised arm by arm and the achieved values
recorded rather than smoothed over: $\|R\| = 1.5$--$3.1\times10^{-4}$ at
$\|R\|_{\rm tol} = 4.0\times10^{-4}$, all at \texttt{info} $=0$. The test
the closure actually applies is not that residual but the elemental
residual $\varepsilon$ of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder}; the radial spread
of the flux over the steady window it is measured on is $0.9$--$2.7\%$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What the crossing does not fix, and what it inherits.} The
line is still too narrow: FWHM $0.280$\,\AA{} at the crossing against the
measured $0.841$, unchanged from Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} and from the
diffusion-off solutions, so the modelled red depth of $3.7$ per cent
overshoots the measured $1.254$ per cent by the same factor the width falls
short, which is why the composition here is inferred from the equivalent
width and not from the depth. And the winds these numbers come from carry
the caveat of Sect.~\ref{sec:validity} in full: they are subsonic to the
$30\,R_p$ domain edge, their mass flux is set at the outer boundary rather
than at a critical point, and the critical region is transitional, so the
$\log_{10}\dot M = 7.40$ quoted above is \emph{unvalidated as a
continuum solution}. The He\,10830 layer that fixes $8.21$ is not: it sits
at $\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.019$.

\subsection{The outer region a converged wind inherits}
\label{sec:outerinherit}

The effect was found on the earlier ladder, at its $12.01$ rung, and it is
why the ladder of Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} is climbed from one common
parent. That rung was first reached in one step from the $8.0$ arm, a
$1.50\times$ jump in reservoir; a control run reached the same reservoir
from $11.11$, a $1.08\times$ step. The two solutions agree on
everything the closure and the chemistry set, and disagree on the line:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Same reservoir}: He/H at the match $12.013522$ against
      $12.013520$, seven digits.
\item \emph{Same lower atmosphere}: cold-trap O/H $1.0656\times10^{-6}$
      against $1.0660\times10^{-6}$ nuclei per H.
\item \emph{Both converged}: \texttt{info}~$=0$ at
      $\|R\| = 1.866\times10^{-4}$ (jump-seeded) and $1.287\times10^{-4}$
      (control), both inside the $4.0\times10^{-4}$ target the arm was run
      to, and both inside the closure's own flux-spread tolerance.
\item \emph{Different line}: $EW = 1.4293$ against $1.2094$\,\%\,\AA{} and a
      red depth of $4.789$ against $3.956$ per cent, an $18$ per cent
      difference in equivalent width at fixed composition.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The difference sits entirely in the outer wind. Two diagnostics
that do not depend on where a spectral window is placed separate the two
solutions, and both are monotonic along the ladder except at the discarded
point:

gives $19.5$, and about twice the outer column share of any other rung
($2.05$ times its control's, $1.67$ times the largest any other rung

against $1959$, $5.8$ per cent apart), so that quantity no longer carries
the point that the two agree on the base; what carries it is the
matching-level composition, identical to seven digits, and the profile ratio

identical base.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Read as the mechanism}: the outer state is inherited from the
seed and the JFNK finish does not re-solve it. The density there is low
enough that it contributes almost nothing to the residual norm, so $\|R\|$
cannot tell the two states apart: both report \texttt{info}~$=0$ at
residuals inside the target, and which of the two reports the smaller one is
not a property of which is right: on the current branching, as on the one
before it, the discarded
solution reports the larger residual, and only on the retired Penning
coefficient did it report the smaller. He\,10830 is
optically thick at these columns, so a hot, extended outer region fills the
line wings and raises the equivalent width even though it carries about one
per cent of the metastable column.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The physical judgement has to be stated plainly.} Measured
on the metastable profiles, the two solutions agree to $2.3$ per cent out to
$11.9\,R_p$ and then separate abruptly (the departure passes $10$ per
cent at $11.95\,R_p$ and $50$ per cent at $12.14$, reaching a factor $5.1$),
so the region in which they differ is $12$--$30\,R_p$, not the
$20$--$30\,R_p$ an earlier draft of this paragraph gave. The exobase of
these two arms is not the $18.8$--$25.9\,R_p$ of
Sect.~\ref{sec:validity} either: that is the three-case range of
Table~\ref{tab:validity}, whose cases are two non-closure runs and the
$2.09$ closure arm. For this pair it is $11.81\,R_p$ (jump-seeded) and
$14.73$ (control). Putting the corrected radii together: for the solution
being discarded the whole differing region lies outside its own exobase,
where $\mathrm{Kn} > 1$; for its control the innermost $2.6\,R_p$ of that
region is nominally still collisional. \emph{The fluid solution is not valid
in most of the region that makes the difference, and in the discarded
solution it is not valid anywhere in it.} The ground for discarding that
solution is therefore continuity of the trend, not a physical criterion; and
by the same token the absolute equivalent widths of the whole ladder carry a
systematic error that the closure tolerance (a $5\%$ flux spread) does
not catch. Its size is small, because the outer region is about one per cent
of the metastable column, but it is not zero. The exobase itself does not
move with either correction: across the ladder it is $11.8$--$18.9\,R_p$ on
the current branching against $11.8$--$18.8$ on the one before it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What was and was not tested.} Path independence was
checked directly at $\mathrm{He/H} = 3.0$, from a seed $3.7\times$ away in
composition and in the downward direction the ladder never takes: it returns
the same $r_{\rm drop}$ to the digit, with everything else inside the
closure tolerance. The $5.0$, $8.0$ and $2.09$ arms were not checked this
way. Separately, the $10.31$ arm was re-run from the same seed in its own
directory and reproduced bitwise, which fixes determinism but says nothing
about the seed dependence. The practical rule the episode leaves is that a
ladder scan must be climbed one step at a time; the record is
\texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md} Sect.~83.

\label{sec:xuvgrid}

$0.6$ per cent in depth. The grid below asks what fraction of the fiducial
XUV reproduces that. The spectrum is the fiducial GJ\,1132 proxy with its

(\texttt{sed/lhs1140\_sed\_gj1132\_at\_b\_xuv*.txt}; \texttt{read\_sed}

held fixed in every case (the closure loop is not re-run, and neither is

depth) is the same one the Penning replacement produced before it; its
consequence for the two crossings is not, and is the subject of the next
paragraph. The result the section rests on is the

\section{Is the wind collisional where it is set?}
\label{sec:validity}

Everything above reads the solutions as continuum solutions. A hydrodynamic
wind is one only where the gas is collisional on the scale over which the
flow varies, and above all through its critical point, which is where the
topology, and with it the mass flux, is fixed. This section measures
that. The tool is
\texttt{src/utils/collisional\_validity.py}; it reads an existing run
directory and changes nothing, and the definitions and full tables are in
\texttt{docs/collisional\_validity.md} (record:
\texttt{docs/Update\_EXHALE.md} Sects.~81 and~85). The numbers below were
measured on the outputs the rest of this memo uses; nothing was re-run.

\subsection{What is measured}

\paragraph{The collisions.} The four momentum-transfer limits are the ones
the binary element-diffusion operator of Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad} already
uses, in the same Chapman--Enskog first approximation, so a mean free path
quoted here and a diffusion coefficient used by the wind cannot drift apart:
rigid-sphere neutral--neutral, non-resonant ion--neutral with the
induced-dipole and rigid-core frictions added, and screened Coulomb with the
Spitzer $\ln\Lambda$ for ion--ion and ion--electron. The step from a binary
diffusion coefficient to a collision frequency is the definition of that
coefficient itself, $\nu_{st} = n_t k T/(\mu_{st}\hat D_{st})$ with
$\hat D = n D$, in which the total carrier density cancels. Species are
H\,\textsc{i}, H\,\textsc{ii}, He\,\textsc{i}, He\,\textsc{ii},
He\,\textsc{iii} and $e$, plus the molecular carriers where a run tracks
them; He\,\textsc{i} $2^3S$ is a level inside the He\,\textsc{i} column and
is not counted twice, and metals are dropped as trace ($<10^{-3}$ of the
particles) except in the electron budget. Two channels are deliberately
absent (resonant charge exchange and electron--neutral momentum transfer),
and both lengthen the mean free path, so the verdict they produce is the
conservative one.

\paragraph{The numbers.} $\lambda_s = \bar v_s/\nu_s$ with the harmonic sum
over partners; the local structure scale is the length over which the state
vector of the local Maxwellian changes,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{L^{2}} = \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}\right)^{2}
  + \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln T}{\mathrm{d}r}\right)^{2}
  + \left(\frac{|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|}{c_s}\right)^{2}
  + \frac{1}{r^{2}},
  \qquad L = \max\!\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cdot}},\ \Delta r_{\rm cell}\right),
\end{equation}
and $\mathrm{Kn}_s = \lambda_s/L$. \emph{The pressure is deliberately not in
it.} The continuum closure expands about a local Maxwellian, which is fixed
by $(n, T, u)$; $p = nkT$ is a derived field, so including it would count
the density and temperature gradients twice wherever they do not cancel and
contribute nothing exactly where they do. It is the second half of that
which bites: across a heating front $\rho$ falls and $T$ rises with nearly
the same logarithmic slope, so $|\mathrm{d}\ln p/\mathrm{d}r|$ passes
through a broad near-zero while the gas is changing fast, and the earlier
definition $L = \min(H_p, L_v, r)$ read a scale height fifteen times longer
than the density and temperature scales there. The sum is taken in
quadrature rather than as a minimum for the same reason a minimum failed: it
jumps to the next length whenever the field it is taken from flattens,
whereas in quadrature a flat field contributes zero and $L$ still cannot
exceed the shortest individual scale. The velocity enters as
$|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|/c_s$ and not $|(1/v)\,\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|$
because the term the closure drops is the viscous stress, whose size
relative to the pressure is $\sim\lambda|\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}r|/\bar v$:
a bulk-velocity change distorts the distribution in proportion to the
thermal speed, not to the local bulk speed. That removes the Mach-number
floor the old $L_v$ needed. The floor at the local cell width says that a
gradient claiming structure below one cell is measuring the mesh; it binds
in three cells of one of the four runs, all inside $1.002\,R_p$, and in no
critical region. The \emph{exobase} is the first crossing of
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 1$ from below, never extrapolated beyond the
domain. The \emph{critical point} is the sonic point taken with the code's
own sound speed, $c_s = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$ at
$\gamma = 5/3$, and not a re-definition. The \emph{critical region} runs
from the peak of volumetric heating to the sonic point. ``Collisional''
means $\max \mathrm{Kn} < 0.1$ across that region, over the bulk and over
every species carrying at least $10^{-3}$ of the mass; where it fails the
solution is reported \textbf{unvalidated}, not overestimated, because which
way a collisionless critical region moves the answer is not something the
continuum solution that assumed it away can decide.

\noindent\textbf{LHS\,1140\,b's wind never reaches its critical point inside
the computational domain.} All three solutions stay subsonic out to
$29\,R_p$, at $\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.54$--$0.55$. A subsonic solution is
not fixed by a critical point: its mass flux is set by the outer boundary
condition, and at that boundary $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is already
$1.39$--$2.37$, with the exobase at $18.9$--$25.3\,R_p$, inside the domain.
The continuum equations were therefore integrated through, and closed at, a
region in which they do not hold. The escape rates quoted throughout this
memo, $\log_{10}\dot M \simeq 7.5$--$7.8$, are \textbf{unvalidated in the
collisional sense}.

Against the p-winds retrieval, whose isothermal Parker solution places its
sonic point at $8$--$9.5\,R_p$: that radius lies \emph{inside} the exobase
measured here, but $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ there is $0.13$--$0.29$, well
past the threshold. So the finding is neither ``the sonic point is
above the exobase'' nor ``the critical region is collisional'': the critical
region of LHS\,1140\,b is \emph{transitional}. The scale separation the
continuum equations need has been lost by the radius at which either code
sets the mass flux, while the gas is not yet free molecular.

\paragraph{It is the neutral hydrogen that decides it, and the line does not
care.} In the critical region of the well-mixed solution the species split
is H\,\textsc{i} $2.38$, He\,\textsc{i} $1.69$, H\,\textsc{ii}
$3.9\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{ii} $2.0\times10^{-5}$, He\,\textsc{iii}
$5.1\times10^{-6}$, $e$ $7.1\times10^{-4}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:knudsen}b). The
neutrals carry the mass and the long mean free path; the charged species are
Coulomb-held. That matters for reading the rest of this memo, because the
He\,10830 metastable population forms at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$, where
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk}$ is $2.5$--$4.2\times10^{-4}$ at $1.1\,R_p$,
$0.004$--$0.006$ at $2\,R_p$ and $0.012$--$0.019$ at $3\,R_p$ in every case
here. \textbf{The line-forming layer is collisional by two orders of
magnitude.} What is unvalidated is the wind solution's mass flux, not the
absorption Sects.~\ref{sec:ew}--\ref{sec:width} measure, and not the
$22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of missing width, which is asked for at radii where the
continuum description is secure.

\paragraph{The single-temperature energy equation is not what fails.}
Electron--ion energy coupling is fast wherever it matters: the largest
$\tau^E_{ei}/\tau_{\rm heat}$ over the critical region is $0.031$, $0.021$
and $0.018$ in the three LHS\,1140\,b solutions and $8.3\times10^{-5}$ in
the control. Momentum coupling times are likewise short compared with the
flow time ($\tau_{\rm H\,I} = 924$\,s at $8\,R_p$ against a flow time of
$1.9\times10^{5}$\,s), which is not a contradiction but a different test:
a strongly subsonic flow gives hundreds of collisions per flow time even at
$\mathrm{Kn}\sim 1$, and it is the Knudsen number that decides continuum
validity, because the terms the closure drops (viscous stress, heat flux,
the non-Maxwellian tail) scale with the gradient over a mean free path
rather than with the collision count.

\paragraph{The kinetic scale, and why it is not a bound.} Jeans escape
through the measured exobase (Chamberlain \& Hunten 1987, eq.~7.2.5) gives
$1.0$--$2.0\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$, a factor about three below the
continuum $\dot M$. That is a \emph{scale}, not a bound. Jeans escape bounds
a continuum rate from below only when the exobase is well bound, and here
$\lambda_J(\mathrm{H}) = 0.82$--$1.53$: the exobase is barely gravitationally
bound, the Jeans integral is most of the Maxwellian rather than its
escaping tail, and the atmosphere is in blow-off. What the number does say
is that the continuum result sits within a factor of a few of what a
collisionless outer atmosphere at the same density and temperature would
lose, so the failure of the assumption is not hiding an order of magnitude:
it is hiding a factor of a few, in an undetermined direction. On
HD\,209458\,b, where $\lambda_J = 10.85$ and the solution is validated
through its critical point, the hydrodynamic rate exceeds Jeans by
$10^{4}$: the signature of a driven wind rather than an evaporating
atmosphere.

\paragraph{Which profile pair is read moves the exobase, not the verdict.}
On the equilibrium profiles instead of the advection-corrected ones the
LHS\,1140\,b solutions are still subsonic across the domain
($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.82$), still reach $\mathrm{Kn}\sim 1$ where the
outer boundary sets the flux, and the control is still collisional through
its critical point. On that pair the well-mixed solution no longer crosses
$\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 0.1$ at all inside the domain, reaching $0.097$
at the top against $0.104$, $0.191$ and $5.2\times10^{-4}$ for the other
three, and the largest species Knudsen numbers are $0.99$, $0.85$, $1.25$
and $0.013$. The exobase, however, moves above $30\,R_p$, because the
equilibrium far field is more ionized and Coulomb collisions there are far
stronger than neutral ones. An exobase radius quoted from this diagnostic
has to state which pair it came from; the numbers above are the
advection-corrected pair throughout.

\subsection{What this changes, and what it leaves open}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The outer boundary condition closes this wind, not a critical
      point.} For a transonic solution the critical point fixes the mass
      flux and the outer boundary is passive. Here it is the other way
      round, and the boundary sits in gas that is already collisionless. Any
      $\dot M$ quoted for LHS\,1140\,b from these solutions, including the
      $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.500$ of the flux closure (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure})
      and the comparison with the rate the retrieval imposes, has to carry
      that warning. The same applies to the p-winds number, whose sonic point
      sits at $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.13$--$0.28$.
\item \textbf{Two converged solutions are free to differ out there, and
      neither convergence test separates them.} The discarded $12.01$ rung
      of Sect.~\ref{sec:outerinherit} matches its control at the base, in
      matching-level composition to seven digits and in residual norm, and
      differs only outside $12\,R_p$, beyond its own exobase, measured
      here at $11.8\,R_p$. Its equivalent width is $18$ per cent higher for that
      reason alone. So this section also bounds what the flux closure and
      the JFNK residual can certify: neither reaches the region where the
      two solutions disagree, and the region they cannot reach is the one
      the continuum equations do not hold in.
\item \textbf{The rate itself is not settled here, and cannot be.} What it
      would take is a kinetic or transitional-flow calculation (DSMC, or a
      13-moment closure carried through the transitional region), and this
      repository contains neither. It is recorded as open, not estimated.
\item \textbf{The line analysis stands.} The He\,10830 layer is collisional
      by two orders of magnitude, so the equivalent-width matching, the
      composition inference conditional on it, and the invariant
      $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ width deficit are unaffected by this section. It
      constrains what the escape rate may be quoted as, not what the line
      says.
\end{itemize}

\section{Status against the goal}

The line is not reproduced. What stands:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Amount of absorption: matched, and the composition it takes
      is a pair set by the lower boundary}, $\mathrm{He/H} = 1.61$ above
      a prescribed metal-free scalar base, $8.21$ above a lower atmosphere
      solved as a photochemical column and handed over as a profile
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}). Both carry H/He element diffusion at
      the same adopted $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, so the base is
      what separates them, not the mixing. Inside the prescribed-base branch
      the answer is further conditional: $0.0483$ well mixed on the GJ\,699
      proxy, $0.4132$ well mixed on the GJ\,1132 proxy, $3.81$ wherever
      $K_{zz} \le 10^{4}$ leaves the molecular coefficient alone. Every one
      of those is read inside a bracket of two solved arms, a chord and
      a quadratic through each ladder agree on it to $0.11$ per cent or
      better, and each has since been solved at its own composition and
      returns the measured equivalent width to within $0.04\sigma$
      (\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}); the confirmation holds on
      the lineage each crossing was read on, and re-seeded from the nearest
      neighbouring arm instead the same compositions move by about $1$ per
      cent in He/H, the path spread quoted elsewhere here. The match is always available, so it constrains the
      composition only as far as the assumptions behind it are secure.
\item \textbf{Those five numbers have moved twice, and the structure
      between them has survived both times.} The first move was one rate
      coefficient, replaced because it was discontinuous in temperature
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:penningrate}); it took every crossing to
      $0.74$--$0.81$ of what the draft before it carried. The second is the
      composition renormalization of the SvS85 secondary-ionization split
      (\emph{Update\_EXHALE} Sect.~96); it moves four of the five by
      $0.1$--$2.7$ per cent and the flux closure by $9.2$ per cent, that one
      only because its ladder is the shallowest in composition. The ratios
      the memo's argument is made of survive: the two lower boundaries
      differ by $5.1$, the well-mixed and $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$ crossings by
      $3.9$, the two proxy spectra by $8.6$, and the exponent
      $\mathrm{d}\log(\mathrm{He/H})/\mathrm{d}\log K_{zz}$ is reproduced
      decade by decade to within $0.003$ by the second revision and
      $0.01$--$0.02$ by the first. \emph{What both revisions moved was the
      level of the inference; neither moved its shape.}
\item \textbf{Line shape: not matched}, by a factor $3.3$ in depth and
      width alike, and not fixable by composition. The missing ingredient is
      $22.22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM of velocity spread ($\sigma =
      9.44$\,km\,s$^{-1}$), measured in Sect.~\ref{sec:broadening} and
      Fig.~\ref{fig:broadened}; supplied as one Gaussian it puts depth,
      blue depth and red/blue ratio inside the observed $1\sigma$ interval
      at once, but its physical origin is unidentified. The demand is
      unchanged under diffusion and the adopted $K_{zz}$: measured the same
      way on the EW-matched diffusive solution it is $22.23$\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
      $0.01$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away (Sect.~\ref{sec:diffbroad}); it is
      $21.95$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ at the flux-closure crossing
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}); and it is
      $21.97$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ on the $5.1\times$ brighter proxy spectrum
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}), $1.3$ per cent of spread over four
      solutions that differ by a factor $170$ in composition. The adiabatic
      metastable bump, which would have supplied it without a free
      parameter, is present and measured (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}) but sits
      too slow to contribute.
\item \textbf{The width shortfall is not a measurement artifact, and it is
      not this code's alone.} Audited term by term, the instrument profile,
      the triplet splitting, stellar rotation, planetary rotation and
      orbital smearing account for at most $0.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of it;
      deconvolving both sides from their own instrument kernels puts the
      like-for-like requirement at $22.00$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ FWHM
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:widthaudit},
      \texttt{docs/lhs1140b\_width\_measurement\_audit.md}). The paper's own
      p-winds fit, measured the same way, is itself $1.5$ times narrower
      than the width printed in the same paper, so the shortfall appears to
      be a property of one-dimensional steady-state wind models rather than
      of this one.
\item \textbf{Escape rate: an output, not a fit, and SED-set.}
      $\dot M \simeq 6\times10^{7}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$ on the GJ\,1132 proxy, a
      third of the value the retrieval imposes, and nearly independent of
      composition. On the GJ\,699 proxy, $5.1\times$ brighter in XUV, it is
      $3.8\times10^{8}$\,g\,s$^{-1}$: a factor $7$ higher and within
      $0.05$\,dex of the value the paper retrieves on that same proxy
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:gj699}).
\item \textbf{That escape rate is unvalidated in the collisional sense; the
      line is not.} All three representative solutions are subsonic to
      $29\,R_p$ ($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = 0.54$--$0.55$), so the mass flux is
      set at the outer boundary, where $\mathrm{Kn}_{\rm bulk} = 1.35$--$2.38$;
      the exobase is at $18.8$--$25.9\,R_p$ and the p-winds sonic point at
      $8$--$9.5\,R_p$ sits at $\mathrm{Kn} = 0.13$--$0.28$, so the critical
      region of this planet is transitional for both codes. The He\,10830
      layer at $1.1$--$3\,R_p$ is collisional by two orders of magnitude
      ($\mathrm{Kn} \le 0.019$), and an HD\,209458\,b control validates
      cleanly at a factor $100$ lower $\mathrm{Kn}$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:validity}). What the true rate is needs a kinetic or
      transitional-flow calculation, which this repository does not contain.
\item \textbf{Composition: base-limited and SED-limited, and weakly
      $K_{zz}$-limited.} In the
      well-mixed limit the equivalent-width crossing is
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 0.4132$ on one proxy spectrum and $0.0483$ on the
      other, helium-rich against helium-poor, with the measurement unable to
      separate them. With binary H/He diffusion active the crossing rises,
      and the whole $K_{zz}$ axis has now been walked: $\mathrm{He/H} =
      3.81$ on the plateau at $K_{zz} \le 10^{4}$, then $3.79$, $3.61$,
      $3.07$, $2.28$, $1.61$, $1.14$ and $0.84$ at $10^{5}$ through
      $10^{11}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ (Table~\ref{tab:kzzcross},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:hehkzz}), a factor $2.0$ across the range the
      literature leaves open, $4.6$ across the entire axis, against the
      factor $8.6$ the SED carries. Those are all conditions inside the
      prescribed-base branch. Replacing that base with the solved
      photochemical column moves the crossing to $8.21$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder}), so end to end the inference spans
      $0.0483$ to $8.21$, a factor $170$. So any composition read off this
      line, the paper's included, is strongly conditional on the assumed XUV
      spectrum and on what is placed at the lower boundary, and weakly
      conditional on the eddy coefficient, as $K_{zz}^{-0.14}$ wherever the
      eddy term acts at all.
\item \textbf{Composition as an output, on one axis, and the lower
      boundary raised to an axis of its own in its place.} With the lower
      atmosphere solved as a
      photochemical column and the elemental fluxes iterated to continuity
      across the $1\,\mu$bar match, He/H at the match is a solution:
      $2.09235$, single-valued to $5.5\times10^{-6}$ from trial fluxes a
      factor of ten apart, at $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.500$
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}). At the adopted
      $K_{zz} = 10^{9}$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ that output is the well-mixed
      value (only $2\times10^{-4}$ of it comes from the escape flux), so
      the conditionality moves onto $K_{zz}$ rather than away. Above the
      match the wind still fractionates strongly, $F_{\rm He}/F_{\rm H} =
      0.73$ by mass against the base reservoir's $8.31$. The handoff also
      brings the column's C, N and O, which take $\log_{10}\dot M$ from
      $7.81$ to $7.49$ and the red-pair depth down by a factor $2.9$;
      \textbf{at that base the flux-closed solution at $2.09$ no longer
      reproduces the equivalent width}, $0.545$ against the measured
      $1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA{} (the comparison is at fixed reservoir).
\item \textbf{The composition the line needs above a photochemical base:
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 8.212$.} A ladder of nineteen flux-closed solutions
      over reservoirs $7.0$--$9.5$, fifteen of them from one common parent
      and each arm's wind re-solved to its own fixed point, has two rungs
      that straddle the measured equivalent width
      directly, $8.21$ at $EW = 1.1075$ and $8.26$ at $1.1098$ against
      $1.108 \pm 0.030$\,\%\,\AA, and every rung from $7.51$ to $9.31$ is
      inside the measurement's
      $1\sigma$ band. The crossing is $8.212$, with $7.474$--$9.395$ for
      that band, both edges of it bracketed by solved arms too
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closureladder},
      Table~\ref{tab:closureladder}). The crossing has also been solved
      directly at $8.2117$ and returns $EW = 1.107775$\,\%\,\AA, on the
      target to $2.2\times10^{-4}$\,\%\,\AA; chord and quadratic readings of
      the bracket agree on it to $0.00$ per cent
      (\texttt{LHS1140b/exhale/crossings\_j96}). \emph{It is the crossing of
      the five that the secondary-ionization split moved most}, from $9.048$
      to $8.212$, not because the closed solution feels the split more
      strongly (at fixed reservoir its line moves by the same $1$--$2$
      per cent the other four do), but because its ladder is the
      shallowest in composition. The chemistry correction before it did not
      move it: solved on Photochem 0.8.4 and again on 0.9.0 with the local
      solver corrections, which take the deepest-level elemental residual
      from $2\times10^{-5}$ to $10^{-14}$, the crossing came back $9.0484$
      and $9.0461$ both times. Two things follow: the closure condition
      alone does not select a reservoir here, every rung satisfies it at
      $2.3$--$4.7\%$ against its $5\%$ tolerance, so the
      line selects, and what the closure adds is consistency with a
      photochemical lower atmosphere carrying its own C, N and O, and
      $\dot M$ is flat across the whole ladder,
      $\log_{10}\dot M = 7.40$--$7.41$, i.e.\ an eighth of the retrieval
      value at the crossing. Both numbers carry the width shortfall
      (FWHM $0.280$ against $0.841$\,\AA) and the collisional caveat above,
      and a seed systematic that this ladder measures at $0.01$--$0.12$ per
      cent in equivalent width on the two arms nearest the crossing. This is
      the answer on the solved-column branch; $1.61$ is the answer
      to the same question above a prescribed metal-free base, and neither
      is an independent prediction of the model.
\item \textbf{A wind inherits its outer region from its seed, and neither
      convergence test sees it.} Two solutions at the same $12.01$
      reservoir, one seeded across a $1.50\times$ jump and one stepped up by
      $1.08\times$, agree at the base, in matching-level composition to
      seven digits and in cold trap, both converge at \texttt{info}~$=0$,
      and differ by $18$ per cent in equivalent width because one carries a
      hotter and more extended atmosphere outside $12\,R_p$. The
      first is discarded here on trend continuity, not on a physical
      criterion, because the discarded solution's own exobase sits at
      $11.8\,R_p$, inside that range, and the continuum solution is not
      valid beyond it
      (Sects.~\ref{sec:outerinherit},~\ref{sec:validity}). The same argument
      puts a systematic error on the absolute equivalent widths of the whole
      ladder that the flux-closure tolerance does not catch: small,
      because the outer region is about one per cent of the metastable
      column, but not zero. Practically: a ladder scan is climbed from one
      common parent, which is how the ladder of
      Table~\ref{tab:closureladder} was run.
\item \textbf{Why the same helium absorbs differently over the two bases,
      measured.} Adding the column's C, N and O at fixed reservoir takes
      radiative cooling from $7.3$--$10.4\%$ of the heating to $75\%$ at
      $1.2\,R_p$ ($95\%$ of it metal-line cooling, largest single
      contributor neutral C\,\textsc{i}) and $T_{\max}$ from $5447$ to
      $3955$\,K; the metastable peak rises, the column inside $2\,R_p$
      falls by $18\%$ ($37.4 \rightarrow 30.7$), and the column outside
      $2\,R_p$ falls by $2.6$, the same factor the equivalent width falls
      by. Raising the
      reservoir to $10.31$ on that same base returns $T_{\max}$ to $5898$\,K
      (past the metal-free $5447$) with the mean mass per particle at
      $2\,R_p$ up by $1.67$ and the heating rate divided by the particle
      density up by $1.98$, and
      with the C/N/O density diluted along with the hydrogen it is referred
      to. In the metastable balance the peak ratio $60.4 \rightarrow 178.5$
      splits as a source up by $1.92$ against a lifetime up by $1.54$, the
      latter because Penning ionization on H\,\textsc{i} ($88\%$ of the
      sink at $2.09$, $57\%$ at $10.31$) slows as hydrogen is thinned
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:basemetals}, Table~\ref{tab:thermostat},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:thermostat}). All three solutions are re-solved on the
      current binary.
      \emph{Read as an
      interpretation}, the ladder is a thermal trade between the metal
      content of the base and the helium content above it, and one line
      cannot separate them.
\item \textbf{The 2025 non-detection sits at about a third of the fiducial
      XUV in both models.} Scaling the irradiation with the
      lower atmosphere held fixed, the red-pair depth falls below the $0.6$
      per cent limit of the 2025 epoch at $\simeq\!0.326$ of fiducial in the
      scalar-base model and at $\simeq\!0.368$ in the flux-closed one
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:xuvgrid}, Table~\ref{tab:xuvgrid}), about
      $11$--$12$\,erg\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ or less. Both grids are on
      the current binary. The two models are a factor $1.13$
      apart there, so the crossing can be quoted as a single number to that
      accuracy; what they still do not agree on is the slope with which they
      get there, which differs by a factor $5.8$ over $0.33$ to $0.10$ and is
      a property of each model and not a result.
\item \textbf{Not verified: the second-chemistry-code cross-check.} VULCAN
      did not converge on this cold helium-rich column, its convergence
      measure rose against the criterion with the timestep pinned, the
      limiting cell being H$_2$O at the cold trap, so no composition from
      a second code, and no cross-code spread, is quoted
      (Sect.~\ref{sec:closure}).
\item \textbf{One solution is not converged to tolerance.}
      $\mathrm{He/H} = 100$ stagnates at $\|R\| = 1.18\times10^{-3}$ from
      every seed tried; neighbours bracket it smoothly, so it is retained
      with the residual recorded.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Four axes have now been walked: the assumed XUV spectrum, the
mixing assumption with its eddy coefficient, composition itself, and what is
placed at the lower boundary, and each separates the two questions rather
than answering them together. The last of them is the one that splits the
answer in two rather than shifting it: $1.61$ above a prescribed metal-free
base, $8.21$ above a solved photochemical column. Every
one of them moves the composition the equivalent width implies, two of them
by a large factor; none of them moves the width. What is left as a possible
source of the missing $22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is therefore what it was: velocity
fields that are three-dimensional, or heating a 1D hydrodynamic energy
equation does not carry. The adiabatic-cooling metastable bump, the one
candidate that would have needed no free parameter, has been looked for on
both spectra and does not supply it for this host (Sect.~\ref{sec:bump}).
