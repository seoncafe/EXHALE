\section{Ionization and chemical state}
\label{sec:ionization}

\subsection{The species carried}
\label{sec:ionization:species}

The composition of a cell is the vector \texttt{f\_sp}$(j,\cdot)$ of species
fractions, laid out once in \texttt{species\_table.f90}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llp{0.46\textwidth}}
\toprule
column(s) & species & note\\
\midrule
1--2   & H\,{\sc i}, H\,{\sc ii} & fractions of the hydrogen nuclei\\
3--5   & He\,{\sc i}, He\,{\sc ii}, He\,{\sc iii} & fractions of the helium
         nuclei; column 3 \emph{contains} the metastable\\
6      & He\,$2^3$S & the metastable triplet, tracked when
         \texttt{Include He23S} is true (default on)\\
7--33  & the 27 metal ion stages & C, O, N, Mg, Si, Ca, Fe in three stages;
         Na, K, S in two\\
34--37 & H$_2$, H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$, HeH$^+$ & molecular option
         (\texttt{Molecular chemistry}, \texttt{thereis\_mol}, default off)\\
38--40 & OH, H$_2$O, CO & oxygen option (\texttt{Oxygen chemistry},
         \texttt{thereis\_oxychem}, default off)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Ten elements carry a nuclear abundance $n_X/n_{\rm H}$, set by
\texttt{metals.inp} (\texttt{metals\_input\_read.f90}) or by a
lower-atmosphere handoff. Masses are in units of the hydrogen \emph{atom}:
helium weighs $m_{\rm He}/m_{\rm H} = 3.9715259$ and the metal weights are
the CIAAW values converted from the unified mass unit by
$1\,{\rm u} = 0.99223573\,m_{\rm H}$.

\subsection{The cell system}
\label{sec:ionization:cellsystem}

For each cell the code solves a system of algebraic balance equations at
fixed temperature, density and radiation field. The unknowns are stage
\emph{fractions}, and one stage of each element is closed as the remainder
rather than being an unknown: $n({\rm H\,I}) = (1-x_{\rm H^+})n_{\rm H}$,
$n({\rm He\,I}) = (1-x_{\rm He^+}-x_{\rm He^{2+}})n_{\rm He}$, and for a metal
element $n(X^{0}) = (1-x_{X^+}-x_{X^{2+}})n_X$. The layout is

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
rows & contents\\
\midrule
1--3 & $x_{\rm H^+}$, $x_{\rm He^+}$, $x_{\rm He^{2+}}$\\
4    & He\,$2^3$S (triplet systems) \emph{or} H$_2$ (molecular systems)\\
5--8 & H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$, HeH$^+$, He\,$2^3$S (molecular systems)\\
$m_{\rm base}$\dots & two rows per metal element
       (\texttt{metal\_row\_base()})\\
$i_{\rm ox}$\dots & OH and H$_2$O (oxygen option)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

with $m_{\rm base} = 4$, or 5 with the triplet. In the molecular layout
$i_{\rm ox} = 8$, or 9 with the triplet, and
$m_{\rm base} = i_{\rm ox}$, raised by two when the oxygen chemistry is on
(\texttt{metal\_row\_base}, \texttt{oxygen\_row\_base}: one definition, read
by the residual and by the driver alike). Seven residual routines cover the
configurations: \texttt{System\_H}, \texttt{System\_HeH},
\texttt{System\_HeH\_metals}, \texttt{System\_HeH\_TR},
\texttt{System\_HeH\_TR\_metals}, \texttt{System\_HeH\_mol} and
\texttt{System\_HeH\_mol\_metals}. The shared rows live once, in
\texttt{ion\_residual\_core.f90}.

\paragraph{The H/He rows.} With photoionization rates $\Gamma$,
recombination coefficients $\alpha$ (case B plus the ground captures whose
photons are not re-absorbed on the spot, \eqref{eq:ion:netalpha}) and
electron-impact ionization coefficients $\beta$, \texttt{heh\_rows}
writes
\begin{align}
0 &= n_{\rm HI}\Gamma_{\rm HI}
   + \left(n_{\rm HI}\beta_{\rm HI}-\alpha_{\rm HII}n_{\rm HII}\right)n_e,
   \label{eq:ion:hrow}\\
0 &= n_{\rm HeI}\Gamma_{\rm HeI}
   + \left(n_{\rm HeI}\beta_{\rm HeI}-\alpha_{\rm HeII}n_{\rm HeII}\right)n_e,
   \label{eq:ion:herow}\\
0 &= n_{\rm HeII}\Gamma_{\rm HeII}
   + \left(n_{\rm HeII}\beta_{\rm HeII}-\alpha_{\rm HeIII}n_{\rm HeIII}\right)n_e,
   \label{eq:ion:he2row}
\end{align}
with $n_e$ the charge sum of every tracked ion,
$n_e = n_{\rm HII}+n_{\rm HeII}+2n_{\rm HeIII}+\sum_i Z_i n_{{\rm m},i}
 + n_{\rm H_2^+}+n_{\rm H_3^+}+n_{\rm HeH^+}$
(\texttt{calc\_ne}), so charge neutrality is imposed identically rather
than solved for. Each metal element contributes
\begin{equation}
0 = n_{X^0}\Gamma_{X^0}
  + \left(n_{X^0}\beta_{X^0}-\alpha_{X^+}n_{X^+}\right)n_e,
\qquad
0 = n_{X^+}\Gamma_{X^+}
  + \left(n_{X^+}\beta_{X^+}-\alpha_{X^{2+}}n_{X^{2+}}\right)n_e,
\label{eq:ion:metalrows}
\end{equation}
the second row being replaced by $x_{X^{2+}}=0$ for a two-stage element, and
the whole pair by $x_{X^+}=x_{X^{2+}}=0$ for an element with zero abundance.

\paragraph{The triplet row.} With the metastable tracked, the He rows are
rewritten in the form of \citet{Oklopcic2018} (\texttt{heh\_tr\_rows}): row 2
is the summed He\,{\sc i} balance, the sum of their singlet and triplet
equations, in which the singlet ground state and the triplet are
distinguished, and the fourth row is the $2^3$S balance
(\texttt{tr\_triplet\_row}, the one expression of it, read by the atomic and
molecular systems and by the transport operator alike),
\begin{equation}
0 = -n_{2^3{\rm S}}\Gamma_{2^3{\rm S}}
    + n_e\left[n_{\rm HeII}\alpha_{2^3{\rm S}}
             + n_{1^1{\rm S}}q_{13}
             - n_{2^3{\rm S}}(q_{31g}+q_{31a}+q_{31b})
             - n_{2^3{\rm S}}\beta_{2^3{\rm S}}\right]
    - n_{2^3{\rm S}}\left(A_{31}+n_{\rm HI}Q_{31}\right),
\label{eq:ion:tripletrow}
\end{equation}
where $q_{13}$ is the collisional feed of $2^3$S out of the singlet
ground state: the direct excitation $1^1$S$\to2^3$S plus the excitation of
every higher triplet term $n\le5$ of \citet[Table~2]{Bray2000}, each of which cascades
into $2^3$S within the triplets (0.019 of the direct rate at 5000\,K, 0.107
at $10^4$\,K, 0.331 at $2\times10^4$\,K); $q_{31g} =
q^{\rm direct}_{13}(g_1/g_3)e^{E_{13}/kT}$ is the electron-impact
(super-elastic) de-excitation $2^3$S$\to1^1$S, the detailed-balance reverse of
the direct excitation, so that the electron collisions between the two levels
drive them toward their Boltzmann ratio $3e^{-19.82\,{\rm eV}/kT}$
(\texttt{deexcitation\_rate\_HeI\_23S\_11S}, written with the Boltzmann factor
cancelled, so it stays finite where $q_{13}$ underflows, and not negligible
in cool gas: $q_{31g}/(q_{31a}+q_{31b}) = 0.054$ at $10^4$\,K, 0.15 at
5000\,K, 0.56 at 3000\,K and 2.7 at 2000\,K, MEASURED 2026-10-05 from the
coded rates); $q_{31a}$ and $q_{31b}$ are the
collisional conversions to $2^1$S and to $2^1$P, the latter together with
every singlet level above it (\texttt{excitation\_rate\_HeI\_23S\_singlets\_n3},
0.019 of $q_{31a}+q_{31b}$ at $10^4$\,K, MEASURED 2026-10-05), all
of the form $C\,\Upsilon\,e^{-E/kT}/(g_i\sqrt{T})$ with the Maxwellian
constant $C = h^2/[(2\pi m_e)^{3/2}k^{1/2}] = 8.629132\times10^{-6}$
cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$\,K$^{1/2}$, the effective collision strengths of
\citet[Tables~2 and 3]{Bray2000} for the network's own transitions and for
the higher levels of the sums alike (interpolated inside the tabulated
$10^{3.75}$--$10^{5.75}$\,K and continued outside it with the temperature
dependence of the CHIANTI v11.0.2 strength of the same transition, scaled to
the table end) and the NIST level energies,
$A_{31} = 1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$ the forbidden radiative
decay, $\beta_{2^3{\rm S}}$ the electron-impact ionization of the
metastable at its own 4.8\,eV threshold, and $Q_{31}$ the \emph{total}
He($2^3$S)$+$H ionization rate coefficient. Both channels of $Q_{31}$
quench the metastable, so the sink takes the sum; only the Penning branch
$f_{\rm P}=0.9$ leaves a lasting proton, and it appears as the source
$f_{\rm P}Q_{31}n_{2^3{\rm S}}n_{\rm HI}$ in the H$^+$ row. The remaining
10 percent is associative ionization to HeH$^+$, which the atomic systems
drop (in an atomic gas HeH$^+$ dissociatively recombines straight back) and
the molecular system carries explicitly. In the molecular system the row
also loses $k^{\rm ion}_{\rm H_2}n_{\rm H_2}n_{2^3{\rm S}}$, the total
He($2^3$S)$+$H$_2$ ionization, whose two branches both quench the level and
neither makes He$^+$.

\paragraph{The molecular rows.} \texttt{mol\_heh\_rows} adds four species
balances on the \citet{Koskinen2022} Table 1 network
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:network}). Schematically, writing $g$ for photorates and
$k_n$ for the Table-1 coefficients,
\begin{align}
\text{H}_2:\quad 0 &= k_6 n_e n_{\rm H_3^+} + k_9 n_{\rm H_2^+}n_{\rm HI}
   + k_{11}n_{\rm H_3^+}n_{\rm HI} + k_{15}n_Mn_{\rm HI}^{2}
   \nonumber\\ &\quad
   - \big[g_{\rm H_2} + g_{\rm LW} + (k_{10}+k_{13})n_{\rm HII}
          + k_{12}n_M + k_{14}n_e + k_8 n_{\rm H_2^+}
          + (k_{17}+k_{23})n_{\rm HeII} + k_{18}n_{\rm HeH^+}
          + k^{\rm ion}_{\rm H_2}n_{2^3{\rm S}}\big]n_{\rm H_2},
   \label{eq:ion:h2row}\\
\text{H}_2^+:\quad 0 &=
   \left(g_{\rm H_2}-g^{\rm di}-g^{\rm dd}-g^{\rm nd}\right)n_{\rm H_2}
   + k_{10}n_{\rm HII}n_{\rm H_2} + k_{11}n_{\rm H_3^+}n_{\rm HI}
   + k_{19}n_{\rm HeH^+}n_{\rm HI} + k_{23}n_{\rm HeII}n_{\rm H_2}
   \nonumber\\ &\quad
   + f_{\rm P}k^{\rm ion}_{\rm H_2}n_{2^3{\rm S}}n_{\rm H_2}
   - \left(k_5 n_e + k_8 n_{\rm H_2} + k_9 n_{\rm HI}
           + k_{\rm He}n_{1^1{\rm S}}\right)n_{\rm H_2^+},
   \label{eq:ion:h2prow}\\
\text{H}_3^+:\quad 0 &= k_8 n_{\rm H_2^+}n_{\rm H_2}
   + k_{13}n_{\rm HII}n_{\rm H_2} + k_{18}n_{\rm HeH^+}n_{\rm H_2}
   - \left[(k_6+k_7)n_e + k_{11}n_{\rm HI}\right]n_{\rm H_3^+},
   \label{eq:ion:h3prow}\\
\text{HeH}^+:\quad 0 &= k_{\rm He}n_{1^1{\rm S}}n_{\rm H_2^+}
   + (1-f_{\rm P})n_{2^3{\rm S}}
     \left(Q_{31}n_{\rm HI}+k^{\rm ion}_{\rm H_2}n_{\rm H_2}\right)
   - \left(k_{16}n_e+k_{18}n_{\rm H_2}+k_{19}n_{\rm HI}\right)n_{\rm HeH^+},
   \label{eq:ion:hehprow}
\end{align}
and the H$^+$ row gains
$(g^{\rm di}+2g^{\rm dd})n_{\rm H_2} + k_9 n_{\rm H_2^+}n_{\rm HI}
 + k_{17}n_{\rm HeII}n_{\rm H_2}
 - (k_{10}+k_{13})n_{\rm HII}n_{\rm H_2}$. The factor 2 on the double
photoionization branch is the stoichiometry, two protons out of one event,
while the H$_2$ destroyed is still one per event, which is why
\eqref{eq:ion:h2row} is not doubled with it. $n_M$ is the H$_2$-equivalent
third-body density of the association R15 and of its reverse R12, the same
density in both directions (\texttt{h2\_third\_body\_density}; the total
particle density with \texttt{EXHALE\_REACTION\_HEAT\_RECIPIENTS=0},
Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:network}); $k_{\rm He}$ is
${\rm H_2^+}+{\rm He}\to{\rm HeH^+}+{\rm H}$ \citep{Black1978} on the ground
singlet $n_{1^1{\rm S}}$, the HeH$^+$ source that replaces the retired
Table~1 entry R20 (Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:network}); and the He$^+$ row loses
$(k_{17}+k_{23})n_{\rm HeII}n_{\rm H_2}$ (with the oxygen option also the
He$^+$$+$CO destruction below).

\paragraph{The oxygen rows.} With the oxygen option
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:oxygen}; \texttt{thereis\_oxychem}, solved only by
\texttt{System\_HeH\_mol\_metals} since oxygen is a metal element) two more
unknowns join the cell system, $x_{\rm OH}=n_{\rm OH}/n_{\rm O}$ and
$x_{\rm H_2O}=n_{\rm H_2O}/n_{\rm O}$ with $n_{\rm O}$ the oxygen family of
the cell, and \texttt{oxygen\_carrier\_rows} adds their balances on the
reactions O1 (OH$+$H$_2\to$H$_2$O$+$H), O2 (O$+$H$_2\to$OH$+$H), their
reverses O1r and O2r, the three H$_2$O photolysis channels O3
($\to$OH$+$H), O4 ($\to$H$_2+$O($^1$D)) and O5 ($\to$O$+$H$+$H), and the OH
photolysis O7 ($\to$O$+$H):
\begin{align}
\text{OH}:\quad 0 &= k_{1r}n_{\rm H_2O}n_{\rm HI} + k_{2}n_{\rm O^0}n_{\rm H_2}
   + (J_3+J_4)\,n_{\rm H_2O}
   - k_{1}n_{\rm OH}n_{\rm H_2} - k_{2r}n_{\rm OH}n_{\rm HI} - J_7 n_{\rm OH},
   \label{eq:ion:ohrow}\\
\text{H}_2\text{O}:\quad 0 &= k_{1}n_{\rm OH}n_{\rm H_2}
   - k_{1r}n_{\rm H_2O}n_{\rm HI} - (J_3+J_4+J_5)\,n_{\rm H_2O},
   \label{eq:ion:h2orow}
\end{align}
and the H$_2$ row \eqref{eq:ion:h2row} gains the exchange with the water
cycle, $-(k_1 n_{\rm OH}+k_2 n_{\rm O^0})n_{\rm H_2}
+(k_{1r}n_{\rm H_2O}+k_{2r}n_{\rm OH})n_{\rm HI}$. Here $n_{\rm O^0}$ is the
\emph{free} atomic oxygen, the remainder of the family once the nuclei bound
in OH, H$_2$O and CO are removed, $n_{\rm O^0}=n_{\rm O}-n_{\rm OH}
-n_{\rm H_2O}-n_{\rm CO}$, which the metal block then splits over
O\,I/O\,II/O\,III from its own balance: the O\,I column of the output means
free atomic oxygen when the option is on. The hydrogen closure gains the
nuclei the carriers hold, $n_{\rm OH}+2n_{\rm H_2O}$. The forward
coefficients $k_1$ and $k_2$ are the \citet{Baulch2005} evaluations
($k_1=3.6\times10^{-16}\,T^{1.52}e^{-1740/T}$,
$k_2=6.34\times10^{-12}e^{-4000/T}+1.46\times10^{-9}e^{-9650/T}$
cm$^{3}$\,s$^{-1}$); the reverses are not transcribed but generated by
detailed balance, $k_{r}=k_{f}/K_c(T)$ with $K_c$ from the NIST-JANAF
Shomate Gibbs energies \citep{Chase1998} of H, H$_2$, O, OH, H$_2$O, CO and
C (\texttt{oxygen\_rates.f90}), so that the hot limit of the network is the
chemical equilibrium of the same thermochemistry. $J_3$, $J_4$, $J_5$ and
$J_7$ are the band-resolved photolysis rates of Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:oxygen}
summed over the four far-ultraviolet bands with their quantum yields. The
O4 branch appears in the OH row as if it made OH directly: its product
O($^1$D) has exactly one sink in the network, O6
(O($^1$D)$+$H$_2\to$OH$+$H), with a lifetime of $1.6\times10^{-3}$\,s at the
HD\,189733\,b base, so its local steady state is exact, the flux through O6
equals the O($^1$D) production $J_4 n_{\rm H_2O}$, and the pair O4$+$O6 is
one channel whose net effect on H$_2$ cancels (O4 makes one, O6 consumes
one), which is why only O1 and O2 enter the H$_2$ row; the O($^1$D) density
is evaluated for the output only. CO is \emph{not} a row of this system: it
is a transported carrier (Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:carrier}) with no formation
channel in the network, destroyed one-sidedly by
He$^+$$+$CO$\to$C$^+$$+$O$+$He at the Langevin value
$1.6\times10^{-9}$\,cm$^{3}$\,s$^{-1}$ \citep[UMIST RATE22 entry
4068;][]{Millar2024} and by photodissociation on the Lyman-Werner beam with
the \citet{Visser2009} shielding of the star-ward CO and H$_2$ columns, both
inside the carrier step; the cell system sees the CO of the cell as a frozen
background and takes the He$^+$ it consumes, $-k_{\rm D1}n_{\rm CO}n_{\rm
HeII}$, in the He$^+$ row, so the one reaction is complete over the pair of
operators and counted once in each.

\paragraph{Normalization of the rows.} Before the Newton solve every row of
the molecular system, the oxygen, metal and charge-exchange terms included,
is divided by the chemical turnover of its element at a composition $x_0$,
\begin{equation}
r_i = \frac{P_i - L_i}{G_E(x_0)},\qquad G_E = \sum_{j\in E}\,(P_j + L_j),
\label{eq:ion:element_turnover}
\end{equation}
with $P_j+L_j$ the sum of the magnitudes of the terms of row $j$ and $E$ the
element the row's species carries (hydrogen: H$^+$, H$_2$, H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$,
HeH$^+$; helium: He$^+$, He$^{++}$, He($2^3$S); each metal its two stage
rows, oxygen together with OH and H$_2$O; \texttt{element\_turnover\_reciprocal},
\texttt{System\_HeH\_mol.f90}). $x_0$ is the starting point of each solve and
the scale is held through it (\texttt{set\_element\_turnover\_scales},
\texttt{System\_HeH\_mol\_metals.f90}): a scale that followed the iterate
would bound every row by one and leave the solve no gradient back from an
iterate far outside the simplex, which on the cold start of the
\texttt{mol\_metals} regression gate left 214 cells without a root. A
candidate is judged with $x_0$ the candidate itself. A row that carries no reaction at the
evaluated state (an absent element's identity row) keeps the reciprocal of the
upper bound with every species at the whole of its element, which is 1 there.
The blocks of one residual vector differ by the element abundances, and the
dogleg step of \texttt{hybrd1} weights rows by their size, so a row far below
the others would not determine its unknown. The upper bound itself is not the
scale: where a trace ion of one element reacts with another element it
exceeds the turnover by the inverse of the ion's fraction (the H$^+$ row
carries He$^+$+H$_2$ and He($2^3$S)+H with He$^+$ and the metastable at all of
the helium, $3.9\times10^{12}$ against a gross rate of
$1.3\times10^{3}$\,cm$^{-3}$\,s$^{-1}$ at the root of the LHS\,1140\,b He/H
$=2\times10^4$ base ghost, MEASURED), which left the H$^+$ row at
$3\times10^{-10}$ of its weight in the solve and in the acceptance test: ten
per cent of H$^+$ off the root moved it by $2.7\times10^{-11}$, against
$5.4\times10^{-2}$ in the normalization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ion:element_turnover}. The row's own $P+L$ is not the scale
either: it asks a species at $10^{-22}$ of its element to balance to one part
in $10^6$ of itself, which neither matters to the gas nor is resolved by a
solve whose unknowns are fractions of order one. Against $G_E$ the error of a
species counts as the nucleus flux it misplaces, which is what the
acceptance tolerance \texttt{ieq\_res\_tol} $=10^{-6}$ is stated in
(\texttt{normalized\_reaction\_residual}). The zeros of the rows are
unchanged. The constrained continuation below keeps the bound on its own
path; the candidate it returns is judged by the same measure.

\subsection{The rate coefficients}
\label{sec:ionization:rates}

All of the following live in \texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90} unless stated. The
default set is selected by \texttt{Legacy\_HHe\_rates: False} (default) and
\texttt{Atomic rate set: default}; the two alternatives are the
\citet{Hui1997} fits and the \citet{Koskinen2022} Table 1 fits, and the
three meet at the accessor functions at the end of that module.

\paragraph{Recombination.} Radiative recombination follows the \citet[2023 update]{Badnell2006}
total ground-level fit
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\rm RR}(T) = \frac{A}
  {\sqrt{T/T_0}\left(1+\sqrt{T/T_0}\right)^{1-B'}
   \left(1+\sqrt{T/T_1}\right)^{1+B'}},
\qquad B' = B + C e^{-T_2/T},
\label{eq:ion:rrbadnell}
\end{equation}
implemented as \texttt{rr\_badnell} (its dummy \texttt{T0} keeps the
published notation and is not the code's temperature scale). Dielectronic
recombination of He\,{\sc ii} adds the adf09 three-term sum
$T^{-3/2}\sum_i c_i e^{-E_i/T}$. Case B is formed by subtracting the
capture into the ground level, $\alpha_1$, from the Milne relation on the
ground-state photoionization cross section the radiative transfer itself
absorbs with (hydrogenic for H\,{\sc i} and He\,{\sc ii}, the
\citet{Verner1996} fit for He\,{\sc i}),
\begin{equation}
\alpha_1(T) = \frac{g_n}{g_+}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}
   \frac{1}{c^2(m_ekT)^{3/2}}
   \int_0^\infty (I+E)^2\,\sigma(I+E)\,e^{-E/kT}\,{\rm d}E,
\label{eq:ion:milne}
\end{equation}
($g_n/g_+ = 2$, $1/2$, $2$ for H\,{\sc i}, He\,{\sc i}, He\,{\sc ii};
\texttt{ground\_capture\_milne}, a 24-point Gauss--Legendre rule in
$\ln(1+E/I)$ accurate to $10^{-10}$), so that the ground captures and the
absorption of the photons they emit are one process and
$\alpha_{\rm B} = \alpha_{\rm A}-\alpha_1$ for H\,{\sc ii}, He\,{\sc ii}
and He\,{\sc iii}. $\alpha_1$ is 0.998 of \citet{Hummer1994} for H\,{\sc i},
0.999 of his hydrogenic scaling for He\,{\sc ii} and 1.006--1.011 of
\citet{Hummer1998} for He\,{\sc i}. With the metastable tracked, the
case-B captures of He\,{\sc ii} are split between the triplets (all ending
in $2^3$S) and the excited singlets (ending in $1^1$S) in the ratio of the
two sums of \citet[Table~5]{Hummer1998}, a triplet share of 0.755 at
10\,K, 0.771 at $10^4$\,K and 0.781 at $2.5\times10^4$\,K.

\paragraph{The coefficients the balance uses.} A ground capture emits a
photon above the edge of the recombined species; the part of those photons
the same species re-absorbs in the cell is a capture and a re-ionization
that cancel. The coefficients of the rows are therefore
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\rm HII} = \alpha_{\rm B} + y_{\rm HI}\,\alpha_1^{\rm H},\qquad
\alpha_{\rm HeII} = \alpha_{\rm B} + y_{\rm gnd}\,\alpha_1^{\rm He},\qquad
\alpha_{\rm HeIII} = \alpha_{\rm B} + y_{\rm HeII}\,\alpha_1^{\rm He^+},
\label{eq:ion:netalpha}
\end{equation}
$y_r = 1 - n_r\sigma_r\,\Delta r\,(1-e^{-\tau})/\tau$ the fraction of the
photons not re-absorbed by $r$ in the cell, $\tau$ the cell optical depth
at the edge of every absorber (\texttt{ground\_capture\_escape\_weights}),
with $(1-e^{-\tau})/\tau$ from the one evaluation of that quotient
(Sec.~\ref{sec:radiation:transfer}, at \eqref{eq:rad:cellmean}).
$y\to0$ is case B, where the gas is thick at the edge; $y\to1$ case A, in
the thin outer wind. \texttt{H\_rec\_escape} (default on) governs
$y_{\rm HI}$ and \texttt{He\_rec\_coupling} (default on) the two helium
weights; a switch that is off sets its weight to zero. The photons taken by
other absorbers ionize them (Sec.~\ref{sec:radiation:internal},
\texttt{recombination\_radiation\_absorbed}), and the photoelectrons of those
ionizations go through the same partition as the stellar photoelectrons, at
the representative energy of each channel and under the same switches
(Sec.~\ref{sec:radiation:secondary}): their secondary ionizations of H\,{\sc
i}, He\,{\sc i} and H$_2$ are added to the photoionization rates $\Gamma$ of
the rows. The metal stages take Badnell RR $+$ adf09
DR through the same two forms, with the one gap (Ca\,{\sc ii}, K-like)
handled separately and noted at the code site.

\paragraph{Collisional ionization.} \citet{Voronov1997} Table~I,
\begin{equation}
\beta(T) = A\,\frac{\left(1+P\sqrt{U}\right)U^{K}e^{-U}}{X+U},
\qquad U = \frac{\Delta E}{k_{\rm B}T},
\label{eq:ion:voronov}
\end{equation}
with $(\Delta E,P,A,X,K)$ equal to $(13.6,0,2.91\times10^{-8},0.232,0.39)$
for H\,{\sc i}, $(24.6,0,1.75\times10^{-8},0.180,0.35)$ for He\,{\sc i} and
$(54.4,1,2.05\times10^{-9},0.265,0.25)$ for He\,{\sc ii}, and the metal
stages take their own rows of the same table. The $\Delta E$
here is Voronov's own fit coefficient, not the measured potential (Mg\,{\sc
ii} 15.2 against 15.035\,eV), for the metals as for H and He; the
energy \emph{removed} per ionization of H and He is the measured one,
$E_{{\rm th}}$ in erg. Voronov's fits cover 1\,eV--20\,keV (3\,eV for
He\,{\sc ii}, 2\,eV for N\,{\sc ii} and O\,{\sc ii}); below that, in the
2000--$10^4$\,K of a photoionized wind, the same expression is evaluated,
which keeps the exact threshold factor $e^{-U}$ but continues the fitted
prefactor where no data constrain it, and no accuracy is claimed there.

\paragraph{Charge exchange.} \texttt{charge\_exchange.f90} carries the
reaction list of \citet{Huang2023} Table 4, 63 rows, plus two rows that
are not in it, but not all of that table's rate coefficients. A rate that a
source calculated or measured is carried as the source gives it, from the
original where it could be read (among others Kingdon \& Ferland 1996 with
Butler \& Dalgarno 1979 for N$+$H$^+$, Dutta et al.\ 2001 and Watanabe et al.\
2002 for Na$+$H$^+$, Watanabe et al.\ for K$+$H$^+$, Rutherford \& Vroom 1972 for Fe$+$X$^+$, Zhao et
al.\ 2005 for S$+$H$^+$, Satta et al.\ 2013 for Si$+$He$^+$, Chenel et al.\
2010 for S$+$C$^+$, Kwolek et al.\ 2019 for Na$+$Ca$^+$); where the source
gives one direction only, the other is computed from it by detailed balance,
$k_r = k_f\,[Q(X^s)Q(Y^{t+1})]/[Q(X^{s+1})Q(Y^t)]\,
e^{[I(X^s)-I(Y^t)]/kT}$ with NIST internal partition functions and
ionization energies, in place of the fitted reverses Table 4 prints. Ten
reverses of Table 4 are not carried (A2, A6, A10, A22, C2, C4, C6, D18,
D28, D30), because their forward channel ends in an excited or radiative
product and so has no collisional reverse from the populated states. One
pair, D1/D2 (C$+$Si$^+$), stays as printed: the paper Table 4 cites for it
contains no such rate. \texttt{src/tests/charge\_exchange\_detailed\_balance}
checks every carried pair against detailed balance and every carried rate
against the threshold law. The default active set is Group A, the 23
metal$+$H/H$^+$ reactions; \texttt{cx\_full} adds the metal$+$He (C) and
metal$+$metal (D) groups. The He$\leftrightarrow$H pair (Group B),
${\rm He}^0+{\rm H}^+ \rightleftharpoons {\rm He}^+ + {\rm H}^0$, is applied
separately by \texttt{he\_h\_cx\_fvec} so that it is available in
\emph{every} system containing helium and is never counted twice; it is
gated by \texttt{He\_H\_charge\_exchange} (default on). Group E is the two
reactions outside Table 4, ${\rm O}^{2+}+{\rm H}^0\to{\rm O}^{+}+{\rm H}^+$
and ${\rm N}^{2+}+{\rm H}^0\to{\rm N}^{+}+{\rm H}^+$ (Barrag\'an et al.\
2006, Tables 1 and 2, held at the ends of $10^2$--$10^5$\,K), on by default
at the published rates because the absence of the first leaves the
O\,{\sc iii} profile wrong by decades in the region a transit probes;
\texttt{cx\_O2p\_H 0} and \texttt{cx\_N2p\_H 0} in \texttt{metals.inp}
restore the Table-4-only set exactly. Each reaction enters as $R = k\,n_{\rm donor}n_{\rm acceptor}$, so
the zero-abundance guard is automatic. The energy defect of each applied
reaction, less the excitation of a product state that leaves as radiation,
is heating channel 20 (Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal:heat}).

\paragraph{The helium-hydrogen pair.} ${\rm He}^+ + {\rm H} \to
{\rm He} + {\rm H}^+$ has a radiative and a non-radiative channel, and both
are carried (\texttt{charge\_exchange.f90}).  The radiative one is row (19)
of \citet{Stancil1998} Table~1, $k = 1.20\times10^{-15}(T/300)^{0.25}$
cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$, the direct radiative charge transfer of Zygelman et al.\
(1989) weighted by the 1/4 of the singlet approach.  The non-radiative one is
the Maxwellian average of the H$^+$\,$+$\,He(1s$^2$) cross section of
\citet{Loreau2014} turned into the exothermic direction by microscopic
reversibility, $E_r = E_f + 10.989$\,eV in the center-of-mass frame with
statistical weights 4 and 1, so that the reverse threshold lies at
$x_{\rm th} = 13.655$\,eV/u.  Their calculation starts at 15\,eV/u and their
fit stays finite below the threshold, where the channel is closed, so since
2026-09-26 the reverse cross section between the threshold and 15\,eV/u is
%
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_r(x) = \sigma_r(15)\,
  \sqrt{\frac{x - x_{\rm th}}{15 - x_{\rm th}}},\qquad
  x_{\rm th} \le x \le 15\ {\rm eV/u},
  \label{eq:ion:heph_threshold}
\end{equation}
%
and zero below $x_{\rm th}$: by reciprocity $\sigma_f v$ is constant at low
forward energy, capture on the ion-induced-dipole potential with a transfer
probability independent of energy, an assumption over forward energies of
0--1.08\,eV that no calculation tests.  Before, $\sigma_r$ was zero below
15\,eV/u, which closed a channel that is open: the forward rate then fell as
$e^{-E/kT}$ and was $1.5\times10^{-21}$\,cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$ at $10^3$\,K, where
it is now $9.56\times10^{-17}$ ($9.64\times10^{-17}$ at $10^4$\,K, against
$4.41\times10^{-17}$).  The rate is tabulated from 300\,K to $10^5$\,K
(generator \texttt{cooling\_data/heii\_h\_charge\_exchange\_rate.py}),
interpolated linearly in $\ln k$ against $1/T$, the first chord continued
below 300\,K and the $10^5$\,K value held above.  The non-radiative channel
is 3.3 per cent of the radiative one at $10^4$\,K and 5.9 per cent at
$10^3$\,K (MEASURED 2026-10-05 from \texttt{he\_h\_cx\_rates}), so He$^+$ + H
is removed almost entirely by radiative charge transfer; the reverse
${\rm He} + {\rm H}^+ \to {\rm He}^+ + {\rm H}$ is the detailed-balance
reverse of the non-radiative channel alone, and its helium reactant is the
ground singlet $n(1^1{\rm S})$, because the rate carries the barrier
$e^{-10.989\,{\rm eV}/kT}$ of ground-state helium (the metastable's own
charge exchange is a different reaction, not carried).

The third reaction of the pair is the radiative capture
${\rm He}^{2+} + {\rm H} \to {\rm He}^+(1s) + {\rm H}^+ + h\nu$
(\texttt{radiative\_electron\_capture\_Hepp\_from\_H}), the Maxwellian average
of the radiative charge-transfer cross section to He$^+(1s)$ of
\citet[Figs.~4--6, digitized, resolved resonances included; the paper gives
no rate coefficient]{West1982},
tabulated over $10^2$--$10^5$\,K and held at the ends:
$2.0\times10^{-13}$\,cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$ at $10^3$\,K and $1.65\times10^{-13}$ at
$10^4$\,K (MEASURED 2026-10-05); over $10^3$--$3\times10^4$\,K it is 16--20
times the constant $1.0\times10^{-14}$ of Kingdon \& Ferland (1996) carried
before 2026-09-26, which is the default value their section~2 assigns to every
reaction without a significant non-radiative channel and not an average of
these cross sections.
The table is a calculation above $10^3$\,K and an extrapolation below. The
photon carries the whole 40.81\,eV defect, so the reaction has no
collisional reverse and deposits no heat. It removes He$^{2+}$ from the He$^{2+}$
row, adds a proton to the H$^+$ row and, where row 2 is the He$^+$ stage
source (the molecular systems), adds He$^+$ there. All three are applied by
\texttt{he\_h\_cx\_fvec} under \texttt{He\_H\_charge\_exchange}, and by
\texttt{he\_h\_cx\_fvec\_adv} in the advection systems.

\paragraph{Penning ionization of the metastable.} The total
He($2^3$S)$+$H ionization rate coefficient (\texttt{ioniz\_HeI23S\_H}) is the
Maxwell-Boltzmann average of the \citet[Fig.~10]{Movre1997} total ionization cross
sections in the closed form \citet[Fig.~5 and network rows 198--199]{GarciaMunoz2025}
publishes,
\begin{equation}
Q_{31}(T) = 10^{-9}\exp\!\left[-\frac{86.4804}{T}
   - 0.286766\ln T + 0.0868445\left(\ln T\right)^{2}
   - 5.73001\times10^{-3}\left(\ln T\right)^{3}\right]\ \mathrm{cm^{3}\,s^{-1}},
\label{eq:ion:penning}
\end{equation}
smooth, positive and single-peaked at $3.8\times10^{3}$\,K, within 2 per cent
of the cross-section average over 200--$10^4$\,K by the paper's statement and
evaluated unclamped outside that range, where no accuracy is claimed. The
code notes at that site why the two-branch power law of \citet{Taylor2025}
Table 2 is \emph{not} used: it jumps by a factor 1.5724 at 4000\,K, which a
rate coefficient cannot do, and it disagrees with that paper's own Figure 19,
the average of the \citet{Cohen1971} and \citet{Morgner1979} cross sections;
the expression above agrees with Figure 19 to 10--20 percent, so the two
independent calculations agree and the published fit is the outlier.
The corresponding He($2^3$S)$+$H$_2$ coefficient is
$k = aT^{b}e^{c/T}$ with the \citet{Cohen1977} amplitudes of the
\citet{GarciaMunoz2025} network file, reproducing its Table A.5 to 0.13
percent. Both split 0.9\,:\,0.1 between the Penning and the associative
branch.

\subsection{Solving one cell}
\label{sec:ionization:solve}

\paragraph{The solver.} \texttt{solve\_ieq} (\texttt{newton\_solver.f90})
tries a damped Newton iteration with an \emph{analytic} Jacobian first
(\texttt{newton\_dense}: backtracking Armijo line search on
$\|f\|_2$, at most 60 iterations and 20 line-search steps), and on any
failure restores the entry guess and calls the in-tree MINPACK
\texttt{hybrd1} from the same guess, so the result can never be worse than
the forward-difference solve. Each residual routine ships its own exact
Jacobian; the photoionization rates are constants of the solve, so those
Jacobians are exact for the system actually solved
(Sec.~\ref{sec:radiation:rates}). Run-wide counters record how many cells each
route handled, and \texttt{EXHALE\_FORCE\_HYBRD1} forces the MINPACK path
for an A/B comparison. \texttt{use\_newton\_ieq} is on by default.

\paragraph{The sweep.} \texttt{ioniz\_eq} (\texttt{ionization\_equilibrium.f90})
takes the temperature and the total density as \emph{inputs} and overwrites
the species fractions in place; chemistry rearranges nucleons and electrons
and creates no mass, so the mass density is never corrected by this step and
the mass sum of the returned composition is a check against it
(\texttt{rho\_recon}), never a correction. A cell's photoionization rates
depend on the columns \eqref{eq:rad:column} outside it and on its own depth.
The sweep runs from the top down in field blocks of
\texttt{xuv\_field\_block\_cells} cells, a compile-time constant that is zero,
one block of the whole grid: every cell is solved in the field of the
composition the sweep was handed, and a positive width would carry the
columns of the cells already solved from one block to the next. Inside a cell
the pair (own attenuation, composition) is solved
\texttt{xuv\_self\_field\_passes} times, one by default, the loop stopping
early when the accepted stage fractions move by less than
\texttt{xuv\_self\_field\_tol} $=10^{-9}$. Neither setting changes a fixed
point: at the fixed point of the coupled source step the composition a cell
enters with and the one it returns are the same state, so every width and
pass count builds the same field, and only the number of passes of the
coupled loop differs (the measured costs that decided both defaults are at
their declarations in \texttt{ionization\_equilibrium.f90}). Cells within a
block are independent and the sweep is OpenMP-parallel with the cell
scratch declared \texttt{threadprivate}; the rate coefficients are evaluated
in blocks of a fixed even length (\texttt{xuv\_rate\_block},
\texttt{util\_ion\_eq.f90}), so that the vectorized transcendental calls pair
the same lanes at any thread count.

\paragraph{Acceptance classes.} Every cell's returned state carries a class,
and the contract is that classes 1, 2, 3 and 5 are \emph{roots} of the
requested equations (each passed the same two tests, a state inside the
element simplex and a normalized reaction residual at or below
\texttt{ieq\_res\_tol}) and differ only in how the root was found:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
class & meaning\\
\midrule
1 & root of the last attempt made\\
2 & root of the best earlier attempt\\
3 & root after projecting the closest attempt onto the element budget\\
4 & not a root; accepted under the relaxation amnesty and reported\\
5 & root of the constrained element-conserving continuation solve\\
6 & not a root, residual above \texttt{ieq\_nonroot\_res\_cap}: the cell
    \emph{keeps the composition it entered the sweep with}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A residual that is not a finite number is a class-4 non-root by
construction, since NaN passes every tolerance test. A consumer counting
states its chemistry does not describe counts classes 4 and 6 and nothing
else.

\paragraph{The constrained continuation.}
\texttt{constrained\_chemical\_equilibrium.f90} is the second formulation of
the molecular network, and it removes the possibility of an unphysical
iterate instead of repairing one. The unknowns are $u_k = \ln n_k$, so
$n_k>0$ for every iterate, converged or not (the logarithmic composition
variables of the CEA method, \citealt{Gordon1994}); element
conservation is an explicit residual row per element rather than a closure,
so a species carrying nuclei of two elements (HeH$^+$, OH, H$_2$O) satisfies
both rows at the accepted root; and $n_e$ is the charge-neutrality sum, never
an unknown. Because the network is bistable in its starting point (a
molecular basin in the shielded base, an atomic basin above the H$_2\to$H
front), the solve is a natural-parameter continuation \citep{Allgower2003} in the
radiation field:
$\lambda$ scales the field from six decades below the cell's own, where the
composition is known in closed form from the H$_2$ dissociation equilibrium
of the local $p,T$, up to $\lambda=1$ on the rungs $\lambda = 10^{-6}$,
$10^{-4}$, $10^{-2}$, 0.1, 0.3, 1, each rung starting from the previous
rung's root and a failed rung being bisected geometrically toward the last
root (down to a field step of $10^{-12}$, at most 40 solves for one cell).
$\lambda=0$ is not a starting point: the trace ionization stages of a shielded
base are linear in $\lambda$, their logarithms diverge there and the
Jacobian columns they own vanish. The rungs use the
general MINPACK \texttt{hybrd} and not \texttt{hybrd1}, whose fixed initial
trust radius $100\|x\|$ would open a first step of $e^{10^{4}}$ in every
density when $\|u\|\sim130$. A species whose fraction is imposed from outside
the cell (H$_2$ by the lower-boundary reservoir, the oxygen carriers by
carrier transport, H$^+$, He$^+$, He$^{2+}$ and He\,$2^3$S by the transported
ionization state of Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:advected}) is marked fixed: it
appears in every reaction and conservation row it belongs to, but it is not an
unknown and its own balance row is removed, so the system stays square.

\subsection{Ionization carried by the flow}
\label{sec:ionization:advected}

Where the recombination time is not short compared with the flow time, the
local balance of Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:cellsystem} is not the answer. Two
places in the code carry the ionization state along the flow instead.

\paragraph{The implicit advection systems.} \texttt{System\_implicit\_adv\_H},
\texttt{\_HeH} and \texttt{\_HeH\_TR} each take one step of
$v_{\rm el}\,{\rm d}x/{\rm d}r = R(x)$ into cell $j$, with the
variable-step second-order backward differentiation formula (BDF2):
$x^{\rm up} \equiv a_1 x_{j-1} - a_2 x_{j-2}$ is the history of the step and
$c_1 = g\,h_j/v_{{\rm H},j}$ (hydrogen row) and
$c_1^{\rm He} = g\,h_j/v_{{\rm He},j}$ (helium rows) its rate weights,
$h_j = r_j - r_{j-1}$,
$w = h_j/h_{j-1}$, $a_1 = (1+w)^2/(1+2w)$, $a_2 = w^2/(1+2w)$,
$g = (1+w)/(1+2w)$, so that the rates and the velocity are both those of
cell $j$. Each fraction is a fraction of one element's nuclei, which move
with that element's velocity: with element continuity
$\nabla\cdot(n_{\rm el}v_{\rm el}) = 0$ the steady stage balance
$\nabla\cdot(x\,n_{\rm el}v_{\rm el}) = S$ is
$v_{\rm el}\,{\rm d}x/{\rm d}r = S/n_{\rm el}$, with
$v_{\rm He} = v + J/(\rho Y)$ and $v_{\rm H} = v - J/[\rho(1-Y)]$, $J$ the
diffusive helium mass flux of the state and $Y$ the helium mass fraction
(both equal $v$ without element diffusion). The stage's own eddy term of
the transported stages is not in the step; its size against the advective
stage flux, $K_{zz}|{\rm d}\ln x/{\rm d}r|/v_{\rm el}$, is 0.035 at
1.3\,$R_p$ and below $10^{-3}$ beyond 1.75\,$R_p$ on the LHS~1140\,b
reference (MEASURED, update log stage~3 section~82). The first step of the recursion, and every step after a cell it
does not integrate, is backward Euler ($a_1 = g = 1$, $a_2 = 0$;
$x^{\rm up} = x_{j-1}$), as is a BDF2 step whose populations come out
negative. BDF2 is L-stable, so a population that relaxes within the cell
settles on its local equilibrium (the trapezoid rule, A- but not L-stable,
would alternate about it); \texttt{post\_process\_adv} states the formula,
its derivation and its zero-stability bound $w\le1+\sqrt2$. The systems
solve
\begin{align}
0 &= x^{\rm up}_{\rm HI}-x_{\rm HI}
   + c_1\left[-\left(\Gamma_{\rm HI}+\beta_{\rm HI}x_e n_{\rm H}\right)x_{\rm HI}
              + \alpha_{\rm HII}x_{\rm HII}x_e n_{\rm H}\right],
   \label{eq:ion:advh}\\
0 &= x^{\rm up}_{\rm HeI}-x_{\rm HeI}
   + c_1^{\rm He}\left[-\left(\Gamma_{\rm HeI}+\beta_{\rm HeI}x_e n_{\rm H}\right)x_{\rm HeI}
              + \alpha_{\rm HeII}x_{\rm HeII}x_e n_{\rm H}\right],
   \label{eq:ion:advhe}\\
0 &= x^{\rm up}_{\rm HeIII}-x_{\rm HeIII}
   + c_1^{\rm He}\left[\left(\Gamma_{\rm HeII}+\beta_{\rm HeII}x_e n_{\rm H}\right)x_{\rm HeII}
              - \alpha_{\rm HeIII}x_{\rm HeIII}x_e n_{\rm H}\right],
   \label{eq:ion:advhe2}
\end{align}
with $x_e = x_{\rm HII} + ({\rm He/H})_{\rm local}(x_{\rm HeII}+2x_{\rm HeIII})
+ x_e^{\rm metal}$, and the same He$\leftrightarrow$H charge-exchange terms
on rows 1, 2 and 3. The helium ratio is the global one on the legacy path and
the \emph{local}, radius-dependent one when the element diffusion is on,
since the global value would misstate $n_e$ by the separation factor. In
\texttt{System\_implicit\_adv\_HeH\_TR} the neutral helium unknown of row 2
is the ground singlet $x(1^1{\rm S})$, whose row is the summed He\,{\sc i}
balance less the metastable's, and the fourth row is the step of the $2^3$S
fraction with the terms of \eqref{eq:ion:tripletrow} on $c_1^{\rm He}$; the
singlet is an unknown of its own rather than a difference, which in a
strongly ionized cell whose neutral helium sits mostly in the metastable
loses every digit. The He$\leftrightarrow$H terms, the He$^{2+}+$H capture
included, are \texttt{he\_h\_cx\_fvec\_adv}. These
are the systems of the \texttt{\_adv} post-process
(Sec.~\ref{sec:postprocessing:adv}).

\paragraph{Transported ionization stages.} With \texttt{Ionization
transport: True} (default off) the ionization state of hydrogen and helium is
carried by the flow instead of being re-solved in every cell as a local
equilibrium. The carried fractions are fractions of the element's
\emph{nuclei}, $x_k = n_k/n_{\rm el}$: $x({\rm H\,II})$ of hydrogen and
$x({\rm He\,II})$, $x({\rm He\,III})$ of helium, carriers \texttt{H+},
\texttt{He+} and \texttt{He++} of the transport-chemistry operator
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:carrier}, \texttt{diffusive\_photochemistry.f90}); the
neutral stage of each element is the remainder and is not a row. The two
elements are carried together, because the electrons of a transported
hydrogen partition would otherwise meet a helium partition that never saw the
flow. The key needs helium in the mixture, and in a molecular gas it needs
\texttt{Molecular carrier transport: True} (the stage sources are then rows
(1)--(3) of the molecular network, whose molecular sinks are written at the
transported molecular densities); it is refused with \texttt{Coupled carrier
solve} (and its \texttt{On stall} form), whose coupled unknowns are mass
fractions and not fractions of the element's nuclei. It applies to an atomic
gas and to a gas with metals (\texttt{input\_read.f90}).

Stage $k$ of an element crosses the face $f$ between cells $j$ and $j+1$ with
(\texttt{ionization\_stage\_transport.f90}, equation 1)
\begin{equation}
F_k(f) = x_k(f)\,N_{\rm el}(f)
 - n_{\rm el}(f)K(f)\,\frac{x_k(j+1)-x_k(j)}{\Delta r(f)},
\label{eq:ion:stageflux}
\end{equation}
with $N_{\rm el}$ the nucleus face flux of the element operator
(\texttt{element\_nucleus\_face\_flux}: advection, gradient, eddy and settling
drift in one object, Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:diffusion}), $x_k(f)$ the face
fraction of the reconstruction, limiter and donor rule the element mass
fractions take, with the donor side chosen by the sign of $N_{\rm el}$ for
every stage alike, and the second term the stage's own eddy term relative to
its element (the element's eddy flux is already in $N_{\rm el}$). The closing
stage takes one minus the carried face fractions and minus the sum of their
eddy terms, so that $\sum_k F_k = N_{\rm el}$ at every face to rounding:
moving charge between stages moves no nucleus. The eddy term is zero at faces
0 and $N$, where the element crosses only with the gas. Every stage of an
element moves with the element's velocity; the drift of an ion against its
neutral in the ambipolar field is not carried, which the code bounds at the
routine: exact inside about $1.2\,R_p$ on the certified LHS~1140\,b atomic
state and of order 0.02--0.6 of the advective stage flux outward of it
(MEASURED, READ from the module header). The source of each row is the
balance the local sweep solves for the same gas, evaluated at the trial stage
densities on the frozen background of the last sweep: the rows of
\eqref{eq:ion:hrow}--\eqref{eq:ion:he2row} in their triplet form or the
molecular rows (1)--(3), the He$\leftrightarrow$H pair and the metal charge
exchange of the same reaction set and coefficients
(\texttt{charge\_exchange\_stage\_sources}).

The admissible set of the carried fractions is the simplex and not a box
(\texttt{stage\_simplex\_projection}): each $x_k\ge0$, and their sum is at
most the share of the element the stages may hold, one for hydrogen (whose
molecular nuclei the molecular carriers hold separately) and
$1-(n_{\rm HeH^+}+n_{2^3{\rm S}})/n_{\rm He}$ for helium, the HeH$^+$ bond and
a metastable the step holds frozen being no nuclei of the stage rows
(\texttt{carrier\_helium\_available\_to\_stages}). A carrier step that leaves
the set is returned to it by raising negatives to zero and then scaling the
carried fractions by the bound over their sum, which keeps their ratios and
so the charge sum they set; the amounts moved are measured and reported
(\texttt{stage\_simplex\_sum\_over\_limit}, \texttt{stage\_fraction\_under\_zero}),
never hidden.

In the sweep, every physical cell $j\ge1$ of a run with the key, once a
transport background exists or on a restart, has its $x({\rm H\,II})$,
$x({\rm He\,II})$ and $x({\rm He\,III})$ rows replaced by the constraints
$x - x^{\rm carried} = 0$, and the solve starts at the imposed values
(\texttt{impose\_transported\_ionization\_fractions},
\texttt{impose\_transported\_fractions}); the two lower ghosts are the inflow
reservoir, for which no key states an ionized fraction, and keep the sweep's
local root. In the marching loop the advection of the carried stages is the
divergence of the hydrodynamic face mass flux taken with the mass row inside
the Runge-Kutta stages \eqref{eq:ssprk3}, and the operator-split carrier step
then carries the element's diffusive part, the stage eddy term and the
chemistry; in the fixed-wind relaxation of the stationary partitioned route
(Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:partitioned}) the rows carry the whole nucleus flux
\eqref{eq:ion:stageflux}, and every sweep of the hydrodynamic solve is handed
the transported fractions. The certification
(Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:certification}) carries the entries \texttt{carrier
balance H+}, \texttt{He+}, \texttt{He++}, gated at $10^{-5}$ in the wind
cells, each row's residual against the sum of its own terms (the face-flux
divergence and the gross production and loss; a stage row that hands out only
its net reads $|S|$ there), and \texttt{ionization stage nucleus sum H} and
\texttt{He}, the identity $\sum_kF_k = N_{\rm el}$ gated at its floating-point
bound. The enthalpy the stage eddy terms carry is
\eqref{eq:mol:qst} of Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:interdiffusion}.

\paragraph{The transported metastable.} With \texttt{He 2\^{}3S transport:
True} (default off; it requires \texttt{Include He23S} and \texttt{Ionization
transport}, and is refused otherwise) the population of $2^3$S is carried as
a fourth helium state of the same operator, carrier \texttt{He2\^{}3S},
$x_3 = n_{2^3{\rm S}}/n_{\rm He}$ on the helium nucleus flux with the face flux
\eqref{eq:ion:stageflux},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial (n_{\rm He}x_3)}{\partial t}
 + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Bigl[r^2\Bigl(x_3N_{\rm He}
   - n_{\rm He}K_{zz}\frac{\partial x_3}{\partial r}\Bigr)\Bigr]
 = S_3 ,
\label{eq:ion:triplettransport}
\end{equation}
with $S_3$ the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:ion:tripletrow} (and the
He($2^3$S)$+$H$_2$ quench in a molecular gas), evaluated at the trial
population with the singlet $n_{1^1{\rm S}} = n_{\rm He} - n_{\rm HeII} -
n_{\rm HeIII} - n_{2^3{\rm S}} - n_{\rm HeH^+}$ closing the three carried
helium states; the helium simplex is then three-dimensional and reserves the
HeH$^+$ bond alone. In a steady state with $N_{\rm He} = n_{\rm He}v$ (no
element diffusion) and $K_{zz}=0$ this is the advection balance of
\citet[eq.~15]{Oklopcic2018}; the sweep's row
\eqref{eq:ion:tripletrow} becomes the constraint $x_3 = x_3^{\rm carried}$,
in row 4 of the atomic systems and row 8 of the molecular one
(\texttt{ieq\_triplet\_row}). A drift of the metastable against ground-state
He\,{\sc i} is not carried: the level moves at the velocity of the helium
nuclei. Its 19.82\,eV are chemical energy, booked by the reactions of the
level where they run (the $q_{13}$ cooling, the $q_{31g}$ heating, the Penning
heat), so no excitation-energy flux enters the energy equation; the
certification reports that flux relative to He\,{\sc i} without gating on it
($3\times10^{-7}$ of the energy row's largest term at most on the LHS~1140\,b
validation state, MEASURED, update log stage~3 section~81). The level's
certification entry is \texttt{carrier balance He2\^{}3S} at the carrier
tolerance, and \texttt{level balance He 2\^{}3S} is not applicable under the
key. Its row is one closed expression of sign-definite channels, so the scale
of its measure is the gross production
$n_e(n_{\rm HeII}\alpha_{2^3{\rm S}}+n_{1^1{\rm S}}q_{13})$ plus the gross loss
(\texttt{tr\_triplet\_row\_channels}) and the transport divergence, not the
net source: in the wind the two halves cancel to $10^{-8}$ at
1.2--1.4\,$R_p$, and a measure on the net asked the coupled state for
$4\times10^{-13}$ of the channel rates (MEASURED, update log stage~3
section~94). For the same reason the carrier relaxation judges the fixed
point of each carrier against that carrier's own largest fraction
(\texttt{carrier\_displacement\_on\_own\_scale}) and asks in addition that
the steady carrier residual stand at or below \texttt{carrier\_accept\_tol},
since an implicit step's displacement bounds its residual only by $n/\Delta
t$. The option is verified on the stationary route in a molecular gas; the
marching route with it and an atomic gas end to end were not exercised (update
log stage~3 section~81).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Excited hydrogen and the Ly$\alpha$ field}{Excited hydrogen and the Lyman-alpha field}}
\label{sec:ionization:n2}

\texttt{excited\_hydrogen.f90} solves the $2s/2p$ statistical equilibrium of
\citet[their eqs.~(12)--(13)]{Christie2013} as a
$2\times2$ linear system,
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix} L_{2p} & -M_{12}\\ -M_{21} & L_{2s}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} n_{2p}\\ n_{2s}\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix} S_{2p}\\ S_{2s}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
n_{2p} = \frac{S_{2p}L_{2s}+M_{12}S_{2s}}{L_{2p}L_{2s}-M_{12}M_{21}},
\label{eq:ion:n2pop}
\end{equation}
whose sources are the recombination cascade, collisional excitation from
$1s$, radiative pumping by the Ly$\alpha$ mean intensity and, in a molecular
gas, the dissociative recombinations ${\rm H_2^+}+e$ and ${\rm HeH^+}+e$ that
leave one hydrogen atom in $n=2$,
$k_5n_{\rm H_2^+}n_e+k_{16}n_{\rm HeH^+}n_e$
(\texttt{dissociative\_recombination\_n2\_source}, the rate coefficients of
the chemistry; zero when \texttt{EXHALE\_REACTION\_HEAT\_RECIPIENTS=0}, and
with the level off the 10.199\,eV it carries leave as radiation,
Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal:heat}), and whose
sinks are radiative decay, the two-photon continuum, collisional
de-excitation, $\ell$-mixing and photoionization by the stellar Balmer
continuum. The atomic data are in one place
(\texttt{hydrogen\_n2\_rates.f90}): $A_{2p1s}=6.2649\times10^{8}$\,s$^{-1}$
(NIST ASD, the nonrelativistic dipole value carrying the reduced-mass
factor), $A_{2s1s}=8.22461$\,s$^{-1}$ (\citealt{Drake1986} eq.~27, with the
finite nuclear mass and his relativistic factor), $g_{1s}=g_{2s}=2$, $g_{2p}=6$,
$f_{\rm Ly\alpha}=0.4162$, the $1s\to2s$ and $1s\to2p$ collisional rates
$C\,\Upsilon\,e^{-E/kT}/(2\sqrt{T})$ with the CHIANTI v11
effective collision strengths (the rates the H\,{\sc i} cooling uses too),
their detailed-balance inverses, and the case-B coefficient of the
ionization balance (the captures into $n\ge2$) split into $2s$ and $2p$ with
the case-B fraction of \citet[Table~I]{Pengelly1964}, $\alpha_{2s} =
f_{2s}(T)\,\alpha_{\rm B}$, $\alpha_{2p} = \alpha_{\rm B}-\alpha_{2s}$
($f_{2s} = 0.322$ at $10^4$\,K). The $2s\to2p$ $\ell$-mixing rate is
\begin{equation}
M_{2s2p} = n_e C^{e}_{2s2p} + n_{\rm HII}q^{\rm p}_{2s2p}
   + n_{\rm HeII}q^{\rm He^+}_{2s2p} + n_{\rm HeIII}q^{\rm He^{2+}}_{2s2p},
\label{eq:ion:lmix}
\end{equation}
$C^e$ the electron rate of \citet{Seaton1955} eq.~(55), his approximation V,
Maxwell-averaged in closed form with the same two final states and
separations ($5.78\times10^{-5}$\,cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$ at $10^4$\,K; the
\citeauthor{Christie2013} Table 2 rate, $5.35\times10^{-6}$, averages a
cross section whose constant is one tenth of Seaton's first-order dipole
constant, and is not used), and
the ion rates
the impact-parameter cross section of \citet{Pengelly1964b}, eqs.~(34)--(41),
Maxwell-averaged in closed form with their cut-off
$R_c = \min(1.12\hbar v/\Delta E, 0.72v\tau, R_D)$; the Lamb shift and the
fine structure set $\Delta E$ ($2s_{1/2}$--$2p_{1/2}$ 0.035\,cm$^{-1}$,
--$2p_{3/2}$ 0.331\,cm$^{-1}$), which is the smallest cut-off below
$n_e\sim4\times10^{12}$\,cm$^{-3}$. The closed form holds the lower
incomplete gamma functions $\gamma(2,x) = 1-e^{-x}(1+x)$ and
$\gamma(3,x) = 2-e^{-x}(x^{2}+2x+2)$, which cancel where they are small
($\gamma(n,x)\sim x^{n}/n$), so below $x = 0.25$ and $0.5$ respectively they
are evaluated by their power series in Horner form (\texttt{lower\_gamma\_2},
\texttt{lower\_gamma\_3} in \texttt{Cool\_coeff.f90}; error at most
$1.1\times10^{-14}$ on either side of the switch). The proton coefficient is
$4.8\times10^{-4}$\,cm$^3$\,s$^{-1}$ at $10^4$\,K, 8.3 times the electron
one, so $2s$ is mixed into $2p$ faster than it decays by two photons
wherever \mbox{$n_e = n_{\rm p}\gtrsim1.5\times10^4$\,cm$^{-3}$}. The removal of a $2p$
atom without emission of a line photon is
\begin{equation}
D_{2p} = n_e q_{2p1s} + \Gamma_{2p}
       + M_{2p2s}\frac{A_{2s1s}}
         {A_{2s1s}+n_eq_{2s1s}+M_{2s2p}+\Gamma_{2s}},
\label{eq:ion:d2p}
\end{equation}
$M_{2p2s} = (g_{2s}/g_{2p})M_{2s2p}$.

\paragraph{Balmer continuum.} The $n=2$ photoionization rate and the
photoelectric heating it delivers are integrals over
$[E_{\rm th}({\rm H},n{=}2), E_{\rm th}({\rm H})]$ of the run's own
spectrum with the hydrogenic cross section
$\sigma_2(E) = \sigma_2^{\rm thr}(E_2/E)^{3}$,
$\sigma_2^{\rm thr} = 1.4\times10^{-17}$\,cm$^{2}$ for $2s$ and $2p$ alike. The
code states no source for that value; it is, to its two figures, the $n=2$
threshold value of the Kramers cross section with the bound-free Gaunt
factor of \citet[eqs.~3 and 10]{Seaton1959}, $1.378\times10^{-17}$\,cm$^{2}$,
and the exact hydrogenic threshold values are $1.478\times10^{-17}$ ($2s$) and
$1.355\times10^{-17}$\,cm$^{2}$ ($2p$), statistical mean
$1.386\times10^{-17}$\,cm$^{2}$ (derived for this document); the $(E_2/E)^3$
form with a constant threshold value leaves out the rise of the Gaunt factor
above the edge:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_2 = \frac{\sigma_2^{\rm thr}E_2^{3}}{h\,\nu_{\rm eV}}
           \int F_E\,E^{-4}\,{\rm d}E,
\qquad
h^{(1)}_2 = \sigma_2^{\rm thr}E_2^{3}
           \int F_E\left(E^{-3}-E_2E^{-4}\right){\rm d}E.
\label{eq:ion:balmer}
\end{equation}
The quadrature resolves the field it integrates, which a fixed rule cannot:
for a loaded table the nodes are the table's \emph{own} rows, and each
segment is integrated in closed form because the interpolation is a power
law there; for the power law the whole band is closed form; only for the
smooth Planck field is a composite five-point Gauss-Legendre rule on a
geometric subdivision used. The feedback is lagged-explicit: it is computed
once per timestep before the ionization and energy solve, and the frozen
arrays are read inside \texttt{ioniz\_eq} and never inside its Newton
iteration. What that lag leaves is measured, not assumed: the certification
entry \texttt{level balance H(n=2)} is the production less the destruction of
each level for the populations held, at the composition of the certified
state (\texttt{excited\_hydrogen\_level\_residual}). The whole module is gated by \texttt{use\_excited\_H}, which is
armed by supplying \texttt{Stellar Teff [K]:} and
\texttt{Stellar radius [R\_sun]:}; it is \emph{off} by default.

\paragraph{The Ly$\alpha$ field.} The mean intensity that pumps $n=2$ has
three forms, selected by \texttt{jlya\_mode} (\texttt{excited\_H\_update}).
The default, mode 0, takes the estimate
$J_{\rm Ly\alpha} = 0.1\,F_{\rm LyC}/\Delta\nu_{\rm D}$ that
\citet[section~2, in the text after their eq.~6]{Huang2017} make near the
peak of the H photoionization, where $F_{\rm LyC}$ is the deposited Lyman
continuum flux and each ionization is assumed to be balanced by a
recombination producing a Ly$\alpha$ photon; EXHALE evaluates it at every
cell with
$F_{\rm LyC} = \xi_{\rm day}F_{\rm inc}(1-e^{-\sigma_{\rm LyC}N_{\rm HI}})$,
$F_{\rm inc} = L_{\rm EUV}/(4\pi a_{\rm orb}^2)$, the ionizing flux the whole
vertical H\,{\sc i} column absorbs, times the cell mean of
$1/(1+\tau)$ over the cell's own top-down line-center depth
(\texttt{lya\_photosphere\_attenuation\_cell\_mean}), so the pumping vanishes
below the Ly$\alpha$ photosphere. The column-integrated $F_{\rm LyC}$ and the
factor $1/(1+\tau)$ are EXHALE's own choices, not taken from
\citet{Huang2017}, who use the estimate only for an order of magnitude
($J_{\rm Ly\alpha} \sim 10^{-8}$\,erg\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$\,Hz$^{-1}$) and
compute the field itself by Monte Carlo transfer; mode 0 is an approximate
estimate.
Mode 1, selected by \texttt{Jlya RT file:}, reads an externally computed
$J_{\rm Ly\alpha}(r)$, for instance from the LaRT Monte Carlo code, and
interpolates it onto the grid. Mode 2, selected by \texttt{Jlya escape-prob:
True} and refused without \texttt{Stellar Lya flux [erg/cm2/s]:} $>0$, is the
in-line transfer of \texttt{lya\_rt.f90}, which closes the line with an
escape probability rather than a Monte Carlo, because the columns involved
reach $\tau_0 \sim 10^{8}$ at line center. With
$\Delta\nu_{\rm D} = \nu_{\rm Ly\alpha}\sqrt{2kT/m_{\rm H}}/c$, the Voigt
parameter $a = (A_{2p1s}/4\pi)/\Delta\nu_{\rm D}$ and the line-center depth
accumulated cell by cell from $n_{\rm HI}C_{\rm Ly\alpha}/\Delta\nu_{\rm D}$,
the escape probability of a photon emitted at the cell center is
\begin{equation}
\beta = \frac{1}{1+\langle N\rangle},
\qquad
\langle N\rangle = \frac{4\sqrt6}{\pi^{2}}
   \frac{\tau_{\rm up}+\tau_{\rm dn}}{2}\,\Psi(\xi),
\qquad
\xi = \frac{\tau_{\rm dn}}{\tau_{\rm up}+\tau_{\rm dn}},
\qquad
\Psi(\xi) = \sum_{n\ \rm odd}\frac{\sin(n\pi\xi)}{n^{2}},
\label{eq:ion:neufeld}
\end{equation}
which is \citet{Neufeld1990} eq.~(3.27) at zero continuum
destruction, reducing for a mid-plane source to \citet{Harrington1973}
eq.~(40), $\langle N\rangle = 0.909316\,B$. $\tau_{\rm up}$ is the depth of
the cell center from the top. By default the planet-ward face is a mirror,
$\tau_{\rm dn}=\tau_{\rm up}$ (Harrington's $B=\tau_{\rm up}$); with
\texttt{Lya absorbing bottom: True} (default off, mode 2 only) it is the rest
of the column down to the inner boundary, the molecular layer below acting as
a sink rather than a mirror. The form $1/(1+\langle N\rangle)$ and not $1/\langle N\rangle$ is used
because $\langle N\rangle$ counts absorptions per photon generated, so a
photon is emitted $1+\langle N\rangle$ times before it leaves; this is exact
at both ends and needs no join between the thin and thick limits. Under
$R_{\rm II}$-A redistribution $\langle N\rangle$ is independent of $a$, so
the Voigt parameter survives only in the one-flight penetration of the
stellar beam. A second channel runs beside the static one: the photon
Doppler-shifts out of resonance over the Sobolev length of the radial
velocity gradient, with the escape probability
$\beta_{\rm S} = (1-e^{-\tau_{\rm S}})/\tau_{\rm S}$,
$\tau_{\rm S} = (\pi e^{2}/m_ec)f_{\rm Ly\alpha}\lambda_{\rm Ly\alpha}
n_{\rm HI}/|{\rm d}v/{\rm d}r|$, on the radial ray (the angle average over
the direction dependence of the Sobolev depth is not taken). Its quotient
comes from the one evaluation of $(1-e^{-x})/x$ at \eqref{eq:rad:cellmean},
so $\beta_{\rm S}\to1$ as $\tau_{\rm S}\to0$ (the closed form used until
2026-10-01 returned 0 below $\tau_{\rm S}\approx10^{-16}$). The two combine
as $\beta_{\rm tot} = \beta + \beta_{\rm S}(1-\beta)$, and $\beta_{\rm tot}$
is the $\beta$ of \eqref{eq:ion:jlya}. The trapped internal field and the stellar term are
\begin{equation}
J_{\rm int} = \frac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}}\frac{g_{1s}}{g_{2p}}
   \frac{P\left(1-\beta\right)}{\left(A_{2p1s}\beta+D_{2p}\right)n_{\rm HI}},
\qquad
J_\star = \frac{\xi_{\rm day}F_{\rm Ly\alpha}}{4\pi\Delta\nu_\star}
          \left\langle T_s E\right\rangle,
\qquad
J_{\rm Ly\alpha} = J_{\rm int}+J_\star,
\label{eq:ion:jlya}
\end{equation}
with $P = \alpha_{\rm B}n_e n_{\rm HII} + C_{1s2p}n_e n_{\rm HI}$ the
internal production, $T_s = {\rm erfc}\!\left(x_1/\sqrt2 X_s\right)$ the
fraction of the broad stellar profile (Gaussian of Doppler width
$X_s$ set by \texttt{Lya stellar halfwidth [km/s]:}, default 70) whose wings
reach depth $\tau$, $E = 1+(b-1)(1-\beta_{\rm tot})T_s$ the bounded trapping
buildup of the penetrating stellar photons, with $b$ = \texttt{Lya stellar
boost [-]:} (default 5, an empirical factor the code states as tuned to
\citealt{Huang2023} Fig.~11, not a computed quantity), and
$\langle\cdot\rangle$ the mean over the cell's own optical depth. The published solution requires $(a\tau)^{1/3}>10$
(\citeauthor{Neufeld1990} section Va); cells outside that are counted by
\texttt{lya\_wing\_domain\_record} and are carried there by the thin limit
$\beta\to1$, which \eqref{eq:ion:neufeld} reaches smoothly.
