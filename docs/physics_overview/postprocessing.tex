\section{Output and post-processing}
\label{sec:postprocessing}

\subsection{The advection-corrected profiles}
\label{sec:postprocessing:adv}

For the reason given in Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:advected},
\texttt{post\_process\_adv.f90} re-solves the profile at the end of a
marching run (its Newton finish included) and in a stationary evaluation,
in ten outer passes (\texttt{n\_pp\_passes}), with the ionization advected:
the systems \eqref{eq:ion:advh}--\eqref{eq:ion:advhe2} are solved cell by cell
from the base outward, each cell taking its history $x^{\rm up}$ from the
solved fractions of the two cells below it and each element's stages
stepping on the velocity of that element's nuclei (below), and the
temperature is then re-solved on the steady internal-energy equation
\eqref{eq:th:teq} with the Brent bracket of Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal:tsolve}.
In a run that solved heat conduction or an enthalpy flux of the element, the
molecular carrier or the carried stage fluxes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:interdiffusion}), the heating of that equation holds them
too, evaluated with the corrected temperature
(\texttt{transport\_terms\_of\_profile}; the stage term enters with the
carrier term, \texttt{carrier\_enthalpy\_divergence\_of\_state}), and cell 1
conducts against the bath the run's conduction operator holds at the base
(\texttt{conduction\_base\_level\_T}, Sec.~\ref{sec:bcic:base}):
conduction \eqref{eq:transport_coeff} couples each cell to both
neighbors, so after the marching sweep the corrected cells are solved
together by a Newton iteration on the column (banded Jacobian, conductivity
and enthalpy flux divergence re-evaluated at each iterate); cells that keep
the run's temperature are fixed values of that solve, while a cell whose
own marching energy step found no root (outside the Brent bracket
$[0.05, 4]\,T_{\rm in}$, or the $[0.5, 2]\,T_{\rm in}$ band that guards the
metal-cooled residual against its spurious hot root) is an unknown of it,
started from the run's temperature and decided by the residual like the
rest (\texttt{cells\_marching\_unrooted} in the record;
\texttt{EXHALE\_ADV\_COLUMN\_EXCLUDE\_UNROOTED=1} makes such cells fixed
values again, for measurement).  That iteration stops when no temperature
moves by more than $10^{-10}$ of itself, or after 100 iterations, which is a
stopping rule and decides nothing; the column is then judged on its
residual: the energy row $R$ of every unknown, formed again at the final
profile with the transport terms of that profile, must satisfy
$|R| \le a_E + 10^{-6}S_E$, $S_E$ the largest of its physically grouped terms
(the advective energy divergence as one term, the $p\,{\rm div}\,v$ work,
heating, cooling, the conduction divergence and the two enthalpy
divergences, of the element fluxes and of the carrier and stage fluxes),
$10^{-6}$ the energy tolerance of the stationary
certification, and $a_E = 10\,\epsilon$ times the sum of the magnitudes of
the parts the row is assembled from (the grouped terms, the two halves of
the advective difference and the three face terms of the conduction
divergence; the stagnated $|R|$ MEASURED on three LHS~1140\,b conduction
states is 0.73--0.99\,$\epsilon$ of that sum).  The temperature band of the
cell-by-cell solve is not applied to the column: it refused a root of the
conduction-coupled equation (a cell at 10.35~$R_p$ whose row closes to
$9.5\times10^{-12}$ of its largest term at twice the run's temperature).
The column is rejected when the banded solve fails, a residual, step or
term is not finite, a temperature is not positive, or the residual test
fails; a rejected column keeps the marching profile and every one of its
unknowns is written \texttt{failed}, and the outcome, the reason, the
triggering cell, the worst residual and the number of cells without a
marching root are recorded in the \texttt{\# adv\_derived\_state:} line of
both \texttt{\_adv} headers.

What that acceptance covers is the column subproblem as it is solved, with
lagged coefficients: the conductivity and the element, carrier and stage
enthalpy divergences are formed on the \emph{input} composition and not on the
derived one, and the fine-structure escape probabilities are those of the
profile the pass started from, which the cooling written after the last
column forms again.  Three things are not tested: the full energy residual
of the derived state on one composition with its own coefficients, the
convergence of the ten outer passes, and the momentum balance of the
corrected pressure on the supplied $\rho$ and $v$.  An accepted column is
therefore recorded as \texttt{converged\_on\_residual} and the product as
\texttt{verdict=unverified}, \texttt{outer=unverified}, never as more; the
verdict is \texttt{rejected} when the column of the last pass was rejected
or any written row is \texttt{failed}, and a file without the line has an
unknown derived state, which is not a pass either.  After every
composition update of a pass (the H/He sweep with its cells restored to
equilibrium, and under \texttt{pp\_metals = 2} the metal re-solve and its
failure branch) the particle count, the electron density and the mean
molecular weight are formed again from that composition, then the ionized
fraction of the photoelectron partition, and only then the heating, the
energy solve and the output pressure (the written $p$ equals
$(n_{\rm tot}+n_e)kT$ of the written composition to $7.8\times10^{-16}$,
MEASURED on \texttt{wasp\_full} with \texttt{pp\_metals = 2}, against
$4.4\times10^{-8}$ before 2026-10-01).  Without these terms
the corrected temperature of a conduction-dominated layer answers another
equation (7900~K against the run's 5500~K at 1.2~$R_p$ on the fiducial
LHS~1140~b atomic case).

\paragraph{The step.} Both recursions are one-step integrations of an ODE in
$r$, ${\rm d}y/{\rm d}r = F(y, r)$, with the variable-step second-order
backward differentiation formula (BDF2),
%
\begin{equation}
  y_j - a_1 y_{j-1} + a_2 y_{j-2} = g\,h_j\,F(y_j, r_j),\qquad
  a_1 = \frac{(1+w)^2}{1+2w},\quad a_2 = \frac{w^2}{1+2w},\quad
  g = \frac{1+w}{1+2w},
  \label{eq:pp:bdf2}
\end{equation}
%
$h_j = r_j - r_{j-1}$, $w = h_j/h_{j-1}$: the left side over $g\,h_j$ is
${\rm d}P/{\rm d}r$ at $r_j$ of the quadratic $P$ through the three points,
so the step is exact for quadratics and the recursion second order in the
cell width (the formula of \citealt{LiLiao2022}, eq.~(1.4) of the preprint).
Every $r$-derivative of a step, the known density profile of the energy
equation included, is taken at $r_j$ by this rule, and every other factor
(rates, heating and cooling, the velocity of the residence time) is the value
at $r_j$.  The recursion starts, and restarts after every cell it does not
integrate, with backward Euler ($a_1 = g = 1$, $a_2 = 0$); a cell whose step
ratio exceeds the zero-stability bound $w \le 1 + \sqrt2$ is integrated by
backward Euler, and a BDF2 step whose populations come out negative or whose
temperature has no root is retaken by backward Euler, which keeps the
populations of a linear rate system nonnegative; the run log counts all
three.  BDF2 is L-stable, like backward Euler, so in the stiff limit a
population that relaxes within the cell returns its local equilibrium; the
trapezoid rule, A- but not L-stable, would alternate about it from cell to
cell.  Until 2026-09-27 every step was backward Euler with the rates at $r_j$
and the residence time at $r_{j-1}$, a first-order recursion that was the
main source of the first-order grid convergence of the He\,10830 equivalent
width.  MEASURED on nine certified LHS~1140\,b states (He/H 1.6, 1.8 and 2.13
on 500, 1000 and 2000 cells) with the state held fixed, the post-process
share of the equivalent-width error falls from $+3.0$/$+1.0$ per cent to
below $3\times10^{-4}$, the \texttt{\_adv} $2^3$S column converges at order
1.98--2.14 (1.00--1.01 before), and a closed-form column test gives 1.99; the
equivalent width itself moves by $-3.9$ per cent (500 cells) to $-0.97$ per
cent (2000 cells) at He/H $=1.6$ and still converges at first order, what
remains being the solved state's own base closure and the transit
integration.

Three properties of that pass are worth stating.

\begin{itemize}
\item The third term of \eqref{eq:th:teq}, the enthalpy flux carried by the
  divergence of the mass flux, $h\,r^{-2}{\rm d}(\rho v r^{2})/{\rm d}r$,
  vanishes for a stationary mass flux and for nothing else, so a correction
  built without it would describe a converged wind and no other state. The
  divergence is the mass row of the state itself, the face mass fluxes of
  its Riemann solve differenced over the cell,
  $(A_{j+1/2}F_{j+1/2}-A_{j-1/2}F_{j-1/2})/{\rm d}V_j$, so the term is an
  exact zero wherever the two faces carry the same flux. (Until 2026-09-27
  it was the same operator applied to the cell-centered $\rho v r^{2}$ of two
  neighboring cells, which at the base of a quasi-hydrostatic layer carries
  the odd-even pattern of the cell-centered velocity; its difference set the
  temperature of the lower layer.)
\item A cell is corrected only where the correction means something. Above
  the Damk\"ohler number $10^{2}$, formed with the \emph{slowest} relaxing
  species of the solved system, the gas reaches local equilibrium many times
  over while it crosses the cell and the equilibrium solution already solves
  the advection equation, so the cell is pinned to it. Below an equilibrium
  ion fraction of $10^{-6}$ the residual, which carries the neutral
  fraction, cannot represent the ion density to better than about a percent.
  Where the mass row of the supplied state exceeds
  \texttt{adv\_conditional\_tol} as a fraction of its own largest term, the
  cell keeps the run's own value; each cell's verdict travels in the output
  file as \texttt{adv\_T\_status}, \texttt{adv\_comp\_status} and
  \texttt{adv\_mass\_row}.
\item The reconstruction treats the gas as H/He plus trace metals and
  \emph{excludes} the molecular species: H$_2$, H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$ and
  HeH$^+$ are not passed in and are not re-solved, so inside a molecular
  layer the \texttt{\_adv} particle count omits the neutral H$_2$ and the
  molecular-ion electrons. The output file says so in its own header rather
  than only in the source.  The same holds for the energy balance: the
  post-process lacks the molecular chemistry heat and the H$_3^+$ and
  molecular band cooling, so wherever the heating and cooling it omits,
  measured on the state handed in as the difference between the run's own
  channels and its own at the run's temperature and composition, exceed the
  ones it carries, the row keeps the run's temperature with
  \texttt{adv\_T\_status} $=3$ (\texttt{unsupported}); MEASURED on the
  LHS~1140\,b molecular states, 196/389/776 rows on 500/1000/2000 cells, up
  to $1.134$--$1.137\,R_p$, with an equivalent-width change of at most
  $1.2\times10^{-4}$ of itself.  The balance of each population the
  advection systems solve is judged the same way (the composition class):
  the systems are molecule-free, so where H$^+$, He$^+$ or He($2^3$S) loses
  faster to H$_2$ (R10 and R13; R17 and R23; the He($2^3$S)\,$+$\,H$_2$
  ionization) than to the channels the systems carry, on the state handed in,
  the row keeps the run's composition and temperature with status 3 in both
  fields, and the metal re-solve of \texttt{pp\_metals = 2} skips it.
  MEASURED on the LHS~1140\,b molecular photochemical state, the rows it
  refuses at 1.005--1.08~$R_p$ had carried an $n({\rm He}\,2^3{\rm S})$ 70 to
  5600 times the run's.  The comparison has no free parameter, and passing it
  does not bound the omitted H$_2$ loss to a small error: it says only that
  the omitted loss is not the larger one.
\end{itemize}

The first of those gates has a fourth companion: wherever $v\le0$, or the
velocity of the hydrogen or helium nuclei is $\le0$, in the cell
or the one below it, the cell keeps the converged equilibrium ionization and
temperature, because the step takes its history from the cells below, which
are not upstream when the gas moves inward, and the residence time $h/v$ is
then negative as well. The relaxation rate
entering the Damk\"ohler number is the total rate at which each solved
population is destroyed and re-formed, and the \emph{minimum} over the
species the system solves is taken, because He($2^3$S) relaxes orders of
magnitude more slowly than hydrogen ($A_{31} = 1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$
sets the floor) and the vector is only as local as its slowest member. Each
element's rates are weighed with the residence time of that element's
nuclei, $h/v_{\rm H}$ or $h/v_{\rm He}$, the times its step uses.

\paragraph{The velocity of each element's nuclei.} The fractions the
advection systems step are fractions of one element's nuclei, and with
\texttt{He\_diffusion} those nuclei do not move with the mass-averaged
velocity: with $J$ the diffusive helium mass flux of the state
(\texttt{helium\_diffusive\_face\_flux}, the flux the element row of the run
is the divergence of; the cell value is the mean of its two faces, and faces
0 and $N$ carry none) and $Y$ the helium mass fraction,
\begin{equation}
v_{\rm He} = v + \frac{J}{\rho Y},\qquad
v_{\rm H} = v - \frac{J}{\rho(1-Y)},
\label{eq:pp:vnuc}
\end{equation}
and the hydrogen row of each step takes $c_1 = g\,h_j/v_{\rm H}$, the helium
rows $c_1 = g\,h_j/v_{\rm He}$ (both equal $v$ without element diffusion;
the ghost cells keep $v$).  With element continuity
$\nabla\cdot(n_{\rm el}v_{\rm el}) = 0$ the steady stage balance is then
$v_{\rm el}\,{\rm d}x/{\rm d}r = S/n_{\rm el}$.  MEASURED on the LHS~1140\,b
He/H\,$=2.09$ reference, $v_{\rm He}/v = 0.82$--0.97 and
$v_{\rm H}/v = 2.1$--1.08 over 1.3--4\,$R_p$, and stepping both elements on
$v$ had put the frozen-in He$^+$ fraction 3.5 per cent low and H$^+$ 6 per
cent high against the transported stages of the solution (update log
stage~3 section~82).  The stage's own eddy term $-n_{\rm el}K\,\partial
x/\partial r$ is not in the step, which is second order in $r$; the step is
valid where $v_{\rm el} \gg K|{\rm d}\ln x/{\rm d}r|$, a ratio MEASURED on
the same state at 0.035 (He$^+$) at 1.3\,$R_p$ and below $10^{-3}$ beyond
1.75\,$R_p$ for $K = 10^9$\,cm$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, and a run with a larger $K$
or a slower wind has to be checked against it.

\paragraph{What a refused row carries.}  A refused field is written with
the run's own numbers, exactly: a composition field that is not
\texttt{corrected} carries the handed-in species and metal columns, a
temperature field that is retained, unsupported or not evaluated the
handed-in temperature, and a row whose composition is refused and whose
temperature is refused but not failed carries the handed-in pressure,
heating and cooling too.  The one exception is the temperature of a
\texttt{failed} row of a rejected column, which is the marching profile of
that pass.  A row with a corrected temperature and a refused composition is
therefore not the solution as a whole.

\subsection{The mass-loss rate}
\label{sec:postprocessing:mdot}

The quantity the run reports as \texttt{Log10 of steady-state Mdot} is
measured at one radius, by \texttt{steady\_mass\_loss\_rate} in
\texttt{EXHALE\_main.f90}, on the state each route writes (the stored pair in
a stationary evaluation), and appended to \texttt{EXHALE\_setup.out}:
\begin{equation}
\dot M = 4\pi\,\rho(j)\,v(j)\,r(j)^{2}\,n_0v_0m_{\rm H} R_0^{2}
\qquad\text{at}\qquad j = \max(N-20,\,1),
\label{eq:pp:mdot}
\end{equation}
the offset of 20 cells keeping the evaluation far enough from the outer
boundary, and the floor at $j=1$ keeping it out of the base ghosts where the
boundary closure and not the wind sets $\rho$ and $v$. A logarithm is taken
only where the flow there is directed outward: $\rho v \le 0$ is an inflow,
which is not a mass-loss rate, and the run then reports the signed flux
instead of a logarithm. The dayside convention of Table~\ref{tab:2d} is applied here and only here:
$\log_{10}\dot M \to \log_{10}\dot M - \log_{10}2$ for
\texttt{Rate/2 + Mdot/2}, and $-\log_{10}4$ for \texttt{Mdot/4}.

A \emph{quantitative} $\dot M$ must come from a state the steady solver has
finished. A run stopped on the $\mathrm{du}$ threshold \eqref{eq:du} alone carries a
path-dependent spread of several percent in \eqref{eq:pp:mdot}; the two-stage
marching of Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics:reconstruction} followed by the stationary
solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:jfnk} is the working recipe, and it is what the
regression matrix uses.

\subsection{Output files}
\label{sec:postprocessing:files}

\paragraph{Which state the products are built from.} A solved
state's advection-corrected products are made by \texttt{Restart intent:
stationary evaluate} (\texttt{stationary\_state\_of\_the\_loaded\_restart} in
\texttt{EXHALE\_main.f90}), which loads the state (with its exact code-unit
file, Sec.~\ref{sec:bcic:restart}), certifies the stored pair and writes
every product from that pair without entering the time integration.  The
stored pair is the conserved state and the composition exactly as loaded,
with the temperature from $u$ through the caloric map of that composition and
the heating, the cooling and the two lower ghosts from one sweep that holds
the composition of the cells above the ghosts and hands it back bit for bit
(\texttt{sources\_and\_ghosts\_of\_the\_held\_composition},
\texttt{ioniz\_eq\_sources\_of\_held\_composition}).  The same routine
rebuilds these quantities for the joint test of every stationary outer pass
and for the certification of every state a steady route writes
(\texttt{certify\_the\_state\_to\_be\_written}), so a certificate is a
statement about the state in the file and its reload measures the same
numbers: the base continuity row reads the lower ghost with a gain of order
the inverse base Mach number, and a ghost left by the last sweep of a solve
at the temperature of an earlier iterate had moved a cell-1 mass row by 13
per cent of its rounding-anchored tolerance between a solve and its reload (MEASURED, update
log stage~3 section~94).  The products are \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt},
\texttt{Ion\_species.txt}, \texttt{conserved\_state.txt}, the channel
breakdowns and the mass-loss rate of the stored pair, the \texttt{\_adv} pair
derived from it, and \texttt{EXHALE\_resolved.out}.  One composition sweep
from the loaded state is then taken, and its movement and certification are
printed beside the stored pair's, entry by entry; it is neither written nor
the verdict. The evaluation exits with status 2 when the loaded state claims
to be stationary and the certification of the stored pair refuses it, 7 when
the input is not refused but the derived product is (a \texttt{rejected} or
absent derived-state record: a rejected column energy solve or a failed row
of the \texttt{\_adv} files), and 0 otherwise; the record is written into the
\texttt{\_adv} headers either way, and 7 is used by no other stop of the
code. The marching route keeps its exit status for the state it solved and
records the derived product only in the \texttt{\_adv} headers and the log.
The route this replaced took one time
step at \texttt{CFL:\ 1.0e-12} with \texttt{Do only PP:\ True}, which
suppressed the movement but did not remove it, reconstructed with whatever
\texttt{Reconstruction scheme:} stated rather than with the scheme the
solution was reached under, and wrote the stepped state back over
\texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt}. The difference is largest where the advection
operator is weakest: the operator's own weighting measure
\texttt{adv\_mass\_row} falls from $1.57$ to $1.7\times10^{-9}$ at the first
cell and from $3.1\times10^{-5}$ to $4.0\times10^{-14}$ at the last, while the
He\,I 10830 equivalent width moves by $5\times10^{-6}$ of itself.

Every file written to \texttt{output/} carries a schema header, so a reader
adapts instead of counting columns. \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} opens with
\texttt{\# EXHALE schema 2} and then
\begin{verbatim}
# columns r[Rp] rho[mH/cm3] v[cm/s] p[cgs] T[K] heat[erg/cm3/s] cool[erg/cm3/s]
\end{verbatim}
(with three further status columns on the \texttt{\_adv} file). Column 2 is
$\rho n_0$, the \emph{mass} density in units of $m_{\rm H}$ per cm$^{3}$
with the metals included under the \texttt{eos\_metals} policy, not a number
density. The first and last \texttt{Ng} rows of every file are ghost cells
filled by the boundary conditions, and their $\rho v r^{2}$ is a boundary
extrapolation. \texttt{Ion\_species.txt} carries the species fractions in the
\texttt{species\_table} order of Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:species};
\texttt{Heating\_breakdown.txt} and \texttt{Cooling\_breakdown.txt} carry the
channel decompositions of Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal}, each built from the array the
energy equation itself used, so the channel sum and the \texttt{heat} or
\texttt{cool} column of \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} are the same number to
round-off; \texttt{Excited\_H.txt}, \texttt{Lyman\_Werner.txt},
\texttt{FUV\_bands.txt}, \texttt{Oxygen\_chemistry.txt} and
\texttt{OI\_levels.txt} carry the optional physics;
\texttt{conserved\_state.txt} is the exact code-unit state beside every
written pair (Sec.~\ref{sec:bcic:restart});
the generation \texttt{pass\_state/<id>/} named by \texttt{pass\_state/current}
is the state of the last completed outer pass of an alternating stationary
run, never certified (Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:partitioned}). The two \texttt{\_adv} files carry a
\texttt{\# derived\_from:} line rather than the \texttt{\# coupling:} header
of a state file (item L9, 2026-09-17): they describe an advection-corrected
composition, which is not the composition any certification was made on, so the
input state's certification pair is carried there as provenance and not as a
claim about the numbers in the file. The Python loader
\texttt{examples/exhale\_io.py} reads all of them through those headers
(\texttt{exhale\_io.Run('output/')} gives \texttt{.r}, \texttt{.T},
\texttt{.Mdot} and the rest), and it should be used in place of any new
column parser.

\texttt{EXHALE\_resolved.out}, written in the run directory and not in
\texttt{output/} (\texttt{write\_resolved\_config},
\texttt{write\_setup\_report.f90}), is the machine-readable record of the
configuration the wind actually uses after \texttt{input.inp} and the
lower-atmosphere handoff are resolved (radius, mass, base temperature, He/H,
orbit, the grid and the physics switches), as \texttt{key value} lines to full
double precision; \texttt{EXHALE\_transit.py} reads it in place of
\texttt{input.inp}.  It is written at startup and again after the
certification of the written state, and then also carries the elemental
fluxes over the overlap window of that state and, when conduction is on, the
heat the wind conducts into the lower atmosphere through the base face,
$q = -\kappa_{1/2}(T_1 - T_{\rm bath})/(r_1 - r_0)$ in
erg\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$, positive leaving the wind
(\texttt{base\_conductive\_flux\_cgs}, with \texttt{base\_conduction\_T\_bath\_K}
and \texttt{base\_conduction\_T\_cell1\_K}; \texttt{base\_conduction\_state}
says \texttt{unmeasured} before the wind exists).  That flux is the base
face's own term of the conduction operator, $T_{\rm bath}$ the bath at
$r(0)$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:bcic:base}, measured on the state the certification
is made on (\texttt{heat\_conducted\_into\_lower\_atmosphere},
\texttt{viscous\_conduction.f90}) and not on the last residual evaluation,
which inside a Newton solve is a perturbed state; it is what the energy
coupling of the elemental-flux closure with the lower atmosphere reads
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mol:handoff}).

\subsection{The transmission spectrum}
\label{sec:postprocessing:transit}

\texttt{EXHALE\_transit.py}, with the library
\texttt{exhale\_transit\_lib.py}, builds transit transmission spectra from
either of the two states a run can leave, selected by
\texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_STATE}: the \texttt{\_adv} profiles by default,
or the solved state pair \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} and
\texttt{Ion\_species.txt} with \texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_STATE=solution},
which is how a stationary run is read.  The lines are the same either way:
the He\,{\sc i} 10830\,\AA{} triplet,
Ly$\alpha$, H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$, and the metal doublets Mg\,{\sc ii}
h\&k, Ca\,{\sc ii} H\&K and Na\,{\sc i} D, the metal lines being skipped
automatically for a metals-off run. It integrates the Voigt line opacity
over impact parameter, averages over the stellar disk, and convolves with
the instrument profile and the planet's rotation; the Balmer branch builds
the non-LTE $2s/2p$ populations with the level balance of
\citet{Christie2013} and the rate coefficients of the Fortran model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:n2} (transcribed in \texttt{exhale\_transit\_lib.py},
\texttt{n2\_populations}, and equal to them to $3\times10^{-13}$ relative), but with a field
of its own, because neither the solver's Ly$\alpha$ field nor its Balmer
rate is in the profiles the tool reads: $J_{\rm Ly\alpha}$ from a file or
the estimate of \citet{Huang2017}, and a diluted stellar blackbody for
the Balmer continuum. An
optional triaxial Roche geometry comes from \texttt{roche\_recon.py}.
Overrides are environment variables (\texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_*}: resolving
power, He window, rotation period, azimuthal samples) and the output keeps
the historical \texttt{tpm\_*.txt} names, each with its own
\texttt{\# transit\_schema 1} metadata block. The physics of that tool is
documented separately in \texttt{docs/transmission\_spectrum.pdf} and is not
repeated here.

\paragraph{Which state the spectrum is built from.}
The two selections are two answers for one column and not two formats of one
answer.  The \texttt{\_adv} pair is a post-process of a written state (the
end state of a marching run or the stored pair of a stationary evaluation): a
steady advective correction cell by cell, second order in the cell width
(variable-step BDF2 along the flow, each element's stages at the velocity of
its own nuclei), with no stage eddy term, refused in a cell where an
assumption of the steady equations fails.  It is an independent
discretization of the same column and no equation of the run was solved for
it.  The solved pair is the state the solver produced and the certification
judged; where the ionization stages are transported its composition is the
partition the stage transport itself produced, so an advective correction on
top of it would be a second and different transport approximation of the same
stages.  The stationary solve of a loaded state (\texttt{Restart intent:
stationary}) therefore writes no \texttt{\_adv} products and none are
manufactured for it by the transit tool; the evaluation of a solved state
(\texttt{stationary evaluate}) runs the post-process on its stored pair and
writes them, and where they are absent the default selection refuses by name
and names the other selection rather than reading it.  The two files of a
selection are read together, the O\,{\sc i} ground-term populations
included: the temperature of one state with the composition of the other
solves neither set of equations.  Every saved curve carries the selection,
the path, md5 and size of each file read, and the state's own provenance,
coupling, boundary model and reservoir lines.  The \texttt{\_adv}
selection is refused with exit status 7 when the profile's

\subsection{A model transit line against an observed one: the width-matching kernel}
\label{sec:postprocessing:widthmatch}

A one-dimensional steady model has no line-of-sight velocity field beyond
its radial outflow and its thermal motion, and its transit lines come out
narrower than the observed ones (on LHS~1140\,b He\,{\sc i} 10830 the model
red pair has a FWHM of about 0.27\,\AA{} against the measured 0.841\,\AA).
For every planet and every line compared with an observed transit profile the
model is therefore compared with the observation through one convention: the
line \emph{width} is matched by a Gaussian velocity convolution, and the
model is fitted to the observed \emph{equivalent width} of that
width-matched curve.  The convention is written once, for LHS~1140\,b
He\,{\sc i} 10830, in \texttt{LHS1140b/he10830\_equivalent\_width.py}, and a
comparison on another planet or line generalizes it rather than introducing
another.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The excess absorption $(\max T - T)/\max T$ is formed from the
  synthetic transit depth of \texttt{tpm\_He10830.txt} (column~3), which is
  already convolved with the planet rotation and the instrument at the
  instrument's own resolving power, on its own air-wavelength grid
  (\texttt{transit\_file\_air\_excess}).
\item That excess is convolved with a Gaussian of FWHM $f$ in velocity, in
  the planet rest frame and with no shift,
  $\sigma_\lambda = f\lambda_0/(2.35482\,c)$ with $\lambda_0 = 10830$\,\AA{}
  in air (\texttt{broaden\_excess}, \texttt{scipy.ndimage.gaussian\_filter1d}
  with its reflecting boundary, which conserves the absorption summed over the
  grid).  The instrument kernel is already inside the curve and Gaussians
  commute, so the order of the two convolutions does not matter.
\item $f$ is chosen for each model separately so that the extractor applied
  to the observation (for He\,{\sc i} 10830 the three-Gaussian
  \texttt{he\_line\_metrics.fit\_metrics}, whose width is the FWHM of the
  blended red pair) returns the observed FWHM of the same feature
  (\texttt{width\_matching\_fwhm\_kms}: Brent's method on $f$ in
  $[1, 60]$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ to $10^{-3}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$; on LHS~1140\,b
  $f\approx 22$\,km\,s$^{-1}$).
\item The equivalent width of the model is the window equivalent width of the
  broadened curve through the same operator as the observed one: the curve,
  converted to vacuum wavelengths, is interpolated linearly onto the
  observation's own samples inside the window (on LHS~1140\,b vacuum
  10832.60--10834.20\,\AA{} in the planet rest frame) and integrated with
  their trapezoid weights (\texttt{model\_equivalent\_width}).  Any fit of a
  planet parameter to the observed equivalent width (He/H, $\dot M$, the
  stellar EUV) is judged on this width-matched value.
\end{enumerate}
\texttt{he10830\_equivalent\_width.py --width-matched <tpm\_He10830.txt>
<output> [f]} writes the broadened excess with its record (the kernel FWHM,
the criterion, and the equivalent width and line metrics with and without the
kernel; \texttt{width\_matched\_product}).  In the equivalent-width record of
a state (\texttt{LHS1140b/models/.P1/fiducial\_conduction/ew\_of\_state.sh}),
\texttt{EW\_NONTHERMAL\_KERNEL=width\_match} adds \texttt{fwhm\_kms=} and
\texttt{EW\_kernel=} to each line of \texttt{EW.txt}, and \texttt{EW=} stays
the unbroadened window equivalent width; a number in place of
\texttt{width\_match} applies a given $f$.

The kernel stands for a velocity field that the one-dimensional model does
not contain.  It is not a computed physics term: it carries no energy or
momentum budget and moves no absorber, and its width is measured from the
data for each model rather than being a parameter of the model.  Every result
that uses it states $f$ and says so.  Because the reflecting convolution
conserves the absorption summed over the grid, the window equivalent width
falls as $f$ grows only by the absorption the broadened wings carry out of
the window.
