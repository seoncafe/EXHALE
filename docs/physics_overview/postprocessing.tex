\section{Output and post-processing}
\label{sec:postprocessing}

\subsection{The advection-corrected profiles}
\label{sec:postprocessing:adv}

For the reason given in Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:advected},
\texttt{post\_process\_adv.f90} re-solves the profile once, at the end of the
run, with the ionization advected: the systems \eqref{eq:ion:advh}--\eqref{eq:ion:advhe2} are solved cell by cell
from the base outward, each cell taking its history $x^{\rm up}$ from the
solved fractions of the two cells below it, and the temperature is then
re-solved on the steady internal-energy equation \eqref{eq:th:teq} with the
Brent bracket of Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal:tsolve}.  In a run that solved heat
conduction or the enthalpy flux of the element and carrier fluxes, the
heating of that equation holds them too, evaluated with the corrected
temperature: conduction \eqref{eq:transport_coeff} couples each cell to both
neighbors, so after the marching sweep the corrected cells are solved
together by a Newton iteration on the column (banded Jacobian, conductivity
and enthalpy flux divergence re-evaluated at each iterate); cells that keep
the run's temperature are fixed values of that solve.  That iteration stops
when no temperature moves by more than $10^{-10}$ of itself (or after 100
iterations), and the column is then judged on its residual: the energy row
of every unknown, formed again at the final profile with the transport terms
of that profile, must be within $10\,\epsilon\sum|{\rm terms}| + 10^{-6}S_E$,
$S_E$ the largest of its physically grouped terms and $10^{-6}$ the energy
tolerance of the stationary certification.  It is rejected when the banded
solve fails, a residual, step or term is not finite, a temperature is not
positive, or the residual test fails; a rejected column keeps the marching
profile and every one of its cells is written \texttt{failed}, and the
outcome, the reason, the triggering cell and the worst residual are recorded
in the \texttt{\# adv\_derived\_state:} line of the \texttt{\_adv} headers.
The ten outer passes are not tested for convergence, so an accepted column is
recorded as \texttt{converged\_on\_residual} and the outer iteration as
\texttt{unverified}.  Without these terms
the corrected temperature of a conduction-dominated layer answers another
equation (7900~K against the run's 5500~K at 1.2~$R_p$ on the fiducial
LHS~1140~b atomic case).

\paragraph{The step.} Both recursions are one-step integrations of an ODE in
$r$, ${\rm d}y/{\rm d}r = F(y, r)$, with the variable-step second-order
backward differentiation formula (BDF2),
%
\begin{equation}
  y_j - a_1 y_{j-1} + a_2 y_{j-2} = g\,h_j\,F(y_j, r_j),\qquad
  a_1 = \frac{(1+w)^2}{1+2w},\quad a_2 = \frac{w^2}{1+2w},\quad
  g = \frac{1+w}{1+2w},
  \label{eq:pp:bdf2}
\end{equation}
%
$h_j = r_j - r_{j-1}$, $w = h_j/h_{j-1}$: the left side over $g\,h_j$ is
${\rm d}P/{\rm d}r$ at $r_j$ of the quadratic $P$ through the three points,
so the step is exact for quadratics and the recursion second order in the
cell width (the formula of \citealt{LiLiao2022}, eq.~(1.4) of the preprint).
Every $r$-derivative of a step, the known density profile of the energy
equation included, is taken at $r_j$ by this rule, and every other factor
(rates, heating and cooling, the velocity of the residence time) is the value
at $r_j$.  The recursion starts, and restarts after every cell it does not
integrate, with backward Euler ($a_1 = g = 1$, $a_2 = 0$); a cell whose step
ratio exceeds the zero-stability bound $w \le 1 + \sqrt2$ is integrated by
backward Euler, and a BDF2 step whose populations come out negative or whose
temperature has no root is retaken by backward Euler, which keeps the
populations of a linear rate system nonnegative; the run log counts all
three.  BDF2 is L-stable, like backward Euler, so in the stiff limit a
population that relaxes within the cell returns its local equilibrium; the
trapezoid rule, A- but not L-stable, would alternate about it from cell to
cell.  Until 2026-09-27 every step was backward Euler with the rates at $r_j$
and the residence time at $r_{j-1}$, a first-order recursion that was the
main source of the first-order grid convergence of the He\,10830 equivalent
width.  MEASURED on nine certified LHS~1140\,b states (He/H 1.6, 1.8 and 2.13
on 500, 1000 and 2000 cells) with the state held fixed, the post-process
share of the equivalent-width error falls from $+3.0$/$+1.0$ per cent to
below $3\times10^{-4}$, the \texttt{\_adv} $2^3$S column converges at order
1.98--2.14 (1.00--1.01 before), and a closed-form column test gives 1.99; the
equivalent width itself moves by $-3.9$ per cent (500 cells) to $-0.97$ per
cent (2000 cells) at He/H $=1.6$ and still converges at first order, what
remains being the solved state's own base closure and the transit
integration.

Three properties of that pass are worth stating.

\begin{itemize}
\item The third term of \eqref{eq:th:teq}, the enthalpy flux carried by the
  divergence of the mass flux, $h\,r^{-2}{\rm d}(\rho v r^{2})/{\rm d}r$,
  vanishes for a stationary mass flux and for nothing else, so a correction
  built without it would describe a converged wind and no other state. The
  divergence is the mass row of the state itself, the face mass fluxes of
  its Riemann solve differenced over the cell,
  $(A_{j+1/2}F_{j+1/2}-A_{j-1/2}F_{j-1/2})/{\rm d}V_j$, so the term is an
  exact zero wherever the two faces carry the same flux. (Until 2026-09-27
  it was the same operator applied to the cell-centered $\rho v r^{2}$ of two
  neighboring cells, which at the base of a quasi-hydrostatic layer carries
  the odd-even pattern of the cell-centered velocity; its difference set the
  temperature of the lower layer.)
\item A cell is corrected only where the correction means something. Above
  the Damk\"ohler number $10^{2}$, formed with the \emph{slowest} relaxing
  species of the solved system, the gas reaches local equilibrium many times
  over while it crosses the cell and the equilibrium solution already solves
  the advection equation, so the cell is pinned to it. Below an equilibrium
  ion fraction of $10^{-6}$ the residual, which carries the neutral
  fraction, cannot represent the ion density to better than about a percent.
  Where the mass row of the supplied state exceeds
  \texttt{adv\_conditional\_tol} as a fraction of its own largest term, the
  cell keeps the run's own value; each cell's verdict travels in the output
  file as \texttt{adv\_T\_status}, \texttt{adv\_comp\_status} and
  \texttt{adv\_mass\_row}.
\item The reconstruction treats the gas as H/He plus trace metals and
  \emph{excludes} the molecular species: H$_2$, H$_2^+$, H$_3^+$ and
  HeH$^+$ are not passed in and are not re-solved, so inside a molecular
  layer the \texttt{\_adv} particle count omits the neutral H$_2$ and the
  molecular-ion electrons. The output file says so in its own header rather
  than only in the source.  The same holds for the energy balance: the
  post-process lacks the molecular chemistry heat and the H$_3^+$ and
  molecular band cooling, so wherever the heating and cooling it omits,
  measured on the state handed in as the difference between the run's own
  channels and its own at the run's temperature and composition, exceed the
  ones it carries, the row keeps the run's temperature with
  \texttt{adv\_T\_status} $=3$ (\texttt{unsupported}); MEASURED on the
  LHS~1140\,b molecular states, 196/389/776 rows on 500/1000/2000 cells, up
  to $1.134$--$1.137\,R_p$, with an equivalent-width change of at most
  $1.2\times10^{-4}$ of itself.
\end{itemize}

The first of those gates has a fourth companion: wherever $v\le0$ in the cell
or the one below it, the cell keeps the converged equilibrium ionization and
temperature, because the step takes its history from the cells below, which
are not upstream when the gas moves inward, and the residence time $h/v$ is
then negative as well. The relaxation rate
entering the Damk\"ohler number is the total rate at which each solved
population is destroyed and re-formed, and the \emph{minimum} over the
species the system solves is taken, because He($2^3$S) relaxes orders of
magnitude more slowly than hydrogen ($A_{31} = 1.272\times10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$
sets the floor) and the vector is only as local as its slowest member.

\subsection{The mass-loss rate}
\label{sec:postprocessing:mdot}

The quantity the run reports as \texttt{Log10 of steady-state Mdot} is
measured at one radius, in \texttt{EXHALE\_main.f90}:
\begin{equation}
\dot M = 4\pi\,\rho(j)\,v(j)\,r(j)^{2}\,n_0v_0m_{\rm H} R_0^{2}
\qquad\text{at}\qquad j = \max(N-20,\,1),
\label{eq:pp:mdot}
\end{equation}
the offset of 20 cells keeping the evaluation far enough from the outer
boundary, and the floor at $j=1$ keeping it out of the base ghosts where the
boundary closure and not the wind sets $\rho$ and $v$. A logarithm is taken
only where the flow there is directed outward: $\rho v \le 0$ is an inflow,
which is not a mass-loss rate, and the run then reports the signed flux
instead of a logarithm. The dayside convention of Table~\ref{tab:2d} is applied here and only here:
$\log_{10}\dot M \to \log_{10}\dot M - \log_{10}2$ for
\texttt{Rate/2 + Mdot/2}, and $-\log_{10}4$ for \texttt{Mdot/4}.

A \emph{quantitative} $\dot M$ must come from a state the steady solver has
finished. A run stopped on the $\mathrm{du}$ threshold \eqref{eq:du} alone carries a
path-dependent spread of several percent in \eqref{eq:pp:mdot}; the two-stage
marching of Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics:reconstruction} followed by the stationary
solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:jfnk} is the working recipe, and it is what the
regression matrix uses.

\subsection{Output files}
\label{sec:postprocessing:files}

\paragraph{Which state the products are built from (2026-09-17).} A solved
state's advection-corrected products are made by \texttt{Restart intent:
stationary evaluate}, which loads the state, derives its pressure and
temperature from the loaded conserved state, runs the ionization sweep,
evaluates the certification and writes every product from that work state
without entering the time integration. The route this replaced took one time
step at \texttt{CFL:\ 1.0e-12} with \texttt{Do only PP:\ True}, which
suppressed the movement but did not remove it, reconstructed with whatever
\texttt{Reconstruction scheme:} stated rather than with the scheme the
solution was reached under, and wrote the stepped state back over
\texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt}. The difference is largest where the advection
operator is weakest: the operator's own weighting measure
\texttt{adv\_mass\_row} falls from $1.57$ to $1.7\times10^{-9}$ at the first
cell and from $3.1\times10^{-5}$ to $4.0\times10^{-14}$ at the last, while the
He\,I 10830 equivalent width moves by $5\times10^{-6}$ of itself.

Every file written to \texttt{output/} carries a schema header, so a reader
adapts instead of counting columns. \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} opens with
\texttt{\# EXHALE schema 2} and then
\begin{verbatim}
# columns r[Rp] rho[mH/cm3] v[cm/s] p[cgs] T[K] heat[erg/cm3/s] cool[erg/cm3/s]
\end{verbatim}
(with three further status columns on the \texttt{\_adv} file). Column 2 is
$\rho n_0$, the \emph{mass} density in units of $m_{\rm H}$ per cm$^{3}$
with the metals included under the \texttt{eos\_metals} policy, not a number
density. The first and last \texttt{Ng} rows of every file are ghost cells
filled by the boundary conditions, and their $\rho v r^{2}$ is a boundary
extrapolation. \texttt{Ion\_species.txt} carries the species fractions in the
\texttt{species\_table} order of Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:species};
\texttt{Heating\_breakdown.txt} and \texttt{Cooling\_breakdown.txt} carry the
channel decompositions of Sec.~\ref{sec:thermal}, each built from the array the
energy equation itself used, so the channel sum and the \texttt{heat} or
\texttt{cool} column of \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} are the same number to
round-off; \texttt{Excited\_H.txt}, \texttt{Lyman\_Werner.txt},
\texttt{FUV\_bands.txt}, \texttt{Oxygen\_chemistry.txt} and
\texttt{OI\_levels.txt} carry the optional physics;
the generation \texttt{pass\_state/<id>/} named by \texttt{pass\_state/current}
is the state of the last completed outer pass of an alternating stationary
run, never certified (Sec.~\ref{sec:steady:partitioned}). The two \texttt{\_adv} files carry a
\texttt{\# derived\_from:} line rather than the \texttt{\# coupling:} header
of a state file (item L9, 2026-09-17): they describe an advection-corrected
composition, which is not the composition any certification was made on, so the
input state's certification pair is carried there as provenance and not as a
claim about the numbers in the file. The Python loader
\texttt{examples/exhale\_io.py} reads all of them through those headers
(\texttt{exhale\_io.Run('output/')} gives \texttt{.r}, \texttt{.T},
\texttt{.Mdot} and the rest), and it should be used in place of any new
column parser.

\subsection{The transmission spectrum}
\label{sec:postprocessing:transit}

\texttt{EXHALE\_transit.py}, with the library
\texttt{exhale\_transit\_lib.py}, builds transit transmission spectra from
either of the two states a run can leave, selected by
\texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_STATE}: the \texttt{\_adv} profiles by default,
or the solved state pair \texttt{Hydro\_ioniz.txt} and
\texttt{Ion\_species.txt} with \texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_STATE=solution},
which is how a stationary run is read.  The lines are the same either way:
the He\,{\sc i} 10830\,\AA{} triplet,
Ly$\alpha$, H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$, and the metal doublets Mg\,{\sc ii}
h\&k, Ca\,{\sc ii} H\&K and Na\,{\sc i} D, the metal lines being skipped
automatically for a metals-off run. It integrates the Voigt line opacity
over impact parameter, averages over the stellar disk, and convolves with
the instrument profile and the planet's rotation; the Balmer branch builds
the non-LTE $2s/2p$ populations with the level balance of
\citet{Christie2013} and the rate coefficients of the Fortran model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ionization:n2} (transcribed in \texttt{exhale\_transit\_lib.py},
\texttt{n2\_populations}, and equal to them to $3\times10^{-13}$ relative), but with a field
of its own, because neither the solver's Ly$\alpha$ field nor its Balmer
rate is in the profiles the tool reads: $J_{\rm Ly\alpha}$ from a file or
the estimate of Huang et al.\ (2017), and a diluted stellar blackbody for
the Balmer continuum. An
optional triaxial Roche geometry comes from \texttt{roche\_recon.py}.
Overrides are environment variables (\texttt{EXHALE\_TRANSIT\_*}: resolving
power, He window, rotation period, azimuthal samples) and the output keeps
the historical \texttt{tpm\_*.txt} names, each with its own
\texttt{\# transit\_schema 1} metadata block. The physics of that tool is
documented separately in \texttt{docs/transmission\_spectrum.pdf} and is not
repeated here.

\paragraph{Which state the spectrum is built from.}
The two selections are two answers for one column and not two formats of one
answer.  The \texttt{\_adv} pair is a post-process of a marching state: a
steady advective correction cell by cell, second order in the cell width
(variable-step BDF2 along the flow at the bulk velocity), with no eddy term and no element drift, refused in a cell where an
assumption of the steady equations fails.  It is an independent
discretization of the same column and no equation of the run was solved for
it.  The solved pair is the state the solver produced and the certification
judged; where the ionization stages are transported its composition is the
partition the stage transport itself produced, so an advective correction on
top of it would be a second and different transport approximation of the same
stages.  A stationary route therefore writes no \texttt{\_adv} products,
none are manufactured for one, and the default selection refuses by name and
names the other selection rather than reading it.  The two files of a
selection are read together, the O\,{\sc i} ground-term populations
included: the temperature of one state with the composition of the other
solves neither set of equations.  Every saved curve carries the selection,
the path, md5 and size of each file read, and the state's own provenance,
coupling, boundary model and reservoir lines.

What the choice costs was MEASURED on \texttt{wasp\_full}, which carries
both states, as the peak transit depth relative to itself (before the
atomic-data corrections of 2026-09-24/25, which moved both states, and
before the post-processor took the solver's $n=2$ rates; not re-measured
since): He\,{\sc i} 10830
$-0.64$ per cent, H$\alpha$ $-1.28$, H$\beta$ $-3.48$, Ly$\alpha$ unchanged
at its saturated ceiling, and the optically thin metal lines $+0.1$ to $+0.8$.
The metastable helium column is 12 per cent \emph{larger} in the solved
state and its line is nonetheless shallower, because the advection correction
moves that column outward (column-weighted mean radius $1.2941$ against
$1.3112\,R_p$) and a saturated line's disk-averaged depth follows how far
the absorbing shell reaches; H$\beta$ moves most because it is the line still
on the linear part of the curve of growth (item P6f).
